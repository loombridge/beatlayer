## The behaviour

Wavetable plays two wavetable oscillators and a sub oscillator into two filters. A wavetable is a series of single-cycle waves, its frames; Position chooses where in the series an oscillator reads, and moving it, by hand or by modulation, morphs the sound through the frames. Each oscillator chooses its table from a category and a list, or takes a sample dropped on it as a table of its own, and shapes it with one of three effects, each with two controls: FM, a sine phase-modulating the oscillator; Classic, pulse width and hard sync; and Modern, warp and wavefolding. The sub oscillator adds a simple wave one or two octaves down. The filters run in series, in parallel or split between the oscillators.

Three envelopes, two LFOs, the MIDI sources and MPE reach the device's parameters through the Matrix tab, a grid of sources across and destinations down. Unison stacks copies of each oscillator in one of six modes, and Hi-Quality, an option in the title bar's menu, trades processing for smoother modulation and less aliasing.

Beatlayer's factory wavetables are its own: made by a generator in the repository from formulae and additive spectra, built when first used and shared between every Wavetable in the set, with categories and names of Beatlayer's own. No table is stored as an audio file in the repository.

## Rules

**Wavetables (WP-8.8)**

- **R1.** Beatlayer's factory wavetables are made by a generator in the repository from formulae and additive spectra: morphs between basic shapes, harmonic series, pulse-width and sync sweeps, FM spectra, vowel-like formant sweeps from published formant frequencies, digital and bit-reduced shapes, and noise-like tables. Each has a Beatlayer name in one of Beatlayer's own categories (Shape Morphs, Additive, Pulse and Sync, FM, Voices, Bit and Step, Grain and Noise), up to 256 frames of 2,048 samples, and an interpolation between frames (none, crossfade or spectral morph). No factory table is a file in the repository. (S9, S10; the categories and names are Beatlayer's own)
- **R2.** The generator makes every table bit-identically on every run and on every platform: its maths uses `libm`, and its random tables follow fixed seeds; a table's hash is the same on macOS, Windows and Linux. (S9)
- **R3.** A factory table is built the first time a device in the session uses it, off the audio thread, into the plug-in kit's process-wide cache, and shared by every device that loads it; an oscillator keeps playing its previous table until the new one is built, and nothing is freed on the audio thread. (S10)
- **R4.** Every frame of every table, factory or user, plays band-limited at every key: a frame at the top key folds back nothing above −80 dB. (S10)
- **R5.** An oscillator's choosers list the categories, then the tables of the chosen category in name order; the arrows step to the next or previous table, crossing into the next or previous category at a category's end. (S1)
- **R6.** A WAV or AIFF file dropped on an oscillator, or named by `device.load_wavetable`, becomes that oscillator's user table: a WAV with a `clm ` chunk is cut into frames of the chunk's frame size, each resampled to 2,048 samples; any other file is cut into consecutive frames of 2,048 samples from its start, up to 256, a last part shorter than a frame dropped, and a file shorter than one frame resampled to fill one. With Raw off (the default) each frame's DC offset is removed and its ends are made to meet over its last 32 samples, so the frame loops without a click; with Raw on the frames are used as they are. (S1, S10; the frame size for a plain file and the clean-up are Beatlayer's own)
- **R7.** A user table is a file the set references, as a sample is: the set saves its path, a missing file is reported with the set's other missing files, and Collect All and Save copies it into the project folder; a reopened set builds the same table from it. After a user table loads, the choosers browse the file's folder, its WAV and AIFF files in name order. (S1, S11)
- **R8.** `device.load_wavetable` (`edit`) loads a table into an oscillator (`oscillator` 1 or 2) by its factory `category` and `name` or by a `file` path, with `raw` for a file, as one undo step, Load Wavetable. It's refused for an unknown table, an unreadable file or a file with no audio. The query tool's `device` result reports each oscillator's table under `wavetables`: its category and name, or its file and Raw, and its frame count. (S1, S11)
- **R9.** A table's frames between two whole frames are read by its interpolation: a factory table's own, and crossfade for a user table. (S10)
- **R10.** Loading a table builds it on a worker thread; an oscillator whose table is being built plays its previous table, and a set opened with user tables plays each from the moment it is built. (S10, S11)

**Wavetable, part 1 (WP-8.9)**

- **R11.** Beatlayer ships Wavetable (`beatlayer.wavetable`), an instrument listed under the browser's Instruments label, with factory presets of Beatlayer's own. (S12)
- **R12.** Each of the two oscillators has its switch (Oscillator 1 on and Oscillator 2 off in a new device), its table choosers (R5), Position (0 to 100 %, 0 % by default), Gain (−∞ to 0 dB, 0 dB), Pan (−50 to 50, centre), Semi (−36 to +36 semitones, 0) and Detune (−50 to +50 cents, 0), its pitch added to the global Transpose. (S1; the ranges are Beatlayer's own)
- **R13.** Position reads frame Position × (frames − 1) of its table, between frames by the table's interpolation (R9); it is a per-frame input to the plug-in kit's oscillator, so a Position swept at audio rate by the matrix scans the frames without a click or a step. (S1, S10)
- **R14.** Each oscillator's effect is None, FM, Classic or Modern, each with two controls from 0 to 100 % (0 % by default); each control keeps its value when the effect changes. FM: Amount phase-modulates the oscillator by a sine at index 4 × Amount; Tune, from −100 % to 100 %, sets the sine's frequency to the note's times 2^(2 × Tune), so ±50 % is an octave up or down, ±100 % two octaves, and the values between are inharmonic. Classic: PW narrows the wave's first half towards a tenth of the cycle (pulse width for any table); Sync resets the oscillator's phase from a hidden oscillator, raising the audible wave's ratio to the hidden one from 1 to 8 as Sync goes from 0 to 100 %. Modern: Warp bends the phase like PW but smoothly, shifting the wave's centre; Fold drives the wave through a sine wavefolder at up to 8 times its level. Each effect is band-limited and oversampled where the kit's shaper needs it. (S1, S10; the laws are Beatlayer's own readings)
- **R15.** The sub oscillator, with its switch (off in a new device), plays one wave per voice: Gain (−∞ to 0 dB), Tone (0 % a sine, 100 % a wave with odd harmonics falling at 6 dB per octave, morphing between) and Octave (−1 or −2, −1 by default). (S2; the Tone law is Beatlayer's own)
- **R16.** Filters 1 and 2 are the instruments' filter (instrument filter R1 to R13), Filter 1 on and Filter 2 off in a new device, routed Serial (the default), Parallel or Split exactly as S3 says; in Split the sub goes half to each, and an off filter passes its oscillator through. (S3)
- **R17.** The Amp envelope has Attack, Decay, Sustain and Release, Attack, Decay and Release each with a slope (R27), shaping each voice's level; a voice ends when it reaches silence after its release. (S5)
- **R18.** Poly and Mono (Poly by default): Poly plays up to Poly Voices (1 to 16, 8 by default) through the voice section (instrument voices R3 to R7); Mono plays one voice with legato envelopes, gliding over Glide (0 ms to 5 s, 0 ms) to each new note, Glide shown greyed in Poly. Transpose (−48 to +48 semitones) and Volume (−∞ to 0 dB) act on the whole device. (S7, S8; the ranges are Beatlayer's own)
- **R19.** Unison Voices (2 to 8 copies per oscillator, 4 by default) and Amount (0 to 100 %) stack each oscillator's copies; in part 1 every mode plays Classic's law (R30), and part 2 adds the rest. (S7)
- **R20.** Sixteen voices with eight unison copies on both oscillators render at 48 kHz inside the device budget, and nothing on the audio thread allocates, locks or blocks; the device's output is the same at any block size. (S12)
- **R21.** Every control is a parameter, automatable and modulatable (rule G2), and Wavetable's banks for control surfaces hold every one of them. (S12)
- **R22.** Wavetable's body is in pages (device framework R41): tabs along the body's top, Oscillator, Matrix, Mod Sources and MIDI, each page's controls drawn by the generic panel until part 3. (S1, S12)
- **R23.** The effects offered are exactly FM, Classic and Modern with their two controls; none of the kit's other shapers is exposed. (S1, S10)
- **R24.** Frame interpolation, the effects and the filters all read their parameters per frame from each voice's modulated values. (S10)

**Wavetable, part 2 (WP-8.10)**

- **R25.** The matrix (instrument modulation R1 to R16) has 64 free slots. Its sources are Amp, Env 2, Env 3, LFO 1, LFO 2, Velocity, Note, Pitch Bend, Aftertouch, Mod Wheel, Random, Pressure and Slide; its destinations are every oscillator's Position, effect controls, Gain, Pan, Semi and Detune, the sub's Gain and Tone, each filter's Frequency, Resonance, Drive and Morph, every envelope's times, slopes and levels, each LFO's Rate, Shape and Amount, Unison Amount, Volume and the matrix's Time and Amount. Volume, the Amp envelope's Sustain, Envelopes 2 and 3's Initial, each LFO's Amount, Unison Amount and the matrix's Amount are multiplicative; the rest are additive. (S4, S5, S6, S7)
- **R26.** The Matrix tab's Time and Amount act as instrument modulation R15 says. Note into a filter's Frequency at 100 % moves the cutoff one octave per octave of key from C3, so the filter follows the note exactly. (S4, S6)
- **R27.** The Amp envelope and Envelopes 2 and 3 have Attack, Decay and Release times from 0 ms to 20 s and Sustain (0 to 100 %); each segment has a slope from −1 to 1 shaped as [Operator](operator.md) R13. Envelopes 2 and 3 also have Initial, Peak and Final (−100 % to 100 %). Each has a loop mode: None (the default) holds Sustain until the note ends; Trigger runs Attack, Decay and Release once from the note's start whatever its length; Loop runs Attack, Decay and Release to the Final level and starts again from there, over and over until the voice ends, a note's end sending it into Release from where it is. (S5)
- **R28.** LFO 1 and LFO 2 play Sine, Saw, Triangle, Square or Random, each with Shape (−100 % to 100 %): Sine and Saw tilt their slope up or down, Triangle moves from ramp to saw, Square sets its pulse width, Random changes how often values near the extremes come. Rate is in hertz (0.01 to 40 Hz) or, with Sync on, a division from 1/64 to 8 bars; Amount (0 to 100 %) scales it; Offset (0 to 360°) sets its starting phase and isn't a matrix destination, though like every parameter it automates; Attack (0 ms to 20 s) fades it in from the note's start. With Retrigger on each note starts it at Offset; off, its phase follows the song position whenever the transport runs, so it sounds the same after the transport restarts, and free-runs while stopped. Each LFO runs per voice. (S5, S8; the ranges are Beatlayer's own)
- **R29.** The MIDI tab's sources are Velocity, Note, Pitch Bend, Aftertouch, Mod Wheel and Random (instrument voices R15), and MPE's Pressure and Slide, each moving only its note; per-note bends follow instrument voices R14, with PB Range (0 to 24 semitones, 2 by default). (S6; the range is Beatlayer's own)
- **R30.** Unison's mode is None or one of six, each copy's pitch, position and phase set by its law for n copies at Amount a (0 to 1): Classic detunes the copies evenly by up to ±25a cents (instrument voices R11) and pans them alternately; Shimmer moves each copy's pitch by a smoothed random walk at about 4 Hz within ±12a cents and its position within ±2a % of the table; Noise is Shimmer's movement at about 40 Hz; Phase Sync is Classic's detune with every copy starting at phase 0 on the note's start; Position Spread spreads the copies' positions evenly by up to ±25a % of the table around Position and detunes them by up to ±5a cents; Random Note draws each copy's position offset within ±50a % and detune within ±25a cents at each note's start and holds them. Every random value comes from the device's seeded generator, so a render repeats run after run. (S7; the laws are Beatlayer's own readings of the manual's descriptions)
- **R31.** Hi-Quality (instrument voices R19, R20) is off in a new device. Off, the matrix's control-rate routes are worked out every 32 frames and each table is read from the mipmap level its note's start chose; on, every route is worked out every frame and the oscillators pick a finer mipmap level and interpolate between levels as the pitch moves, so a saw table swept from key 96 to key 127 folds back at least 12 dB less energy above −80 dB with Hi-Quality on than off. (S7, S8; the margin is Beatlayer's own)
- **R32.** Every slot's routes and every setting of part 2 save with the set and read back through the `device` result's `matrix` and `device.parameters`. (S4, S12)
- **R33.** Each envelope, LFO and unison mode is the same at any block size and run after run, and nothing allocates, locks or blocks on the audio thread. (S12)
- **R34.** Every parameter the manual lets the matrix reach is a destination, and nothing else is (R25). (S4)

**Wavetable, part 3: the panel (WP-8.11)**

- **R35.** Each oscillator's display draws its table from the device's display data (device framework R35): up to 32 of its frames, each decimated to 128 points, drawn in perspective from back to front with the frame at Position drawn in the accent, or flat as the frame at Position alone in a polar view, the display's view switch choosing (view state). An agent reads the same with `device.display`. (S1, S12; the drawing is Beatlayer's own)
- **R36.** Dragging horizontally across an oscillator's display moves its Position as one undo step named as a knob drag on Position is, Change "Osc 1 Position" or Change "Osc 2 Position". (S1, S12)
- **R37.** A WAV or AIFF dragged over an oscillator's display outlines it, and dropping loads it as that oscillator's user table (R6, R8); a drop of anything else is refused there. (S1, S12)
- **R38.** The filter display draws the combined response of both filters as the routing makes it, each filter's Frequency and Resonance as a dot; dragging a dot moves them as one undo step. The envelope and LFO displays draw each one's shape; dragging a breakpoint moves its time and level, one undo step per drag. (S3, S5, S12)
- **R39.** The Matrix tab draws the matrix as a grid: the sources as columns, the destinations in use as rows, each cell showing its amount as a bar. Dragging vertically in a cell sets its amount from −100 % to 100 %, one undo step; dragging in an empty cell takes a free slot for that source and destination (instrument modulation R8) in the same step; Delete in a cell clears its route (R9). An Add Destination chooser under the grid lists the destinations not shown, and a row's menu removes it, clearing its routes. (S4; the grid's layout and gestures are Beatlayer's own)
- **R40.** The accessibility tree names every cell ("LFO 1 to Osc 1 Position") with its amount as its value, every display as an image with its readings, and every tab in a tab list. (S12)
- **R41.** At 50 %, 100 % and 200 % zoom the panel's controls, displays and grid lie inside the device's body with nothing overlapping. (S12)
- **R42.** The panel is Beatlayer's own look, drawn in Beatlayer's typeface and colours on Live's placement. (S12)
