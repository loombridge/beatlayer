# Arrangement editing: selecting, moving, resizing, splitting, and the clipboard

- **Status:** partial (R1 to R23 each have a passing test, though a time-signature marker's drag, R22, waits for WP-6.61's markers)
- **Work packages:** WP-1.8b (this spec, R1 to R18); WP-6.25 (clip edits carrying automation, R19); WP-6.24 and WP-6.24b (locators among the insert marker's stops, and Shift with an edit command, R20); WP-6.60 (snapping to clip edges, locators and time-signature changes, R21 to R23); building on WP-1.8a ([arrangement-view](arrangement-view.md)); WP-1.4 plays clips, and WP-1.4b moves an audio clip's Start marker when it's trimmed or split (R16); WP-1.5 the loop brace and the transport ([transport](transport.md)); fades are WP-1.4's
- **Parity-matrix row:** Views and workflow: Arrangement View; Editing: Split, Crop, Crop to Time Selection (split); Interface placement and key bindings
- **Checked against:** Live 12.4.3, through the UI parity reference recorded on 10 October 2026, and the Live 12 reference manual as published that day

## The behaviour

In the Arrangement you select clips by clicking their title bars, Cmd-clicking (Ctrl-clicking) to add or remove one and Shift-clicking to take in a range, or select time by dragging across the lanes, which may cut through clips. Dragging a clip's title bar moves the selected clips in time and between tracks, snapping to the editing grid; holding Option (Ctrl on Windows) copies them instead, and holding Cmd (Alt) ignores the grid. Dragging a clip's edge makes it longer or shorter; the left edge shows more or less of what the clip holds without moving it. Split (Cmd-E) cuts clips at the insert marker or at the time selection's edges, and each part plays exactly what it did. Delete, Duplicate (Cmd-D), Cut, Copy, and Paste act on the selected clips, or on the material in the time selection. A track plays one clip at a time, so whatever is placed over part of a clip replaces that part. Right-clicking a clip, or the lanes, opens Live's clip or time-selection menu with these commands and the grid's settings.

Every edit is one undo step, however long the drag behind it, and none of the selecting is. Every edit is a registered command that an agent can run with the clips, tracks, and positions spelled out, and the mouse and keys run those same commands.

This spec covers what WP-1.8b builds. The view itself, the insert marker, the grid, and time selection by dragging are [arrangement-view](arrangement-view.md)'s (R11, R12). Looping the selection (Cmd-L) and selecting the loop (Cmd-Shift-L) are here; the loop brace itself, and dragging and nudging it, are WP-1.5's and WP-1.8a's; fades and crossfades WP-1.4's; consolidating, cropping, and reversing (M4) and the Time commands that move later material (M6) come later.

## Rules

- **R1.** Clicking a clip's title bar selects that clip alone; Cmd-click (Ctrl-click) adds it to or removes it from the selection; Shift-click adds it and every clip lying wholly between it and the first selected clip, in time and in the track list. Selecting clips selects their tracks and makes the time they span the time selection (so Z zooms to them). On a lane too short for a title row, the whole clip is its title bar. (S2, S4)
- **R2.** Clicking a clip's body places the insert marker as clicking the background does (arrangement-view R12), and dragging across clip bodies or the background selects time; either one deselects clips. Shift-click and Shift-drag extend the time selection from its far end (or from the insert marker) to the pointer, over the tracks between. Shift-Option (Shift-Alt) dragging a title bar selects time instead of moving the clip. (S2, S4)
- **R3.** Select All (Cmd-A) selects every clip in the Arrangement; with none, it selects the time from 1.1.1 to the arrangement's end on every track that holds clips. (S4, S6)
- **R4.** Dragging a clip's title bar moves the selected clips, selecting the grabbed clip first if it wasn't: sideways in time, and to the track under the pointer, every other clip keeping its distance in time and in the track list. With snapping on, the grabbed clip's start lands on the nearest grid line, or on a clip edge, locator or signature marker as R21 says; Cmd (Alt) held does the opposite of the grid's setting (R23). A move that would put a clip on a track of the other kind, a group, a return, or Main leaves the clips where they were, and no clip goes before 1.1.1. (S1, S4)
- **R5.** Holding Option (Ctrl on Windows) during the drag copies instead: the originals stay, the copies land where the clips would have moved, and the copies become the selection. (S8)
- **R6.** A clip placed on a track over part of another, by moving, copying, resizing, duplicating, or pasting, replaces the part it covers: a clip covered entirely is deleted, one covered at an end is shortened from that end, and one covered in the middle is split around it. After any edit, no two clips on a track overlap. (S1; tracks-and-undo's note on overlaps)
- **R7.** Dragging a clip's left or right edge moves that edge of every selected clip by the same amount, the dragged edge snapping as in R4 and R22. The left edge keeps the clip's contents where they play, showing more or less of them (R16), and can't go before where they begin (an audio clip's file's start, a MIDI clip's contents) or before 1.1.1. A clip keeps at least one grid step of length while snapping, and a tick without. (S1)
- **R8.** Split (Cmd-E) splits the clips on the selected tracks at the time selection's start and end, or at the insert marker when no time is selected (or clips are selected), and selects the parts inside a time selection. Each part plays exactly what it played before, from the same point in its media or notes; a MIDI clip keeps all its notes in both parts, each showing its own window. With Create Fades on Clip Edges on, two audio parts meet in the 4 ms crossfade of [audio clips](audio-clips.md) R26, whose gains add to 1, so they still play what the whole clip did. (S3, S4)
- **R9.** Delete deletes the selected clips, or the material in the time selection on the selected tracks, shortening and splitting the clips its edges cross; nothing after it moves. It's in the destructive class. (S3, S4, S6, S9)
- **R10.** Duplicate (Cmd-D) places a copy of the selected clips right after the span they cover, on the same tracks, or a copy of the time selection's material right after it, and selects the copy, so pressing it again lays down another. (S3, S4, S6)
- **R11.** Copy (Cmd-C) puts the selected clips, or the time selection's material, on the clipboard with the tracks they came from. Cut (Cmd-X) copies, then deletes as R9. Paste (Cmd-V) places the clipboard's material at the time selection's start or the insert marker, its first track's clips on the highest selected track and the rest on the tracks after it at the same spacing, and selects it. Only the clips are placed: copied empty time doesn't clear the destination. A paste that would put a clip on a track of the other kind changes nothing. The clipboard isn't part of the set, lasts from set to set, and copying is never an undo step. (S3, S4, S6)
- **R12.** In the Arrangement, Left and Right nudge the selected clips one grid step earlier or later, and with no clip selected move the insert marker one grid step. Option-Left and Option-Right (Alt) move the insert marker to the previous or next clip edge on the selected tracks. Shift-Left and Shift-Right move the time selection's end a grid step; Shift-Up and Shift-Down take in the track above or below. With clips selected, Enter starts resizing their ends from the keyboard: Left and Right move them a grid step, Enter keeps the result, and Esc puts them back. (S4, S5)
- **R13.** Every edit is one undo step, however many frames its drag lasts or commands it takes: a gesture's first frame opens a transaction, each frame runs the edit again from where the gesture began, so the set follows the pointer, and releasing commits it; Esc during the drag puts the set and the selection back and records nothing. Undo returns the set exactly as it was before the edit, and redo exactly as after. Selecting and copying are never undo steps. While a gesture is under way, other edits and keys wait. (S7, S9)
- **R14.** Every edit is a registered command (`arrangement.move_clips`, `resize_clips`, `split`, `delete`, `duplicate`, `cut`, `copy`, `paste`, `nudge`, `select_clips`, `select_all`, `loop_selection`, `select_loop` in [commands/arrangement.md](../commands/arrangement.md)) with a schema and a description for a model, run headless through `Shell::run` with explicit clips, tracks, and positions in ticks or as bars, beats, and sixteenths ("5.1.1"), or, with those left out, on the selection, as keys and menus run them. Delete and Cut are destructive; moving, resizing, splitting, duplicating, pasting, and nudging are edits; selecting and copying are read-only. The mouse and keys run the same commands. (S9)
- **R15.** Selected clips are drawn with their title row and an outline in the selection colour, and assistive technology reads each visible clip as selected or not. Over a clip's edges the pointer shows the resize cursor, over its title bar the move cursor, and while copying the copy cursor. (S2)
- **R16.** Splitting a clip or trimming its left edge keeps what each part plays where it played. An audio clip's Start marker moves, as Live's does: the part that starts later starts later in its file by the time between the two starts under the tempo map, and dragging the left edge earlier moves the marker earlier, down to the file's start (the audio clips spec, R16); an audio clip's `Clip::offset` is always 0. Live moves a MIDI clip's Start marker the same way, wrapping it inside the loop brace when the clip loops (observed in Live 12.4.6 on macOS, 11 October 2026, the audio clips spec's S15), but MIDI clips have no markers until WP-2.1, so for now a MIDI clip's contents begin `offset` before its start (`Clip::offset`, 0 for a new clip), and a split or trim raises the later part's offset by as much as its start moved. The waveform drawn for a part carries on from where the whole clip's was: it's drawn from where the file's start falls, the Start marker's time before the clip's start (`Clip::origin`). (S1, S3; the audio clips spec's S11 and S15)
- **R17.** Right-clicking a clip opens the clip's menu, selecting the clip first if it wasn't selected; right-clicking elsewhere in the lanes opens the time selection's menu, placing the insert marker there if the click is outside the time selection. Each menu holds menus.md's rows in Live's order with its separators (C-CLIP-ARR-001 to 024, C-TIME-001 to 017): each item runs what its shortcut runs in the Arrangement and shows its keys, an item no package has built yet shows disabled, switches show their tick, and the Adaptive Grid and Fixed Grid rows offer each setting, the current one marked. Split, Paste, and Delete aren't in these menus: they're reached by their keys and the Edit menu. (S10)
- **R18.** Loop Selection (Cmd-L) sets the loop brace to the time selection, or the span of the selected clips, and switches the loop on, as one undo step; with nothing selected it switches the loop on or off. Select Loop (Cmd-Shift-L) selects the time inside the loop brace on every track, which isn't an undo step. (S11)
- **R19.** With Lock Envelopes off, as in a new set, moving, copying, cutting, deleting, duplicating and pasting clips or a time selection's material (R4, R5, R9, R10, R11) also carry the tracks' automation over the clips' spans, as [automation-editing.md](automation-editing.md) R9 says, within the same undo step; with it on they leave automation where it is (R10 there). A time selection in automation lanes is edited as [automation-editing.md](automation-editing.md) R5 says. (S12)
- **R20.** Option-Left and Option-Right (Ctrl on Windows, S-A11Y-001) stop at locators as well as at the clip edges of R12 ([time-editing.md](time-editing.md) R12). In the Arrangement, Shift with Cut, Copy, Paste, Duplicate or Delete runs the …Time commands instead of these edits ([time-editing.md](time-editing.md) R21, WP-6.24b). (S5, S12)
- **R21.** While snapping is on, a clip being moved (R4, R5) snaps to the nearest of: a grid line, under its start; and, under its start or its end, another clip's edge on any track, a locator ([time-editing.md](time-editing.md) R1), or a time-signature marker. A clip edge, locator or marker snaps only within 6 points of the edge it would meet, and wins over a grid line within the same distance; beyond that the grid line R4 gives holds. This replaces R4's snapping to the grid alone. The distance and the order of preference are Beatlayer's own. (S1, S13)
- **R22.** A clip edge being dragged (R7), a locator being dragged ([time-editing.md](time-editing.md) R7) and a time-signature marker being dragged ([time-editing.md](time-editing.md) R32) snap the same way, each to the others and to clip edges, never to itself or to the edges of the clips being moved with it. (S13)
- **R23.** Cmd (Alt on Windows) held during the drag turns all of R21's snapping off while snapping is on, and turns grid snapping on while it's off (I-DRAG-021); while snapping is off, clip edges, locators and markers don't snap. During the drag, the point snapped to shows as a line through the lanes. Both are Beatlayer's own. (S1, S13)

## Not built yet

- **Audio clips past the end of their media.** The document doesn't know how long a clip's media is (the media store does), so a clip's right edge can be dragged past the end of its file, or its End marker, where it plays silence, and a split there leaves a part whose Start marker is past its End marker (the audio clips spec, R16). Dragging an unlooped MIDI clip's right edge past its End marker takes the End marker with it in Live (observed in Live 12.4.6, the audio clips spec's S15), which WP-2.1's MIDI markers will follow.
- **MIDI clips' hidden notes before their start.** A MIDI clip can't show notes before its contents begin, so its left edge can't move earlier than its offset allows; MIDI clip markers come with WP-2.1.
- **Context menus elsewhere.** S-CTX-001 (right-click any item) and S-CTX-002 (the context-menu key) stay pending in the keymap: the Arrangement's clip and lane menus are built here (R17), but the track headers', Session's, browser's, and devices' menus come with their packages.
