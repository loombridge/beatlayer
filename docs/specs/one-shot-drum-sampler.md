## The behaviour

Drum Sampler is Beatlayer's one-shot drum sampler: an instrument that plays one sample once per note, the way a drum hit does. A note starts a voice that plays a region of the sample (a Start and a Length, both shares of it) at the pitch the note, Transpose and Detune give, through an attack-hold-decay envelope, an optional playback effect, an optional filter, and a global stage of Volume, Pan and Velocity to Volume. Beatlayer's name, layout, algorithms and ranges are its own; the manual gives the controls and what they do, and where it gives no number the spec writes Beatlayer's and says so.

The envelope has two modes. In Trigger mode the note's end is ignored: the level rises over Attack, stays at its peak for Hold (or, with Hold Inf on, until the sample ends, so the whole sample plays), then falls to silence over Decay. In Gate mode the level rises over Attack, stays at its peak while the note is held, and falls over Decay from the note's end; Hold does nothing.

One playback effect runs at a time, chosen from nine and turned on by its switch. Each has two parameters, FX1 and FX2, which are also the names the modulation route gives its last two destinations: Stretch (the sample's length against its pitch, and the grain), Loop (where in the region a loop starts, and how long it is), Pitch Env (how far the pitch starts from its base, and how long it takes to come back), Punch (how far the level is ducked after the attack, and how long it takes to return), 8-Bit (a lower sample rate with 8-bit values, and the decay of its built-in low-pass), FM (how deeply a sine modulator bends the sample's read position, and its frequency), Ring Mod (how much the sample is multiplied by a sine, and its frequency), Sub Osc (the level of a sine under the sample, and its frequency) and Noise Osc (the level of noise, and its colour). The time and frequency controls of Stretch, Loop, FM, Ring Mod, Sub Osc and Noise Osc follow the note's pitch.

The filter is a low-pass at 12 or 24 dB per octave, a high-pass at 24 dB per octave, or a peak that boosts or cuts a band; the first three have a Resonance, the peak a gain. The global section holds Volume, Pan and Velocity to Volume, and one modulation route: a source (the note's velocity or its slide), a destination (the filter's frequency, Attack, Hold, Decay, FX1 or FX2) and an amount.

Two switches live in the device's context menu after its common items: Enable Per-Note Pitch Bend, on in a new device, and Envelope Follows Pitch, which scales the envelope's times with the note's pitch so a transposed sample is shaped over the same part of it. The menu then offers Swap to Simpler, which replaces Drum Sampler with the Simpler sampler in One-Shot mode, keeping the sample, its Start and its Length, in one undo step, and Save as Default Pad, which waits for the Drum Rack's pads.

This spec covers Drum Sampler's parameters, voices, effects, filter, modulation route and menu. It leaves to [the device framework spec](device-framework.md) everything every native device has (descriptors, smoothing, layouts, automation, presets), to [the sampler spec](sampler.md) how a sample reaches a device (R11) and Simpler itself, and to [the polysynth spec](polysynth.md) the per-note expressions' source.

## Rules

- **R1.** Beatlayer ships Drum Sampler, a native instrument of its own layout, described like any native device: every parameter has a descriptor and a place in the layout, or is one of its two menu options (S5), and its parameters are numbered in the order its description lists them. Enable Per-Note Pitch Bend is on in a new device and Envelope Follows Pitch off. (S5, S7)
- **R2.** A note starts a voice that plays the region from Start (a share of the sample, 0 to 100 %, default 0) for Length (a share, 0 to 100 %, default 100), once, at the pitch the key, Transpose (whole semitones, −48 to 48, default 0) and Detune (cents, −50 to 50, default 0) give, with C3 playing the sample at its own pitch. A device with no sample is silent. (S1)
- **R3.** Sample Gain (default 0 dB) and Volume (default 0 dB) are gains that reach −∞ dB at their bottoms; Sample Gain's top is 24 dB and Volume's 36 dB. A device at −∞ on either is silent. (S1, S4, S6)
- **R4.** In Trigger mode (the default) the level rises in a straight line over Attack (0 to 10 s, default 0), stays at its peak for Hold (0 to 10 s, default 300 ms), then falls in a straight line to silence over Decay (0 to 10 s, default 200 ms), whatever the note does. With Hold Inf on (the default) the level stays at its peak until the sample's region ends, so the whole of it plays and Decay has no effect on the level. (S1)
- **R5.** In Gate mode the level rises over Attack and stays at its peak while the note is held, whatever Hold is, and falls to silence over Decay from the level it had when the note ended. (S1)
- **R6.** With Envelope Follows Pitch on, Attack, Hold and Decay are divided by the playback speed the note's pitch gives at its start: an octave up, the times are halved. (S5)
- **R7.** With the effect on and Stretch chosen, the sample is played by overlapping Hann-windowed grains whose spacing is half the grain, so its length is changed without changing its pitch. FX1 is the factor, 0.25 to 4 times the sample's length in a logarithmic span (50 % is 1, the sample unchanged); FX2 the grain, 10 to 200 ms in a logarithmic span (50 % is about 45 ms). At a factor of 1 the output is the plain playback. (S2)
- **R8.** Loop repeats part of the region: FX2 is its length, 5 ms to 2 s in a logarithmic span (50 % is 100 ms), and FX1 places its start across the rest of the region (0 % at the region's start, 100 % at the latest the loop still fits). When the voice reaches the loop's end it returns to its start. With Hold Inf on, the voice lasts as long as the region takes to play once at the note's pitch. (S2)
- **R9.** Pitch Env starts the pitch away from its base and brings it back in a straight line in semitones: FX1 is the amount (50 % is none, 100 % is +24 semitones, 0 % is −24), FX2 the time it takes to return, 10 ms to 2 s in a logarithmic span (50 % is 141 ms). (S2)
- **R10.** Punch leaves the first 5 ms of the sound untouched, then ducks it in over 1 ms by FX1 of 24 dB and lets the duck go back to nothing in a straight line in decibels over FX2, 20 ms to 2 s in a logarithmic span (50 % is 200 ms). (S2)
- **R11.** 8-Bit holds the sample at a rate of FX1, 500 Hz to 44 kHz in a logarithmic span (50 % is about 4.7 kHz), rounds each held value to 8 bits, and passes it through a one-pole low-pass that starts at 16 kHz and falls to a quarter of that rate (not below 500 Hz) over FX2, 20 ms to 2 s in a logarithmic span. (S2)
- **R12.** FM reads the sample at a position bent by a sine modulator: FX1 is the depth, up to 1 ms of the sample at 100 %, and FX2 the modulator's frequency at C3, 20 Hz to 5 kHz in a logarithmic span (50 % is 316 Hz), which follows the note's pitch. The depth falls to nothing in a straight line over the device's Decay, so a short Decay confines the modulation to the transient. (S2)
- **R13.** Ring Mod multiplies the sample by a mix of one and a sine: FX1 is the share of the sine, FX2 its frequency at C3 as in R12, following the note's pitch. The share falls to nothing in a straight line over the device's Decay. (S2)
- **R14.** Sub Osc adds a sine of level FX1 (as a share of full scale) at a frequency of 30 Hz plus FX2 times 90 Hz at C3, following the note's pitch. It shares the voice's envelope, so Attack, Hold and Decay shape it, and it sounds on after the sample has played out for as long as the envelope allows, unless Hold Inf is on. (S2)
- **R15.** Noise Osc adds white noise, made from the voice's own seed so a render repeats, through a one-pole low-pass of FX2, 200 Hz to 20 kHz at C3 in a logarithmic span and following the note's pitch, at a peak level of FX1 times 0.5. It shares the voice's envelope as R14 says. (S2)
- **R16.** The filter's switch (off by default) sends the sound through it; off bypasses it. Its types are a low-pass at 12 dB per octave, a low-pass at 24 dB per octave, a high-pass at 24 dB per octave and a peak, each a cascade of the audio-EQ cookbook's second-order sections: the 12 dB low-pass one section of Q 0.7071 plus 11.3 times Resonance (0 to 100 %, default 0); the 24 dB types that section followed by one of Q 0.7071; the peak one section of Q 1 and a gain of Peak Gain (−24 to 24 dB, default 0). Frequency is logarithmic from 20 Hz to 20 kHz (default 1 kHz). Each type's magnitude is its prototype's within 0.2 dB. (S3)
- **R17.** Velocity to Volume (0 to 100 %, default 100 %) scales the voice's level by one minus its amount times the velocity's shortfall from full: at 0 the velocity doesn't matter, at 100 % the level follows it in a straight line. Pan (−50 to 50, default 0) places the sound at equal level in both channels at 0 and in one at an end. (S4)
- **R18.** The modulation route moves its destination by Amount (−100 to 100 %, default 0) times the source (the note's velocity from 0 to 1, or its slide from 0 to 1, read continuously while the note sounds). The filter's frequency moves up to four octaves each way; Attack, Hold and Decay are multiplied by two to the power of three times the product, up to a factor of eight each way, when each stage begins; FX1 and FX2 move by the product over their whole range, within it. (S4)
- **R19.** With Enable Per-Note Pitch Bend on, a per-note bend of the note's ID (or key, when it has no ID) moves only that note, by its value from −1 to 1 of 48 semitones (Beatlayer's own range, the polysynth's); with it off the bend is ignored. Slide reaches the modulation route (R18). (S5, S9)
- **R20.** Drum Sampler is polyphonic up to eight notes. A note on a key that already sounds fades the earlier note out over 5 ms (Beatlayer's own), and a ninth note fades the oldest out the same way. (S1)
- **R21.** Every value that changes within a block is read frame by frame and every voice counts its own frames, so a render is the same at any block size, and nothing on the audio thread allocates, locks or blocks: a voice's buffers, filter states and grains are fixed in size and made with the device. (S10)
- **R22.** A sample comes into Drum Sampler through `device.load_sample`, as into Simpler (the sampler spec, R11): a file imported into the media store by content hash, kept by the device, saved with the set and played when it is reopened. Dropping a file on Drum Sampler's frame in the Device View does the same. Drum Sampler's descriptors and commands are reachable by ID through the agent API, each with its readback. (S7, S8, S10)
- **R23.** Drum Sampler's context menu follows the common items with its two switches, ticked while on and turned over as parameter changes in one undo step (S5, S7), then Swap to Simpler (`device.swap_to_snapshot`), which replaces the device at the same place in the chain with Simpler in One-Shot mode, carrying the sample, the on or off state, and its Start and Length as the region's flags ([the sampler](sampler.md) R22), in one undo step named Swap to Simpler and selecting the new device, and then Save as Default Pad, disabled and naming the Drum Rack's pads until they exist. Any other device refuses the swap. (S5)
- **R24.** Every parameter is automatable and modulatable (rule G2), and every change a control makes is one undo step named for the parameter (the device framework spec, R3 and R18), saved with the set. (S6, S7)
- **R25.** On a track that plays the set's tuning ([tunings.md](tunings.md) R12, R14; a Drum Rack's track never does), a voice plays at the pitch R2 gives with its key's tuned pitch in place of the key, C3's twelve-step pitch still playing the sample at its own pitch, Transpose and Detune adding to it; the playback effects that follow the note's pitch (R12 to R15) and Envelope Follows Pitch follow the tuned pitch, and a per-note bend (R19) moves the note by steps of the tuning ([tunings.md](tunings.md) R13). (S11, S12)

- **R26.** Drum Sampler's sample is swapped for the next or previous similar sample from its display's Previous Similar and Next Similar buttons and with Cmd-Right and Cmd-Left, keeping every parameter, as [sound similarity](sound-similarity.md) R21 and R22 say. (S1)
- **R27.** The display's menu holds Show Similar Files, Save as Similarity Reference and Return to Reference, which run [sound similarity](sound-similarity.md) R12 with the device's reference, `device.set_similarity_reference` and `device.return_to_reference`. (S1)
- **R28.** The reference saves with the set and with Drum Sampler's presets, and reads back in the query tool's `device`. (S1)

## Not built yet

- The sample display (a waveform, Start and Length handles) and the effects' X-Y pad are not drawn: the generic layout has no X-Y pad (the reverb spec's note on its X-Y controller is the same), so FX1 and FX2 are knobs, and Drum Sampler's display cell is left black for them.
- Save as Default Pad waits for the Drum Rack's pads (T145); its menu item is disabled and says so.
- A double-click on a sample in the browser still adds it to the Arrangement (the browser spec, R14); loading the selected sample into a Drum Sampler with a key or double-click is not built, as for Simpler (the sampler spec, R11), because where Live puts that path is not recorded.

## Details

- The manual gives no default, no range for Attack, Hold, Decay, Pan, Velocity to Volume, the filter or the modulation amount, no unit or span for any effect's parameters, and says nothing of Drum Sampler's polyphony or what a repeated note does. Every such number above is Beatlayer's own, chosen so a dropped sample plays whole at its own pitch (Hold Inf on), and is listed to observe in Live.
- FX1 and FX2 are one share of 0 to 100 % each so one parameter can mean a different thing for each effect and still be automated and modulated like any other; the device's own help text and the effect's rule say what each means. A display that names them per effect waits for the X-Y pad.
- Hold Inf is a switch beside Hold rather than a setting at Hold's end, so Hold keeps one range and both are automatable.
