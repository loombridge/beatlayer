## The behaviour

MIDI Tools change a MIDI clip's notes from the Clip View. They live in two panels of the panel selector's right group, beside Pitch and Time Utilities: the Transform panel holds the transformations, which take notes and change them, and the Generate panel the generators, which write new notes. A panel's selector chooses the tool, and the tool's controls sit under it with an Auto switch, an apply button and a Reset button.

A transformation acts on the notes in the note editor's time selection, or on the selected notes, or, with neither, on the notes in the clip's loop, and replaces them with what it makes. A generator writes notes into the time selection or the loop; a note already there at the same key and the same time is replaced, and the others stay. With Auto on, which is how every tool starts, moving a tool's control applies it at once, and moving it again runs the tool again on the notes it started from, so a person can try values while listening. With Auto off, the controls change nothing until the apply button, Cmd+Enter or Edit ▸ Apply Transformation/Generator applies the tool. When the clip has a scale, the tools' pitch controls count scale degrees and generators keep to the scale.

Applying a tool is one undo step. Undo puts the notes back but leaves the tools' settings as they are, and the settings are saved with the set, with each track remembering which transformation and generator its panels show. Tools that choose at random take a seed, so the same notes, settings and seed always give the same result, for tests and agents alike.

Once a tool is applied, applying further transformations to the notes it made builds a chain: a person can go back to any tool in the chain, change it, and the tools after it run again on its new result. The Status Bar lists the chain's tools.

Every tool is described as data, its parameters with their ranges, defaults and descriptions, and runs as a function of its input notes, its settings and its seed, the shape the extension tools of milestone M9 will share. Every action here is a registered command an agent runs.

This spec covers the panels, choosing a tool, its settings, what it acts on, applying, undo, seeds and chains. Each tool's parameters and what it does to notes are [midi-transformations.md](midi-transformations.md)'s and [midi-generators.md](midi-generators.md)'s; the Pitch and Time Utilities panel is [pitch-and-time-utilities.md](pitch-and-time-utilities.md)'s; a clip's scale and the scale arithmetic are [scales.md](scales.md)'s; the note editor's notes, selection, grid and quantise settings are [piano-roll.md](piano-roll.md)'s.

## Rules

**The panels (WP-6.11)**

- **R1.** A MIDI clip's Clip View has three panels in the panel selector's right group, in this order after the left group's Main Properties and Launch Properties: Pitch and Time Utilities, MIDI Transformation Tools (the Transform panel) and MIDI Generative Tools (the Generate panel). One of them shows at a time, in the place L-CLIP-006 gives, beside the left group's panel; clicking its button in the selector row shows it (`clip_view.show_panel`). Which panel shows is view state: no undo step, and not saved with the set; a new Clip View shows Pitch and Time Utilities (both Beatlayer's own). An audio clip's right group holds the Audio Utilities panel and a Transform panel, and no Generate panel. (S3, S10)
- **R2.** The Transform panel holds the Transformation Selector, a chooser of the transformations, and the Generate panel the Generator Selector, a chooser of the generators. Under the selector are the chosen tool's controls, then its Auto switch, labelled Auto, its apply button, labelled Transform in the Transform panel and Generate in the Generate panel, and its Reset button. (S2, S5, S7)
- **R3.** The Transformation Selector lists Arpeggiate, Chop, Connect, Glissando, LFO, Ornament, Quantize, Recombine, Span, Strum, Time Warp and Velocity Shaper; the Generator Selector lists Rhythm, Seed, Shape, Stacks and Euclidean; each in that order, the order the manual lists them (Beatlayer's own reading of the selectors' order). An audio clip's Transform panel lists Quantize alone ([midi-transformations.md](midi-transformations.md)). (S1, S3, S5, S7)
- **R4.** Each MIDI track keeps which transformation and which generator its clips' panels show: choosing one in a selector (`midi_tool.choose`) sets it for the track whose clip the Clip View shows. A new track shows Arpeggiate and Rhythm (Beatlayer's own). Choosing changes no notes and is no undo step, and the choice is saved with the set beside the song, as Fold is ([piano-roll.md](piano-roll.md) R16); a set saved before MIDI Tools loads with every track on Arpeggiate and Rhythm. (S2, S7)
- **R5.** Every tool has a list of parameters, each with a name, a kind (a number, a whole number, a choice, a switch, a pitch, a pitch range, a note division, or a display's data such as breakpoints, levels or toggles), a range, a default, and a description; [midi-transformations.md](midi-transformations.md) and [midi-generators.md](midi-generators.md) give them for each tool. A tool has one set of settings for the whole set, not one per track (Beatlayer's own): its parameters' values and its Auto switch. The settings are saved with the set beside the song, and changing them is never an undo step. A new set's tools start at their defaults with Auto on; a saved value outside its parameter's range, or for a parameter the tool doesn't have, loads as the default. (S2, S5)
- **R6.** Reset (`midi_tool.reset`) puts every parameter of the shown tool back to its default and leaves its Auto switch as it is. It changes no notes and is no undo step. It is disabled while every parameter is at its default. (S2, S5)
- **R7.** A tool's controls show their parameters' values and take the drags, arrow-key steps, typed values and double-click resets that the device framework's controls take ([device-framework.md](device-framework.md) R16, R17). While the pointer is over one, the Info View shows the parameter's name and registered description ([info-view.md](info-view.md) R4, R6), and assistive technology reads each control by its kind, with its name and value ([accessibility.md](accessibility.md) R1). (S12)
- **R8.** With the Clip View's clip in Scale Mode, every parameter a tool's rules mark as scale-aware counts degrees of the clip's scale, and moves keys as [scales.md](scales.md) R2 says; with Scale Mode off it counts semitones, the degrees of the chromatic scale. A generator in Scale Mode writes only keys of the clip's scale. A parameter keeps the number it holds when Scale Mode switches. (S2, S3, S11)
- **R9.** On a track whose instrument is a Drum Rack, the keys some chain of the rack receives ([drum-racks.md](drum-racks.md) R1, R2) take the scale's place for every tool: a scale-aware step counts through those keys in order, and a tool writes new notes only on them. A chain on All Notes makes every key one of them. (S6, S11; Beatlayer's own for the tools S6 doesn't name)

**What a tool acts on (WP-6.11)**

- **R10.** A tool's range is the note editor's time selection ([piano-roll.md](piano-roll.md) R24) when the editor has one; otherwise the clip's loop brace while its Loop switch is on, and its Start marker to its End marker (or the clip's end, with no End marker) while it is off (the Loop-off case is Beatlayer's own). Positions are ticks in the clip's contents ([piano-roll.md](piano-roll.md) R4). (S2, S4)
- **R11.** A transformation's input is the selected notes; with none selected, the notes that start in the time selection (at its start or after, and before its end); with neither, the notes that start in the range. Selected notes come first because selecting notes also selects their time ([piano-roll.md](piano-roll.md) R24), and Enter turns a selection of notes into a selection of their time alone (Beatlayer's own reading of the order S2 gives). Its output replaces the input notes, and the clip's other notes don't change. (S2, S3, S4)
- **R12.** A generator's notes go into the range and join the clip's notes. A note already in the clip at the same key as a generated note, whose span overlaps that note's, is removed; the clip's other notes stay. Two notes overlap when one starts before the other ends (Beatlayer's own reading of "overlap"). (S1, S2, S3)
- **R13.** A generator writes notes that start in the range and end by its end, cutting a note that would run past the end there. A tool never writes a note that starts before the contents' start (tick 0), or one shorter than a tick, and a note whose key a tool's rules would put outside 0 to 127 moves by octaves until it is inside (Beatlayer's own, as [midi-transformations.md](midi-transformations.md) says Live does for Arpeggiate). (S1)
- **R14.** A note a tool makes from an input note takes that note's release velocity, chance, velocity deviation and deactivation, except where the tool's rules set them. Its expression envelopes ([mpe.md](mpe.md) R1) follow as [mpe.md](mpe.md) R4 carries them through an edit: a note a tool moves keeps them; one it shortens or lengthens keeps them as a shortened or lengthened note does; one whose time it scales (Time Warp) has their positions scaled by the same factor; and a note it makes over part of an input note's span (Chop's pieces, Arpeggiate's steps) takes the stretch of each envelope over that part, as a split note's part does. A note a tool makes outside its input note's span, and a generated note, has no envelopes. A generated note has release velocity 64, chance 100 % and velocity deviation 0, and is active. (S6, for Arpeggiate, Ornament and Connect; S7, for Time Warp's expressions; S11; Beatlayer's own for the rest)
- **R15.** After a tool runs, the notes it wrote are selected, and no others, and the time selection follows them as [piano-roll.md](piano-roll.md) R24 says. While an application is open, its range stays the one it started with (R17). (Beatlayer's own)
- **R16.** A tool's notes depend only on its input notes, the range, the note editor's grid step at the range's start (the step `note.quantize` uses when its grid is unset, [piano-roll.md](piano-roll.md) R19), the clip's time signature, the clip's scale (R8), the track's drum keys (R9), the tool's settings, and the application's seed (R20): the same of each gives the same notes. A run that would write more than 65,536 notes is refused and changes nothing (Beatlayer's own limit). (S13)

**Applying a tool (WP-6.11)**

- **R17.** With a tool's Auto switch on, changing one of its parameters applies the tool at once: a transformation only while the note editor has a time selection or selected notes, a generator whatever is selected. The first such change opens an application on the target R10 to R12 give: Beatlayer keeps the input notes and the range as they were. Each further change of that tool's parameters runs it again on the kept input, not on the notes the change before made. Choosing a tool applies nothing until a parameter changes (Beatlayer's own). (S2, S7)
- **R18.** With Auto off, changing a parameter changes no notes. Switching Auto off (`midi_tool.set_auto`) while the tool's application is open puts the notes back as they were before it and closes it, as one undo step. Switching Auto on applies nothing until a parameter changes (Beatlayer's own). (S2)
- **R19.** The apply button, Cmd+Enter (S-NOTE-033) and Edit ▸ Apply Transformation/Generator (M-EDIT-023) apply the tool once (`midi_tool.apply`), with its settings, to the target as it is now, whether Auto is on or off. Applying again applies the tool again to the notes the last application left, so its effect builds up. Cmd+Enter and the menu item apply the chosen tool of whichever of the Transform and Generate panels showed last in the Clip View (Beatlayer's own); the item is disabled while no MIDI clip shows, and a transformation with no input is refused, saying why. (S2, S10)
- **R20.** Every application has a seed, a whole number from 0 to 4,294,967,295, and every random choice a tool makes comes from that seed through Beatlayer's own generator, so the same input, settings and seed give the same notes on every platform. `midi_tool.apply` takes a `seed`; with none given, and on every click of the apply button and every Cmd+Enter, Beatlayer draws a new one. Changing a parameter of an open application keeps its seed (Beatlayer's own), so a change of an amount scales the same random draws. `midi_tool.reseed` gives the open application a new seed, or the one it's given, and runs it again, as a tool's Reseed button does. (S2, S13)
- **R21.** The open application closes when another clip shows, the selected notes or the time selection change other than by the application itself, a command other than a MIDI Tool's changes the clip's notes, undo or redo runs, Reset runs, `midi_tool.close` runs, or another tool is applied, except as R27 says. After it closes, the next change starts a new application on the notes as they are then. (Beatlayer's own)
- **R22.** Each application is one undo step named after its tool, such as "Arpeggiate" (Beatlayer's own names), however many frames the gesture that drives it lasts ([piano-roll.md](piano-roll.md) R11), and it is a step even when the notes come out as they were (S6). Esc during the gesture puts the notes back as they were before it. Choosing a tool, changing parameters with Auto off, switching Auto on and Reset are never undo steps; undo and redo change notes only, and leave every tool's settings as they are. (S2, S6)
- **R23.** Every action here is a registered command with a schema, a description and a permission class, run by ID through the agent API with the clip named or, left out, the one the Clip View shows: `midi_tool.list` (`read`): every tool with its kind and parameters, each parameter's kind, range, default, unit, description and whether it is scale-aware; `midi_tool.state` (`read`): the shown panel, the track's chosen transformation and generator, each tool's settings and whether they are at their defaults, the target (time selection, selected notes or range) and the open application with its tool, input, range and seed; `clip_view.show_panel` and `midi_tool.close` (`read`, view state); `midi_tool.choose`, `midi_tool.set` (one or more parameters' values), `midi_tool.set_auto` and `midi_tool.reset` (`edit`, settings saved with the set, read back in `midi_tool.state`); `midi_tool.randomize` (`edit`, Euclidean's randomise button: [midi-generators.md](midi-generators.md) R16, read back in `midi_tool.state`); and `midi_tool.apply` and `midi_tool.reseed` (`edit`, read back in the query tool's `clip` as `data.midi.notes`). `midi_tool.apply` takes the tool, values that replace its settings (and are kept as its settings), the notes or the range to act on in place of the Clip View's selection, and a seed, so an agent applies a tool in one call. A gesture on a tool's control runs one of these. (S13)
- **R24.** `midi_tool.apply`, `midi_tool.set`, `midi_tool.set_auto`, `midi_tool.reseed` and `midi_tool.randomize` are destructive as well as edits when, tried against the set as it is when they run, they would leave the clip fewer notes than it has, as [agent-permissions.md](agent-permissions.md) R11 decides for `clip.set_data`: they ask first, the question names the clip, and a dry run gives `permission` and `fewer_notes`. (S13)
- **R25.** A tool's notes reach the engine as a change of the clip's notes, as any note edit does ([midi-clips.md](midi-clips.md) R9, R10): notes that stop sounding get their note-offs, and no program is rebuilt. (S12)
- **R26.** Every tool, native or added by an extension in milestone M9, is a description (its name, kind and parameters, as R5 and `midi_tool.list` give them) and a function from its input (R16) to the notes it writes, which changes nothing else: the target, replacing and merging notes (R11 to R13), the seed, undo and chains are Beatlayer's, around the tool. (S13)

**Chained MIDI Tools (WP-6.15)**

- **R27.** While an application is open and the notes it wrote are the target, applying a transformation, by its apply button, Cmd+Enter, or with Auto on a change to its parameters, adds it to the application instead of closing it: the application becomes a chain, which holds the input notes and each tool applied, in order, with its settings and seed. The clip's notes are the input run through each tool in turn, each taking the one before's notes as its input. Applying a tool already in the chain adds it again. Applying a generator closes the chain and opens a new one with the generator as its first tool; transformations applied after it join that chain. A tool applied with notes or a range of its own (`midi_tool.apply`'s `notes` and `range`) isn't acting on the notes the chain wrote, so it opens a new chain, and an application of one tool is a chain of one step (Beatlayer's own). (S7)
- **R28.** Choosing in a selector a tool that is in the open chain shows the settings of its step in the chain (its last step, when it's in the chain more than once), and changing them with Auto on runs the chain again from that step: the step's new notes go through every step after it, each with its own settings and seed. Each such gesture is one undo step, named after the tool changed. `midi_tool.set` takes a `step`, counting from 0, to name another step of the same tool; `midi_tool.reseed` gives its new seed to, and runs again from, the chain's last step (Beatlayer's own). (S7)
- **R29.** The Transformation and Generator Selectors mark each tool that is in the open chain, in the chooser and its list (the look is Beatlayer's own). (S9)
- **R30.** While a chain is open, the Status Bar's message area (L-STAT-002) lists its tools in order, separated by " › " (Beatlayer's own wording), until another message replaces it. (S7, S10)
- **R31.** Switching Auto off for a tool in the open chain takes its step out (its last step, when it's in the chain more than once): the steps after it run again on the notes the step before it made (or on the input, for the first), as one undo step. Taking out the last step that's left closes the chain and puts the input back. (S2, S7; Beatlayer's own reading for a chain)
- **R32.** A chain closes as R21 says for an application. A note command that isn't a MIDI Tool closes it: Split and Chop (S8), the Pitch and Time Utilities, and every other note edit (Beatlayer's own for those but Split and Chop). A chain isn't saved with the set (Beatlayer's own). (S8)
- **R33.** `midi_tool.state` reports the open chain: its clip, how many input notes it has, its range, and each step's tool, settings and seed, in order. (S13)

**Mapping tools and chains to keys and MIDI (WP-7.3b)**

- **R34.** Each tool's parameters, its Auto switch, its apply button and its Reset button are mappable controls, named by the tool and the parameter as `midi_tool.set` names them, so one mapping acts on the tool's settings whichever MIDI clip the Clip View shows, and whether or not the tool's panel shows. A mapped apply button or Auto switch with no MIDI clip in the Clip View does nothing. (S7, S14; acting with the panel hidden is Beatlayer's own)
- **R35.** A mapped change to a tool's parameter is a change of its settings as a drag on its control is: with Auto on it applies the tool (R17), and a run of messages to one parameter is one gesture, one undo step named after the tool (R22). A mapped apply button applies the tool once with a new seed (R19 and R20), a mapped Auto switch switches Auto (R18), and a mapped Reset resets the tool (R6). (S7, S14)
- **R36.** A mapped change to a tool that is in the open chain changes that tool's step and runs the chain again from it, as R28 says, and a mapped change is never an outside edit that closes the chain (R21, R32); a mapped transformation applied while the chain's output is the target joins the chain (R27). (S7, S14)
