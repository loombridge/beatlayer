# Recording: arm, monitoring, and writing armed audio tracks to disk

- **Status:** implemented (every rule has a passing test)
- **Work packages:** WP-2.13 (R1 to R15); WP-6.21 (R16: every recording onto take lanes, and MIDI takes); WP-7.32 (R17 to R23: Record Quantization)
- **Parity-matrix row:** Recording
- **Checked against:** the Live 12 reference manual, read 15 October 2026 (chapter 19, "Recording New Clips": arming, recording into the Arrangement, and monitoring with In, Auto, and Off), and [routing](routing.md)'s Monitor rules; for R16, the Live 12 reference manual (online edition), read 20 October 2026 (chapter 21, section 21.3); for R17 to R23, the online edition read 23 October 2026 (chapter 19, sections 19.3.3, 19.5 and 19.10; chapter 36, section 36.4.2)

## The behaviour

Arming an audio track and running the transport with recording on writes the track's input — the interface channels its Audio From names — to a WAV in the project's `Samples/Recorded/`. When the recording ends, the file becomes one audio clip on its track in one undo step, and stays on disk, as Live keeps recorded files. Monitoring follows the In/Out section's Monitor switch: with Monitor Auto, a track's input is heard while it's armed and not while it plays clips, and the recording is the input either way.

This spec covers arming, the Monitor rules as recording uses them, the take, the files, and the clips a take becomes. It leaves to other parts of WP-2.13 the Control Bar's Arrangement Record button and the F9 bindings, which wait on the Record page's settings; and recording when the Audio From is another track, or Resampling, which later packages bring. Armed MIDI tracks record notes (WP-2.14), R6 to R8. Punch-In and Punch-Out, and recording-latency compensation, are R9 and R10; loop recording into take lanes and the "Warp recorded audio" setting are R11 to R13. Every recording also goes onto take lanes, for MIDI tracks too, as [comping](comping.md) R10 to R13 say (R16); take lanes and comping themselves are that spec's. Record Quantization (Edit ▸ Record Quantization) moves the notes a recording makes onto a grid, in the Arrangement, in Session slots and in Session overdubs; step recording and Capture MIDI place notes their own way (R17 to R23).

## Rules

- **R1.** Arm belongs to audio and MIDI tracks. While the transport records, every armed audio track's input — the interface channels its Audio From names, a mono channel or a stereo pair — is written from the frame playback started on to the frame the take ended on, or a jump away, whether the track monitors it or not. A channel the interface doesn't have records silence, as it hears. (S1)
- **R2.** `transport.set_recording` sets the transport's recording state (`transport.stop_recording` turns it off), and the metronome's "only while recording" follows it ([metronome](metronome.md) R7). A take ends on Stop, on recording off, or on a locate; with the Loop switch on, the wrap doesn't end the take (R11); a device change that closes the stream ends it as a stop does ([device-changes](device-changes.md) R1). Each of those ends the recording state with it. (S1, S4)
- **R3.** Files go to the project's `Samples/Recorded/`: WAV, 32-bit float, named `<track label> <take> [YYYY-MM-DD HHMMSS].wav` (the track's own label, `5-Guitar`, and the stamp in UTC), with the characters a file name can't hold replaced by `-`. The take number counts the recordings of the running app, so two in one second don't share a file. A set that has never been saved has no project folder: it is refused with a message in the Status Bar, and the transport still records. (S1, S2)
- **R4.** When the take ends, each file becomes one audio clip on its track, at the take's frames through the tempo map, in one undo step named `Recorded Clip` (a take that went round the loop is placed as R12 says); the files stay. New clips' fades follow Create Fades on Clip Edges. (S3)
- **R5.** The audio thread copies each recorded track's input into a lock-free ring, and a writer thread drains the rings into the files, so nothing on the audio thread allocates, takes a lock, or waits. A ring that fills ends the take before the frames it cannot take and counts them, and the shell reports them when the take ends: nothing is dropped silently. A file that cannot be made or written is reported in the Status Bar, makes no clips, and leaves no partial file. (S6)
- **R6.** While the transport records, the audio thread copies each note-on and note-off an armed MIDI track's input received, and its controllers, pitch bend and pressure, into a lock-free ring, stamped with the song frame it landed on, whether the track monitors it or not. The controllers become the clip's envelopes and its notes' pressure ([midi-controller-data.md](midi-controller-data.md) R10 to R16). A ring too full for a part's messages ends the take before them and counts them, as R5. Notes played during a count-in are not recorded, because the take starts when it ends ([metronome](metronome.md) R5), and Stop during the count-in records nothing. A MIDI track needs no project folder: an unsaved set still records MIDI, and only the audio tracks are refused (R3). From a port whose MPE switch is on, each note's per-note pitch, slide and pressure become its expression envelopes ([MPE](mpe.md) R12). (WP-2.14, WP-2.29b)
- **R7.** When the take ends, each note-on pairs with the next note-off (or note-on) on its key; a note still held ends with the take, and a note-off with nothing held is ignored. Each MIDI track that received a note, or a controller ([midi-controller-data.md](midi-controller-data.md) R12), gets one MIDI clip over the whole take, replacing what was under it, in the same undo step as R4's audio clips; a track that received neither gets no clip. Note starts go through the tempo map, so each sits on the sample it was played on. (WP-2.14)
- **R8.** MIDI Arrangement Overdub, `transport.set_midi_overdub` (Options ▸ MIDI Arrangement Overdub, M-OPT-027), is saved with the set and off in a new one, and switching it is one undo step. With it on, each recorded note joins the MIDI clip it starts in, and notes that start in no clip make new clips over the gaps between the track's clips, so no clip is replaced. The Control Bar's overdub switch is still drawn disabled. (WP-2.14)
- **R9.** Punch-In and Punch-Out, `transport.set_punch_in` and `transport.set_punch_out` (the Control Bar's switches), are saved with the set, off in a new one, and each switch is one undo step. The punch points are the Arrangement loop brace's start and end, whether or not the Loop switch is on. With Punch-In on, playback that starts before the brace records nothing until the brace's start frame, and playback that starts inside the brace records from play start; with only Punch-In on, the take runs until it ends as R2 says. With Punch-Out on, the take ends at the brace's end frame while playback carries on and the transport keeps recording, writing nothing more; with only Punch-Out on, the take runs from play start. With the Loop switch on as well, the loop's wrap ends the take (R11). (WP-2.13; the Live 12 manual, 19.3.1)
- **R10.** A recording is moved earlier by the device's reported input and output latency plus Driver Error Compensation (Settings, Audio page: milliseconds, two decimals, 0.00 by default, kept within -1000.00 to +1000.00 ms, which is Beatlayer's own bound; one value per machine, saved with the Audio page's settings; changing it doesn't restart the stream, and it changes recording only, not playback or monitoring). A device that reports no latency counts as 0. The audio of the take's first `delay` frames isn't written, and the clip is placed at the take's start; a negative total keeps the file whole and places the clip that much later. MIDI isn't moved. The Audio page's Overall Latency shows the total. The take's last `delay` frames aren't heard back, so the clip ends that much before the take does. Core Audio reports each direction's device latency, safety offset and stream latency plus the current buffer; ASIO reports `ASIOGetLatencies`, which holds its buffer; WASAPI, ALSA and JACK report the measured gap between the stream's own callback timestamps over its first 16 callbacks, which holds the buffer, and 0 before that. The buffer is counted once in every case. (WP-2.13)
- **R11.** With the Loop switch on and Punch-Out off, a take that reaches the loop's end goes on into the next pass instead of ending: the file holds the audio from the take's start to the loop's end, then each pass one after another, the last as far as recording got. With Punch-Out on, the wrap ends the take as R9 says. Any other jump, or a loop changed during the take, still ends it as R2 says. (S1; the Live 12 manual, 19.3.1)
- **R12.** A take that went round the loop is placed in parts. Audio from before the loop's start is a clip of its own on the main lane. Each pass, the first from where recording began and the last as far as it got, goes on a take lane of its own, a new one named `Take N` after the track's lanes unless one is free from where the pass starts (comping R11), in recording order with `Take 1` nearest the main lane, and is copied to the main lane over what was there, as one undo step named `Record` per pass, so Undo run again and again unrolls the passes from the last. Each clip plays its slice of the one file, with its loop brace on the pass, and is named after the file. A track's take lanes are saved with the set, shown in the agent's track query, and added by `track.add_take_lane`. A MIDI track takes the passes of a recording over the loop as R16 says. Take lanes, reusing them, and comping are [comping](comping.md)'s. (S1; the Live 12 manual, 19.3.1 and 21.3)
- **R13.** Warp recorded audio, `settings.set_warp_recorded_audio` (the Record, Warp & Launch page's switch, after Create Fades on Clip Edges), is kept in the preferences, on by default, and on for preferences saved before it existed. On, a recorded clip is warped ([warping.md](warping.md) R37): a warp marker at the take's start and one at each tempo change inside it, so it plays where it was recorded and at the speed it was played, and follows the tempo from then on; off, it is unwarped. (IMP-02)
- **R14.** A recording's report is also kept for `transport.state` (`last_recording`) until the next recording ends: the files it wrote with their tracks, frames and channels, the MIDI tracks that received notes, the frames dropped, the delay applied (R10), whether its clips were made, and the error when it made none. The Status Bar shows the same facts when it ends.
- **R15.** `transport.set_recording` from an agent starts a recording as the Control Bar's Arrangement Record does: the shell sets up the take's rings and writer before the engine records, and reports in the Status Bar a reason no files can come of it (a set never saved, say).
- **R16.** Every recording into the Arrangement puts each armed audio or MIDI track's take, or each pass of a take round the loop, on a take lane as well as on the main lane, reusing a take lane that has no clip after the take's start, as [comping](comping.md) R10 to R13 say. A MIDI track's passes round the loop each go on a take lane of their own and are copied to the main lane one by one, as R12 copies audio passes, in place of one clip merging them; with MIDI Arrangement Overdub on, the main lane merges them as R8 says. (WP-6.21; the Live 12 manual, 21.3)
- **R17.** Record Quantization (Edit ▸ Record Quantization, M-EDIT-038a to 038i) is one of No Quantization, 1/4, 1/8, 1/8T, 1/8 and 1/8T, 1/16, 1/16T, 1/16 and 1/16T, and 1/32, the submenu's items in that order, one ticked: the set's value. It is saved with the set, No Quantization in a new set and in a set saved before it existed, and each change is one undo step. `transport.set_record_quantization` sets it (`edit`) and reads back in `transport.state`'s `record_quantization`; the submenu's items run it, each a choice of the group. The set also keeps the last value other than No Quantization chosen, reported beside it as `record_quantization_last`, so a switch that turns record quantising on and off ([Push 2's notes](push-2-notes.md) R58) can turn it back on at that value; `transport.set_record_quantization` with `on` set to true or false does that (Beatlayer's own). (S7, S8, S9)
- **R18.** Record-quantising a note moves its start to the nearest line of the grid, the earlier line where two are as near, and moves the note's end with it, so it keeps its length, with its velocity, release velocity and per-note expression ([MPE](mpe.md) R12) unchanged and travelling with it (keeping the length and the tie's way are Beatlayer's own). The lines of 1/4, 1/8, 1/16 and 1/32 fall at every quarter, eighth, sixteenth or thirty-second note from each bar's start, 1/8T and 1/16T at every third of a quarter or of an eighth note, and an "and" value's lines are both grids' together. It applies all the way, with no swing and no groove (S9). Controllers, pitch bend and channel pressure recorded with the notes stay where they were played. (S7, S9)
- **R19.** In the Arrangement, the bars are the song's, by its time signatures. When a take ends with Record Quantization on, the clips are first made as R7, R8, R12 and R16 say, unquantised, and then one further undo step, named Record Quantization, quantises the notes the recording added (and no note that was in a clip before it, with MIDI Arrangement Overdub on), every pass's at once for a take round the loop: Undo then takes back the quantising alone and leaves the recording. A note whose start would land at or past its clip's end, or before its clip's start, goes to the nearest line inside the clip (Beatlayer's own). (S7)
- **R20.** In a Session slot recording ([session recording](session-recording.md) R7), the grid's bars are the clip's own, counted from its start, and each note is quantised as it joins the clip, inside the recording's own undo step (Beatlayer's own: the manual names the separate step only for the Arrangement); a note whose start would land at the clip's end goes to its start, the start of the loop it then plays. (S7, S10)
- **R21.** While an Arrangement recording or a Session slot recording runs, the value it started with applies to the whole take: the submenu's items are disabled, and `transport.set_record_quantization` is refused with the reason, until it ends (Beatlayer's reading of "can't be changed mid-recording"). (S7)
- **R22.** While a Session overdub runs ([session recording](session-recording.md) R8) on a clip whose loop is on, the setting can be changed, and each note is quantised as it reaches the main thread with the value in force then, inside its pass's undo step, never as a step of its own; the grid's bars are the clip's, and a note that would land on the loop's end goes to the loop's start, in the pass it was played in. An overdub of a clip that doesn't loop takes its value at the start, as R21 says. (S7, S10)
- **R23.** Step recording ([step recording](step-recording.md) R7), Capture MIDI ([capture MIDI](capture-midi.md)), and notes drawn, pasted, or made by a command are never record-quantised. Record quantising runs on the main thread as recorded notes become a clip's notes; the engine records the notes where they were played, as R6 says, with nothing more on the audio thread. (S10, S6)
