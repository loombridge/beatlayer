## The behaviour

Drift is Beatlayer's polysynth: a subtractive, polyphonic instrument whose manual section is the one on Live's compact polyphonic instrument. The user plays it from MIDI notes and shapes the sound with two oscillators and a noise source, a switchable low-pass and high-pass filter with key tracking and drive, and two envelopes. Oscillator 1 offers the manual's seven waveforms and a Shape control that morphs the selected waveform; Oscillator 2 offers the manual's five and can be transposed in octaves and semitones; the noise generator adds white noise. Each source has a switch and a gain, and the three gains share the manual's −6.0 dB default.

The filter is Beatlayer's own zero-delay-feedback pair: Type I is a 12 dB per octave state-variable low-pass whose resonance path saturates, Type II is a 24 dB per octave four-pole whose stages soft-clip their resonance, and both match their analogue prototypes. Key tracking moves the low-pass cutoff with the played note; the high-pass removes the low end. The manual's two saturation points sit before and after the filter, and the oscillator gains drive them. Envelope 1 shapes the amplitude; Envelope 2 is the second envelope and can be chosen as a modulation source.

The LFO offers the manual's nine waveforms and its four time modes — hertz, a ratio of the played note, milliseconds, or a division of the song's tempo — with a Retrigger switch, an Amount, and a slot that modulates its own rate. The Mod section has three slots, each a source, a destination and an amount, and the manual's dedicated slots sit beside them: Oscillator 1's Shape Mod, two Pitch Mods for both oscillators, and two Freq Mods for the low-pass cutoff. An MPE controller bends each note on its own through Note PB and PB Range, and its pressure and slide are per-note modulation sources. The Global section's voice modes stack copies of each note: Mono plays one note with Thickness's copies, Stereo two copies spread left and right, Unison four detuned copies. The Drift control moves every voice's pitch and cutoff a little, at random, as analogue voices wander; Legato and Glide join notes in Mono; Envelope 2 can cycle like an LFO that restarts with each note, and its rate is a modulation destination, Cyc Env Rate. Hi-Quality is an option in the title bar's menu. Drift plays on the instruments' shared voice section and modulation matrix, with its rules unchanged.

This spec covers the instrument's behaviour. The framework every native device is built on (parameters, smoothing, layout, automation, rule G1 and G2) is the [device framework spec](device-framework.md); the MIDI note path it plays is WP-2.1's, and how per-note expressions reach it from clips and MPE controllers is [MPE](mpe.md)'s (WP-6.17).

## Rules

- **R1.** Beatlayer ships Drift, an instrument device type of its own layout, described like any native device: every parameter has a descriptor and a place in the layout. (S4)
- **R2.** Oscillator 1 offers the manual's waveforms in its order — Sine, Triangle, Shark Tooth, Saturated, Saw, Pulse, Rectangle — and Oscillator 2 its five — Sine, Triangle, Saturated, Saw, Rectangle. Each has a switch, its own gain (default −6.0 dB) and an Oct control that transposes it in whole octaves; Oscillator 2's Detune transposes it in whole semitones. Oct runs from −4 to +4 and Detune from −12 to +12 semitones. (S1)
- **R3.** The oscillators are band-limited at every key: a saw at the top key and at a middle key folds less than −80 dB of its energy away from its harmonics. (S1)
- **R4.** Oscillator 1's Shape, 0 to 100 % and 0 % by default, morphs its waveform. A Pulse or Rectangle narrows its duty cycle towards 20 %; the others compress their rise and stretch their fall, so a saw moves towards a triangle. The curves are Beatlayer's own; the manual says only that each waveform responds differently and that the effect is similar to pulse-width modulation. (S1)
- **R5.** The Noise switch, off by default, adds per-voice white noise at the Noise Gain, whose default is −6.0 dB. (S1)
- **R6.** Retrigger, on by default, restarts both oscillators at the same phase, phase 0, on every note; off lets them run freely, so a note carries on from the phase the voice left. (S1)
- **R7.** The Filter switch, on by default, runs the three sources through the low-pass, the high-pass and both saturation points; off bypasses them entirely. (S1)
- **R8.** The low-pass is a zero-delay-feedback filter with Type I, 12 dB per octave and the default, and Type II, 24 dB per octave. Type I's resonance path saturates, as the manual says its circuit feeds back more of its distortion; Type II's stages soft-clip their resonance, as the manual says of its Sallen-Key design. At resonance 0 each type's response is its analogue prototype's on the warped frequency axis, within 0.2 dB from 200 Hz to 8 kHz at a 1 kHz cutoff. (S1)
- **R9.** Freq, 20 Hz to 20 kHz, sets the low-pass cutoff; HP, 20 Hz to 20 kHz, sets the high-pass cutoff, whose minimum leaves the signal alone. (S1)
- **R10.** Key, 0.00 to 1.00 and 0.00 by default, moves the low-pass cutoff with the played note from C3 (key 60): at 1.00 one octave of key is one octave of cutoff, and at 0.00 the note doesn't move it. The high-pass doesn't key-track. (S1)
- **R11.** Res, 0 to 100 % and 0 % by default, raises the low-pass resonance, which the types' saturators tame at high levels. (S1)
- **R12.** The manual's two saturation points: the louder the enabled sources' gains, the more the filter's drive clipper saturates the signal before the filter and the harder a second clipper bends it after the filter. The first point starts working as the gains leave their −6.0 dB default; the second starts above 0.0 dB. Both slopes are Beatlayer's own. (S1)
- **R13.** Envelope 1 is the amplifier envelope: Attack, Decay, Sustain and Release, each in milliseconds or per cent, with Beatlayer's own ranges, defaults and curves; the times run from 0.1 ms to 20 s. A voice sounds under it from its note's frame and ends when the envelope has reached silence. (S1)
- **R14.** Envelope 2 has the same four controls and runs with every note; the manual says it isn't mapped to the amplitude and can be chosen as a modulation source. (S1)
- **R15.** Volume is a gain parameter, 0 dB by default, that reaches −∞ dB and sets the instrument's output; Vel > Vol, 0 to 100 % and 0 % by default, moves the volume with each note's velocity, and at 0 % the velocity doesn't change it. A note's gain is 1 − a + a × v, where a is Vel > Vol as a fraction (0 to 1) and v is the velocity divided by 127: linear in amplitude, never above 1 and never negative, so velocity 127 plays at the Volume knob's level at any setting and, at 100 %, velocity 1 plays about 42 dB below it. (S1, S3, S8)
- **R16.** Voices, 1 to 32 and 16 by default, is the most notes that sound at once: a note past the limit steals the oldest voice, and its envelope restarts from the level it had reached, so a steal doesn't click. (S1) Since WP-8.2 Drift's voices are the instruments' voice section's ([instrument voices](instrument-voices.md) R3, R4, R22), with this rule unchanged.
- **R17.** Transpose, −48 to +48 semitones and 0 by default, moves both oscillators, and the notes' glide, bend and MPE pitch reach the oscillators through the voice manager. (S1)
- **R18.** Sixteen voices at 48 kHz render faster than four times real time in an optimised debug build, inside the architecture's device budget. (S2)
- **R19.** The LFO plays the manual's nine waveforms in its order — Sine, Triangle, Saw Up, Saw Down, Square, Sample & Hold, Wander, Linear Envelope, Exponential Envelope — in one of its four time modes: Rate in hertz, Ratio as a multiple of the played note's frequency, Time in milliseconds, or Sync as a division of the song's tempo. Its Amount sets its overall intensity. Retrigger, on by default, restarts each voice's LFO at the beginning of its cycle on every note; off leaves one free-running LFO, which every voice reads, and whose rate routes then read their sources from the first sounding voice. In the Ratio mode the LFO always follows the note's own frequency, so a Pitch Mod through it makes an FM tone, and a note starts its LFO at the beginning of the cycle. The waveforms' curves, the division list and the free-running reading are Beatlayer's own. Rate runs from 0.01 to 100 Hz (1 Hz by default), Ratio from 1/16 to 64 times the note (1.0), Time from 1 ms to 20 s (500 ms), and Sync over "1/16", "1/8", "1/4", "1/2", "1 Bar", "2 Bars" and "4 Bars" (1/4); the shape is Sine and the Amount 100 % by default. Wander has an S-shape, and the envelope waveforms are one-cycle decay curves. (S5)
- **R20.** The Mod section has three slots, each a source, a destination and an amount from −100 % to 100 %, and the instrument adds the manual's dedicated slots: Oscillator 1's Shape Mod, two Pitch Mods that move both oscillators, and two Freq Mods that move the low-pass cutoff. The sources are Off, Env 1, Env 2 / Cyc, LFO, Key, Velocity, Modwheel, Pressure and Slide; the destinations are Off, Osc 1 Gain, Osc 1 Shape, Osc 2 Gain, Osc 2 Detune, Noise Gain, LP Frequency, LP Resonance, HP Frequency, LFO Rate and Main Volume. The manual's Cyc Env Rate arrives with Envelope 2's cycling mode, which this part leaves out. An amount is a fraction of the destination's travel, as the plug-in kit's modulation law reads it: the parameter's position on its control's travel moves by the source times the amount, and the routes' sum is clamped to the travel. A route into a gain reaches silence at the bottom of its travel (G1), and every destination is one of the device's parameters, so an agent reaches them all by command (G2). (S3, S4, S5) Since WP-8.4 the three slots are chosen slots, and the dedicated ones fixed slots, of the instruments' modulation matrix ([instrument modulation](instrument-modulation.md) R17), with this rule unchanged.
- **R21.** A source runs in its own units: Env 1, Env 2, Velocity, Modwheel, Pressure and Slide from 0 to 1, the mod wheel reading MIDI's controller 1 through the voice manager, from the control changes that reach the device ([device framework](device-framework.md) R39); the LFO and Key from −1 to 1, Key moving from C3 (key 60) by ±1 four octaves either side. A full Pitch Mod amount moves both oscillators by ±24 semitones, and a full route into LFO Rate multiplies the LFO's speed by 2⁴; the LFO's own slot moves its rate the same way. The Key centre and span and both ranges are Beatlayer's own. (S5)
- **R22.** Note PB, on by default, follows an MPE controller's per-note pitch bend, and PB Range, 1 to 96 semitones and 48 by default, is the instrument's pitch bend range: a bend of ±1 moves the note it names by ±PB Range semitones, through the voice manager, and no other note moves. The pitch wheel bends every sounding note too: a pitch bend message moves them all by up to ±PB Range semitones, on the frame it lands. (With Note PB on, a bend on channels 2 to 16 is an MPE member channel's and moves only that channel's notes, and the wheel is channel 1's; with Note PB off every channel's bend is the wheel's.) With Note PB off, per-note bends are ignored. Pressure and Slide are per-note sources from 0 to 1 and are not gated by it. (S1, S5)
- **R23.** Every LFO control, every route and its amount, every destination parameter and the MPE settings are the device's parameters: an agent sets and reads each through the device commands, as any parameter. (S4)
- **R24.** On a track that plays the set's tuning ([tunings.md](tunings.md) R12, R14), a voice's pitch is its key's tuned pitch where it was the key, and Oct, Osc 2's Detune, Transpose, the pitch modulation routes and the pitch wheel add to it as they add to a key without a tuning. The filter's Key (R10) follows the tuned pitch, measured from C3's twelve-step pitch; the Key modulation source (R21) follows the key number (both Beatlayer's own). (S6, S7)
- **R25.** Under a tuning, Note PB's per-note bend moves a note by steps of the tuning: ±1 moves it ±PB Range steps ([tunings.md](tunings.md) R13); the pitch wheel's bend stays in semitones. (S6, S7)

**Drift completed (WP-8.7)**

- **R26.** The Global section's Voice Mode is Poly (the default), Mono, Stereo or Unison, and Voices (1 to 32, 16 by default) counts copies: a note in Poly or Mono takes one voice per copy it plays, in Stereo two and in Unison four, so at 32 Voices Poly plays 32 notes, Stereo 16 and Unison 8, a note past the limit stealing the oldest (R16). (S9)
- **R27.** Mono plays one note at a time (instrument voices R6) as four copies: the first at the note's pitch and the other three at −10, +5 and +10 cents, each at Thickness (0 to 100 %, 0 % by default) times the first's level, so at 0 % only one copy sounds; a new note takes over the held one. (S9; the detunes are Beatlayer's own)
- **R28.** Stereo plays two copies per note, detuned by ±Spread × 10 cents and panned left and right by Spread (0 to 100 %, 50 % by default), at 0 % both centred and in tune. Unison plays four copies per note, detuned evenly by up to ±Strength × 25 cents (instrument voices R11) and each moved further by a value drawn at the note's start within ±Strength × 5 cents from the device's seed, so each copy is detuned on its own; Strength runs from 0 to 100 % (50 % by default). Stacked copies keep the note's level (instrument voices R13). (S9; the laws are Beatlayer's own)
- **R29.** Drift (0 to 100 %, 0 % by default) moves each voice's pitch by up to ±Drift × 25 cents and its low-pass cutoff by up to ±Drift × 2 semitones, each by a slow random walk (about 0.5 Hz) drawn from a generator seeded by the device's ID and the voice's number ([device framework](device-framework.md) R29): a render repeats run after run, two Drift devices drift differently, and at 0 % nothing moves. (S9; the depths and speed are Beatlayer's own)
- **R30.** Legato, in Mono (off by default), moves the voice to a new note held over the last without restarting its envelopes or a retriggering LFO (instrument voices R7); Glide (0 ms to 5 s, 0 ms by default) glides an overlapping note to its pitch (instrument voices R8, R9 with Glide's Legato option on), in every voice mode. (S9)
- **R31.** Envelope 2's switch chooses ADSR (the default) or Cycling. Cycling makes Envelope 2 a per-voice cycle that starts at 0 with each note and repeats while the voice sounds: it rises to 1 over Tilt's share of the cycle left after Hold, stays at 1 for Hold's share, and falls to 0 over the rest; Tilt (0 to 100 %, 50 % by default) moves the top from the cycle's start to its end, and within 10 % of either end bends the slope beside it from straight towards exponential; Hold runs from 0 to 100 % (0 % by default). Its rate has the LFO's four time modes and ranges (R19): Rate in hertz, Ratio of the note, Time in milliseconds and Sync, and the Sync divisions follow the song's tempo. While Cycling, Attack, Decay, Sustain and Release don't act on it. (S9; the shapes are Beatlayer's own)
- **R32.** Cyc Env Rate joins the Mod section's destinations (R20) after LFO Rate: a full route multiplies the cycle's rate by 2⁴, as into LFO Rate (R21); while Envelope 2 is an ADSR it moves nothing. (S5, S9)
- **R33.** Hi-Quality (instrument voices R19), off in a new device, is in the title bar's menu: off, Drift plays exactly as it does without it; on, each oscillator reads the two mipmap levels around its pitch and crossfades between them as the pitch moves, so a saw swept from key 96 to key 127 folds back at least 12 dB less energy above −80 dB. (S10; the law is Beatlayer's own)
- **R34.** A set saved before WP-8.7 opens with Poly, Drift at 0 %, Legato off, Glide at 0 ms, Envelope 2 as an ADSR and Hi-Quality off, and renders as it did, sample for sample. (S9; Beatlayer's own)
- **R35.** Every control R26 to R33 adds is a parameter (Hi-Quality a menu option), automatable and modulatable, set and read by `device.set_param` and `device.parameters`, and in Drift's banks; renders are the same at any block size, and nothing on the audio thread allocates, locks or blocks. (S3, S4)
