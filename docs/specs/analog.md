# Analog: two oscillators and noise into two multimode filters and two amplifiers, four envelopes with loop modes, two LFOs, the global section and MPE

- **Status:** part 1 built (R1 to R17: `bl_devices::analog`, on the instruments' voice section and the kit's oscillators and filters); R18 to R30 draft
- **Work packages:** WP-8.14 (Analog, part 1: the oscillators, the noise generator, the filters, the amplifiers and the envelopes, with the shell-and-display panel's pages for them: R1 to R17), WP-8.15 (Analog, part 2: the LFOs, the global parameters, the MPE sources and the panel with its displays: R18 to R30). Voices, unison, glide and the keyboard's tuning are [the instruments' voice section](instrument-voices.md); the MPE routings ride on [the instruments' modulation matrix](instrument-modulation.md); the shell and display is [the device framework](device-framework.md)'s pages (R41 to R45)
- **Parity-matrix row:** Instruments: Analog
- **Checked against:** the Live 12 reference manual (online edition), read 30 October 2026 (chapter 31, section 31.1: 31.1.1 to 31.1.9); G. E. Peterson and H. L. Barney, "Control Methods Used in a Study of the Vowels", 1952

## The behaviour

Analog is a subtractive synthesiser modelled on analogue circuits. Two oscillators and a noise generator each send their signal to Filter 1, Filter 2 or both in a balance of their own. Each filter is a multimode filter, low-pass, band-pass, notch, high-pass or formant at two orders, with resonance and a drive stage; Filter 1 can also feed Filter 2, and Filter 2's cutoff can follow Filter 1's. Each filter feeds its own amplifier, with a level and a pan. Four envelopes, one for each filter and each amplifier, can loop in three ways while a note is held; two LFOs move the oscillators, filters and amplifiers of their side. The global section holds vibrato, unison, glide, four quick routings of the filters and amplifiers, the keyboard's tuning, the voices and their priority; the MPE section sends pressure and slide to destinations of their own.

The device is drawn as Live draws it: a shell of sections, each with its main controls, and a display showing the selected section's further controls and drawings. Every filter and drive law here is Beatlayer's own, built as zero-delay-feedback models of the circuits the manual names, and the formant filter's vowels come from published formant measurements.

## Rules

**Oscillators and noise (WP-8.14)**

- **R1.** Beatlayer ships Analog (`beatlayer.analog`), an instrument listed under the browser's Instruments label, with factory presets of Beatlayer's own. (S11)
- **R2.** Each oscillator has its switch (Oscillator 1 on and Oscillator 2 off in a new device), Level (−∞ to 0 dB), F1/F2 (0 to 100 %, 50 % by default: at 0 % all to Filter 1, at 100 % all to Filter 2, between shared in equal power), Shape (Sine, Saw, Rectangle or White Noise; Saw by default), Octave (−3 to +3), Semi (−12 to +12 semitones) and Detune (−300 to +300 cents). (S2; the ranges are Beatlayer's own)
- **R3.** Saw and Rectangle are band-limited at every key: a saw at the top key folds back less than −80 dB, as Drift's ([polysynth](polysynth.md) R3). Pulse Width (0 to 100 %, 50 % by default) sets the rectangle's duty cycle from 1 % to 99 %, and its Width slider (−100 % to 100 %) moves it by the oscillator's LFO by up to half the duty cycle's range. (S2, S11)
- **R4.** Pitch Mod's LFO (−100 % to 100 %) moves the oscillator's pitch by up to an octave at 100 %; Key (−100 % to 200 %, 100 % by default) is how the pitch follows the key from C3, 100 % one semitone per key; the pitch envelope's Initial (−48 to +48 semitones, 0) is where a note starts, gliding to its pitch in a straight line in semitones over Time (0 ms to 10 s). (S2; the depths are Beatlayer's own)
- **R5.** Sub/Sync is Off, Sub or Sync, with one Amount control (0 to 100 %) that is the sub's level or the sync's ratio. Sub adds a square wave an octave below the oscillator at its Level (0 to 100 %); Sync restarts the oscillator from a hidden oscillator at the note's pitch, its own pitch raised by Ratio (0 to 100 %, a ratio of 1 to 8), band-limited. (S2; the sub's wave and the ratio's span are Beatlayer's own)
- **R6.** The noise generator, with its switch (off in a new device), plays white noise per voice from the device's seeded generator through a one-pole low-pass at Color (20 Hz to 20 kHz, 20 kHz by default), at Level (−∞ to 0 dB) and F1/F2 as R2. (S3)

**Filters (WP-8.14)**

- **R7.** Each filter has its switch (Filter 1 on and Filter 2 off in a new device), Mode (LP12, LP24, BP12, BP24, Notch12, Notch24, HP12, HP24, Formant12 and Formant24, the twelves second order and the twenty-fours fourth order; LP12 by default), Freq (20 Hz to 20 kHz) and Reso (0 to 100 %). Each mode is a zero-delay-feedback model whose response at Reso 0 matches its analogue prototype on the warped axis within 0.2 dB from 200 Hz to 8 kHz at a 1 kHz cutoff. (S4, S11)
- **R8.** A Formant mode is two band-pass resonators at the first two formants of a vowel: Reso from 0 to 100 % moves through the vowels A, E, I, O and U, crossfading the formant frequencies between neighbours, from the average adult male formants of Peterson and Barney, and Freq transposes both formants together, 1 kHz leaving them where the table puts them. (S4, S10; the vowels and the transposing law are Beatlayer's own)
- **R9.** Filter 1's To F2 (0 to 100 %) sends that share of Filter 1's output into Filter 2's input, beside the oscillators' and noise's own shares; with Filter 2 off, nothing goes into it, and Filter 2's Follow (off in a new device, and independent of whether Filter 1 is on) makes Filter 2's cutoff Filter 1's (after its modulation) times 2^o, its own Freq knob read as the offset o from −5 to +5 octaves over its travel. (S4; the offset's span is Beatlayer's own)
- **R10.** Freq Mod and Res Mod each take the filter's LFO, the key and the filter's envelope with amounts from −100 % to 100 %: the cutoff moves by up to four octaves from the LFO, one octave per octave of key from C3 at 100 % Key, and six octaves from the envelope at its full level; resonance moves by up to its whole travel. (S4; the depths are Beatlayer's own)
- **R11.** Drive is Off (the default), Sym 1, Sym 2, Sym 3, Asym 1, Asym 2 or Asym 3, a saturator before the filter: the symmetrical modes a tanh curve after 6, 12 and 18 dB of gain, the asymmetrical modes the same gains with a bias that bends positive and negative halves differently and adds even harmonics, the output's DC removed. Higher numbers distort more. (S4; the curves and gains are Beatlayer's own)

**Amplifiers and envelopes (WP-8.14)**

- **R12.** Each filter feeds its amplifier: each amplifier has its switch (both on in a new device), Level (−∞ to 0 dB) and Pan, with Level Mod and Pan Mod from the amplifier's LFO, the key (Level by up to 12 dB per four octaves from C3, middle C unchanged) and the amplifier's envelope, amounts −100 % to 100 %. The envelope's Level amount is 100 % in a new device, so the level follows the envelope: at 100 % the level is the envelope's own, at 0 % it ignores it, and a negative amount turns the envelope over (1 minus it at −100 %); a voice ends when both amplifiers' envelopes have. With a filter off, its amplifier takes the oscillators' and noise's shares for that filter unfiltered. (S5; the key's depth is Beatlayer's own)
- **R13.** Four envelopes, one each for Filter 1, Filter 2, Amplifier 1 and Amplifier 2, have Attack, Decay and Release (0 ms to 20 s) and Sustain (0 to 100 %); the slope switch makes each segment straight or exponential. Att < Vel (0 to 100 %) shortens the attack with velocity, by up to a factor of 16 at velocity 127; Env < Vel (0 to 100 %) scales the envelope's level by the voice section's velocity law ([polysynth](polysynth.md) R15); S.Time (0 ms to 60 s, or Off, the default) makes the sustain level fall to 0 over that time while the key is held. (S6; the factor and ranges are Beatlayer's own)
- **R14.** Legato (off by default) starts a new note's envelope from the level the previous note's envelope had reached; Free skips Sustain, running Attack and Decay (to the Sustain level) then Release whatever the note's length. (S6)
- **R15.** Loop is Off (the default), AD-R, ADR-R or ADS-R: AD-R repeats Attack and Decay from the current level while the key is held and plays Release when it's let go; ADR-R repeats Attack, Decay and Release while held; ADS-R holds Sustain while held and, when the key is let go, plays Attack again to the peak and then Release, like a damper falling. (S6; the reading of ADS-R is Beatlayer's own)
- **R16.** Analog's body is a shell and display (device framework R41): a strip of sections, Oscillator 1, Oscillator 2, Noise, Filter 1, Filter 2, Amplifier 1, Amplifier 2, LFO 1, LFO 2 and Global, each showing its main controls, the display showing the selected section's further controls (part 2 adds its drawings). A new device shows Oscillator 1's page. Until part 2, the Global section holds PB Range alone, which the voice section needs for the pitch wheel. (S1)
- **R17.** Every control is a parameter, automatable and modulatable (rule G2), Analog's banks hold every one, sixteen voices render inside the device budget, nothing on the audio thread allocates, locks or blocks, and the output is the same at any block size, as long as no pitch is moving: a gliding pitch is the same at 64, 512 and 4,096 frames, the kit placing a mipmap change by block until it places its pitch ramps by frame. The LFO amounts of R3, R4, R10 and R12 act on the LFOs of WP-8.15. (S11)

**LFOs, global and MPE (WP-8.15)**

- **R18.** LFO 1 and LFO 2, each with its switch (off in a new device), run per voice: Wave is Sine, Triangle, Rectangle, S&H (a new random value each cycle, held) or Noise (smooth ramps between random values), from the device's seeded generator; Rate in hertz (0.01 to 30 Hz) or, synced, a division from 1/64 to 8 bars; Width (0 to 100 %) skews the triangle and sets the rectangle's duty cycle; Delay (0 ms to 10 s) holds it at 0 from the note's start and Attack (0 ms to 10 s) then fades it in; Retrig restarts it at Offset (0 to 360°) on each note. LFO 1 is the LFO of Oscillator 1's, Filter 1's and Amplifier 1's modulation controls, LFO 2 of the second side's. (S7; the ranges and the sides are Beatlayer's own)
- **R19.** Volume (−∞ to 0 dB) is the device's output. Vibrato (off in a new device) moves every voice's pitch by a sine at Rate (0.1 to 20 Hz) by up to a semitone at Amount 100 %, after Delay (0 ms to 10 s) and fading in over Attack; Error (0 to 100 %) varies each voice's rate and amount by up to ±50 % from the device's seed; Amt < MW (0 to 100 %) adds the mod wheel's share to the amount. (S8; the ranges are Beatlayer's own)
- **R20.** Unison (off in a new device) stacks 2 or 4 copies (instrument voices R11 to R13) at Detune (0 to 100 %, up to ±50 cents) and Delay (0 to 100 %, up to 20 ms between the first copy and the last). (S8)
- **R21.** Glide (off in a new device) is the voice section's Portamento over Time (0 ms to 10 s) with Legato and Mode, Const or Prop (instrument voices R8, R9). (S8)
- **R22.** Quick Routing's four buttons each set the routing as one undo step, Quick Routing, changing no oscillator level or tuning: Parallel (both filters and amplifiers on, Oscillator 1 and the noise to Filter 1, Oscillator 2 to Filter 2, To F2 0 %); Split (as Parallel, the amplifiers panned hard left and right); Filter 1 Only (Filter 2 and Amplifier 2 off, every source to Filter 1); Serial (both filters on, every source to Filter 1, To F2 100 %, Amplifier 1 off and Amplifier 2 on). (S8; the settings are Beatlayer's own readings)
- **R23.** The keyboard's Octave (−3 to +3), Semi (−12 to +12), Detune (−50 to +50 cents), PB Range (0 to 24 semitones, 2 by default), Stretch and Error follow instrument voices R14 and R17; Voices (1 to 32, 8 by default) and Priority (High, Low or Last, Last by default) follow instrument voices R3. (S8)
- **R24.** The MPE switch (off by default) shows the MPE section; while it is off, Pressure and Slide do nothing and per-note bends use Note PB at 0. Pressure and Slide each have two destination choosers with amounts (−100 % to 100 %), chosen slots of the matrix (instrument modulation R2), whose destinations are Off, Osc 1 Pitch, Osc 2 Pitch, Osc 1 Level, Osc 2 Level, PW 1, PW 2, Noise Level, F1 Freq, F1 Reso, F2 Freq, F2 Reso, Amp 1 Level, Amp 2 Level, LFO 1 Rate, LFO 2 Rate and Vibrato Amount; Note PB (0 to 48 semitones, 48 by default) is the per-note bend's range (instrument voices R14). Activity lights show each source's value for the newest note. (S9; the destination list is Beatlayer's own)
- **R25.** Every routing, global and MPE setting is a parameter, automatable, modulatable and set and read by `device.set_param` and `device.parameters`. (S11)
- **R26.** The display draws, for the selected section, its envelope's shape (filter and amplifier sections), its filter's response with the cutoff and resonance, or its LFO's wave, from the device's display data, which `device.display` reports headless (device framework R35 to R37). (S1, S11)
- **R27.** Selecting a shell section switches the display's controls (device framework R43) without an undo step; at 50 %, 100 % and 200 % zoom the shell and display place every control with nothing overlapping. (S1, S11)
- **R28.** The panel is Beatlayer's own look on Live's placement. (S11)
- **R29.** Every LFO, vibrato and unison random value comes from the device's seed, so a render repeats run after run, and is the same at any block size. (S11)
- **R30.** Nothing the device does on the audio thread allocates, locks or blocks. (S11)
