## The behaviour

A rack is a device that holds other devices in parallel chains. Beatlayer has the four kinds Live 12 has, its equivalents of Live's Instrument, Audio Effect, MIDI Effect and Drum racks, called here instrument racks, audio effect racks, MIDI effect racks and drum racks. A rack sits in a device chain like any other device, in the place its role gives it. Every chain inside it takes the rack's input at the same moment, runs its own devices in order, and passes through a small mixer of its own (volume, pan, an activator, and solo). The rack's output is the sum of its chains. A chain can hold racks too, so racks nest. Group (Cmd-G) wraps the selected devices in a new rack, and Ungroup (Cmd-Shift-G) takes them out again.

In the Device View a rack shows a column of view selectors, then, left to right, its Macro Controls, its chain list, its zone editor and the selected chain's devices. In instrument, MIDI effect and audio effect racks, zones decide what reaches each chain: a range of keys, a range of velocities, and a range of the rack's chain selector, each with a fade range at either end. Another track can take its input from any chain of a rack, before the chain's devices, after them, or after the chain's mixer. A whole rack saves to a preset and loads from one, and Q hot-swaps the selected device, a rack included, from the browser.

This spec covers what every rack has: the rack in the document and the engine, its chains and their mixers, the rack in the Device View, its chains in the Session mixer, zones, chain taps, presets and hot-swap. It leaves to [macros](macros.md) the Macro Controls and their variations, which sit at the rack's left (L-DEV-008), and to [drum racks](drum-racks.md) everything only drum racks have: notes in place of zones, the pad view, choke groups, return chains and sends, and pulling a drum chain out with its notes. Inside a rack, each chain follows [device chains](device-chains.md) as a track's chain does.

## Rules

**The rack in the document and the engine (WP-5.1)**

- **R1.** A rack is a device of one of four kinds, holding an ordered list of chains, each an ordered list of devices. An audio effect rack's chains hold only audio effects, and a MIDI effect rack's only MIDI effects; each chain of an instrument rack or a drum rack holds MIDI effects, then at most one instrument, then audio effects, in [device chains](device-chains.md) R3's order. A rack has the role of what it holds: an audio effect rack is an audio effect, a MIDI effect rack a MIDI effect, and instrument and drum racks are instruments. A command that would put a device in a chain where its role doesn't fit, or break a chain's order, is refused. (S2, S12)
- **R2.** A rack goes where a device of its role goes ([device chains](device-chains.md) R2, R3): MIDI effect, instrument and drum racks only on MIDI tracks; an audio effect rack on any track, after the instrument on a MIDI track; and into another rack's chain wherever its role fits there, so racks nest to any depth. (S1, S2)
- **R3.** Every chain of an instrument, MIDI effect or audio effect rack takes the rack's input at the same frame: in an audio effect rack the same audio, in the other two the same MIDI, each through its own zones (R31 to R34). Each chain runs its devices in order and then its mixer (R5), and the rack's output is the sum of its chains' outputs. A chain with no devices passes its input to its mixer as it is, so it makes a dry path, and a rack with no chains passes its input through (both Beatlayer's own). (S1, S5)
- **R4.** A MIDI effect rack's output is every chain's notes and messages merged in time order; where two fall on the same frame, the chain higher in the list comes first (Beatlayer's own). The MIDI effects in an instrument rack's chain act only on that chain's notes. (S1, S5)
- **R5.** Every chain has a Chain Activator and a Solo switch, and a chain of an instrument, drum or audio effect rack also has Volume and Pan. Volume is a gain that reaches −∞ dB (rule G1), with the track fader's range, and Pan follows the track mixer's constant-power law; a new chain's Volume is 0 dB and its Pan centre (Beatlayer's own, after [mixer](mixer.md) R2 and R3). With its activator off a chain adds nothing to the rack's output. While any chain of a rack is soloed, that rack's unsoloed chains add nothing; a solo inside one rack leaves every other rack and every track as it is (Beatlayer's own). (S4, S12, S13)
- **R6.** A change to a chain's Volume, Pan, activator or Solo reaches the engine as a parameter change, with no new program, and glides in a straight line over 20 ms from the frame it's sent for, as the track mixer's changes do ([mixer](mixer.md) R18, R22). Volume, Pan and the activator are automation targets on the rack's track and can be modulated (rule G2). A chain at 0 dB and centre with its activator on passes its signal to the rack's sum to the bit. (S12, S13)
- **R7.** A rack is a device: its activator, rename, Show Preset Name, fold, clipboard and moves follow [device chains](device-chains.md) R6, R10, R11, R15 and R25, so a rack switched off passes its input through untouched. Every device command works on a device in a rack's chain as it does on a track's chain, and a device moves between a track's chain and a rack's chain, or between racks, by the same commands and drags, keeping its settings and running state ([device chains](device-chains.md) R4). (S3, S12)
- **R8.** A chain can be added (empty, or holding a device or preset), deleted, moved within its rack or into another rack, cut, copied, pasted, duplicated, renamed (Rename, Cmd-R), given info text (Edit Info Text), and coloured from its context menu's palette, each one undo step. A chain made with a device in it is named after that device's title, and an empty one is named Chain (Beatlayer's own). (S4)
- **R9.** Group (Cmd-G, Ctrl-G on Windows and Linux; S-DEV-001, M-EDIT-014, C-DEVICE-007) replaces the selected devices of one chain with a new rack in the first one's place, holding one chain that holds them in order, and selects the rack. The rack is the kind that can hold them: an audio effect rack for audio effects alone, a MIDI effect rack for MIDI effects alone, and an instrument rack for any other selection (Beatlayer's own, from R1). Grouping devices that are already in a rack nests the new rack in their chain. A selection that grouping would put out of the chain's order is refused. Group is one undo step, and every device keeps its settings and running state. (S2, S10, S11)
- **R10.** With several chains of one rack selected, Group replaces them, in the first one's place, with one new chain holding a new rack of the same kind whose chains they are, each keeping its mixer and zones; the new chain is at 0 dB and centre and its zones pass everything (Beatlayer's own). [Drum racks](drum-racks.md) R26 says how a drum rack's chains group. (S2)
- **R11.** Ungroup (Cmd-Shift-G, Ctrl-Shift-G on Windows and Linux; S-DEV-002, M-EDIT-015), with a rack of one chain selected, puts that chain's devices in the rack's place, in order, and selects the first of them; a rack with no chains is removed. The chain's mixer and zones and the rack's Macro Controls go with the rack. With a rack of several chains selected, Ungroup is disabled (Beatlayer's own). It is one undo step. (S2, S10, S11)
- **R12.** Option-click on a rack's activator (Alt-click on Windows and Linux; I-CLICK-024, S-DEV-003) switches the rack and every device inside it, at every depth, to the state the click gives the rack's activator, as one undo step. (S10, S11)
- **R13.** A rack, its chains (with their names, colours, info text, mixers and zones) and their devices are part of the set and saved with it, and sets saved before racks existed load unchanged. Every edit to a rack is one undo step, a drag included, however long the gesture. (S12, S13)
- **R14.** Adding, deleting or moving a chain, or a device inside a rack, and grouping and ungrouping, compile a new program in which only the devices new to the set get new nodes; every other node migrates with its state, a device moved into or out of a rack included ([device chains](device-chains.md) R7). (S12, S13)
- **R15.** A rack's chains run inside its track's task, each on a buffer made with the program, and their sum is made in place, so running a rack allocates nothing, takes no lock and blocks on nothing on the audio thread. A rack's output is the same to the bit at any block size. (S13)
- **R16.** A rack's sum is a summing point for delay compensation ([delay compensation](delay-compensation.md) R2): each chain's output is held back to the latency of the rack's slowest chain, and the rack reports that latency as its own, so its chains reach the sum aligned and its track counts the rack once. A device switched off in a chain changes that chain's latency as [delay compensation](delay-compensation.md) R1 says, and the alignment follows; a chain's activator and Solo don't change its latency (Beatlayer's own). (S7, S12)
- **R17.** Every rack action is a registered command with a schema, a description and a permission class (D17), which agents run with the rack, chain or device named; a chain has a stable ID of its own. What each command sets reads back through the query tool's `device` object: a rack's `chains`, and each chain's `devices`, mixer and `zones`. Deleting a chain is destructive. (S13)

**The rack in the Device View (WP-5.2)**

- **R18.** A rack's frame has a device's title bar ([device chains](device-chains.md) R22), and below it, down its left edge, a view column of view selectors: Macro Controls, chain list and devices, and in a drum rack its own as well ([drum racks](drum-racks.md) R11, R21). Inside the rack, left to right, come the Macro Controls ([macros](macros.md)), the chain list, the zone editor (R35) and the selected chain's devices, which the rack's end bracket closes. A rack inside a chain shows there as a bracket inside its parent's. (S3, S11)
- **R19.** Showing and hiding a rack's views changes only the view: it isn't an undo step, and the set keeps which views each rack shows (Beatlayer's own). With every view hidden the rack folds into its view column; a double-click on its title bar, or Fold, folds it as any device folds ([device chains](device-chains.md) R11). (S3, S12)
- **R20.** View ▸ Show Chains (Plus; S-DEV-012, M-VIEW-041) and Hide Chains (Minus; S-DEV-013, M-VIEW-042) show and hide the selected rack's chain list, and are disabled while no rack is selected. With a rack selected, Minus is Hide Chains while its chain list shows and Fold while it doesn't (Beatlayer's own, as the two share the key: S-DEV-014). (S11)
- **R21.** The chain list has a row per chain, in order, each with its name, Chain Activator, Solo and Hot-Swap buttons, and, in instrument, drum and audio effect racks, its Volume and Pan sliders. Below the rows, a drop area takes devices, presets and chains: a device or preset dropped there makes a new chain holding it at the end of the list, and a chain dropped there moves to the end of the list. (S4)
- **R22.** Clicking a chain's row selects it, and the devices view shows the selected chain's devices; Up and Down select the chain above or below. Shift-click selects every chain from the selected one to the clicked one, and Cmd-click (Ctrl-click) adds or removes one, as tracks are selected (Beatlayer's own); with several selected, the devices view says how many and shows no devices. Cut, Copy, Paste, Duplicate, Delete, Rename and Group act on the selected chains, and S solos the selected chain while the chain list has the focus (S-SES-023). Selecting isn't an undo step. (S4, S10, S11)
- **R23.** Clicking a chain's Solo button solos it and unsolos the rack's other chains, and Cmd-click (Ctrl-click) adds or removes it alone, as a track's Solo button does with Exclusive Solo on (I-CLICK-022; Beatlayer's own for chains). Its activator switches the chain on and off. Its Hot-Swap button arms the chain for hot-swap (R45). (S4, S11)
- **R24.** Dragging a chain's row moves the chain within its rack, or into another rack's chain list; held over another track's title bar or lane, the drag focuses that track and opens its Device View, so the chain can be dropped into a rack there. A chain dropped into a track's chain outside any rack puts only its devices there, in order. Each drop is one undo step. (S4, S6)
- **R25.** Right-clicking the Device View selector (S-DEV-005) lists every device on the selected track as a hierarchy, each rack's devices under it, chain by chain; choosing one selects it and scrolls it into view, showing its chain in its rack's devices view. (S3, S11)
- **R26.** A rack's title bar menu is a device's (C-DEVICE, [device chains](device-chains.md) R24) with Group working on the selection, Ungroup straight after it (Beatlayer's own place), and the two Compare items disabled, because A/B compare doesn't apply to racks. A chain's context menu holds Rename, Edit Info Text and the colour palette. (S2, S4, S7, S11)

**Its chains in the mixer (WP-5.19)**

- **R27.** In the Session View's mixer, a track holding an instrument or drum rack with more than one chain has a fold button in its title bar that shows or hides the rack's chains as strips after the track; a rack inside a shown chain has its own fold button on that chain's strip. A chain's strip is drawn as a track's without clip slots, with the chain list's controls for that chain, and a change in either place shows in the other at once. (S6)
- **R28.** Clicking a chain strip's title bar selects the chain and shows only its devices in the Device View. With several chain strips selected, changing a control on one changes the same control on each, keeping their differences, as for tracks (I-CLICK-023); the same change in the chain list changes only its own chain. (S6, S11)
- **R29.** Chains are reordered by dragging their strips, renamed and grouped from their strips as from the chain list, with the same commands and undo steps. (S6)

**Zones and Auto Select (WP-5.3)**

- **R30.** Each chain of an instrument rack or a MIDI effect rack has a key zone, a velocity zone and a chain select zone; each chain of an audio effect rack has only a chain select zone; drum racks have none ([drum racks](drum-racks.md) R1). A zone is a range with a fade range inside each end: a low value, the value its fade-in reaches full strength at, the value its fade-out starts at, and a high value, in that order or equal. A new chain's zones pass everything: its key zone is every note (0 to 127), its velocity zone 1 to 127, and its chain select zone the value 0 alone, with no fades. (S5)
- **R31.** A note-on reaches a chain only if its pitch is in the chain's key zone, then its velocity in its velocity zone, then the rack's chain selector in its chain select zone. Its note-off reaches every chain its note-on reached, whatever the zones are by then (Beatlayer's own). Messages other than notes reach every chain, unless Chain Selector Filters MIDI Ctrl is on (off in a new rack; the chain select ruler's context menu), when the chain select zone filters them too. (S5)
- **R32.** A note inside a key or velocity zone's fade range reaches the chain with its velocity scaled by the fade's share at its value, in a straight line (Beatlayer's own curve): for a fade-in from a low value L to a full value F, the share at v is (v − L + 1) / (F − L + 1); for a fade-out from a value G to a high value H, it's (H − v + 1) / (H − G + 1); a value outside the fades has the share 1. The scaled velocity is rounded to the nearest whole number and is never below 1. (S5)
- **R33.** The chain selector is a parameter of the rack, a whole number from 0 to 127, 0 in a new rack, automatable and modulatable like any parameter (rule G2). In an instrument or MIDI effect rack it filters notes as R31 says. In an audio effect rack, a chain whose chain select zone doesn't hold the selector's value takes silence as its input, so its devices' tails ring out; its input fades in or out over 20 ms as the selector enters or leaves the zone (Beatlayer's own). (S5, S13)
- **R34.** A chain select zone's fades act on the chain's output gain in instrument and audio effect racks, and on its notes' velocities in a MIDI effect rack, by R32's share at the selector's value. Where a zone ends in a fade, the chain's output is silent while the selector is beyond that end; where it ends without one, its output isn't attenuated, and whatever it still sounds decays on its own. A change in the gain glides over 20 ms. (S5)
- **R35.** The zone editor sits right of the chain list, a row per chain level with the chain's row, under the zone's scale: every MIDI note for keys, 1 to 127 for velocity, and 0 to 127 for chain select, with the draggable chain selector above it. The Key, Vel and Chain buttons above the chain list each show their editor in that place, one at a time, and Hide closes it; an audio effect rack has only Chain. Dragging a zone's lower bar by an edge moves that end, dragging its body moves the zone, and dragging the narrow bar above it by an edge sets that fade; each drag is one undo step and keeps R30's order. (S5)
- **R36.** Each zone of a chain, with its fades, and Chain Selector Filters MIDI Ctrl, are saved with the set and set by commands, each one undo step, and read back through the chain's `zones` in the query tool. (S5, S13)
- **R37.** With Auto Select on, the chain list selects every chain that is processing: in instrument and MIDI effect racks each chain a held note reached, and in audio effect racks each chain whose chain select zone holds the selector's value; the devices view follows. Its switch sits above the chain list beside the zone buttons (Beatlayer's own place). The engine publishes which chains each rack is processing after every block; the switch and the selections it makes aren't undo steps. (S4)

**Rack chain taps (WP-5.7)**

- **R38.** An audio track whose Audio From names a track holding instrument or audio effect racks, at any depth, offers in its channel chooser, after that track's own Pre FX, Post FX and Post Mixer, three taps for every chain of those racks. The track may be an audio or group track, a return or Main, as [routing](routing.md) R2 lists them, or a MIDI track with an instrument, which Audio From lists as a source once it holds one. The taps are: Pre FX (the audio entering the rack), Post FX (the chain's devices' output, before its mixer) and Post Mixer (after the chain's mixer, before the rack's sum). A drum rack offers its return chains the same way ([drum racks](drum-racks.md) R10). (S9)
- **R39.** Each chain tap reads as the rack's title, the chain's name and the tap, with vertical bars between (`Keys | Pad | Post FX`); racks come in signal order, a nested rack straight after the chain that holds it, and each rack's chains in their order (Beatlayer's own order). (S9, S12)
- **R40.** A chain tap carries the chain's signal at that point, and taking it doesn't change the rack's output. Solo never silences a chain tap: a soloed track that takes one still hears it, at each of the three taps. A chain tap is a route into the track that takes it, held back for delay compensation as every route is ([delay compensation](delay-compensation.md) R2), and the tapped track runs first ([routing](routing.md) R14). (S9, S12)
- **R41.** Taking a chain tap is `routing.set_input` with the chain and its tap, one undo step saved with the set, and reads back as the track's input. A track isn't offered its own racks' chains ([routing](routing.md) R2). Deleting the chain, its rack or its track leaves the input at No Input, and undo restores both ([routing](routing.md) R7). (S12)

**Rack presets and hot-swap (WP-5.8)**

- **R42.** Save Preset on a rack writes the whole rack to one preset file in Beatlayer's own format: its kind and name, its chains in order with their names, colours, mixers and zones, each device with its state, nested racks whole, and its Macro Controls ([macros](macros.md)). It keeps each plug-in's own state, so putting a plug-in in a rack and saving the rack keeps the plug-in's settings, and it keeps the samples its devices play, so the preset loads in a set whose media store lacks them. The file goes in the User Library's folder for the rack's role ([device chains](device-chains.md) R17) and is written whole or not at all; a rack preset from a newer Beatlayer, or a damaged one, is refused. (S7, S8, S12)
- **R43.** The browser lists an empty rack of each kind with the devices of its role (the instrument rack under Instruments, the drum rack under Drums, the audio effect rack under Audio Effects, the MIDI effect rack under MIDI Effects; L-BRW-004), and the User Library's rack presets in their folders. Adding one, by a drag, Enter or a double-click, inserts the rack as [device chains](device-chains.md) R12 inserts a device, in one undo step; an empty rack has no chains. Save as Default Preset on a rack makes every new rack of its kind start as it is ([device chains](device-chains.md) R18). (S2, S7, S8)
- **R44.** Q (S-VIEW-006, S-DEV-008) switches hot-swap on and off for the selected device, as its Hot-Swap Presets button does ([device chains](device-chains.md) R19); with no device selected, it arms the first audio effect on an audio track, or the instrument on a MIDI track. Q again, Esc, the hot-swap bar's close button, the device's button, or going from the browser to another view ends it. (S7, S10, S11)
- **R45.** While a device or rack is armed, the browser opens the presets of its own kind, and loading any device, preset or rack preset of the same role (an audio effect or audio effect rack for an audio effect; an instrument, instrument rack or drum rack for an instrument; a MIDI effect or MIDI effect rack for a MIDI effect) replaces it in its place, without the automation of a device of another kind; a preset of its own kind loads into it as [device chains](device-chains.md) R16 says. Each load is one undo step, and hot-swap stays on, so the next load replaces what the last one put there. A chain armed by its Hot-Swap button (R23) is replaced by a loaded chain preset, and takes a loaded device or preset as its only device, keeping its mixer and zones (Beatlayer's own). (S4, S7)
- **R46.** Dragging a chain's row into the browser's User Library, or a folder in Places, saves it as a chain preset file of Beatlayer's own format, as a clip is saved (I-DROP-011); a chain preset dropped on a chain list's drop area adds it as a chain, and one loaded while a rack is armed adds it to that rack. (S4, S11)
- **R47.** Saving and loading rack and chain presets and hot-swapping are registered commands (D17): loading reads back through the query tool's `device` object, and saving, which writes a file, names the file it wrote. (S13)
