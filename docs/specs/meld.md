## The behaviour

Meld is a bi-timbral synthesiser: two engines, A and B, each an oscillator with two macro controls, a filter with two macro controls, an amplitude envelope, a modulation envelope and two LFOs, mixed per voice and played together. An engine's oscillator is one of twenty-five types, from basic shapes and swarms of detuned copies to FM, chip sounds, an endless Shepard tone, noise textures and a four-note chord; its two macros are the controls each type gives. Its filter is one of seventeen types, from state-variable and analogue-modelled filters to a phaser, a bit-crusher, a vowel filter, combs and two modal resonators.

Meld is scale-aware: with Use Current Scale on, the oscillators that can follow a scale do (Dual Basic Shapes, the four swarms, Chip and Chord), the resonator filters tune to it, and the pitch controls count scale degrees. A matrix routes each engine's envelopes, LFOs, the MIDI sources and MPE to its parameters. The panel is drawn in tabs, with an Expanded View that shows every source and target at once.

Every oscillator and filter algorithm here is Beatlayer's own reading of the manual's description of its type and macros.

## Rules

**The engines (WP-8.17)**

- **R1.** Beatlayer ships Meld (`beatlayer.meld`), an instrument listed under the browser's Instruments label, with factory presets of Beatlayer's own. (S11, S12)
- **R2.** Meld has two engines, A and B, each with its switch (A on and B off in a new device), an oscillator with a type and two macros, a filter with its switch, a type and two macros, an amplitude envelope, a modulation envelope and two LFOs. An engine switched off is silent and so is its filter; a filter switched off passes its engine through. Each voice plays both engines and mixes them in the Mix section (R18). (S1, S2, S11)
- **R3.** Each engine's pitch is the note's plus Octaves (−3 to +3), Semitones (−12 to +12) and Cents (−50 to +50); with Use Current Scale on, Semitones counts degrees of the device's current scale and reads "sd" instead of "st". (S2, S10)
- **R4.** Each engine's Type is one of the twenty-five oscillator types in the manual's order: Basic Shapes, Dual Basic Shapes, Noisy Shapes, Square Sync, Square 5th, Sub, Swarm Sine, Swarm Triangle, Swarm Saw, Swarm Square, Harmonic FM, Fold FM, Squelch, Simple FM, Chip, Shepard's Pi, Tarp, Extratone, Noise Loop, Filtered Noise, Bitgrunge, Crackle, Rain, Bubble and Chord (Basic Shapes in a new device). The arrows beside the chooser step through the list and wrap at its ends. (S2, S3, S11)
- **R5.** Each engine has two macro parameters, Macro 1 and Macro 2 (0 to 100 %), whose names on the panel and in `device.parameters`' readings are the type's (Shape and Tone for Basic Shapes, and so on), and which keep their values when the type changes; the parameters' IDs never change, so automation and mappings survive a change of type. (S3; Beatlayer's own)
- **R6.** Every oscillator type is band-limited at every key: none folds back more than −80 dB at the top key, the noise types aside. (S12)

**The first ten types (WP-8.17)**

- **R7.** Basic Shapes: Shape morphs sine (0 %), triangle (33 %), saw (67 %) and square (100 %), crossfading neighbours between; Tone narrows the square's and the saw's duty cycle from 50 % to 5 %. Noisy Shapes is Basic Shapes whose Rough adds band-limited noise to the phase, up to a quarter of a cycle at 100 %, from the device's seed. (S3; Beatlayer's own)
- **R8.** Dual Basic Shapes: two Basic Shapes waves at Shape; Detune from 0 to 50 % detunes the second up by up to 50 cents, and from 50 % to 100 % moves it up through the intervals of an octave in semitones, or through the degrees of the current scale with Oscillator Scale Awareness on, landing on each in turn. (S3, S8; Beatlayer's own)
- **R9.** Square Sync: a square hard-synced to the note's frequency, its own frequency set by Freq 1 (a ratio from 1 to 8) and a second synced square at Freq 2 (a ratio from 1 to 8), mixed equally. Square 5th: a square at the note, with a square a fifth above (ratio 3 / 2) mixed in by 5th Amt, both at P Width (5 to 95 %, 50 % at its middle). (S3; Beatlayer's own)
- **R10.** Sub: Tone morphs a sine at the note into a square; Aux mixes in a sine an octave below. (S3)
- **R11.** Swarm Sine, Swarm Triangle, Swarm Saw and Swarm Square: eight copies of their wave per voice. Motion drifts each copy's pitch by a smoothed random walk from the device's seed, by up to ±30 cents at a speed rising with Motion; Spacing fades the copies from a unison spread of ±10 cents towards a chord of octaves and fifths above the note (0, 7, 12, 19, 24, 31, 36 and 43 semitones), or, with Oscillator Scale Awareness on, towards every second degree of the current scale. (S3, S8; Beatlayer's own)

**Filters, Mix, global and Settings (WP-8.17)**

- **R12.** Each engine's filter has Frequency (20 Hz to 20 kHz) and its type's two macros (Filter Macro 1 and 2, named by the type as R5 says). In this part the types are SVF 12dB and SVF 24dB, the instruments' Clean state-variable filter at 12 and 24 dB per octave, with Q (resonance) and L-B-H-N (morphing low-pass, band-pass, high-pass and notch as instrument filter R10 sweeps its first three-quarters). (S9, S12)
- **R13.** The amplitude envelope (Attack 1 ms, Decay, Sustain, Release; R59 adds slopes and loop modes) shapes its engine's level per voice; a voice ends when both engines' amplitude envelopes have reached silence. (S4, S11)
- **R14.** Mono or Poly (Poly by default): Poly plays up to its voices (2 to 12, 8 by default); Mono plays one voice, with Legato (off by default) continuing the envelopes on a new note (instrument voices R6, R7). Stacked Voices (1, 2, 3 or 4, 1 by default) plays that many copies of both engines per note (instrument voices R11 to R13). Until the matrix (R62) routes the pitch wheel, it moves every note by PB Range (1 to 96 semitones, 7 by default), and a per-note bend moves only its note over MPE's 48 semitones (instrument voices R14). (S10; PB Range is Beatlayer's own)
- **R15.** Spread (0 to 100 %) sets the Spread source's range: with Stacked Voices above 1, copy i of n reads (2i / (n − 1) − 1) × Spread; with one copy, each new note reads a value from −Spread to +Spread from the device's seed. The source moves nothing until a matrix route uses it. (S10; the laws are Beatlayer's own)
- **R16.** Settings, per engine: Osc Key Tracking (on by default; off, the engine plays C3, or the current scale's root in C3's octave while Use Current Scale is on); Phase Reset (on by default) starts the oscillator at phase 0 on each note, and Phase Spread (0 to 100 %, only while Phase Reset is on) offsets that start by up to a whole cycle times the Spread source; Oscillator Scale Awareness and Filter Scale Awareness switches; Glide as Portamento or Glissando with Glide Time (0 ms to 10 s), in Mono and Poly (instrument voices R8, R10), Glissando stepping scale degrees while Use Current Scale and Oscillator Scale Awareness are on. (S8, S11)
- **R17.** Use Current Scale is Meld's switch in its title bar ([scales](scales.md) R16, R26). It changes the pitch the engines play, never the notes the device receives: a note's key, its note-off and its expressions are as the clip or controller sent them ([scales](scales.md) R17, R27). (S2, S10, S12)
- **R18.** The Mix section, per engine: Volume (−∞ to 0 dB), Pan, Tone (−100 % to 100 %: positive values a high-pass from 20 Hz up to 2 kHz, negative values a low-pass from 20 kHz down to 200 Hz) and Limit. After the engines are mixed, Mixer Drive (0 to 24 dB) saturates each voice, then Limit, while either engine's switch is on, holds the voice below 0 dBFS with a per-voice limiter with no lookahead and a 50 ms release. Volume (−∞ to 0 dB) sets the device's output. (S10; the ranges and the limiter's law are Beatlayer's own) Limit is a switch of the Global section, on in a new device; an engine's Volume is −3 dB in a new device and the device's Volume −6 dB.
- **R19.** Meld's body is in pages (device framework R41): the engine selector A and B choosing which engine's controls the panel shows (view state), and the tabs Envelopes, LFOs, Matrix, MIDI, MPE and Settings; the generic panel draws them until part 4. (S1, S12)
- **R20.** Every control is a parameter, automatable and modulatable (rule G2), and Meld's banks hold every one. (S12)
- **R21.** Under a tuning every engine plays its key's tuned pitch within 0.1 cent (instrument voices R18). (S12)
- **R22.** Sixteen voices of Swarm Saw on both engines with Stacked Voices at 1 render at 48 kHz inside the device budget. (S12)
- **R23.** The device's output is the same at any block size and run after run; nothing on the audio thread allocates, locks or blocks. (S12)

**The other fifteen types (WP-8.18)**

- **R24.** Harmonic FM: a sine carrier phase-modulated by a sine at a whole-number Ratio (1 to 16 over the macro's travel), at index 8 × Amount. (S3; Beatlayer's own)
- **R25.** Fold FM: a carrier whose wave Shape morphs from sine to triangle, phase-modulated by a sine at the note at index 8 × Amount, the result passed through a sine wavefolder. (S3; Beatlayer's own)
- **R26.** Squelch: a sine carrier phase-modulated by a sine at twice the note at index 8 × Amount, the modulator feeding back on itself by up to 1.5 radians at Feedback 100 % ([Operator](operator.md) R3's law). (S3; Beatlayer's own)
- **R27.** Simple FM: a sine carrier phase-modulated by a sine at a continuous Ratio (0.5 to 16, logarithmically), inharmonic between whole numbers, at index 8 × Amount. The FM types use the FM operator core ([Operator](operator.md) R1). (S3; Beatlayer's own)
- **R28.** Chip: a pulse wave in 16 levels whose Tone moves its pulse width from 50 % to 12.5 % and its pitch up through 0, 12 and 24 semitones in thirds of its travel; Rate cycles the pitch through the note, a third above and a fifth above, as a chip arpeggio, from once a second to 50 times, or through the first, third and fifth degrees of the current scale with Oscillator Scale Awareness on. (S3, S8; Beatlayer's own)
- **R29.** Shepard's Pi: sine partials an octave apart under a fixed bell over the logarithm of frequency, as many octaves as Width gives (1 to 10), sliding together up (Rate above 50 %) or down (below 50 %) at up to an octave a second at the ends and still at 50 %; a partial that leaves the bell's top re-enters at its bottom at zero level, so the rise or fall never steps. (S3; Beatlayer's own)
- **R30.** Tarp: a noise burst from the device's seed exciting a comb tuned to the note, Decay setting the comb's feedback (the tone's ringing from 10 ms to 4 s) and Tone a low-pass in its loop. (S3; Beatlayer's own)
- **R31.** Extratone: a sine kick retriggered at the note's frequency, each hit's pitch falling from Pitch (1 to 8 times the note) to the note with a depth set by Env Amount, so the repeated hits sound at the note. (S3; Beatlayer's own)
- **R32.** Noise Loop: a loop of noise from the device's seed one period of the note long, so it sounds pitched; Rate replaces the loop's contents with fresh noise more often, from never to every period, the sound turning to noise; Fade crossfades each loop's ends over up to half its length. (S3; Beatlayer's own)
- **R33.** Filtered Noise: white noise from the device's seed through a band-pass whose centre is the note times Freq (a ratio from 0.25 to 4) and whose width is Width (from a twentieth of an octave to four octaves). (S3; Beatlayer's own)
- **R34.** Bitgrunge: a square at the note times Freq (a ratio from 0.5 to 4) combined by exclusive-or with squares at successive octaves below it, as many as Mult gives (seven at its minimum, none at its maximum). (S3; Beatlayer's own)
- **R35.** Crackle: clicks at random times from the device's and the voice's seed, on average Density (1 to 2,000) per second, each a decaying impulse whose loudness and brightness are drawn from a distribution Intensity widens. Rain: drops (short decaying sines whose pitch rises in each drop) at Rate (1 to 500 per second) over filtered wind noise, Tone setting how strongly drops and wind resonate at the note's pitch. Bubble: bubbles (sines chirping upward as they decay) at Density (1 to 200 per second), each one's size and so its pitch varied around the note by Spread. Each random time and size comes from a generator seeded per device and voice, never from time. (S3; Beatlayer's own)
- **R36.** Chord: four saws at the note and three intervals above it from a list of chord shapes chosen by Shape (from a third and a fifth up to sixths, sevenths, ninths and suspended chords, in order of their span); Inversion moves the lowest notes up an octave in turn. With Use Current Scale on, the shape's intervals are degrees of the current scale counted from the note; otherwise degrees of a major scale on the note. (S3, S11; the shapes are Beatlayer's own)
- **R37.** Every one of the fifteen is band-limited, or for the noise types filtered, so nothing folds back above −80 dB at the top key; the random types render identically run after run from their seeds. (S12)
- **R38.** With Oscillator Scale Awareness on, the scale-aware types (Dual Basic Shapes, the swarms, Chip and Chord) follow the current scale as their rules say, from the frame the current scale changes ([scales](scales.md) R15). (S8, S12)
- **R39.** The fifteen add nothing that allocates, locks or blocks on the audio thread, at any block size. (S12)

**The filter types (WP-8.19)**

- **R40.** Each engine's filter Type is one of seventeen, in this order: SVF 12dB, SVF 24dB, MS2 LP, MS2 HP, OSR, LP Crunch 12dB, LP Switched Res, Filther, EQ Peak, EQ Notch, Phaser, Redux, Vowel, Comb +, Comb −, Plate Resonator and Membrane Resonator. MS2 is two types, its low-pass and its high-pass. (S9; the reading of seventeen is Beatlayer's own)
- **R41.** Where a type's description gives no other, its macros are Q (resonance, 0 to 100 %) and Drive (0 to 24 dB into the filter). (S9)
- **R42.** MS2 LP and MS2 HP are the instruments' MS2 circuit at 12 dB per octave (instrument filter R6); OSR is the instruments' OSR circuit as a band-pass (instrument filter R5). (S9, S12)
- **R43.** LP Crunch 12dB is a 12 dB low-pass whose resonance path passes through a soft clipper and feeds its own distorted output back into its input, so distortion grows with Q and Drive. (S9; Beatlayer's own)
- **R44.** LP Switched Res is a 12 dB low-pass whose state updates only at a switching rate set by Lofi, from 44 kHz down to 500 Hz, holding between, so its output steps; higher Lofi crushes more. Its other macro is Q. (S9; Beatlayer's own)
- **R45.** Filther is a 24 dB low-pass with a hard diode clipper on its input (Drive) and a tanh saturator on its output. (S9; Beatlayer's own)
- **R46.** EQ Peak boosts a band around Frequency by Boost (0 to 24 dB) at Q; EQ Notch cuts it by Cut (0 to −∞ dB) at Q. (S9)
- **R47.** Phaser is six first-order all-pass stages at Frequency with the output fed back inverted by Feedback (0 to 95 %), with no delay in the feedback (solved implicitly), and Spread setting the ratio between the stages' frequencies, so the notches spread wider as it rises. (S9, S12)
- **R48.** Redux resamples the signal at Frequency's rate (the cutoff read as a sample rate) and reduces it to Crush's bit depth (16 down to 1); Lofi mixes the downsampled signal from fully filtered (a low-pass at half the reduced rate) to unfiltered. (S9, S12)
- **R49.** Vowel is three band-pass formants that Morph moves through the vowels A, E, I, O and U from Peterson and Barney's average formants (as [Analog](analog.md) R8), Frequency transposing them and Q setting their sharpness. (S9; Beatlayer's own)
- **R50.** Comb + adds a copy of the signal delayed by one period of Frequency (feedforward and feedback, positive), and Comb − subtracts it; Feedback (0 to 99 %) sets how long it rings and Damp a low-pass on its output; the delay is fractional and tuned within 1 cent as a waveguide is ([physical models](physical-models.md) R8). (S9, S12)
- **R51.** Plate Resonator is the physical models' Plate with 32 modes (physical models R1 to R3), its fundamental at Frequency, its macros Resonance (the modes' decay, 10 ms to 10 s) and Ratio (the plate's sides, 0.25 to 4). Membrane Resonator is the Circular Membrane with 32 modes, its macros Resonance and Damping (the physical models' Material, reversed). Both are excited by the engine's signal. (S9, S12)
- **R52.** With Filter Scale Awareness on and Use Current Scale on, Plate Resonator's and Membrane Resonator's Frequency moves to the nearest note of the device's current scale (its twelve-step or tuned pitch), from the frame the scale changes. (S8, S9)
- **R53.** Each type at small signal (−40 dBFS) follows its own linear prototype within 0.5 dB from 20 Hz to 20 kHz, where it has one (the SVFs, MS2, OSR, EQ Peak, EQ Notch, Phaser, Vowel and the combs), and each nonlinear type keeps its output below +12 dBFS at any setting. (S12)
- **R54.** The resonator types ring at their physical models' ratios within 1 cent. (S12)
- **R55.** Every type runs per voice in the plug-in kit's lanes, its macros read per frame. (S12)
- **R56.** Changing an engine's filter type lands on its frame without a click (instrument filter R11). (S12)
- **R57.** Every type's output is the same at any block size, and nothing allocates, locks or blocks. (S12)
- **R58.** Every filter macro and Frequency is a parameter, automatable and modulatable. (S12)

**Modulation and the panel (WP-8.20)**

- **R59.** Each engine's amplitude and modulation envelopes have Attack (1 ms by default), Decay and Release (0 ms to 20 s) and Sustain (0 to 100 %), each segment with a slope shaped as [Operator](operator.md) R13, and a loop mode: None (the default), Trigger (Attack, Decay and Release once, ignoring Sustain and the note's end), Loop (the whole envelope over and over without holding Sustain, until the note ends) or AD Loop (Attack and Decay over and over while the key is held, then Release). The modulation envelope has Initial, Peak and Final levels (−100 % to 100 %). Link Envelopes (off by default) makes an engine's modulation envelope take its amplitude envelope's times and slopes. (S4, S11)
- **R60.** LFO 1 of each engine has a type, Basic Shapes, Ramp, Wander, Alternate, Euclid or Pulsate, with Rate (0.01 to 40 Hz, or synced from 1/64 to 8 bars) and two macros: Basic Shapes morphs sine, triangle, saw and square (Shape) with a width (Tone); Ramp rises or falls (Direction) with a curve (Curve); Wander moves smoothly between random values (Smooth, Range); Alternate switches between two levels (Level A, Level B); Euclid plays a Euclidean rhythm of pulses (Steps, Pulses); Pulsate repeats an envelope-like pulse (Width, Decay). LFO 2 is Sine, Tri, Saw Up, Saw Down, Rectangle or Random S&H at its Rate. Each LFO has Phase Offset (0 to 360°) and Retrigger, restarting it at the offset on each note; free-running, it follows the song position while the transport runs. Random values come from the device's seed. (S5; the macros' names and laws are Beatlayer's own)
- **R61.** LFO 1 FX has two effect slots in series after LFO 1, each one of eighteen effects with an amount macro: Off, Scrambler (permuting four ranges of the input's amplitude), Quantize, Smooth, Slew Up, Slew Down, Rectify, Invert, Fold, Wrap, Bias, Gain, Sample & Hold, Stutter, Reverse, Delay, Swing and Chaos (Beatlayer's own names beside Scrambler, which the release notes give). LFO 1, LFO 1 FX and LFO 2 are three separate sources. (S5, S11)
- **R62.** Each engine's matrix sources are its Amp Env, Mod Env, LFO 1, LFO 1 FX and LFO 2, and the shared Velocity, Pitch (the key), Random, Pitch Bend, Press, Mod Wheel, MPE Slide, MPE Press and Spread; its destinations are every one of its engine's parameters and the global ones, named with the engine's letter ("A Filter Frequency"), with Pitch Quant (a transposition in semitones, or in scale degrees with Use Current Scale on). A route into an envelope's or an LFO's time speeds it up for a negative amount and slows it for a positive one. The engines' levels, Volume and the LFOs' amounts multiply; the rest add. Meld has 64 free slots. (S5, S6, S7, S10)
- **R63.** The Matrix tab shows, as a grid, the sources across and, down, the destinations of the engine shown that have a route; clicking a control in the panel while the Matrix tab shows adds its parameter as a row, and a row without a route leaves when another is added. Dragging in a cell sets its amount, one undo step (instrument modulation R8). (S6)
