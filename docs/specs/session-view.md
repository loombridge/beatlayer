# Session View: clip slots and scenes

- **Status:** implemented
- **Work packages:** WP-3.1, WP-3.7, WP-3.6, WP-3.5, WP-3.8
- **Parity-matrix row:** Views and workflow: Session View; Clips and launching
- **Checked against:** the Live 12 reference manual, chapter 7 "Session View", read 15 October 2026, and Live 12.4.3 on macOS, observed on 15 October 2026.

## The behaviour

The Session View is a grid: every audio and MIDI track is a column with one clip slot per scene, and every scene is a row, listed at the Main track's column with its number, or its name where the user gave it one. A slot holds a Session clip or nothing; a Session clip is a clip like an Arrangement clip — MIDI notes, or audio named by content hash — but with no position in the Arrangement. Scenes are inserted, deleted, moved, duplicated, and renamed, and a clip is put in a slot, taken out of it, or moved between slots. A new set opens with eight scenes. Everything here is in the document, so it saves with the set and every edit is one undo step; nothing here reaches the audio thread.

The scenes are held at the document level and the slots in the tracks, so deleting a track deletes its slot clips and leaves the scenes (`track.delete`'s own spec covers it). The user can delete every scene and insert more afterwards; the grid builds and scrolls any number of them, but only `session.scroll` reaches its scenes so far (the wheel doesn't).

This spec covers the grid's structure in the document, the scenes, the clips in slots, and the commands that edit them. It leaves to other packages: launching, the slot states and stop buttons (WP-3.2, [launch-quantisation.md](launch-quantisation.md)); the clip's launch settings (WP-3.4). The grid's drawing, selection, dragging and dropping are rules R15 to R19 (WP-3.7), Session clips over the Arrangement and Back to Arrangement are rules R20 to R24 (WP-3.6), a scene's tempo and time signature, the Scene View, and Capture and Insert Scene are rules R25 to R30 (WP-3.5), and launching from the grid is rules R31 and R32 (WP-3.8); what it doesn't do yet is under "Not built yet". What a clip holds is [midi-clips.md](midi-clips.md) and [audio-clips.md](audio-clips.md).

## Rules

- **R1.** A new set holds eight scenes, in order, each unnamed: a scene without a name shows its number, which is its position. (S1, S2, S3, S6)
- **R2.** Every audio and MIDI track holds a clip slot in every scene, and a slot holds a clip of its track's kind or nothing: an audio slot takes an audio clip and refuses a MIDI one, a MIDI slot the other way round. Return tracks, group tracks, and the Main track hold no slot clips. (S1, S3)
- **R3.** A slot clip is a clip as an Arrangement clip is — MIDI notes with their markers and loop, or audio named by content hash with its settings — and it has no position in the Arrangement: its start is 0, and a clip meant for a slot with another start is refused. (S2, S4)
- **R4.** `scene.create` inserts a new, unnamed, empty scene at the index it's given: every audio and MIDI track then has an empty slot in it, and the scenes below it are renumbered. (S1, S2, S3)
- **R5.** `scene.delete` deletes a scene and the clips in its slots with it; the clips in the other scenes stay where they are. It is destructive, so an agent asks first. (S2, S3, S5)
- **R6.** `scene.move` moves a scene to the index it's given — counted after the scene is taken out of its place — and the clips in its slots move with it; the scenes it passes change number. (S1, S2)
- **R7.** `scene.duplicate` copies a scene and the clips in its slots into a new scene directly after it, with the copies' IDs given as its arguments, one for each track that holds a clip in the scene; the copy keeps the scene's name and its clips are independent. (S1, S3, S5)
- **R8.** `scene.rename` gives a scene a name; the name may be empty, which brings back its number. (S1, S2, S3)
- **R9.** `clip.create_in_slot` puts a clip in a track's slot at a scene: only audio and MIDI tracks hold slot clips, the slot must be empty, and the clip's data must match the track. Insert Empty MIDI Clip (Cmd+Shift+M) builds a one-bar empty MIDI clip and runs it. (S1, S2, S3, S5)
- **R10.** `clip.move_to_slot` moves a clip into a track's slot at a scene, out of the slot or the Arrangement lane it sits in; the target slot must be empty unless it's the clip's own slot. Dragging moves a clip and Option-drag copies it, so a copy is another clip with its own ID: `clip.move_to_slot` for the drag and `session.copy_clip_to_slot` for the Option-drag (Ctrl-drag on Windows and Linux). (S7, S5)
- **R11.** The clip commands that don't place a clip act on a slot clip the same way as on an Arrangement clip: renaming, muting, replacing its data, an audio clip's markers, loop, gain and fades, and its length. `clip.delete` deletes a clip from a slot as it does from the Arrangement. `clip.move` places a slot clip on its track's Arrangement lane, at the start it's given; `clip.move_by` and `clip.trim_start`, which change an Arrangement position in place, refuse a clip that sits in a slot. (S5, S6)
- **R12.** Every scene and slot edit is a registered command run by ID with JSON arguments, a schema, a description, and a permission class, and each is one undo step whose change set names what changed: the scenes that were added, removed, renamed, or renumbered, and the clips that were added, removed, or modified. (S5, S6)
- **R13.** A set saves its scenes, in order and with their names, and each track's slot clips; loading it gives them back. A slot that names a scene the set doesn't hold, a slot clip with a position, two clips in one scene's slot of one track, and a slot clip on a track that holds none don't load. (S4, S5, S6)
- **R14.** A set saved before this package — its file has no scenes — loads with the default a new set opens with: eight unnamed scenes. Their IDs sit after the document's largest ID, and the project's allocator moves past them, so no ID is reused. (S3, S6)
- **R15.** The grid draws the Session View from layout.md's L-SES rows: the audio and MIDI tracks' columns from x 320, 94 points wide by default, one 18-point clip slot row per scene under the 17-point title bars, the "Drop Files and Devices Here" drop area between the last regular track and the returns, the returns' 94-point columns, whose slots are blank, and the Main column (1487 to 1591) with each scene's launch button at its left, its name across the middle, and its number at its right edge. Only the rows on screen are built, with one extra above and below. (S2, S3)
- **R16.** A slot with a clip is filled with the clip's colour (the track's colour by default) and shows its launch triangle at the left; an empty slot shows a stop square, or a record circle on an armed track. The clip's name and waveform aren't drawn on the slot. (S2)
- **R17.** A click selects the slot under the pointer, Cmd-click (Ctrl on Windows and Linux) adds it to the selection or removes it when it's already in it, Shift-click selects the rectangle from the last click, and Home and End select the first and last track of the current scene, a second End selecting the scene itself (S-SES-020 to S-SES-022). Esc moves the focus to the highlighted track's title bar (S-SES-019); the arrows and Page Up and Page Down move the selection by a slot, a scene, or eight scenes (S-SES-002, S-SES-010, S-SES-011). Clicking a scene's row in the Main column selects the scene, and clicking a title bar the track. (S2, S7)
- **R18.** Files dropped on a slot become Session clips through `session.drop_files`: one after another down the track's scenes, or, with the command modifier (Cmd; Ctrl on Windows and Linux) held while dropping, across the tracks at the scene dropped on; each target slot must be empty. One undo step. (S7)
- **R19.** The grid registers one clip slot in a Session strip's Tab order, after the track's title bar and before the In/Out choosers, holding the selected scene's slot, or the first scene's when none is selected: the order observed in Live 12.4.6's strip (the accessibility spec, R4). (S8)
- **R20.** A Session clip that starts takes its track over from the Arrangement on the frame it starts, its launch's grid line, so the Arrangement sounds until then. While the track is taken over its Arrangement clips are silent, audio and MIDI, also after the Session clip stops, until a return; a MIDI track lets its Arrangement notes go on the frame it is taken over. Stopping the transport or moving the playhead keeps the takeover. (S1, S9)
- **R21.** A stop on a track with no Session clip playing takes the track over on the stop's grid line, and Stop All Clips does so for every audio and MIDI track. (S1, S9)
- **R22.** Back to Arrangement (`session.back_to_arrangement`, F10, S-TRANS-009, M-PLAY-012) returns every track: their Session clips stop at once, not on the launch grid, launches and stops still queued on them are dropped, and the Arrangement plays from the playhead, clips already underway from their offset; a MIDI note that began before the return isn't restarted. The transport keeps running. The command is disabled while no track is taken over, as in a new or loaded set. (S1, S2, S9)
- **R23.** `session.back_to_arrangement_track` returns one track the same way and leaves the others; on a taken-over track with nothing playing it just gives the Arrangement back, and on a track nothing took over it changes nothing. (S1, S9)
- **R24.** The engine publishes the taken-over tracks after every block (`session_over`): the Back to Arrangement button lights while any track is listed, and a track's own button while that track is. A track the set no longer holds is forgotten, and a device restart forgets the takeovers with the playing Session clips. (S3, S9)
- **R25.** A scene holds an optional tempo, 20 to 999 BPM, and an optional time signature, a numerator of 1 to 99 over a denominator of 1, 2, 4, 8, or 16. Both save with the set and a set without them loads unchanged; `scene.duplicate` copies them; `scene.set_tempo` and `scene.set_time_signature` set each, or switch it off with `null`, in one undo step. (S1, S3, S10)
- **R26.** View ▸ Scene Tempo and Time Signature (`mixer_view.scene_tempo`, M-VIEW-035, hidden in a new set) shows each scene's tempo, numerator, and denominator fields across the Main column's name area (L-SES-007), and a selected scene takes the detail area for the Scene View: a title bar with the scene's name or number, its Tempo and Signature fields, and the follow-action controls, drawn disabled until WP-3.10 (L-SES-011). A field is grey, showing the set's current value, until the scene has its own. Typed values are read as the Control Bar's are: a tempo comes within 20 to 999 BPM and to hundredths, a numerator within 1 to 99, a denominator is the largest of 1, 2, 4, 8, and 16 that doesn't pass what was typed, and text that is no number leaves the field. Up and Down, a vertical drag, and a screen reader's increment step the value; Delete and a double-click switch it off (I-CLICK-010). (S3, S10)
- **R27.** Launching a scene that has a tempo or a signature applies them on the scene's launch frame, the grid line its clips start on, inside the running program and without a rebuild: the playhead, the scheduled commands, the loop, and the Session View's frames keep their beats ([transport.md](transport.md) R15). A scene with no clips applies them too, even with the transport stopped, and starts nothing; launching while the Arrangement plays does the same, on the same frame. Nothing is written into the set's tempo or time-signature map. A scene's signature moves the bar lines that bar-length launch quantisation waits for. (S1, S2, S10)
- **R28.** A launched tempo or signature overrides the set's tempo map and time-signature map, as a tempo set by hand overrides tempo automation, and Re-Enable Automation lights. The override holds through clip stops, transport stop and play, and later edits to the scene or its deletion, until Re-Enable Automation or Back to Arrangement gives back the set's maps at the playhead. It is session state: it isn't saved, and a device restart forgets it. The Arrangement's clips and envelopes play under it. (S2, S10)
- **R29.** Capture and Insert Scene (`session.capture_and_insert_scene`, M-CREATE-006, C-SCENE-008, Cmd+Shift+I, S-SES-009) inserts one scene, in one undo step, holding a copy of each playing clip in its track's slot, in track order, with the tempo and signature in force as its own. It goes after the scene launched last since the set opened, or after the selected scene when none has launched. With nothing playing it inserts an empty scene with the current tempo and signature and launches nothing. (S1, S3, S10)
- **R30.** The captured clips play on in the copies without a break: the engine moves each playing slot to the new scene's slot, keeping when it started and any stop it waits for. (S3, S10)
- **R31.** The grid launches and stops through the commands. Clicking a slot's launch triangle runs `session.launch`, a scene's launch button runs `session.launch_scene`, and clicking a stop square (an empty slot that still has its stop button; one removed draws nothing) or a track's stop button in the status row runs `session.stop_track`; Stop All Clips at the Main column's left runs `session.stop_all`, and the Back to Arrangement button at its right runs `session.back_to_arrangement`. The triangle is grey while the slot is stopped, flashes white and green while a launch is queued, and is green while the clip plays; a track's stop button turns green while a stop is queued; the Back to Arrangement button lights while `session_over` lists any track. All of it is drawn from the states the engine published, and every button reaches AccessKit named with its track and scene, with its pressed state. The global follow-action switch is drawn at the right of Main's status row, disabled, until WP-3.10. (S1, S2, S3)
- **R32.** `session.launch_selected` (Enter, S-SES-001) runs `session.launch` for each selected slot that holds a clip and `session.launch_scene` for each selected scene; a selected empty slot is skipped, and with nothing to launch it refuses. (S1, S3)

## Not built yet

- The Arrangement's per-track and scrub-area Back to Arrangement buttons (L-ARR-007, L-ARR-019) aren't drawn yet; F10, both commands and the Main column's button (R25) work, and `Shell::session_over` gives the buttons their state.
- A track's status display shows the playing clip's length in beats and nothing else: Live also counts up as the clip loops and draws a progress circle, which need the engine to publish the clip's position. How a queued-to-stop slot and the follow-action switch look on and off, and what Enter does on a selected empty slot with a stop button, aren't observed yet.
- A playing Session clip doesn't close its track's input under Monitor Auto ([routing.md](routing.md) R11).
