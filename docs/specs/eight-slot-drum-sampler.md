# The eight-slot drum sampler

- **Status:** partial (the individual outputs are T192's, a slot's Play button and the sample display wait for the audition path and the sample display; every other rule has a passing test)
- **Work packages:** WP-5.17; WP-6.9 (tuning systems: R21)
- **Parity-matrix row:** Instruments: Impulse, Drum Sampler
- **Checked against:** Live 12's reference manual, chapter 31 "Live Instrument Reference", section 31.7 "Impulse" (31.7.1 "Sample Slots", 31.7.2 "Start, Transpose and Stretch", 31.7.3 "Filter", 31.7.4 "Saturator and Envelope", 31.7.5 "Pan and Volume", 31.7.6 "Global Controls"), read October 2026

## The behaviour

Impulse is Beatlayer's eight-slot drum sampler: an instrument with eight sample slots, each played by a key of its own, each holding one sample named in the set by its content hash and held by the media store, the way the sampler's and a clip's audio is. A note plays the sample of the slot its key names, from its start, through that slot's own start offset, transposition, time-stretch, filter, saturator, decay, pan and volume. Slot 1 plays C3 and each slot after it the next white key up, so slot 8 plays C4. A slot plays one voice at a time: a note on a slot that is still sounding fades the old voice out over a few milliseconds and starts the new one.

A slot's controls are its own; the panel shows one slot's at a time, the one selected under the row of eight slot cells along its top, with the global controls beside it. Which slot is selected is a view setting, not an undo step, and it is not saved with the set. A sample is loaded into a slot by dropping it on the slot's cell, by hot-swapping and choosing a file in the browser, or by `device.load_sample`; it is cleared with Delete on the slot's cell or by `device.set_sample`. Loading and clearing are one undo step each.

A slot's Start, Transpose, Stretch, Filter, Saturator, Decay, Pan and Volume all take effect from the next note, as the manual says of Impulse's parameters: a note copies its slot's parameters when it starts and keeps them. Transpose, Stretch, the filter's frequency and the pan each have a Velocity amount and a Random amount, and Volume has a Velocity amount; the random amounts come from a generator seeded per device, so an offline render repeats exactly. Slot 7 and slot 8 can be linked so that each stops the other, as a closed hi-hat stops an open one. The global Volume, Transpose and Time scale the whole instrument; Time scales every slot's stretch and decay.

This spec covers the device, its parameters and its panel. It leaves to [the device framework spec](device-framework.md) everything every native device has (parameters, smoothing, layout, automation, presets) and to [the sampler spec](sampler.md) how a sample reaches a device (R2, R11). The individual outputs (manual 31.7.7) need an instrument's extra outputs as Audio From sources, which belong to the multi-output instruments package.

## Rules

- **R1.** Beatlayer ships Impulse, an instrument device type with the ID `beatlayer.kitbag`. Every parameter has a descriptor and a place in the layout: for each of the eight slots 24 parameters, numbered from the slot's number times 32 (Start 0, Transpose 1, Transpose Velocity 2, Transpose Random 3, Stretch 4, Stretch Mode 5, Stretch Velocity 6, Filter 7, Filter Type 8, Filter Freq 9, Filter Res 10, Filter Velocity 11, Filter Random 12, Saturator 13, Drive 14, Decay 15, Decay Mode 16, Pan 17, Pan Velocity 18, Pan Random 19, Volume 20, Volume Velocity 21, Mute 22, Solo 23), named `Slot <n> <what>`, and then the globals Volume (256), Transpose (257), Time (258) and Link (259). (S1 to S6)
- **R2.** The white keys C3, D3, E3, F3, G3, A3, B3 and C4 play slots 1 to 8: a note on it starts a voice at the start of that slot's sample, at the sample's own pitch when the slot is not transposed. Other keys, the black keys between them included, and a slot with no sample, play nothing. A slot plays one voice at a time: a note on a slot whose voice still sounds fades that voice out over 5 ms and starts its own. (S1, S7, S8; the white keys are Beatlayer's reading of the manual's "in the octave from C3 to C4", the only eight keys that span it, and the one voice is Beatlayer's own, as the manual does not say what a repeated note does)
- **R3.** Start (0 to 100 ms, default 0) skips that many milliseconds of the sample at the start of each note. (S2)
- **R4.** Transpose (whole semitones, −48 to 48, default 0) and the global Transpose (the same range) add, and a voice plays at that many semitones from the sample's own pitch, changing its speed with it: at +12 it plays twice as fast, at −12 half as fast. Transpose Velocity (−100 % to 100 %, default 0) moves the transposition by the amount times 24 semitones times (velocity − 1), so a full-velocity note is as the knob says and softer ones go down at a positive amount; Transpose Random (0 to 100 %, default 0) moves it by the amount times 24 semitones times a random value from −1 to 1, drawn once for each note. (S2, S5; the amounts' scale is Beatlayer's own)
- **R5.** Stretch (−100 % to 100 %, default 0) makes a voice last 2 to the power of Stretch / 100 times as long, without changing its pitch: at 100 % twice as long, at −100 % half as long. Stretch Velocity (−100 % to 100 %, default 0) moves the stretch by the amount times 100 % times (velocity − 1). Stretch Mode A and B are two algorithms of Beatlayer's own, both overlap-add of Hann-windowed grains whose starts are searched for where their waveform matches what the previous grain carries on with, so a tone keeps its pitch through the overlaps: A has grains of 40 ms, B of 12 ms. A stretched sound keeps its attack: the first grain starts at the top of its window. The pitch of a stretched tone stays within 0.5 % of the unstretched tone's, in both modes. A stretch of exactly nothing plays the sample directly. (S2)
- **R6.** The filter (off by default) runs a voice through the kit's state-variable filter: Filter Type is one of low-pass, high-pass, band-pass and notch, each at 12 and 24 dB per octave (default low-pass 12), Filter Freq is logarithmic from 20 Hz to 20 kHz (default 1 kHz) and Filter Res runs from 0 to 100 % (default 0); each response is within 0.2 dB of its analogue prototype's. Filter Velocity (−100 % to 100 %, default 0) moves the cutoff by the amount times four octaves times (velocity − 1), and Filter Random (0 to 100 %, default 0) by the amount times four octaves times a random value from −1 to 1, drawn once for each note. The manual's list of filter types is not known to Beatlayer, which offers these. (S3; the types and scales are Beatlayer's own)
- **R7.** The saturator (off by default) shapes a voice, after its filter, with a hyperbolic tangent: Drive (0 to 100 %, default 0) boosts the signal into it by up to 24 dB and the result is scaled so a signal at full scale stays at full scale. With the saturator off, Drive does nothing. (S4)
- **R8.** Decay (1 ms to 10 s, logarithmic, default 10 s) is the time a voice takes to fall in a straight line from its full level to silence, when the voice ends. In Trigger mode (the default) the fall starts with the note and the note's end is ignored; in Gate mode the voice holds its level until its note ends and the fall starts then. A voice also ends when its sample does. (S4)
- **R9.** Pan (−50 to 50, default 0) places a voice at equal level in both channels at 0 and turns the other channel down in a straight line to silence at the ends. Pan Velocity (−100 % to 100 %, default 0) moves the pan by the amount times 50 times (velocity − 1), and Pan Random (0 to 100 %, default 0) by the amount times 50 times a random value from −1 to 1, drawn once for each note. Volume is a gain (top +6 dB, default 0 dB, reaching −∞ dB: rule G1) and Volume Velocity (0 to 100 %, default 100 %) scales the level by one minus the amount times (1 − velocity), so at 100 % the level follows the velocity in a straight line. (S5, S6)
- **R10.** Mute (off by default) silences a slot from its next note. Solo (off by default) lets only the soloed slots play, from the next note, silencing every slot that is not soloed; a slot that is soloed and muted is silent. (S1)
- **R11.** With Link on (off by default), a note on slot 7 fades slot 8's sounding voice out over 5 ms, and a note on slot 8 does the same to slot 7's; no other slots are linked. (S1)
- **R12.** A note copies its slot's parameters when it starts. A parameter changed while a note sounds does not change that note, and reaches the next one on its frame. (S1, S6)
- **R13.** The global Volume is a gain (top +6 dB, default 0 dB, reaching −∞ dB) that scales the whole output on every frame. The global Time (25 % to 400 %, logarithmic, default 100 %) multiplies the stretch factor and the decay time of each note from the next note; the global Transpose is R4's. (S5, S6)
- **R14.** The random amounts' values come from a generator seeded with the device's ID, drawn in the same order for every note whatever the settings, so two renders of the same set are bit for bit the same. (S6)
- **R15.** A render is the same at any block size and under the allocation trap: the same samples at blocks of 1, 100, 512 and 4,096 frames, with every slot playing and stretching, filtering, saturating and panning at random, with the link, a parameter change, and Gate, and nothing on the audio thread allocates, locks or blocks. (S6, S7)
- **R16.** Each slot's sample is named by its content hash in the device's `media` list (slot 0 first), held by the media store and decoded by the media crate at the engine's rate where the node is made; a slot whose media the library does not hold is silent and reported, and the others play. The list saves with the set, and a set reopens with its eight samples; a set saved when a device held one hash (the sampler's) loads it as slot 0. (S7)
- **R17.** Loading a sample into a slot (`device.load_sample` with a `slot`, a file dropped on the slot's cell, or Enter on a file in the browser while the device is hot-swapped, which loads into the selected slot) is one undo step named Load Sample; clearing a slot (`device.set_sample` with a null `media` and a `slot`, or Delete on the slot's cell) is one undo step named Clear Sample. A slot past the eighth, and a device that plays no sample, are refused with nothing changed. (S1, S7)
- **R18.** The panel is the eight slot cells along the top, each showing its number, its key and whether it holds a sample, and below them the controls of the selected slot, with the global controls beside them. Clicking a cell, or Enter on it, selects its slot; a sample dropped on a cell loads into that slot, and dropped elsewhere on the device into the selected one. Selecting a slot (`device.select_slot`) changes only the view: it is not an undo step, is not saved with the set, and is read back as `selected_slot` in the device's panel in `device.state`. (Beatlayer's own look)
- **R19.** Every action here is a registered command that an agent runs by ID and reads back: `device.load_sample` and `device.set_sample` (the device's `media`), `device.set_param` (its `params`, every slot's parameters by number) and `device.select_slot` (the panel's `selected_slot`). (S6)
- **R20.** Every parameter can be automated and modulated (rule G2) to the frame, and every gain, Volume of each slot and the global Volume, reaches −∞ dB (rule G1); every parameter has a written description. (S6)
- **R21.** Impulse's keys choose its slots (R2) and a slot's pitch doesn't follow the key, so a set's tuning changes nothing Impulse plays ([tunings.md](tunings.md) R12). (S9, S10)

## Not built yet

- The individual outputs (manual 31.7.7) need an instrument's extra outputs as Audio From sources: the multi-output instruments package (T192) builds them, and whichever of it and this package lands second wires the slots' outputs.
- A slot's Play button (audition) needs a way to play a note into a device from the Device View; there is none yet. Mute and Solo are parameters in the selected slot's controls.
- The slot cells show a number, a key and whether the slot holds a sample; the sample's waveform and its name wait for the sample display.
- Beatlayer's filter is the kit's state-variable filter with eight types; the manual's wider list of types is not known.
