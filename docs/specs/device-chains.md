## The behaviour

Every track has a device chain: devices in signal order, which the track's audio runs through after its clips and inputs and before its mixer. A chain keeps Live's order, MIDI effects first, then the instrument, then audio effects, and only MIDI tracks hold MIDI effects and instruments. Devices are inserted, moved (within a chain or to another track), deleted, switched off and on, renamed, and copied, each edit one undo step named as Live names it. A device switched off passes its input through untouched.

The Device View, in the detail area, shows the selected track's chain left to right. Each device sits in a frame whose title bar holds, left to right, the Device Activator, the device's name (the Device Title Bar), and the Hot-Swap Presets, Save Preset, and Show Options buttons. Clicking the title bar selects the device; double-clicking it folds the device to a strip a title bar wide; dragging it moves the device; right-clicking it or clicking Show Options opens the device's context menu. An empty chain says "Drop an Instrument or Sample Here".

A device's state, its parameter values, saves to a preset file in Beatlayer's own format and loads from one: Save Preset writes it into the User Library, and Hot-Swap Presets opens the device's presets in the browser so the next preset loaded goes into that device. Save as Default Preset makes a device type's new devices start from the device's state.

A native device can also float in a window of its own (IMP-14, a deliberate Beatlayer improvement, WP-2.5b): Float in its menu (Option-F on macOS, Ctrl-Alt-F on Windows and Linux) floats it, leaving a strip in its place in the chain so the signal order stays readable, and the window's Dock button, or closing the window, docks it back. Floating and docking are undo steps; moving and resizing the window aren't, though the set saves where each window sits. Enlarging the window grows only the device's displays; its controls keep their size and place, and the docked size is the smallest. The window stays above the main window and, with Auto-Hide Plug-In Windows on (as in a new install), hides while another track is selected, as Live's plug-in windows do. Plug-ins keep their own editor windows.

Beatlayer ships no device in WP-2.4. The engine's device interface (a factory that lists device types and makes nodes for them) is what native devices (WP-2.5) and plug-in hosts (WP-2.6 to 2.8) implement; the tests use test-only devices.

## Rules

- **R1.** A track's device chain is part of the set, saved with it, in signal order. A device's role, its own name, and its Show Preset Name are saved only where they differ from a new audio effect's, so sets saved before WP-2.4 load unchanged. A native device takes its type's name when it is made; a set saved before the devices took Live's names (schema version 7) opens with the new name on a device still called what Beatlayer first called it, and a device the user renamed keeps its name. (S1, S6)
- **R2.** Only MIDI tracks hold MIDI effects and instruments; audio effects go on any track. A document command that would put one elsewhere is refused. A plug-in whose type sends notes on from a note output can be placed in the MIDI-effect position of a MIDI track's chain, before the instrument, even where its type's own role is an instrument or an audio effect; one whose type has no note output can't, and neither can a native device take a role other than its type's (IMP-24). (S1)
- **R3.** A chain holds its MIDI effects first, then at most one instrument, then its audio effects. A command that would insert or move a device out of that order is refused, and so is a second instrument in one chain; a rack holds several, each in a chain of its own ([racks](racks.md) R1). Placing a device in the MIDI-effect position puts it after the chain's MIDI effects and before its instrument, in one undo step, and the placement is saved with the set (IMP-24). A plug-in in that position sends its notes on and the audio it receives passes through it: its own audio output is dropped. (S1)
- **R4.** Moving a device, within its chain or to another track, keeps its settings and its running state. (S1, S6)
- **R5.** Each track's chain runs in order after its clips and inputs and before its mixer, so the mixer's volume scales the chain's output. A MIDI track's first device gets its clips' notes merged, in frame order and a note-off before a note-on on one frame, with the notes its input played while it monitors ([MIDI I/O](midi-io.md) R12). (S1, S6)
- **R6.** A device switched off passes its input through untouched, keeps its state, and takes parameter changes while off. It isn't run while it is off: the changes to its parameters still reach it, so it comes back on with them (IMP-15). Its activator is lit (the theme's accent) while on and dark while off. Switching it is an undo step named Change "Device On". (S2, S3, S6)
- **R7.** A chain edit (insert, delete, move, switch off or on) compiles a new program that makes only the inserted devices' nodes; every other node migrates into it with its state, and a deleted device's node is freed off the audio thread. A device switched off keeps its node, which migrates into the new program as any other (S6).
- **R8.** An edit that leaves the chain as it is (renaming, Show Preset Name, a parameter, loading a preset) sends no new program: changed values reach the running nodes as parameter changes. (S6)
- **R9.** Devices come from node factories, which list the device types they offer (kind, own name, role) and make a node for a device of one of them. WP-2.4 offers no device of its own; its tests use test-only devices (a gain and a pass-through). (S6)
- **R10.** Rename gives a device the name its title bar shows; an empty name gives it back its own name. From the keyboard (Cmd-R, Ctrl-R) and the menu, the name is typed in the title bar and Return renames; Esc cancels. The undo step is Change "Device Name". With Show Preset Name on, as on a new device, the title shows the preset's or given name; off, the device's own name; switching it is an undo step named Toggle Show Preset Name, and its menu item is ticked while it's on. (S3, S4)
- **R11.** Double-clicking a device's title bar, or Fold in its menu (the minus key, S-DEV-014), folds it to an upright strip a title bar wide, its title bar on its side: the activator at the top, the name reading upwards, Hot-Swap Presets, Save Preset, and Show Options at the bottom. Double-clicking the strip unfolds it. Folding isn't an undo step and isn't saved with the set. (S2, S3)
- **R12.** A device added as a person adds one (`device.add`, from the browser once devices exist) goes on the selected track, after the selected device if that keeps the chain's order and otherwise at the end of its role's place, and becomes the selected device: undo step Insert Device. An instrument or MIDI effect added while an audio, group, or return track is selected goes on a new MIDI track at the end of the track list, named its number and the device's name, which becomes the selected track: one undo step, Insert Device and Track. (S3, S4)
- **R13.** A device added whose type has a default preset (R18) starts with the preset's state, in the same undo step, and the Status Bar says "Using Default Preset <name>.blpreset.", following Live's message for its own preset files. (S3)
- **R14.** Device edits are undo steps named as Live 12.4.6 names them: Insert Device, Insert Device and Track, Duplicate Device, Delete Device(s) (for Delete and Cut), Copy Device (for Paste), Change "Device Name", Toggle Show Preset Name, Change "Device On"; and Move Device and Load Preset. (S3, S4)
- **R15.** Copy puts the selected device, with its settings, on the device clipboard (not an undo step); Cut does the same and deletes it. Paste inserts a copy before the selected device, or at the chain's end with none selected, and selects it. Duplicate puts a copy straight after the device and selects it. The chain's order holds for each. (S4)
- **R16.** A preset is a file of Beatlayer's own format (`.blpreset`, UTF-8 JSON: format, schema, the device's kind, role, and own name, and its parameter values). It loads only into a device of the kind it was saved from. Loading one sets every parameter to the preset's value, or the default where the preset sets none, and the title's name to the preset's name, as one undo step; a document command (`device.set_state`) does the same with values given directly. A preset from a newer Beatlayer, or a damaged one, is refused. A preset saved before the devices took Live's names reads with the new device name. A rack's preset is schema 2 of the same format ([racks](racks.md) R42). (S6)
- **R17.** Save Preset writes the device's state to the User Library, in `Presets/<Audio Effects, Instruments, or MIDI Effects>/<the device's own name>/`, named after the device's title, numbered ("Utility 2") if the name is taken; the Status Bar names the file. The file is written whole or not at all.
- **R18.** Save as Default Preset writes the device's state to the User Library's `Defaults/<category>/<the device's own name>.blpreset`, replacing any default before it.
- **R19.** Hot-Swap Presets arms the device: the button lights, a bar in the theme's accent tops the browser reading "Swapping Audio Effect" (or Instrument, or MIDI Effect) with a close button, and the browser opens the User Library's folder of the device's presets. Loading a preset from the browser (Enter or a double-click) while armed loads it into the device (R16). The close button, or the Hot-Swap button again, ends it. Until the browser lists devices (WP-2.5), it shows the User Library's folder rather than presets under the device's own entry. (S4)
- **R20.** A device command run from a key or a menu item names no device and acts on the selected one. Every action is a registered command with a schema, a description, and a permission class, which agents run with the device named. (S3, S6)
- **R21.** The Device View, right of the Info View in the detail area (L-DEV-001), shows the selected track's chain. An empty chain shows "Drop an Instrument or Sample Here" centred. (S5)
- **R22.** Devices sit left to right in signal order, 7 points apart. Each frame's title bar is 16 points tall and holds, left to right, the Device Activator, the name, then Hot-Swap Presets, Save Preset, and Show Options at its right end. A device is its own width; the test devices are 120 points wide. (S3, S4)
- **R23.** Clicking a device's title bar selects it; the selected device's title bar is drawn in the light highlight colour and the others dark grey. A newly added or pasted device becomes selected. (S4)
- **R24.** Right-clicking a device's title bar, or clicking Show Options, opens the device's menu (C-DEVICE) with Live's items in Live's order and separators. Cut, Copy, Duplicate, Rename, Delete, Group ([racks](racks.md) R9, WP-5.1), Fold, Show Preset Name, and Save as Default Preset work; the two Compare items work on a native device, and are shown disabled for a plug-in or a rack ([device-compare.md](device-compare.md) R6); Edit Info Text works ([info-view.md](info-view.md) R10). A device's own items follow when devices exist. (S3, S5)
- **R25.** Dragging a device's title bar moves it: a line shows where it would go, only at places that keep the chain's order (R3), and releasing there moves it, one undo step. (S3)
- **R26.** A native device floats in a window of its own, or docks back, by `device.set_floating`: Float in its menu, straight after Fold, ticked while it floats (choosing it again docks it), and its key act on the selected device: Option-F on macOS, Ctrl-Alt-F on Windows and Linux (S-DEV-015), scoped by focus, so it reaches Float only with the Device View or a floating device's window focused; with the Arrangement focused the same keys are Create Fade/Crossfade's (S-ARR-008). A floating device's window in front selects its device, so the Device View's keys act on it. Floating and docking are one undo step each, named Float Device and Dock Device. A floating device's place in the chain is a strip a title bar wide, as a folded device's, outlined in the accent; double-clicking it doesn't fold it. (S7)
- **R27.** A floating window shows the device unfolded, under its title bar, with a Dock button at the title bar's right end. The Dock button, closing the window, and Close Window (Cmd-W) with the window in front dock the device, as R26's undo step. (S7)
- **R28.** A floating window's smallest size is the device at its docked size under its title bar, scaled by the interface zoom; a larger window gives its extra room only to the device's display cells (device framework R11), and a device with no display cell that grows stays at its docked size. Moving or resizing the window (`device.set_window`) isn't an undo step and doesn't mark the set as changed. (S7)
- **R29.** Whether each device floats is part of the set; where each floating window sits and how big it is (its frame, in points) is saved with the set beside the document (`windows`), so a set reopens with the same devices floating in the same places. A frame for a device that isn't there, or a damaged one, is dropped. Undoing a dock or a delete puts the window back where it was. (S7)
- **R30.** Plug-ins don't float: they keep their own editor windows, as in Live. A copy of a floating device (Paste, Duplicate) goes in the chain docked. (S7)
- **R31.** With Settings, Plug-Ins, Plug-In Windows, Auto-Hide Plug-In Windows on (as in a new install), a floating window hides while a track other than its device's is selected, and comes back when its track is selected again. Hiding isn't docking: the device still floats, it isn't an undo step, and the set saves it floating. Off, windows stay shown. A hosted plug-in's own editor window (R13 of clap-hosting.md) follows the same rule: it hides while another track is selected and comes back with its track, and hiding isn't closing. (S7, S8)
- **R32.** A floating window is a utility window, as Live's plug-in windows are, and stays above the main window. On macOS it has a short title bar (16 points) with only a small close button, sits at AppKit's floating level, and hides while Beatlayer isn't the active app. On Windows it's a tool window (the short title bar with only a close button) owned by the main window, which keeps it above the main window and out of the taskbar. On Linux under X11 it's a utility window, which window managers keep above the app's windows; Wayland gives no such hint. Its title is the device's title then its track's label, as Live titles a plug-in's window (`Utility/3-Audio`). The Dock button stays at the right end of the device's own title bar, just under the window's title bar. (S8)
- **R33.** View ▸ Plug-In Windows (Cmd-Option-P, Ctrl-Alt-P on Windows and Linux; S-DEV-009, M-VIEW-028), `view.toggle_plug_in_windows`, hides every floating device's window and shows them again, as it does Live's plug-in windows. It also hides and shows every hosted plug-in's own editor window (R13 of clap-hosting.md). It's ticked while they're shown and disabled while no device floats and no editor is open. It only hides and shows: the devices still float, the editors stay open, it isn't an undo step, and nothing in the set changes. Floating a device, or opening an editor, shows the windows again. (S7)
- **R34.** A device whose layout has an expanded part (device framework R10) has an expand toggle in its title bar, a small triangle right of the Device Activator, pointing right while the part is hidden and down while it shows; a device without one has none. Clicking it, or `device.expand`, shows or hides the part, which sits to the right of the device's body and makes the frame (and a floating window's smallest size) as wide as both. Like Fold (R11), it changes only the view: it isn't an undo step, isn't saved with the set, and shows in `device_view.state` as the frame's `expandable` and `expanded`. `device.expand` on a device with no expanded part is refused. (S9, S10)
- **R35.** A native device type can mark some of its switch parameters as menu options. They are listed in the device's menu after Save as Default Preset and a separator, in the type's order, each ticked while it is on, and the layout places no control for them. Choosing one turns the switch over with `device.set_param`, one undo step; it can be automated and modulated like any parameter, and `device.parameters` reports it, marked `menu_option`. A new device's options start at the type's default preset like any parameter (R13). (S9, S10)
- **R36.** A MIDI track's notes take this path through its chain. The clip player's notes, merged with the monitored input's ([MIDI I/O](midi-io.md) R12), reach the first device; each device sends its notes to the next, so the notes a device sends, not those it received, are what the next device plays; the instrument plays the notes that reach it and sends none, and the audio effects after it receive none. A device switched off passes the notes it receives to the next unchanged (R6) and ends the notes it started (device framework R32). The notes of a device that was removed or moved are ended through the devices that follow it. (S6)
- **R37.** A MIDI effect is listed with the other native devices under MIDI Effects: `device.add` of one inserts it before the instrument, hot-swapping it reads "Swapping MIDI Effect" (R19), and its presets are saved under `Presets/MIDI Effects/<its name>/` (R17). (S1, S6)
- **R38.** A MIDI track's control changes, pitch bends and channel pressure take R36's path with its notes. What the monitored input receives on a channel the input takes ([MIDI I/O](midi-io.md) R12) enters the chain ahead of the first device, in order with the clips' notes; each device sends its messages to the next with its notes, so the messages a device sends, not those it received, are what the next device reads (device framework R39). A device switched off passes them on unchanged (R6). The instrument plays the ones it answers and sends none on, and the audio effects after it receive none. (S6)
- **R39.** A track's context menu has Deactivate All Devices (IMP-25) in the group with Freeze Track and Flatten, just before Freeze Track, running `track.set_devices_enabled` on the selected tracks (or the `tracks` it names). It switches every top-level device on them off, in one undo step named Deactivate All Devices; when every top-level device on them is already off and there is at least one, the item reads Activate All Devices and turns them back on, one undo step named Activate All Devices. It reaches only the tracks' own devices, never the contents of a rack (the racks spec): a rack itself switches off, the devices inside it stay as they are. With no top-level device on the selected tracks the item is disabled. The effect reads back through each device's `enabled`. (S11)
- **R40.** View ▸ Close All Plug-in Windows (IMP-25, no key), `view.close_plug_in_windows`, sits straight after Plug-In Windows (R33) and closes every open plug-in editor window (R13 of [clap-hosting](clap-hosting.md)) at once. It closes editors only: a floating native device window (R26 to R33) stays as it is, so it never docks a device, changes nothing in the set, and is never an undo step. It is in the read class, returns how many editors it closed, and is disabled while no editor is open. (S11)
- **R41.** A device's menu (R24) holds Lock to (C-DEVICE-014) after Save as Default Preset and before the device's own items, with a separator before it: one item per running control surface that supports locking, in the Control Surfaces table's row order, reading "Lock to <surface name>", ticked while that surface is locked to this device. Choosing an unticked one runs `control_surface.lock` and a ticked one `control_surface.unlock` ([control surfaces](control-surfaces.md) R23); neither is an undo step. With no such surface running, a single "Lock to Control Surface" item is shown disabled (Beatlayer's own wording). A rack's menu holds the same items. (S12)
- **R42.** While a surface is locked to a device, its title bar shows the hand icon (Beatlayer's own drawing) straight after the name, before Hot-Swap Presets, the name giving up the room; a folded device shows it under its name. Hovering it shows "Locked to <surface name>" in the Status Bar, every surface named when there are several, and it reaches assistive technology as an image with that text. `device_view.state` reports each device's locks ([control surfaces](control-surfaces.md) R35). (S12)
- **R43.** With the Device View holding the keyboard and none of its controls using the arrow keys, Left and Right select the device before or after the selected one in the chain shown, and with none selected the first or the last (S-A11Y-014), as `device.select`. The last device stays selected on Right and the first on Left. Enter in the browser loads the selected device (S-DEV-007, S-TRACK-015, the key S-BRW-004 runs). Shift-click to add devices to the selection (S-DEV-004) and Cmd-click on a plug-in window button (S-DEV-010) wait for WP-6.76 and WP-6.75. (S13)
- **R44.** A chain wider than the Device View scrolls sideways with the wheel, a middle-button drag, or choosing a device from the list (racks R25), and every move is `device_view.scroll`, which an agent runs too: `x` points, positive towards the last device, kept between the first device and the chain's end. It changes only the view, and `device_view.state` reports where the chain is as `scroll`. (S12)

## Details

- After the selected device is deleted or cut, no device is selected.
- A device's frame sits 7 points in from the Device View's top and bottom edges, and the Hot-Swap bar at the top of the browser is 20 points tall.
- The Delete item in a device's menu shows the Delete key it is bound to.
- On macOS a floating window's close button is 10 points across, centred 9 points from the left of its title bar.
- The expanded part (R34) is the kind that sits within the device, as Phaser-Flanger's LFO and Envelope Follower sections do. Panels above the Device View (Roar's, EQ Eight's Frequency Display), which the same toggle shows in Live, are not built; where Live draws a within-device section, and the toggle's exact look, are to observe.
- Shift-click to add devices to the selection (S-DEV-004, WP-6.76) and the level meters between devices (L-DEV-003) are not built yet.
