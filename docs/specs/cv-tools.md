## The behaviour

Modular synthesisers are played with control voltages: a pitch voltage, one volt to the octave, that sets an oscillator's note; a gate that is high while a key is held; short triggers that fire drum modules; clock pulses that keep sequencers in step; and slow voltages that move any knob a patch cable reaches. An audio interface whose outputs pass DC (a DC-coupled interface) can send such voltages, because to the interface a control voltage is just a channel of audio whose sample values hold still. Beatlayer's CV devices make those signals in the engine and write them to the interface's channels, and read the voltages the modular sends back on its inputs.

Ten devices make up Live's CV Tools pack; Beatlayer builds native equivalents of nine of them, with Live's names. CV Instrument turns a MIDI track's notes into pitch, gate and modulation voltages for one modular voice, and tunes the oscillator it plays to the cent by calibrating against its sound coming back. CV Triggers turns notes into triggers or gates for drum modules. CV Clock Out sends a clock locked to the song; CV Clock In lets a modular clock lead the song's tempo. CV In turns a voltage from the modular into a modulator that moves any parameter in the set. CV Utility makes and combines control voltages from automation and audio, and works without any modular gear. CV Envelope Follower, CV LFO and CV Shaper send the Envelope Follower's, the LFO's and the Shaper's signals out as voltages.

This spec covers CV as a signal in the engine (volts as sample values, the CV choosers, timing, rendering) and the nine devices. It leaves the Rotating Rhythm Generator, a MIDI effect of the same pack, without a package.

## Rules

**CV as a signal in the engine (WP-7.17)**

- **R1.** A control voltage is a channel of audio whose sample values stand for volts at the interface's jack: a sample value of 1.0 is +10 V and −1.0 is −10 V, so a volt is 0.1 and 1 V per octave is 0.1 per octave (Beatlayer's own scale). Every CV device's voltage controls and readouts are in volts, from −10 V to +10 V, and a CV device clamps what it writes to that range, so it never asks the interface for more than full scale. (S1; Beatlayer's own)
- **R2.** Nothing between a CV device and the interface's output channel, or between the interface's input channel and a CV device, removes DC, dithers, limits or otherwise changes a value: a constant written by a CV device arrives on its channel as that constant on every frame, and a constant on an input channel reaches a CV device as it came in. A track whose Audio To is Ext. Out (routing R3) carries a constant to its channels in the same way, through its own mixer like any audio, and an audio track's Ext. In brings an input's DC in unchanged. (S1, S2; Beatlayer's own)
- **R3.** Each CV output of a CV device has a chooser listing No Output, the interface's output channels one by one, by number or by the name the Audio settings give them (S2), and Configure…, which opens Settings on the Audio page (L-SET-012); a new device's outputs are No Output. An output writes its signal to its channel at full value: a CV output is one channel, never a stereo signal summed and lowered 6 dB. What it writes adds to whatever else plays on that channel (Main Out, Cue Out, tracks on Ext. Out, an external effect's output, another CV output), as external devices R18 says. Each CV input has a chooser listing No Input, the interface's input channels one by one, and Configure…, No Input on a new device. A channel the interface lacks, or no audio device, sends nothing and reads 0 V; the device keeps the channel's number and shows it dimmed until the channel is there again, as an external device keeps a missing port (external devices R9). (S2, S9; Beatlayer's own)
- **R4.** The CV choosers are settings, not parameters, as an external device's choosers are (external devices R15): each change is one undo step named Change "Routing", saved with the set, through `device.set_external` (`edit`), whose `cv_outputs` and `cv_inputs` name each output or input by its name in the device (Pitch, Gate, Mod 1, Clock, CV and so on) with a channel number or none, and which reads back in the query tool's `device` result under `external`. (S7, S8)
- **R5.** A CV output of CV Clock Out, CV Utility, CV Envelope Follower, CV LFO or CV Shaper leaves so that its value for a frame reaches the interface's output at the moment that frame of the song is heard at Main: the engine holds it back by the delay compensation its track's path into Main is given, in a delay line the program makes, as MIDI To's messages are stamped (external devices R4). The outputs of CV Instrument and CV Triggers leave at the device's place, without that compensation, as the External Instrument's notes do (external devices R10), and the sound they bring back lines up at Main through their reported latency (R10). (S3, S7; Beatlayer's own)
- **R6.** A CV input is read at the frame the interface delivered it, as a track's Ext. In is, with no compensation of its own. (S2; Beatlayer's own)
- **R7.** An export renders in real time when a rendered output's path runs through a CV device with any CV output or input chosen, or with Audio From chosen on CV Instrument or CV Triggers, as external devices R22 to R26 say for the external devices. An offline render (an export R22 doesn't make real time, a preview, a freeze or a bounce) sends no CV, reads 0 V on every CV input, and hears silence on CV Instrument's and CV Triggers' Audio From, and the render's result names those devices with the external devices it silenced (external devices R16 and R27). CV Utility's, CV Envelope Follower's, CV LFO's and CV Shaper's work on their track's audio renders offline as it plays. (S7)
- **R8.** A CV device writes its outputs and reads its inputs through buffers the program makes, and nothing it does on the audio thread allocates, locks or blocks. What it writes for a frame depends only on the song, its parameters, its inputs and its seed, never on how blocks fall, so a CV output is the same sample for sample at any block size. Every random part comes from a generator seeded per device and started again when the transport starts, as a modulator's (modulation R21). (S7, S8)
- **R9.** The nine devices take Live's names: CV Instrument and CV Triggers are instruments, listed under the browser's Instruments label; CV Clock Out, CV Clock In, CV Utility, CV Envelope Follower, CV LFO and CV Shaper are audio effects, listed under Audio Effects; CV In is a modulator, listed under Modulators (L-BRW-004). The labels are Beatlayer's own places for them. Each publishes, as its display data (device framework R35), the last value in volts of each of its outputs and inputs, which `device.display` reports (device framework R37), and its panel shows them. (S1, S7, S9; Beatlayer's own)

**CV Instrument (WP-7.17)**

- **R10.** CV Instrument (`beatlayer.cv_instrument`) is an instrument on a MIDI track. It has four CV outputs, Pitch, Gate, Mod 1 and Mod 2 (R3), and the External Instrument's return: Audio From, listing the interface's inputs as the External Instrument's does, Gain (−∞ to +24 dB, 0 dB) and Hardware Latency with its unit switch, which work, report their latency and are disabled while delay compensation is off exactly as the External Instrument's (external devices R11 to R14). Its output is that return; with No Input chosen it is silent. (S1, S3, S7; the four outputs and the return are Beatlayer's own)
- **R11.** It plays one voice. Of the keys held, the sounding one is chosen by Note Priority: Last (the most recent, the default), Low or High; releasing the sounding key while others are held moves to the next by the same priority (Beatlayer's own). The voice's pitch, in semitones, is the key's pitch under the set's tuning (tunings R1, R6, R12), plus Transpose (−48 to +48 semitones, 0), Fine (−100 to +100 cents, 0) and the pitch bend times Bend Range (0 to 24 semitones, 2). A change of key glides to the new pitch in a straight line, in volts, over Glide (0 to 5,000 ms, 0 ms), starting on the frame the key arrives. (S1; Beatlayer's own)
- **R12.** Uncalibrated, Pitch writes (p − 60) / 12 volts for a voice at pitch p semitones: key 60 (C3) at 0 V and one volt to the octave. Calibrated (R14), it writes the voltage the calibration says makes the oscillator sound that pitch's frequency. (S1; Beatlayer's own)
- **R13.** Gate writes Gate Level (0 to 10 V, 5 V) while any key is held and 0 V otherwise, rising on the frame of the first key's note on and falling on the frame of the last one's note off. With Retrigger on (the default) a new key that becomes the sounding one while the gate is high takes the gate to 0 V for 1 ms first, so the modular's envelope starts again; with it off the gate stays high (legato). Mod 1 and Mod 2 each write Min + s · (Max − Min) volts, Min and Max from −10 to +10 V (0 V and 5 V by default), s being the share their Source gives: Velocity, Modwheel, Pitchbend, Pressure, Keytrack, Expression, Slide or Sustain, read as Expression Control's sources read them ("The Expression Control's sources" in modulation), Velocity and Modwheel by default. (S1; Beatlayer's own)
- **R14.** Calibrate (`cv.calibrate`, `external`, since it plays through the interface) tunes the oscillator patched to Pitch, whose sound comes back on Audio From: with Gate high, it writes each whole volt from −2 V to +5 V on Pitch in turn, holds each for 250 ms and measures the returning pitch over the last 150 ms with the Tuner's detector (the Tuner R2), then fits the octaves the oscillator rises per volt and its frequency at 0 V by least squares on the logarithm of frequency, keeping each step's remaining error so the pitch law corrects it, straight between steps and along the fitted line past them. The detector reads only 41 Hz to 2 kHz, about five and a half octaves, and mistakes what lies above for a lower octave, while the eight steps cover seven or more octaves of an oscillator at one volt to the octave: a step that gives no steady pitch in that range, and any step that does not rise half an octave to one and a half over the one before it, is left out, the fit takes the longest run of the steps that remain, and the keys beyond that run follow the fitted line. The calibration is saved with the device as one undo step (`Calibrate CV Instrument`) and its result reports the fit, each step's error in cents and the pitch each step gave. It's refused while Pitch or Audio From is No Output or No Input or no audio engine runs, and fails, changing nothing, when fewer than three steps in a run give a steady pitch, saying which was the first step that gave none; notes reaching the device while it runs are ignored. `cv.clear_calibration` (`edit`) takes the calibration away, one undo step. The steps, times, fit and names are Beatlayer's own. (S1, S7; Beatlayer's own)
- **R15.** After a calibration, with Transpose, Fine and the bend at 0, every key whose voltage lies within the calibrated range sounds its frequency under the set's tuning within 1 cent of it, on an oscillator whose volts per octave, offset and bow stay as they were measured. (S1; Beatlayer's own)

**CV Triggers (WP-7.17)**

- **R16.** CV Triggers (`beatlayer.cv_triggers`) is an instrument on a MIDI track with eight trigger outputs (R3) and the same return as CV Instrument (R10). Each output has a Note (a key; the eight take keys 36 to 43, C1 to G1, on a new device), a Mode (Trigger, the default, or Gate), a Length (1 to 100 ms, 10 ms; Trigger only), a Level (0 to 10 V, 5 V) and a Velocity switch (off), which scales the level by the note's velocity. A note on no output's key does nothing. The count, the keys and the ranges are Beatlayer's own. (S1; Beatlayer's own)
- **R17.** In Trigger mode a note on at its key writes the level for Length from its frame, then 0 V; a note on during a pulse takes the output to 0 V for 1 ms and starts a new pulse. In Gate mode it writes the level from the note on's frame to the note off's, and a second note on while it's high takes it to 0 V for 1 ms first. Several outputs may share a key and fire together. (S1; Beatlayer's own)

**Throughout CV Instrument and CV Triggers (WP-7.17)**

- **R18.** Every control of CV Instrument and CV Triggers besides the choosers, Hardware Latency and its unit (R4) is a parameter, automatable and modulatable (rule G2). The transport stopping, the device being switched off, removed or moved, and the audio stream stopping or restarting take every gate and Gate-mode output to 0 V on the next frame, as the External Instrument sends its note-offs (external devices R6). (S7, S8)
- **R19.** A MIDI track's notes reach CV Instrument and CV Triggers after its MIDI effects, as they reach any instrument; the sounding note's own pitch bend adds to its pitch in semitones as the channel's bend does, and its own slide and pressure are what the Slide and Pressure sources read (Beatlayer's own). (S7; Beatlayer's own)
- **R20.** Every action here is a registered command with a schema, a description and a permission class, run by ID through the agent API: `device.set_external`'s CV fields, `cv.calibrate` and `cv.clear_calibration` read back in the query tool's `device` result under `external` (the choosers) and `cv` (the calibration: its fit, each step's error, and whether one is held), and parameters through `device.set_param` and the query tool's `device` result. (S7, S8)

**CV Clock Out (WP-7.18)**

- **R21.** CV Clock Out (`beatlayer.cv_clock_out`) is an audio effect that passes its audio through untouched and has one CV output, Clock (R3). Its controls: Rate, in pulses per quarter note, 1, 2, 4 (the default), 8, 12, 16, 24 or 48; Width, 1 % to 99 % of a pulse's period, 50 %; and Level, 0 to 10 V, 5 V. The list, ranges and defaults are Beatlayer's own. (S1; Beatlayer's own)
- **R22.** While the transport plays, a pulse rises on the frame at which each multiple of 1 / Rate of a quarter note is played, counted from the song's start, holds Level for Width of the time to the next multiple and falls to 0 V; while it's stopped Clock is 0 V. A start on a multiple fires a pulse on its first frame; a start between multiples waits for the next. The multiples follow the tempo map, its changes and ramps, frame by frame, a loop's jump back puts the next pulse on the multiple after the loop's start, and the pulses reach the interface when their frames are heard at Main (R5). (S1; Beatlayer's own)

**CV Clock In (WP-7.18)**

- **R23.** CV Clock In (`beatlayer.cv_clock_in`) is an audio effect that passes its audio through untouched and has one CV input, Clock (R3). Its controls: Sync, a switch (on); Rate, in pulses per quarter note, from the same list as CV Clock Out's (4 by default); and Threshold, 0 to 10 V, 1 V. A pulse is a rise of the input through Threshold after it has been at least 0.1 V below it, timed to the frame it crosses, between frames by a straight line. The ranges, defaults and the 0.1 V are Beatlayer's own. (S1; Beatlayer's own)
- **R24.** While Sync is on and pulses arrive, the song plays at the clock's tempo, one pulse being 1 / Rate of a quarter note, as it plays at an incoming MIDI clock's while EXT is on ([MIDI clock](midi-clock.md) R17 and R18: the Tempo field shows it and takes no edits, tempo automation waits, and the arrival times are smoothed and glitches set aside). A CV clock carries no start, stop or position: the transport starts and stops only as the person or an agent starts and stops it, and while it plays the beat is kept to the pulses as MIDI clock R19 keeps it to clocks, the song position at each pulse being the position at the first pulse after following began plus 1 / Rate of a quarter note per pulse since. With no pulse for twice the last interval between pulses, and at least 500 ms, the clock counts as silent and the song plays on at the last followed tempo, as MIDI clock R21 says. The device's Sync switch turns following on, not the Control Bar's EXT switch, and the device's panel shows the measured tempo and lights with each pulse. Counting from the first pulse, the silence time and the switch are Beatlayer's own. (S1, S6, S7)
- **R25.** One clock leads the song at a time: while a MIDI clock leads through External Sync, or a CV Clock In earlier in the song's track and chain order leads, a CV Clock In with Sync on follows nothing and its panel says what leads (Beatlayer's own). With Link on, a CV clock that leads sets the Link session's tempo, as an external clock does under External Sync ([Link](link.md)). In an offline render the song keeps its own tempo. (S7; Beatlayer's own)

**CV In (WP-7.18)**

- **R26.** CV In (`beatlayer.cv_in`) is a modulator: it maps its targets as the LFO does, with Map, Unmap and Show/Hide Multimap's eight slots, the Mod switch, Polarity, Depth and each slot's Amount, Min and Max (modulation R13 to R19). Its output is the voltage on its CV input, CV (R3), as a share u = (V − Low) / (High − Low) clamped to 0 to 1, Low and High from −10 to +10 V (−5 V and +5 V on a new device; Low above High inverts it), followed by Smooth, a one-pole smoother of 0 to 1,000 ms (0 ms). The ranges and the smoother are Beatlayer's own. (S1, S7; Beatlayer's own)
- **R27.** CV In takes its input at the 32-frame grid points of modulation R20, the input's value on each grid frame, and its output runs in a straight line from each to the next, as the LFO's does. Since the interface's input for a block is known before any track runs, CV In works its output out with the LFO and the Shaper, before any track, and reaches its targets on any track on the frames it is worked out for, with no step's lag (Beatlayer's own). (S7)

**CV Utility (WP-7.18)**

- **R28.** CV Utility (`beatlayer.cv_utility`) is an audio effect that treats signals as control voltages. It has two inputs: A, its track's signal at its place in the chain, both sides averaged, and B, the source its sidechain section chooses, both sides averaged, or nothing while the sidechain is off. Each has a Gain from −200 % to +200 % (100 %). Mode is Add (A + B, the default) or Multiply (A times B as a share of full scale, so B works on A as a voltage-controlled amplifier would). Offset (−10 to +10 V, 0 V) is added after. The result, clamped to ±10 V, replaces the track's signal on both sides and goes to its CV output, CV (R3), if one is chosen. The inputs, Mode and ranges are Beatlayer's own. (S1; Beatlayer's own)
- **R29.** With A's Gain at 0 % and the sidechain off, CV Utility's output is its Offset alone: automating or modulating Offset makes a voltage that follows the envelope frame by frame, on its track or on its CV output. (S1)

**Throughout CV Clock Out, CV Clock In, CV In and CV Utility (WP-7.18)**

- **R30.** Each control besides the choosers is a parameter, automatable and modulatable (rule G2). CV Clock Out's, CV In's and CV Utility's display data (R9) give their outputs' and inputs' last values in volts, and CV Clock In's the measured tempo, whether it leads, and the frame of its last pulse. (S7, S8)
- **R31.** Every action here is a registered command run by ID through the agent API, reading back as R20 says; the song's tempo while a CV clock leads reads back in `transport.state` as the tempo it plays, with the device that leads it. (S8)

**CV Envelope Follower, CV LFO and CV Shaper (WP-7.18b)**

- **R32.** CV Envelope Follower (`beatlayer.cv_level`), CV LFO (`beatlayer.cv_wave`) and CV Shaper (`beatlayer.cv_curve`) are audio effects that pass their audio through untouched and send a signal from 0 to 1, made as the Envelope Follower's, the LFO's or the Shaper's output is made, to one CV output, CV (R3), as Min + Depth · u · (Max − Min) volts, u being the signal and Depth the device's Depth (0 % to 100 %, 100 %). Min and Max run from −10 to +10 V, −5 V and +5 V on CV LFO and 0 V and +5 V on the other two; Min above Max inverts it. They have no Map buttons, slots or Mod switch. The ranges and defaults are Beatlayer's own. (S1, S5; Beatlayer's own)
- **R33.** Their signal is worked out on every frame from the same law as the modulator they share it with, not on the modulators' 32-frame grid, so a square wave, a step or a shape's jump lands on its frame (Beatlayer's own); it is the same at any block size (R8). (S7; Beatlayer's own)
- **R34.** CV LFO has the LFO's controls and laws (modulation R22 and "The LFO's waveforms"): the nine waveforms, Shape, Steps, Jitter, Smooth, Rate in Hz with ×10 or in beat divisions, Offset, Phase, Hold and R, with the ranges and defaults of the LFO's table, locked to the song position in beat divisions and free in Hz. (S5, S7)
- **R35.** CV Shaper has the Shaper's controls and laws (modulation R23 and "The Shaper's shape"): its breakpoint shape, edited by the same gestures and by `modulator.set_shape`, the six presets, Clear, Grid and Snap, Jitter, Smooth, Phase, Offset, Rate and R, and the trigger modes Loop, 1-Shot with T, and Manual. (S5, S7)
- **R36.** CV Envelope Follower has the Envelope Follower's controls and detector (modulation R24 and "The Envelope Follower's detector"): Gain, Rise, Fall, Delay with its Time and Beats modes, and the sidechain section with Audio From, its tap and Sidechain Mix, and its Direct and S.C. meters. (S5, S7)
