## The behaviour

A MIDI track without an instrument can send what it plays to a hardware synthesiser: its MIDI To chooser names a MIDI output port whose Track switch is on, and a channel. Each message leaves the port at the moment its frame is heard at the audio interface's output, so a synthesiser heard on its own outputs plays in time with the set. Beatlayer schedules the messages ahead of time where the platform's MIDI system can (Core MIDI, the ALSA sequencer) and from a timer thread where it can't (Windows Multimedia MIDI), rather than sending them the moment the audio thread makes them.

Beatlayer's equivalent of Live's External Instrument (this spec calls it the external instrument; WP-5.34 gives it its name) is an instrument on a MIDI track that sends the track's notes to a port and channel and brings the synthesiser's sound back from the interface's inputs, with a Gain and a Hardware Latency. Beatlayer's equivalent of Live's External Audio Effect (the external effect; WP-5.35 names it) sends a track's audio out of the interface to a hardware processor and brings it back, with a Gain each way, Dry/Wet, Invert and a Hardware Latency. Both report the round trip through the interface plus their Hardware Latency to delay compensation, so what comes back lines up with the rest of the set; Options ▸ Delay Compensation switches compensation, and with it the Hardware Latency controls, off and on. An export whose sound runs through either device renders in real time on the running audio engine, after a waiting period, and starts again when a dropout spoils it.

This spec covers MIDI out on a track's MIDI To, the two external devices, the Delay Compensation switch as they need it, and real-time export. It leaves to [routing](routing.md) the In/Out section's other choosers; to [MIDI I/O](midi-io.md) the ports and their switches; to [delay compensation](delay-compensation.md) how a reported latency is compensated; to [export](export.md) everything an offline export does; and to Phase 7 MIDI clock out (a port's Sync switch). The manual's routing of the external instrument to a multi-timbral plug-in on another track, its audio taken from the plug-in's auxiliary outputs (sections 31.6 and 17.5.2.6), waits for plug-in instruments with auxiliary outputs, which the plug-in hosts don't offer yet: here, MIDI To names ports only.

## Rules

**MIDI out on a track's MIDI To (WP-5.34)**

- **R1.** A MIDI track's MIDI To lists every MIDI output port whose Track switch is on, by name in the system's order, then Configure… ─ the other MIDI tracks ─ No Output, No Output staying the default; the ports come first, as the input ports do in MIDI From (Beatlayer's own place for them). With a port chosen its channel chooser lists 1 to 16; choosing a port takes channel 1 (Beatlayer's own), and choosing the same port again keeps its channel. With a track chosen it lists Track In, as before. MIDI To applies to a MIDI track without an instrument. (S1, S9, S10)
- **R2.** `routing.set_midi_output` takes a port by name with its channel, besides a track or nothing, as one undo step named Change "Routing", saved with the set where it isn't No Output, so a set saved before MIDI out opens as it was; it reads back in the query tool's `track` result under `routing.midi_output`. A track keeps its port's name when the port goes away or its Track switch goes off, sends nothing to it meanwhile, and shows the name dimmed in its chooser; it sends again once a port of that name is back with Track on, as a track's input does (MIDI I/O R8). (S1, S9, S10)
- **R3.** Every note on and note off, with its velocity and release velocity, and every controller, pitch-bend and pressure message (a note's own pressure leaves as polyphonic key pressure) that reaches the end of the track's chain (its clips and its monitored input, after its MIDI effects) leaves on the port, rewritten onto the chosen channel. A note's own bend and slide are MPE's, which a hardware synthesiser takes on channels of its own, and don't leave. System messages never leave a track, and a program change has no place in the note path between devices yet, so none leaves. (S1)
- **R4.** Each message leaves the port at the moment its frame is heard at the audio interface's output: the time the engine estimates for its block's start (MIDI I/O R16), plus its offset in the block, plus the device's reported output latency, plus the delay compensation the track's path into Main is given. A synthesiser heard on its own outputs then plays in time with the Main output, at any block size. (S10, S11; Beatlayer's own)
- **R5.** The audio thread hands each message, stamped with that moment, to a MIDI output thread on a lock-free queue, without allocating, locking or blocking; a full queue drops the message and counts it. On macOS the thread passes each message to Core MIDI ahead of time with its time stamp, and on Linux to the ALSA sequencer's queue with its time, so the system sends it on time; on Windows a timer thread sends each one within 1 ms of its moment (Beatlayer's bound). A message whose moment has gone by when the thread takes it is sent at once and counted late. The queue and the thread belong to the MIDI output bus, which outlives the ports: an engine that starts takes a queue of its own from it, and what the engine before queued, its stopping note-offs included, goes out first. (S11; Beatlayer's own)
- **R6.** Every note sent on gets its note off on the same port and channel: when it ends, when the transport stops, when the track's MIDI To or its channel changes, when the track or the port goes, and when the audio stream stops or restarts, so no note is left hanging on the hardware. (S10; Beatlayer's own)
- **R7.** The Control Bar's MIDI track output indicator (L-CTRL-026) lights for a moment whenever a message leaves for a port. (S3, S12)
- **R8.** Offline renders (an export not made real time by R22, agents' previews, freezing and bouncing) send nothing to MIDI ports. (S8; Beatlayer's own)

**The external instrument (WP-5.34)**

- **R9.** Beatlayer ships an instrument device type of its own layout with Live's name, the External Instrument (`beatlayer.hardware_instrument`), with no sound of its own; it takes a MIDI track's instrument place. Its controls: MIDI To, listing the output ports with Track on and No Output (the default), and its channel (1 to 16, default 1); Audio From, listing the interface's input channels as Ext. In's channel chooser does (the single channels, then the pairs), the first pair by default; Configure… at the end of each, opening Settings on the Tempo & MIDI or the Audio page (L-SET-014, L-SET-012); Gain; and Hardware Latency with its unit switch. The choosers and Hardware Latency sit in a section down the left of the device's body, Gain's knob beside it (L-DEV-022). A device keeps the name of a port that is away or has Track off, dimmed, as a track's MIDI To does (R2). (S5, S10, S12)
- **R10.** The notes and messages that reach the device leave on its port and channel as R3, R5 and R6 say, each at the moment its frame would reach the interface's output, without the track's compensation into Main: the device's own latency is what compensation lines up (R13). (S5; Beatlayer's own)
- **R11.** The device's output is its Audio From channels, a pair as left and right and a single channel on both sides, scaled by Gain (a gain from −∞ to +24 dB, default 0 dB, rule G1; automatable and modulatable, rule G2). A channel the interface lacks, or no audio device, gives silence. (S4, S5, S11)
- **R12.** Hardware Latency is how much later than the interface's own round trip the sound comes back: in milliseconds (0 to 1000.00 ms) or in samples (0 to 192,000), as its unit switch says, with 0 ms in a new device (Beatlayer's own ranges and default). A value in milliseconds keeps its milliseconds when the sample rate changes, so its frames follow the rate; a value in samples keeps its samples. Switching the unit converts the value at the current rate, to whole samples, which the switch does by sending the converted value with the new unit in `device.set_external`. (S5, S6)
- **R13.** The device reports its latency to delay compensation (delay compensation R1 to R6) as the audio device's reported output and input latency, plus Driver Error Compensation (recording R10's total), plus Hardware Latency, in frames, so its returning sound lines up with the rest of the set at Main. A change to Hardware Latency, its unit, the sample rate or the device's reported latency compiles again with the new latency: the audio host tells the engine the output latency and the round trip whenever either changes (a backend that measures its latency from its first callbacks, Driver Error Compensation). (S5, S7, S10; the sum is Beatlayer's own)
- **R14.** Every Hardware Latency control, of the external instrument (R9) and the external effect (R17), is disabled while the set's delay compensation is off, keeping its value; the Options ▸ Delay Compensation (M-OPT-008) and Reduced Latency When Monitoring (M-OPT-009) switches themselves are [delay compensation](delay-compensation.md) R7 and R8, which say what turning them off does. (S5, S6, S7, S12)
- **R15.** The device's choosers, Hardware Latency and its unit are settings, not parameters: each change is one undo step saved with the set, through `device.set_external` (`edit`), which reads back in the query tool's `device` result under `external`; they aren't automation targets, because each changes routing or latency, which compiles a new program. (S10; D17)
- **R16.** In an offline render (a preview, a freeze or a bounce, and an export R22 doesn't make real time) the device sends nothing and returns silence, and the render's result names it: an export's `silenced_external_devices`, a preview's notes. (S8; Beatlayer's own)

**The external effect (WP-5.35)**

- **R17.** Beatlayer ships an audio effect device type of its own layout, equivalent to Live's External Audio Effect. Its controls: Audio To, listing the interface's output channels (the single channels, then the pairs, the first pair by default), and Audio From, listing its input channels the same way, each with Configure… at its end; a peak indicator under each; a Gain beside each, out and in (gains from −∞ to +24 dB, default 0 dB); Dry/Wet (0 to 100 %, default 100 %); Invert (off by default); and Hardware Latency with its unit switch, as R12 says. (S6, S12)
- **R18.** The device sends its input, times the out Gain, to Audio To's channels: a pair as left and right, a single channel with the two sides added and 6 dB down. What it sends adds to whatever else plays on those channels (Main Out, tracks on Ext. Out), as routing R3 says. (S4, S6, S10)
- **R19.** The returning signal is Audio From's channels (a pair as left and right, a single channel on both sides) times the in Gain, its phase flipped while Invert is on. The device's output is (1 − w) times its input, delayed by the device's latency, plus w times the returning signal, w being Dry/Wet, so the dry and wet parts line up whatever the latency. (S6; the law is Beatlayer's own)
- **R20.** The device reports its latency as the external instrument does (R13), and R14 disables its Hardware Latency in the same way, so a loopback from an output to an input through the device lines up with the track's other audio at Main. (S6, S7)
- **R21.** Each peak indicator shows the highest level its signal has reached, outgoing or returning, published with the meters after every block; a click resets it and changes nothing in the set. Gain, Dry/Wet and Invert are parameters, automatable and modulatable (rule G2); the choosers, Hardware Latency and its unit are settings, as R15 says. In an offline render the device sends nothing and hears silence back, so its output is its dry part alone. (S6, S11)

**Exporting with external hardware (WP-5.35)**

- **R22.** An export renders in real time when a rendered output's path runs through an external instrument or external effect whose routing reaches the interface (a MIDI To port, or interface channels): the Main output when a track reaching it does, an individual track when its own path does. The outputs without one render offline as before; an export with both renders its offline outputs first and then all its real-time ones together in one pass. (S8)
- **R23.** Before a real-time render a dialog counts down a waiting period, 10 seconds unless its seconds field is given a longer one, so the hardware's last sound dies away; Restart starts the count again and Skip starts the render at once. A progress bar then shows the render, with the number of attempts once there has been more than one, an Auto-Restart on Drop-Outs switch (on, Beatlayer's own default), and Cancel, which stops the render and deletes the partial file. The dialog opens for an export started from the Export Audio/Video dialog (L-OTH-003, M-FILE-015). (S8, S12)
- **R24.** The real-time render plays the range on the running audio engine, from its start with the Arrangement loop off, as an offline export plays it (export R3), and Main Out plays it as it goes. The audio thread copies each rendered output into a lock-free ring at the point an offline render takes it from, and a writer thread writes the file, so the file holds exactly the range's frames, starting where an offline render's would. The transport stops at the range's end and stays stopped. (S8, S10)
- **R25.** A dropout during the render (an xrun the backend reports, a break in the device clock, a callback that overran its block, or a ring too full for a block) starts it again while Auto-Restart is on: the partial file is emptied, the waiting period runs again and the attempt count rises. With Auto-Restart off the render carries on, and its result lists each dropout with its song position. (S8, S11; the ring's place and the list are Beatlayer's own)
- **R26.** A real-time render runs at the audio device's rate; an export at another rate converts the render, as export R6 converts a render down, and Normalize makes its second pass over the scratch file (export R8). A real-time render isn't the same bytes every time (export R14 holds for offline renders), and its result says it rendered in real time. Without a running audio device, an export that needs real time is refused before anything renders. (S8, S10; Beatlayer's own)
- **R27.** `export.audio` takes `wait_seconds` (default 10), `auto_restart` (default true) and `max_attempts` (no limit when left out), and its result reports `real_time`, the attempts and the dropouts; it stays in the `external` class. Agents' previews (`perception.preview`) never render in real time: they render offline with R16's and R21's silence and name the external devices they silenced. (S8, S10; D17)

**Throughout**

- **R28.** Every action here is a registered command with a schema, a description and a permission class, run by ID through the agent API, naming in the readback table where its effect reads back; every change to the set is one undo step and saved with it. (D17)
- **R29.** Nothing on the audio thread allocates, locks or blocks: the external devices read the interface's input and write its output through buffers the program makes, outgoing MIDI leaves on a lock-free queue, and a real-time render's rings are made before it starts. (S11)
