## The behaviour

Every native device is built on one framework. A device type describes each of its parameters: its range, its unit and how its value reads, its default, and that it can be automated. The engine keeps every value inside its range and moves continuous values and gains smoothly, so a change never clicks, and every change lands on its exact frame, so automation is sample-accurate. Gains reach −∞ dB (rule G1), and every parameter can be automated and modulated (rule G2); a device type that breaks either is refused when it registers. A preset loads into a device clamped to its parameters, leaving out values for parameters it doesn't have. An oversampler runs a nonlinear stage at 2, 4, or 8 times the rate, so its harmonics don't fold back.

A device's body is laid out as fixed bands of controls and stretchable display cells (IMP-14). Docked, every part has its own size, which is the device's width in the chain; given more room, as a floating window (WP-2.5b) gives it, only the display cells grow, shared by their weights, and the controls keep their size and stay pinned to their edges. A device with no display cell doesn't grow.

The Device View draws any described device from its descriptors and layout, the generic UI: a knob for a continuous value or gain, a switch, a chooser, or a number box, each with its name above and its value, and the display cells framed and left for the device to draw in. Dragging a control, double-clicking it, or pressing Delete while it has the keyboard changes the parameter as one undo step named for it. Agents read every parameter's descriptor and value with `device.parameters`.

A device with a display also publishes the display's latest data after each block, through a lock-free handoff made with its node, as the engine publishes its meters, and the shell reads the newest. A device type turns that data and its parameters into what its display shows: curves and spectra, markers on them, and single readings. The display cell draws them inside the framework's frame, growing with a floating window, and a screen reader says them; agents read the same with `device.display`. A display can offer views, one shown at a time, which is view state.

Beatlayer ships no native device in WP-2.5: three test devices prove the framework.

### MIDI effects

A native device with the MIDI-effect role works on notes rather than audio. It reads the notes that reach it in each stretch, each with its frame, and sends the notes that go on to the next device, on their frames; whatever it doesn't change, it forwards, and the block's audio passes through untouched. Because a MIDI effect can multiply or delay notes, it keeps a table of the notes each input note started, so the input note's note-off, its release velocity and its per-note expressions reach the notes it made and not the key that came in, whatever changed in between. It ends every note it started: the engine asks it to when it is switched off, removed or moved, and nothing it started is left sounding or waiting. Its tables and queues are made with it and fixed in size.

### Plug-in kit plug-ins

A plug-in built on Loombridge's plug-in kit can be compiled into Beatlayer and run as a native device, with no plug-in boundary: its parameters' descriptors are already the framework's, its audio-thread half runs in the framework's node, taking each parameter change and note on its frame, and its own commands are commands of its device, which agents run through Beatlayer's agent API as they run a CLAP plug-in's (the CLAP hosting spec, R18). Its values live in the set like any native device's, and its own state is saved with the set. Reagent will be the first; until then the kit's two example plug-ins prove it, behind a build option that's off by default.

### Device pages

Many of Live's instruments hold more controls than a device's body can show at once, and switch between sets of them. Wavetable, Meld, Collision and Tension draw tabs along the body, each showing one page of controls; Analog, Operator and Electric draw a shell, a strip of sections each showing its main controls, and a display beside it that fills with the selected section's further controls. Beatlayer's layouts hold either as a pages node: a named group of pages, each a layout of its own, one shown at a time. Which page shows is view state, like a display's view: switching pages is never an undo step, and an agent switches and reads it by command. A parameter on a hidden page is still the device's parameter like any other, placed once, automatable, mappable and in its banks.

## Rules

- **R1.** Each parameter of a native device type has a descriptor: its number, name, and description; its kind (a continuous value with its range, unit, and taper; a gain; a switch; a choice with its names; or a whole number with its range and unit); its default; and whether it can be automated. Its value moves along its control's travel from 0 to 1 (logarithmically for a logarithmic taper), reads with its unit, and is parsed back from typed text. (S6)
- **R2.** A gain's value is a linear amplitude factor whose range starts at 0, which is −∞ dB and reads `-inf dB`; the bottom of its control's travel reaches it, and a device at −∞ on a gain that acts on its audio is silent (a gain that acts on one band of the device, as each of the three-band EQ's does, silences it together with the device's other band gains). (S1, S4)
- **R3.** Every parameter can be automated. A descriptor that can't is refused, and so is a device type with one, or with a broken descriptor (an empty range, a default outside it, a logarithmic range reaching 0, a choice with no choices, two parameters with one number), when it registers. (S1)
- **R4.** On the audio thread every value is clamped to its range. A switch, a choice, or a whole number changes at once; a continuous value or a gain moves to each new value along a straight line over 20 ms, frame by frame, the same at any block size. A switched-off device takes changes at once, so it comes back on at its new values. (S6)
- **R5.** Values read in three significant figures with their unit: dB with two decimals below 1 dB and one from there (`0.00 dB`, `-4.9 dB`), Hz to 999 then kHz (`80.0 Hz`, `1.24 kHz`), ms to 999 then s, `0 ms` for zero, per cent with no decimals from 10 % and one below (`21 %`, `0.0 %`), a ratio as `4.00 : 1`, a plain number with two decimals. Negative numbers use the ASCII minus, as the mixer's readouts do. (S3, S4)
- **R6.** A parameter change lands on its exact frame: the device's audio before it is unchanged, and from it the value moves (R4). (S1, S6)
- **R7.** A preset loads into a described device clamped to its parameters' ranges, without values for parameters it doesn't have. (S6)
- **R8.** The oversampler runs a process at 2, 4, or 8 times the rate through linear-phase filters, passing the band below about 0.34 of the rate and keeping what would fold back about 80 dB down, and reports its latency as a whole number of frames (32, 48, or 56), which the device reports for delay compensation. (S6)
- **R9.** Nothing the framework does on the audio thread allocates, locks, or blocks: every buffer is made with the device. (S6)
- **R10.** A device's layout is bands of controls, each its own size, and display cells, each with a docked size and a weight, in rows and columns. Its docked size, every part at its own size, is the smallest it can be. A layout places every parameter's control exactly once, inside its band. (S2)
- **R11.** Given more room than its docked size, a layout gives the extra width and height only to its display cells, shared by their weights; bands keep their size and stay pinned to the parts around them, and rows of controls inside a cell's top and bottom edges stay on those edges. (S2, S3)
- **R12.** A layout with no display cell stays at its docked size whatever room it's given. The generic layout, for a device without its own, is columns of controls in the parameters' order, as tall as a device's body (158 points), with no cell. (S2, S5)
- **R13.** In the Device View a described device's frame is its layout's docked width; a device whose type isn't described keeps WP-2.4's 120 points. `device_view.state` gives each described device its descriptors, layout, and values. (S2)
- **R14.** `device.parameters` reports a device's parameters, in its type's order, each with its descriptor, its value, and how the value reads; it's empty for a device whose type isn't described. (S6)
- **R15.** The generic UI draws a described device's body from its layout at its docked size: each parameter's control, and each display cell black with a thin border. A floating window places the same layout at its own size (device chains R28, WP-2.5b). (S2, S3)
- **R16.** Continuous values and gains are knobs (the name above, an arc and pointer, the value below), switches are text buttons, choices are choosers, and whole numbers are number boxes; each is a screen reader's slider, switch, combo box, or spin button with its name and value. (S3)
- **R17.** A knob or number box drags relatively, 125 points of vertical drag for its whole travel, three times finer with Shift; the arrow keys and a screen reader's steps move it a step (1% of the travel, or one step of a stepped value); a double-click, or Delete while it has the keyboard, sets its default. While it has the keyboard, Delete doesn't delete the device. (S3, S4)
- **R18.** Every change from a control is one undo step named Change "<parameter name>", however long the drag. While a control is dragged, the Status Bar reads `<name>: <value>`. (S3, S4)
- **R19.** Clicking a switch turns it over; a chooser opens a menu of its choices. (S3)
- **R20.** While a device is switched off, its knobs' arcs, lit switches, and number boxes' fill are grey; names and values keep their colour. (S3)
- **R21.** A kit plug-in compiled in is a native device type: its kind is the plug-in's ID, its name the plug-in's, its role an instrument when it has audio out and none in, a MIDI effect when it has notes out and no audio, and an audio effect otherwise; its description is its own parameters' descriptors with the generic layout, checked as any native type's (R3). (S7)
- **R22.** Its audio-thread half runs in the framework's node: each stretch's parameter changes reach it as events on the stretch's first frame, the stretch's notes on their frames, and an instrument's buffer starts silent. Its main-thread half stays on the main thread, one instance per device, which makes the audio-thread half when a node asks and takes it back when the node goes; until it arrives an effect passes its input through and an instrument is silent. Nothing it does on the audio thread allocates, locks, or blocks (R9). (S6, S7)
- **R23.** Its own commands are device commands, named and served as the CLAP hosting spec's R18 says, from the moment the device is in the set. Running one runs it on the main thread, checked against its schema; the parameters it changed go into the set in the same undo step (an agent's labelled one), and the engine brings them to the node on their frame (R6). A change the plug-in makes outside a command, as from its own window, is one undo step per gesture, named for its parameter (R18). (S7)
- **R24.** The set holds its values, which win when the set is opened again, and its own state, in the kit's state layout, taken in without an undo step each time the set is saved or the device copied; a reopened set's device starts a new instance from that state. Offline renders pass it through until WP-2.6b, as they pass CLAP plug-ins (the CLAP hosting spec's decisions). (S7)
- **R25.** The kit's two example plug-ins, Example Gain and Example Sine, join the device list only with `bl-devices`' `kit-examples` feature, which is off by default, so nothing user-facing ships. (S7)
- **R26.** The effects LFO (`bl_devices::lfo`) has twelve waveforms, a device offering the ones its manual lists under the manual's names: Sine (starting at 0, rising), Triangle (starting at its bottom), Shark Tooth (a triangle with a pointed top and a rounded bottom), Triangle Analog (a rectangle through a 10 ms low-pass, so its edges soften as the rate rises), Triangle 8 and Triangle 16 (a triangle in 8 and 16 steps to the cycle), Saw Up, Saw Down, Rectangle (high for the first half), and three that take a new random value each cycle: Sample & Hold (held), Wander (a straight line to the next value) and Random (eased to it). Every one runs from −1 to 1, at any duty cycle and morph, and repeats at its rate within 0.1 %. A duty cycle from −1 to 1 squeezes the wave into the front (positive) or the back (negative) of its cycle, down to a tenth of it at ±1 and holding its end (or start) value for the rest, and leaves the random waves alone; a morph blends the wave toward a second one. (S8)
- **R27.** The LFO's rate is cycles per second, seconds per cycle, or quarter notes per cycle (so synced, dotted, triplet, and sixteenth rates are all counts of quarter notes). While the song plays and the rate is in quarter notes, its phase is a function of the song position through the tempo map, so a cycle starts on the beat grid at any tempo and across a tempo change, within 0.1 % of a cycle, plus a phase offset in degrees; otherwise it free-runs from where it was, a synced rate at the tempo of the stretch's first frame. Phase puts the right channel's LFO a number of degrees ahead of the left's (180° is antiphase); Spin runs the right LFO's rate a percentage above or below the left's, within 0.1 %. What an LFO does at any frame is the same at any block size. (S8, S9)
- **R28.** The envelope follower follows the level of a signal with an attack and a release, each the time the level takes to cover 63 % of a step up or down, within 5 %. With Hold on, an attack that has begun finishes, reaching 99 % of the peak it was heading for, before the release begins, so a pulse shorter than the attack still raises the level to its peak. (S8)
- **R29.** The random source follows a seed: the device's own ID, so a render repeats run after run and two devices differ. Both channels' LFOs follow the same random values, Phase and Spin setting where each is in them. Neither the source nor the parts above allocate, lock, or block on the audio thread; they are made with the device. (S9)
- **R30.** A native device reads the notes that reach it in each stretch, with their offsets from its first frame, and can send notes on from it: each goes out at an offset within the stretch, in the block's order of offset, a note-off before a note-on on one frame and otherwise in the order sent, without allocating. A note past the out buffer's room is dropped and counted. A MIDI effect forwards what it doesn't change and leaves the block's audio as it came. A block of several hundred notes goes through a chain without allocating, the MIDI clip player's own notes included. (S6)
- **R31.** A MIDI effect ends every note it started with a matching note-off, on the key and channel it started it on, whatever changes while the note is held: a parameter, a preset, a block boundary. A note-off for a note the device never saw, such as one started while it was switched off, passes through as it came. A note held for later (a strummed note, a delayed note-off) is dropped, not sounded, if its input note ends first. (S6)
- **R32.** A device switched off ends the notes it started on the first frame of the block in which it goes off, and then passes its input through (device chains R6). A device removed from its chain, or moved relative to the others, is asked once, as the new program takes over, to end its notes: the note-offs enter the chain in front of the device that sat after it, in the first block of the new program, so the devices after it end what it made as they would have. Nothing allocates. (S6)
- **R33.** A per-note expression (bend, pressure, slide) for a note a MIDI effect moved or generated follows it, to the key and note ID of the note it made; a note the device dropped takes its expressions with it. A note a device generates has a note ID of its own, apart from the IDs controllers give. (S6)
- **R34.** A MIDI effect's table of notes (1,024) and queue of notes held for later (1,024) are made with it. A note past their room is dropped and counted, and the count is readable (`NativeNode::dropped_notes`). (S6)
- **R35.** A device with a display publishes its display data (a fixed number of values its type lays out) after each block, through a lock-free triple buffer made with its node; the compiler takes the reader when it makes the node, and the shell reads the newest, with a count of the blocks that published. Nothing on the audio thread allocates, locks, or blocks, at any block size, and a device that leaves the program loses its reader. (S6)
- **R36.** A device type works out its display from its parameters' values and the newest data: curves and spectra (values over an axis), markers on them, and single readings, each with its unit and how it reads. The display cell draws them inside its frame (R15) in place of the black frame alone, in the accent and in grey while the device is switched off (R20), and a floating window's larger cell draws them at its size (R11). The cell is an image in the accessibility tree whose name is the cell's and whose value says the readings, the highest point of a spectrum, and the markers. (S2, S3)
- **R37.** `device.display` reports a device's display by its ID: the series, markers and readings, the view shown, and whether an engine is publishing (`live`). Without an engine it holds what the parameters give; for a device whose type draws no display it is empty. It is a `read` command. (S6)
- **R38.** A display can offer views, listed in its description with the first the one it starts in. `device.set_display_view` chooses one (a `read` command: it changes only the view, so it isn't an undo step); `device_view.state` gives each device's frame the view shown, and the cell draws buttons that choose a view, each reachable from the keyboard. A view the display doesn't offer is refused. A description can name, for each view in order, a parameter that a vertical drag on the display changes as one undo step named for it, as a drag on its knob does (Roar's shaper display, [Roar](roar.md) R12). (S2)
- **R39.** A control change, a channel's pitch bend and a channel's pressure travel in a block beside the notes, in time order, as events of their own kind (`NoteKind::Control`, `PitchBend` and `ChannelPressure`) on the channel they belong to: a controller number from 0 to 127 with a value from 0 to 1, a bend from −1 to +1 with 0 at the wheel's centre, and a pressure from 0 to 1. A device reads them with the notes it plays and sends them with the notes it sends (R30): they go out in the order sent, after what is already on their frame, a note-on included, so a note and the messages behind it keep their order, while a note-off still goes before a note-on. A MIDI effect forwards the messages it doesn't change; a device switched off passes them on with its notes (device chains R6). A message starts no note, so nothing ends it, and a device that is removed or moved leaves none behind. A MIDI effect rack merges its chains' messages with their notes ([racks](racks.md) R4). They share the notes' room (R34), and a message past it is dropped and counted. (S6)
- **R40.** A device with sample slots (WP-5.17, the eight-slot drum sampler) numbers slot `k`'s copy of a parameter as the first slot's number plus `k` times a stride, and its layout can hold display cells that stand for a slot and bands of slot controls that name the first slot's parameters. A band of slot controls is placed for the slot the panel has selected, so the panel shows one slot's controls at a time, and counts as placing that parameter once for every slot, so rule R10 holds. The selected slot is the view's, not the set's. (Beatlayer's own)
- **R41.** A device's layout can hold pages nodes. A pages node has a name and an ordered list of pages, each with a name and a layout node of its own (bands and cells, R10), and a style. Tabs: a row of text buttons, one per page in order, along the top of the node's area (in the body, as Collision, Tension, Wavetable and Meld have them) or in the title bar after the name (as the multisampler's are, L-DEV-017), the shown page's button lit and its layout filling the node's area. Shell and display: a strip of sections, one per page, each a band of that page's main controls under a section button, beside a display area that the selected section's page fills. (S10, S11; the two styles' drawing is Beatlayer's own)
- **R42.** A pages node's docked size is, in each direction, the largest of its pages' docked sizes (and, in the shell style, the strip's), so the device keeps its size whichever page shows; extra room (R11) goes to the display cells of the page shown. R10's check counts every page: each parameter's control is placed exactly once across the whole layout, a hidden page's included, and a parameter placed on two pages, or on a page and outside the node, is refused when the type registers. (S2)
- **R43.** Which page each pages node shows is view state of the device: a new device shows each node's first page; clicking a tab or a section button shows its page; `device.set_page` (`read`) shows a page by the device's ID, the node's name and the page's name or index, and is refused for a node or page the device doesn't have. Switching pages is never an undo step and never marks the set changed. The page shown is saved with the set when it is saved, as a display's view is (R38), and `device_view.state` gives every device's pages nodes with their pages and the one shown. A floating window (R15) shows the same page as the docked device. (S10, S11)
- **R44.** A tab or a section button carries a small indicator at its top-right corner, lit while any parameter whose control is on its page is modulated (by a clip envelope, a modulator, a macro or the device's own modulation matrix). The tabs are a screen reader's tab list, each tab with its name and whether it is selected, reachable from the keyboard, the arrow keys moving between them; a shell's section buttons are the same. (S11)
- **R45.** A parameter on a hidden page plays, automates, is modulated and is set and read by command exactly as a shown one: its value acts on the sound whether its page shows or not, it has its automation lane and its place in its device's banks for control surfaces, and in the map modes its mapping is made while its page shows and kept, and works, while it doesn't. (S1, S2)
- **R46.** A device's display can hold a spectrogram: a grid of magnitudes with a fixed number of columns over time and rows over frequency (linear or logarithmic, between a lowest and a highest frequency), all set in the device type's description and never by the size of the cell or window it is drawn in. The device publishes its newest columns after each block through the display's triple buffer (R35); the display cell draws the grid as an image in Beatlayer's own colour ramp between the floor and ceiling levels its description gives, newest at the right, in grey while the device is switched off (R20); `device.display` reports the grid as numbers in dB with its axes. Publishing allocates, locks and blocks nothing on the audio thread. (Beatlayer's own)
- **R47.** A device type may declare impulse-response slots, each named, beside its sample slots (R40): `device.load_sample` with `slot` fills one and `device.set_sample` clears it, as [impulse responses](impulse-responses.md) R1 to R15 say. A slot is media, not a parameter: it has no automation lane and isn't in the device's banks. An audio file dropped on a display cell of a device with such a slot loads into the slot the cell names, or the device's only slot. (Beatlayer's own)
- **R48.** A device from an extension ([extensions](extensions.md) R24) declares its parameters as this framework's descriptors and is held to G1 and G2 as a native device is; it gets the generic layout unless it draws display cells of its own (R49). (Beatlayer's own)
- **R49.** An extension device's display cell is drawn from the drawing list its extension gives ([extensions](extensions.md) R32), in Beatlayer's typeface and theme colours; a floating window's extra width and height go to its display cells as to a native device's (IMP-14), and its regions reach assistive technology with the names its extension gives them. (Beatlayer's own)
- **R50.** A device type may declare that two parameters share one control, which a switch turns between: while the switch is on the control is the second parameter's, and while it is off the first's (Limiter's Maximize, which turns Ceiling into Threshold). The layout places the first parameter's control only; the second is placed through it, so R10's check counts it placed. The control's label, value, range, automation lane and accessibility name follow the parameter it shows. Both parameters keep their values, lanes, banks and clip-envelope targets whichever is shown, and both play exactly as R45 says of a hidden page's; `device.parameters` marks each parameter with whether it is shown. A description that pairs a parameter twice, pairs one with itself, or names a switch that isn't a switch of the device is refused when the type registers. (S2)

## Details

- A gain parameter holds a linear amplitude factor, as the mixer's volume does, so −∞ dB is the finite value 0 the document can hold. Its control's travel is even in dB from −70 dB (the fader's lowest step) to its top, and its very bottom is −∞.
- Smoothing is 20 ms in a straight line, as the mixer's gains.
- The generic layout is Beatlayer's own, since Live has no generic panel for its native devices. Each native device that ships (WP-2.16 onwards) gives its own layout, matched to Live's device.
- While a device's value control has the keyboard, the Device View's keys give way to the value field's (S-DEV-006), and global keys still work.
- A kit plug-in's commands and window change values through the kit's host link, which Beatlayer turns into set edits; the engine then brings them to the node as it brings any change, so automation, undo, and the saved set agree, and the audio-thread half never takes the kit's own edit queue.
- The audio-thread half of a kit plug-in comes from the device's one main-thread instance rather than a fresh instance per node, so a plug-in's own queues between its halves (a wavetable loaded by a command, say) work as in any host.
- The effects' parts (R26 to R29) are Beatlayer's own where the manual gives no number: it names the waveforms, the duty cycle, the stereo modes, the offset, and the follower's controls, but not the shapes' formulas, the rate ranges, the offset's range, the follower's ranges, Triangle Analog's filter, how far a full duty cycle squeezes a wave, or what a synced LFO does while the song is stopped. A device chooses its own ranges and defaults from its manual section. Triangle Analog's filter is 10 ms, a duty cycle of ±100 % leaves a tenth of the cycle, an attack counts as done at 99 % of its peak, and a synced LFO with the song stopped keeps running at the current tempo from where it was. The test effects device (`bl_devices::testing::EFFECTS`) uses every part.
- Delay's LFO (the delay spec, R8) runs on the effects LFO. Its Sine, Triangle, Ramp Up, Ramp Down, and Square are the shared Sine, Triangle, Saw Up, Saw Down, and Rectangle; its S&H and Wander take their random values from the device's seed rather than a fixed one, so their sequence differs from before.
- Knobs sit in 48 × 52-point slots, with 4-point padding, and sweep a 300° arc. Shift divides a drag by 3, as it does on pan, and a click away from a knob's centre doesn't jump the value. The Status Bar reads `<name>: <value>`, as the mixer's does. Hz turns to kHz and ms to s at 1,000. A number box drags as a knob. The colours of a display's curve and of a lit number box's fill are Beatlayer's own theme tokens.
