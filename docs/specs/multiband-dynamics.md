# The multiband dynamics device

- **Status:** implemented (every rule has a passing test)
- **Work packages:** WP-5.22. The framework it sits on is WP-2.5 ([the native device framework](device-framework.md)), its display path is WP-5.43's (device framework R35 to R38), its sidechain is WP-2.19b's ([routing](routing.md) R22 to R31), and a device's latency is reported for delay compensation as [delay compensation](delay-compensation.md) R1 has it
- **Parity-matrix row:** Audio effects: Multiband Dynamics
- **Checked against:** the Live 12 reference manual, chapter 29 "Live Audio Effect Reference", sections 29.26 to 29.26.4.3 "Multiband Dynamics", read on 18 October 2026; the ranges, defaults, crossover design and detector the manual does not give are Beatlayer's own

## The behaviour

Multiband Dynamics is Beatlayer's Multiband Dynamics. It splits its input into up to three bands at two crossover frequencies and gives each band two kinds of dynamics processing at once: one above a threshold and one below another. Each side has its own ratio, attack and release, and a ratio over 1 compresses (the level range narrows) while one under 1 expands (it widens), so a band can do downward or upward compression or expansion on each side of its level. Each band also has its own Input and Output gain, an activator and a solo. The High and Low switches remove the outer bands; with both off the whole signal is the Mid band. Soft Knee, the Peak or RMS detector, Time (every attack and release together), Amount (every ratio toward 1) and Output belong to the whole device.

A band's detector hears the band's own signal after its Input gain, or the same band of the device's sidechain key. Each side turns the detector's level into a gain in dB, the static curve, and smooths it with its own attack and release; the band's output is its signal through both sides' gains and its Output gain. The bands are summed, with the soloed ones only if any is soloed, and go through Output. The display shows each band's transfer curve and thresholds and, as the engine runs, each band's input and output levels and its gain reduction. The sidechain section, source and mix are [routing](routing.md)'s (R22 to R31); the framework (parameters, smoothing, layout, automation) is the [device framework spec](device-framework.md).

## Rules

- **R1.** Beatlayer ships Multiband Dynamics, an audio-effect device type with a sidechain input, of its own layout: every parameter has a descriptor and a place in the layout. The device-wide parameters are Low and High (the band switches), Low Freq and High Freq (the crossovers), Soft Knee, Detector (Peak or RMS), Time, Amount and Output; each of the three bands, named Low, Mid and High, has Active, Solo, Input and Output, and for each side (Above, Below) a Threshold, a Ratio, an Attack and a Release, named for their band and side (for example `Mid Above Ratio`). The layout runs the crossovers and switches, the display, the three bands' controls and the device-wide controls from left to right. The device is wide because it holds forty-five controls; its layout is Beatlayer's own. (S2, S7)
- **R2.** The crossovers are Linkwitz-Riley filters of 24 dB per octave, each a low-pass and a high-pass of two Butterworth sections (Q = 1/√2) at the crossover. The bands are minimum-phase and the crossovers add no latency. The low band goes through the upper crossover's all-pass as well, so with both bands on the three sum to the all-pass of both crossovers, whose magnitude is 1: with every ratio at 1 or Amount at 0 %, and every gain at 1, the device changes the signal's phase and nothing else, within 0.1 dB at any frequency, and nulls against a reference of those filters below −100 dB. The upper crossover is held at or above the lower one. Low Freq and High Freq run from 20 Hz to 20 kHz, 500 Hz and 2 kHz at first (the manual's example); that range is Beatlayer's own. (S1, S2, S7)
- **R3.** Low and High switch the outer bands on and off. With both on the signal splits at both crossovers. With Low on and High off it splits at the lower crossover into the Low band and a Mid band holding everything above it; with High on and Low off, at the upper into a Mid band holding everything below it and the High band; with both off there is one band, the Mid, and no crossover: with every Mid ratio at 1 the signal comes out as it went in, bit for bit. The settings of a band that doesn't exist do nothing. All the filters keep running whichever bands exist, so switching one finds them current; the switch changes the band content at once, as a stepped control does (device framework R4). (S2, S7)
- **R4.** Each band's detector and processing see only that band: a tone in one band raises only that band's input level (the display's, R15), and compressing that band lowers that tone and leaves a tone in another band within 0.1 dB. (S1, S2)
- **R5.** A side turns the detector's level `L` in dB into a gain in dB. With a ratio `r` scaled by Amount (R9) to `r′`, the Above side's gain is `(1/r′ − 1)(L − T)` for a level over its threshold `T` and 0 under it, so a ratio over 1 cuts the level above the threshold (downward compression) and a ratio under 1 raises it (upward expansion); the Below side's gain is `(1 − 1/r′)(T − L)` for a level under its threshold and 0 over it, so a ratio over 1 raises the level below the threshold (upward compression) and a ratio under 1 lowers it (downward expansion). Both are the ratio of S2's dragging: down in the Above block is a ratio over 1, up under 1, and the Below block the other way about. The gains are held between −120 dB and +24 dB, Beatlayer's own limit. The two sides' gains add. The Below threshold is held at or under the Above one. Thresholds run from −70 dB to 0 dB, ratios from 0.25 to 100 (1 at first), both Beatlayer's own, with the Above threshold at −12 dB and the Below at −48 dB at first. The static curve of each of the four kinds matches this within 0.1 dB at three thresholds. (S1, S2, S7)
- **R6.** With Soft Knee on, each threshold turns gradually over a knee of 6 dB centred on it (Beatlayer's own). The distance past the threshold that R5 multiplies by the side's gain per dB is 0 from half the knee on the far side of the threshold, the plain distance from half the knee on the near side, and between them the square of the distance from the knee's far edge over twice the knee, so that at the threshold itself it is an eighth of the knee (0.75 dB). With it off the threshold is a corner. (S2, S7)
- **R7.** Each side's gain moves to its target with a first-order response: Attack is the time constant while the gain's magnitude is growing (the level passing the threshold on the Above side, falling under it on the Below), and Release while it is shrinking, each the time in which the gain closes 1 − 1/e of a step, within 5 %. Attack runs from 0.1 ms to 500 ms, Release from 1 ms to 5 s, 10 ms and 100 ms at first (Beatlayer's own). (S2, S7)
- **R8.** Time, from 10 % to 1,000 % (100 % at first, Beatlayer's own), multiplies every Attack and Release together, within 5 %. (S2, S7)
- **R9.** Amount, from 0 % to 200 % (100 % at first, Beatlayer's own), scales every ratio toward 1 as `r′ = r^(Amount/100)`: at 0 % every ratio is 1 and no band is changed, at 100 % the ratios are as set, and above it they are stronger. (S2, S7)
- **R10.** Detector chooses Peak, which follows the magnitude of every sample of the band, or RMS, which follows the root of a 10 ms mean of its square, so that a short peak barely moves it (as Compressor's detector does). The detector's level of both channels is the louder one's, so one gain acts on both. The level is floored at −120 dB. (S2, S7)
- **R11.** A band's Input gain scales its signal before the detector hears it and before its dynamics, and its Output gain scales it after; both are gains reaching −∞ dB (rule G1), up to +15 dB, 0 dB at first. A band whose Active is off passes its signal as it is: no Input, no dynamics, no Output. (S2, S5)
- **R12.** With any existing band soloed, only the soloed bands reach the sum, and the others are at −∞ dB. A solo on a band that doesn't exist (R3) counts for nothing. (S2)
- **R13.** Output is a gain on the sum reaching −∞ dB (rule G1), up to +12 dB, 0 dB at first. At −∞ the device is silent. (S2, S5)
- **R14.** With the sidechain on (routing R22 to R31), each band's detector hears the same band of the key: the key goes through a copy of the crossovers and the detector of band `b` listens to band `b` of it, after the sidechain's own gain and mix, with no Input gain on it. The key changes no audible level. With it off, each detector hears its band of the device's own signal (R10). (S3)
- **R15.** After each block the device publishes nine values through the framework's display data (device framework R35): for each band, the gain now in dB (the two sides' sum), and the band's input and output peaks since the last block in dB. Its display (device framework R36) holds, for each existing band, the Transfer Curve, the output level for each input level from −70 dB to 0 dB, a point to the dB, from the band's parameters and Amount, and two markers on it, the band's Above and Below thresholds; and with an engine publishing, the readings Gain Reduction, Input and Output for each band, in dB, a negative gain reduction being a cut. The display offers the views Time, Below and Above, in that order and starting in the first, the manual's T, B and A buttons; the view shown is view state (device framework R38). (S2, S6)
- **R16.** Every block runs without allocating, locking or blocking, and a render is bit-identical at block sizes 1, 64, 512 and 4,096, with or without a key, and run after run. The device reports no latency. (S6)
- **R17.** A set with the device saves and reopens with every parameter. (S5)
- **R18.** Every parameter can be inserted, set by ID and read back through the agent API (`device.insert`, `device.set_param`, `device.parameters`), is automatable and modulatable (rule G2), and every gain reaches −∞ dB (rule G1): the same gain of all three bands at −∞ dB silences the device, as a band's gain acts on its band alone. (S5)
