# The Arrangement View: layout, navigation, Follow, and the editing grid

- **Status:** implemented (every rule has a passing test)
- **Work packages:** WP-1.8a (this spec); WP-1.8b adds editing (clip selection, moving, resizing, splitting, the rest of the selection gestures: [arrangement-editing](arrangement-editing.md)); WP-1.5 the transport behind the playhead, the loop brace, and the scrub area; WP-1.6 and WP-1.9 the mixer controls in the track headers. In Phase 6: WP-6.38 (R18: the return tracks scrolling with the tracks, IMP-01), WP-6.49b (R19: panning with the middle button), WP-6.64 (R20 to R24: hiding the Arrangement Track Controls' In/Out, Volume and Return Tracks)
- **Parity-matrix row:** Views and workflow: Arrangement View; Interface placement and key bindings; [the improvements](../05-improvements.md), IMP-01 (return tracks scrolling with tracks)
- **Checked against:** Live 12.4.3, through the UI parity reference recorded on 10 October 2026, and the Live 12 reference manual as published that day; for R18 to R24, the manual (online edition) and the Live 12 release notes, read 20 October 2026

## The behaviour

The Arrangement View shows the song along a timeline: tracks stacked top to bottom, each a lane holding its clips where they play, with the track headers at the right. Above the lanes are the Overview (the whole arrangement in miniature, with the part in view outlined), the beat-time ruler (bars and beats, with the loop brace), and the scrub area; below them the return tracks and Main are pinned, then the time ruler in minutes and seconds. Clips show their colour, name, and, for audio, their waveform.

You move around it by zooming (the + and − keys, Cmd with the wheel or a pinch, dragging up and down in the beat-time ruler or the Overview), by scrolling (the wheel, Shift with the wheel, dragging sideways in the rulers, Cmd-Option dragging), by zooming to the selection and back (Z and X), and by fitting everything in (H and W). With Follow on, the view turns the page as the playhead plays past its edge; scrolling by hand pauses that until playback starts or stops or a click lands in the lanes. Clicking a lane places the insert marker and selects the track; dragging selects time. The editing grid, zoom-adaptive or fixed, decides where clicks snap and is drawn across the lanes.

This spec covers what WP-1.8a builds: the view, navigation, Follow, the grid, track heights and folding, and the insert marker and time selection the zoom commands work from; and, from Phase 6, the return tracks scrolling with the tracks (IMP-01), panning with the middle button, and which of the Arrangement Track Controls show. Everything in it changes only the view, never the set, and none of it is an undo step. Editing clips and the selection's other gestures are WP-1.8b's; playing from the scrub area, moving the loop brace, and the transport behind the playhead are WP-1.5's; the mixer controls are WP-1.6's and WP-1.9's.

## Rules

- **R1.** At 1600 × 1000 points the view's parts sit at layout.md's coordinates: the Overview at y 61 to 74, the beat-time ruler 81 to 99, the scrub area 99 to 115, the lanes from x 320 (the browser's right) to 1295 and from y 119 down to the pinned rows, the header column from x 1297 to the window's right margin (title, In/Out, mixer, and meter sub-columns at 1297, 1403, 1479, and 1580), Set Locator and the locator arrows at x 1299 on y 82 and 100, the grid indicator at x 1265 to 1293 near y 734, the time ruler at y 746 to 760, and the waveform zoom, Optimize Height, and Optimize Width at x 1303, 1372, and 1390 on y 748. The lanes and rulers stretch with the window; the header column keeps its 294 points. With the Overview hidden, the rulers and lanes move up into its place. (S1)
- **R2.** The lanes list the set's tracks in display order (each group's tracks under it), the regular tracks scrolling, the return tracks and Main pinned under them in 18-point rows. A new track is 88 points tall; a folded one shows its 18-point title row; a folded group hides its tracks. Under the last track is the drop area. (S1, S7)
- **R3.** Each track's header shows its title, filled with its colour, with the fold button and its name; then In/Out, the mixer's activator (the track's number, a return's letter), Solo, Arm, volume, pan, and sends, and the meter, as far as the track's height allows. Controls other packages build are drawn and named where Live puts them, disabled, and say which package brings them. Tracks take Beatlayer's own colours in turn as they're created (the document has no colours yet), and clips their track's. (S1)
- **R4.** A clip is drawn where it plays on its track: its colour, a title row with its name where the lane is tall enough, and for audio its waveform, one band per channel, from its media's peaks at the level with at least one peak per pixel. Until the peaks are ready the clip shows without a waveform; when they become ready the view redraws with it; when the cache invalidates them (the file changed) the waveform goes until new ones are built. A deactivated clip is grey. Short lanes draw the waveform in a darker shade of the clip's colour, down to a line. (S1, S11, S12)
- **R5.** The beat-time ruler numbers bars as bl-core does, so there's no bar 0 (−1 precedes 1), labelling every bar, or every 2, 4, 8 bars as the zoom narrows them, with beat (bar.beat) and sixteenth (bar.beat.sixteenth) labels when they fit; the time ruler shows minutes and seconds through the tempo map, with milliseconds when its labels are under a second apart. (S2, S9)
- **R6.** + and − zoom the time axis in and out by a factor of two around the time selection's middle if it's in view, else the insert marker if it's in view, else the view's middle; Cmd (Ctrl) with the wheel and a pinch zoom around the pointer; dragging down in the beat-time ruler or the Overview zooms in around where the drag started, up zooms out. The zoom stays between a sixteenth across 400 points and 2,048 bars across 200. A new set shows bar 1 at the left at 30 points a bar. (S2, S3, S8)
- **R7.** The wheel scrolls the tracks; Shift with the wheel, or a sideways trackpad swipe, scrolls along the song; dragging sideways in the beat-time ruler moves the lanes with the pointer, in the time ruler too, and in the Overview moves the outline; Cmd-Option (Ctrl-Alt) dragging in the lanes pans both ways. The view never scrolls before 1.1.1 or past the last track and the drop area. (S2, S3, S8)
- **R8.** Z zooms to the time selection (the whole arrangement if there's none: from 1.1.1 to the end of its last clip or loop brace, and at least 16 bars) and remembers the view before; X goes back, a step at a time. Z pressed again straight away goes back, so holding Z zooms only while it's held (Live's momentary keys). Z only scrolls the selected tracks into view; it doesn't fit them vertically. Double-clicking the beat-time ruler zooms to the selection; double-clicking inside the Overview's outline zooms out to the whole arrangement. (S2, S3, S6, S8)
- **R9.** H switches Optimize Height: every shown track shares the lanes' height, and keeps doing so as tracks come and go; off, tracks have their own heights again, and changing a height by hand turns it off. W switches Optimize Width: the whole arrangement fits across the lanes, keeping up as it grows; off returns to the view before, and zooming or scrolling sideways turns it off where the view is. The two buttons under the header column do the same. (S1, S3, S8)
- **R10.** Follow (the Control Bar's switch, the Playback menu's Options, Option-Shift-F) is on in a new set. While it's on and the song plays, the view turns the page when the playhead leaves it, putting the playhead at the left edge. Scrolling sideways by hand or clicking the beat-time ruler while the song plays pauses it; starting or stopping playback, clicking in the lanes, or clicking the scrub area resumes it; switching Follow resumes it too. There is no Follow Behavior setting yet (S10). (S4, S10)
- **R11.** The editing grid is zoom-adaptive (Widest to Narrowest, Narrow in a new set) or fixed (8 Bars to 1/32), with triplets and snapping, snapping on in a new set. Its lines lie from each bar's start, at note values that divide the bar evenly and then at whole bars, 2, 4, 8, and so on; an adaptive grid takes the finest whose lines are at least 8, 16, 32, 64, or 128 points apart (Narrowest to Widest). In the Arrangement Cmd-1 (Ctrl-1) narrows the grid, Cmd-2 widens it, Cmd-3 switches triplets (not while the grid is a bar or more), Cmd-4 snapping, Cmd-5 fixed or adaptive, the fixed grid being 1/16 in a new set; each can't go past its end. The lanes draw the grid's lines (bars more strongly) and the indicator in the lanes' lower right shows the spacing, as `1/16`, `1/8T`, `1/1` (a whole note, which is the bar in 4/4), `1 Bar`, or `4 Bars`, in brackets while snapping is off. (S1, S5, S6)
- **R12.** Clicking a lane places the insert marker at the click, on the nearest grid line while snapping is on, and selects that track; it isn't an undo step and clears any time selection. A new set has no track selected, so the marker shows only once a lane is clicked. Dragging across lanes selects the time dragged over, snapped, on the tracks dragged across. The insert marker is drawn across the selected tracks; a time selection is drawn over them. (S7)
- **R13.** The playhead is drawn through the rulers and the lanes at the transport's position; the loop brace is drawn in the beat-time ruler from the transport's loop, in one colour while the loop is on and another while it's off. Since WP-1.18 the position is the engine's, taken before each frame, and the brace the set's ([control-bar-transport.md](control-bar-transport.md), R14). (S1, S2)
- **R14.** The fold button beside a track's name folds or unfolds it, with Option (Alt) every track; with title bars selected, U toggles them, Left folds and Right unfolds; Option-U unfolds every track; + and − on a group's title bar show and hide its tracks. Dragging a track's lower edge sets its height, with Option (Alt) every track's; Option-+ and Option-− (Alt on Windows) make the selected tracks 16 points taller or shorter, and Option (Alt) with the wheel the track under the pointer. Heights stay from 18 to 800 points. (S3, S7, S8)
- **R15.** Up and Down select the track above or below; clicking a title bar selects its track and title bar, so the track-header keys apply; Esc in the Arrangement goes to the highlighted track's title bar. (S8)
- **R16.** Every action above is a registered command in the read class, with a schema and a description for a model, reachable headless through `Shell::run` (`arrangement.*` in [commands/arrangement.md](../commands/arrangement.md)); `arrangement.state` reports the view. (S11)
- **R17.** A frame builds only what's on screen: the rows found by binary search over their tops and, on each, the clips overlapping the lanes' span, from per-track lists sorted by start that are rebuilt only when the document changes; a frame in which nothing the lanes show changed draws the last frame's instances again without building them. With the benchmark scene (200 tracks, 5,000 audio clips with waveforms) the view meets the architecture's 60 fps target. (S11)

**Return tracks that scroll with the tracks (IMP-01, Beatlayer's own option; WP-6.38)**

- **R18.** While Return Tracks Scroll with Tracks is on ([mixer-view.md](mixer-view.md) R21), the return tracks' rows come after the drop area under the last track and scroll with the tracks, each at the height its pinned row has, and only Main stays pinned under the lanes; the view scrolls as far as the last return (R7's limit moving with it). With the switch off, as on a new install, R2's pinned rows are unchanged. (S13)

**Panning with the middle button (WP-6.49b)**

- **R19.** Dragging with the middle mouse button held over the lanes pans the view both ways, the content moving with the pointer, as Cmd-Option (Ctrl-Alt) dragging does (R7), within R7's limits; it places no insert marker, selects nothing and moves no clip ([view-options.md](view-options.md) R1). (S14)

**The Arrangement Track Controls (WP-6.64)**

- **R20.** View ▸ Arrangement Track Controls' In/Out, Volume and Return Tracks (M-VIEW-033a, 033b, 033d) are switches, each ticked while its part shows and all three ticked in a new set; they run `arrangement.show_in_out`, `arrangement.show_volume` and `arrangement.show_return_tracks`, read-class commands that change only the Arrangement's track headers, never the mixer's sections ([mixer-view.md](mixer-view.md) R12), and are reported in `arrangement.state`. The submenu is disabled while the Session View is in front and its items have no keys. The switches are view state: not saved with the set and no undo step. (S15, S16, S17)
- **R21.** With In/Out off, no track header shows its In/Out sub-column (L-ARR-006), pinned returns and Main included, and the title sub-column takes its width, so the header column keeps its 294 points and the lanes don't move. The way the freed width is used is Beatlayer's own. (S16, S17)
- **R22.** With Volume off, no track header shows its mixer sub-column's second and third rows (L-ARR-009: the volume field and pan; the sends); the first row's activator, Solo and Arm, and the meter column, stay where they are. What stays is Beatlayer's own. (S16, S17)
- **R23.** With Return Tracks off, the return tracks' rows aren't drawn in the lanes, pinned or scrolling (R18), Main's row stays, and the lanes take the height they leave; the returns play as before and the Arrangement's mixer still shows them as its own Return Tracks switch says. (S15, S17)
- **R24.** A part that's hidden leaves the accessibility tree and the keyboard's Tab order, and its values stay reachable in the mixer and by command. At every interface zoom the parts that show keep their places (interface-zoom R1). (S11, S16)

## Not built yet

- **Clip and track colours.** The document has no colours, so tracks take Beatlayer's palette by their ID and clips their track's colour. Choosing both needs a colour in the document and the colour commands, with the context menus (WP-1.8b) or later.
- **Warping and clip regions.** Waveforms assume the media plays from its first frame at the clip's start at its own speed, as WP-1.4 plays it until clip regions; warping (M4) will map song time to media time through warp markers, and the shader will need a piecewise mapping.
- **Waveforms zoomed in below 256 frames a pixel** draw from level-0 peaks rather than samples (waveform-peaks, "The peak data"), so a very close zoom shows steps.
- **Peak data over 128 MiB** (about 37 hours of stereo at 48 kHz in one set) doesn't fit the waveform shader's buffer; clips past it draw without waveforms.
- **The time-signature lane** (L-ARR-004) shows only when a set has signature changes; it comes with signature changes in the arrangement (WP-2.20).
