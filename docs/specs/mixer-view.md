- **Status:** partial (R1 to R23 and R40 each have a passing test, and the pieces listed under "Pending" are drawn in place and say what brings them; R24 to R39 are Phase 6's)

- **Parity-matrix row:** Mixing and routing: Mixer, with sections you can show or hide; Views and workflow: Interface placement and key bindings; [the improvements](../05-improvements.md), IMP-01 (return tracks scrolling with tracks) and IMP-25 (the Mixer's device section)

## The behaviour

Every track has a mixer strip. In the Session View the mixer sits under the clip slots, one strip per track column, the return tracks at the right and the Main track at the right edge, with the In/Out and Sends sections over the strips and the track's colour along each column's bottom. In the Arrangement View the same mixer, the same columns and sections, opens under the lanes with a row of track title bars along its top; and each track's header at the right of its lane carries a compact copy of the controls. Both mixers and the headers show the same values, because they're the same settings.

A strip has, at its top left, the peak level readout (not the volume: it reads −∞ until something plays), the pan knob under it with a small reset triangle above it, the activator (the track's number, or a return's letter), Solo, and Arm in a column at the left, and the volume fader beside the meter, with the meter's scale (0, 12, 24, 36, 48, 60 dB below 0) on which the fader's handle sits at those values. The fader runs from +6 dB to −∞, its lowest finite value −69.6 dB. Dragging it is relative: a click away from the handle changes nothing, and a drag moves the handle a point per point of the pointer's movement along that scale; while it moves the Status Bar shows `Track Volume: -21.0 dB`. Pan drags linearly, 62.5 points from the centre to either end, up to the right. Up and Down step a dB, Shift a tenth; a double-click returns it to 0 dB. The Arrangement's headers have a volume field that also takes a typed value. A MIDI track without an instrument shows only a dotted MIDI meter and its switches; a group has the activator and Solo but no Arm.

A click on the activator switches the track; a click on Solo solos it and releases every other solo, and Cmd (Ctrl) with the click adds it instead; Arm works the same way (Exclusive Solo and Exclusive Arm are on in Live's settings). With several tracks selected, changing one changes them all by the same dB. With a title bar selected, S, 0, and C solo, switch, and arm the selected tracks; F1 to F8 switch tracks 1 to 8, counting groups in the sequence.

The mixer is shown and hidden per view from View ▸ Mixer (Cmd-Option-M) and the Status Bar's mixer toggle; its sections from View ▸ Mixer Controls and the menu arrow beside the toggle. Navigate ▸ Mixer (Option-Shift-M) puts the keyboard focus in the mixer at the selected track, and Left and Right then move the selection. The track columns scroll sideways, continuously, while the returns and Main stay put.

Every one of these is a registered command: the values are `bl-model`'s mixer commands (WP-1.6), and showing, hiding, scrolling, and focusing the mixer are view commands (`mixer_view.*`). A drag is one undo step however far it goes, and the engine glides every change over 20 ms ([mixer](mixer.md) R18), so a fader moved through the UI never zippers.

Beatlayer adds two things of its own to the mixers, each off until a person turns it on (IMP-01, IMP-25). Return Tracks Scroll with Tracks lets the return tracks scroll with the other columns, for a set with more returns than fit, Main staying pinned. The Devices section, between In/Out and Sends, lists each track's devices: a click on a name shows the device, and a click on its activator switches it. Since Live 12.0 a drag with the middle mouse button held pans the mixer, as it pans every view that scrolls.

This spec covers the mixer's placement in both views, the controls in a strip and in a track header's mixer column, the gestures and keys that change them, the meters, the View, Navigate, and Status Bar items, and the agent's view commands. What the controls do to the sound is [mixer](mixer.md)'s.

## Rules

- **R1.** In the Session View at 1600 × 1000 points, track columns start at the main view's left (x 320), 94 points each, in display order, groups taking their place in it (a folded group's tracks hidden); the returns sit at the right, 94 points each, ending where the Main column (the main view's last 104 points) starts; the drop area fills the space between. Each column has its title bar at y 61 to 78. The mixer runs up from the main view's bottom: the 6.5-point colour strip, then the Crossfader section (28 points, hidden in a new set) when it shows, then the strip (123), Sends (56), and In/Out (115), so with the default sections it starts at 465.5; the 17-point status row is above it and the clip slots take what's left. Hiding a section gives its height to the slots; hiding the mixer gives the slots the whole height. (S1, S2, S6)
- **R2.** In the Arrangement View the mixer is the Session mixer's columns and sections, at the same widths and x positions, with a 17-point row of track title bars along its top; its sections start at the height the Session mixer's do, so it runs from that row to the main view's bottom. The rulers, the lanes, the pinned returns and Main, the grid indicator, the time ruler, and the buttons under the header column are laid out above it as without it; hiding it gives them their height back. (S7)
- **R3.** A strip's controls sit where Live 12.4.6 has them, from the strip section's top-left corner: the peak level readout (x 8 to 42, y 13 to 24), which shows the meter's peak in dB (`-inf` with nothing playing) and isn't the volume; the pan knob centred at (25, 46), 25 points across, with its reset triangle above it (in Split Stereo Pan Mode two more controls sit beside it, one placing each input channel, [mixer](mixer.md) R29); the activator (x 15 to 36, y 64 to 85), Solo (y 91 to 102.5), and Arm (y 106 to 119) in a column; the fader's handle column (x 46 to 59) over the meter's scale; the meter (x 60 to 71, y 11 to 119); the scale's labels 0, 12, 24, 36, 48, 60 (x 74 to 86, at y 23.5, 44.5, 64.5, 84.5, 99, 111). The strip has no volume number field. Group tracks have the activator and Solo, no Arm; returns show their letter and have no Arm; a MIDI track without an instrument (in Beatlayer, one with no devices, since there are no instruments yet) shows only a dotted MIDI meter, where the meter sits, and its switches; Main has the peak readout, pan, the fader, and the meter, and its cue volume and Solo/Cue button are pending (Phase 3), to sit where a track's activator and Solo do. A deactivated activator shows its number on grey. (S2, S6, S9)
- **R4.** The Arrangement's track headers carry the same values in their mixer column (L-ARR-009): the activator, Solo, and Arm in its first row, the volume field and pan in its second (the split controls beside the pan in Split Stereo Pan Mode, [mixer](mixer.md) R29), the sends in its third ([routing](routing.md) R18), as far as the track's height allows; the meter fills the header's meter column. Every control there works as the strip's does (R6 to R10). (S3, S11)
- **R5.** The In/Out section shows its rows in Live's order (From, input type, input channel, Monitor with In, Auto, and Off, To, output type, output channel; a group's and a return's Audio To and output; Main's Cue Out and Main Out), and the Sends section a knob per return in two staggered rows (the In/Out rows spread evenly over the section's 115 points, the knobs staggered over its 56), labelled with the return's letter (dimmed on returns; on Main, the returns' Pre/Post switches). Since WP-2.19 their controls work as [routing](routing.md) R16 to R19 say, a group's and a return's Audio To with its channel under it; what other packages bring is drawn, named, and disabled, saying the package. (S2, S6)
- **R6.** Volume reads in dB to one decimal (`0.0`, `-6.0`, `-inf`), pan as `C` or a number from 1 to 50 and `L` or `R`. The fader's range is +6 dB to −∞, with −69.6 dB its lowest finite value; its handle sits on the meter's scale (+6 dB at 13 points down the strip, 0 dB at 23.5, −12 at 44.5, −24 at 64.5, −36 at 84.5, −48 at 99, −60 at 111, −∞ at 118.5, straight between). A drag on the fader or the header's volume field is relative: a click away from the handle changes nothing, and a drag moves the handle a point per point of vertical drag along that scale (its travel from −∞ to +6 dB is 105.5 points), up raising the volume, measured from where the drag began and kept within the fader's ends; while it moves the Status Bar shows `Track Volume: <value> dB`. A drag on pan moves it linearly, its whole travel from the centre to either end in 62.5 points (0.8 steps of 50 per point), up to the right. Shift with a drag moves three times more finely. Up and Down step 1 dB and Shift with them 0.1 dB, from −∞ Up landing on −69.0 dB, below −69.6 dB going to −∞, and stopping at +6.0 dB. A double-click, or Delete, returns volume to 0 dB, and pan to the centre (as does the triangle above the pan knob). The header's volume field takes a typed value (digits, Minus, Period; Return sets it, Esc drops the value and the keyboard focus), the keys going to it rather than to shortcuts while it has the keyboard. Every change runs `mixer.set_volume` or `mixer.set_pan`; a drag is one undo step named `Change "Track Volume"` or `Change "Track Panning"`. (S4, S5a, S10, S11, S12)
- **R7.** A click on the activator runs `mixer.set_mute` with the other state. A click on Solo runs `mixer.solo_exclusive` (Exclusive Solo is on), or, on the only soloed track, `mixer.set_solo` off, and on a track soloed among others it leaves that track soloed alone; with Cmd (Ctrl) held it runs `mixer.set_solo` with the other state, leaving the rest. Arm works the same way with `mixer.arm_exclusive` and `mixer.set_arm`. Each click is one undo step. (S9, S10, S11)
- **R8.** With several tracks selected, a change to a control on one of them changes it on every selected track that has it, in the same undo step: volume, by keys, drags, or a typed value, moves every selected track by the same dB, each kept within range (a silent track stays silent, and the others go silent when the changed track does); pan moves each by the same amount; a click on the activator, Solo, or Arm sets every selected track's switch to the clicked one's new state, and an exclusive click releases only the tracks that aren't selected. A change to a track that isn't selected changes only that track. (S5, S10)
- **R9.** With a track's title bar selected, S toggles its Solo (`mixer.set_solo`), 0 its activator (`mixer.set_mute`), and C its Arm (`mixer.set_arm`), on every selected track at once, as one undo step, each set to the opposite of the first selected track's state; F1 to F8 toggle the activators of tracks 1 to 8, numbered as the mixer numbers them, groups included, wherever the focus is. (S9, S11; [command-bindings](command-bindings.md) R5)
- **R10.** Each strip's and each header's meter shows the engine's latest peak and RMS levels for the track, left and right side by side, on the fader's scale (shared, as observed), both levels drawn; while any meter shows a level the window redraws every frame, and it stops once every meter has fallen to silence. (S6, S10, S12)
- **R11.** View ▸ Mixer (Cmd-Option-M, Ctrl-Alt-M) and the Status Bar's Mixer View toggle run `mixer_view.toggle`, which shows or hides the mixer of the view in front, and the item is ticked while it shows. Each view has its own switch: in a new set the Session View's mixer shows and the Arrangement's is hidden. The switches aren't saved with the set. (S7, S11)
- **R12.** View ▸ Mixer Controls has In/Out (Cmd-Option-I), Sends (Cmd-Option-S), Volume, a separator, Track Options, Crossfader, Performance Impact, a separator, and Return Tracks (Cmd-Option-R); In/Out, Sends, Volume, and Return Tracks run `mixer_view.in_out`, `mixer_view.sends`, `mixer_view.volume`, and `mixer_view.return_tracks`, which show or hide that part of the mixer of the view in front, each view keeping its own (R37), each ticked while it shows in that mixer and all four ticked in a new set, and Crossfader runs `mixer_view.crossfader`, which shows or hides the Crossfader section — the tracks' A and B assign buttons and Main's crossfader ([mixer](mixer.md) R23) — ticked while it shows and unticked in a new set (observed hidden); the Status Bar shows the crossfader's centre as `C`, as it does pan's. The Mixer Config Menu arrow beside the Status Bar's toggle opens the same choices. Track Options runs `mixer_view.track_options`, which shows or hides the Track Options section of both mixers — each track's delay with its ms/samples switch and its Keep Monitoring Latency switch ([mixer](mixer.md) R28, R30) — ticked while it shows and unticked in a new set (observed hidden). Performance Impact runs `mixer_view.performance_impact`, which shows or hides the Performance Impact section of both mixers — under each strip, the six segments that light up from the left with the track's share of the set's processing time ([mixer](mixer.md) R31) — ticked while it shows and unticked in a new set (observed hidden). View ▸ Arrangement Track Controls has In/Out, Volume, Track Options, a separator, and Return Tracks, with no keys; it's disabled while the Session View is in front, and its In/Out, Volume, and Return Tracks items show and hide the track headers' parts ([arrangement-view.md](arrangement-view.md) R20 to R23), Track Options working as the Session menu's does. (S8)
- **R13.** Navigate ▸ Mixer (Option-Shift-M, Alt-Shift-M), with the Arrangement focused, runs `mixer_view.focus`, which shows the Arrangement's mixer if it's hidden and puts the keyboard focus in it at the selected track's column (the first track's if none is selected), scrolling it into view; Left and Right then move the selection to the previous or next track, the focus with it; Up and Down do nothing there. With Outline View in Focus on, as it is in a new install, the focus is outlined ([accessibility](accessibility.md) R7). (S8)
- **R14.** The mixer's track columns scroll sideways, continuously (a column can be partly in view), with the wheel, Shift with the wheel, or a sideways trackpad swipe over the mixer, and `mixer_view.scroll`; the returns and Main stay put, and the scroll never goes before the first track or past the last. Each view's mixer scrolls on its own. (S8)
- **R15.** Every control in a strip, in the Arrangement's headers, in the Status Bar's mixer controls, and in the Crossfader section is in the accessibility tree with a role, a name (`Peak Level`, `Track Volume`, `Track Pan` (`Main Pan` on Main), each send its return track's name, `Track Activator`, `Solo`, `Arm Recording`, `Track Meter`, `MIDI Meter`, the Crossfader section's `Crossfade A` and `Crossfade B` and `Crossfader`, and so on) and a value; volume and pan are sliders with increment and decrement for screen readers; a pending control is disabled and says what brings it; each column is a group named for its track. (S13)
- **R16.** Every action above is a registered command reachable through the agent API: the values through `mixer.set_volume`, `mixer.set_pan`, `mixer.set_mute`, `mixer.set_solo`, `mixer.solo_exclusive`, `mixer.set_arm`, `mixer.arm_exclusive`, `mixer.set_crossfader`, and `mixer.set_crossfade_assign` (edit class, [mixer](mixer.md) R21, R23); the view through `mixer_view.state`, `mixer_view.toggle`, `mixer_view.in_out`, `mixer_view.sends`, `mixer_view.volume`, `mixer_view.crossfader`, `mixer_view.return_tracks`, `mixer_view.fold_chains`, `mixer_view.scroll`, and `mixer_view.focus`, which change only the view, never the set, and are in the read class. (S13)
- **R17.** A frame builds only the columns in view: with 200 tracks a frame draws the dozen or so columns on screen and reads their meters, whatever the set's size. (S13)
- **R18.** A fader dragged through the UI changes the sound without zipper noise: a scripted drag on a strip's fader, run frame by frame through the window and the shell as the app runs it, gives a series of `mixer.set_volume` commands which, rendered through the engine one 60 Hz frame apart, glide from value to value at no more than a glide's slope a frame (mixer R18). The render is a golden reference. (S12, S13)
- **R19.** At every interface zoom from 50% to 200%, every mixer row keeps its place relative to the others and scales by the zoom, and nothing overlaps that doesn't at 100% (interface-zoom R1). (S12)
- **R20.** Exclusive Solo and Exclusive Arm behave as on: Live has both on (S9), and their switches belong to the Record, Warp & Launch settings page, which another package builds. (S9, S10)
**Return tracks that scroll with the tracks (IMP-01, Beatlayer's own option; WP-6.38)**

- **R21.** View ▸ Mixer Controls has a switch, Return Tracks Scroll with Tracks, straight after Return Tracks (M-VIEW-032g), and View ▸ Arrangement Track Controls has the same switch straight after its Return Tracks (M-VIEW-033d); both run `mixer_view.return_tracks_scroll` and are ticked while it's on. It's one switch for the Arrangement, the Session View and both mixers, off on a new install, so the returns stay pinned as in Live. It's an application setting, kept between launches, not saved with the set, and not an undo step. Its name and places are Beatlayer's own. (S14, S16)
- **R22.** While it's on, the return tracks' columns in both mixers come after the track columns and the drop area and scroll sideways with them, and only Main stays pinned at the right; the drop area keeps its place between the last track and the first return and is at least one column (94 points) wide. When every column fits in view, the mixer is laid out exactly as with the returns pinned (R1, R2). Scrolling (R14, `mixer_view.scroll`) reaches as far as the last return. Hiding the return tracks (R12) hides them either way. The drop area's least width is Beatlayer's own. (S14)

**Panning with the middle button (WP-6.49b)**

- **R23.** Dragging with the middle mouse button held over either mixer pans its columns sideways, as the wheel scrolls them (R14), the content moving with the pointer ([view-options.md](view-options.md) R1). (S15)

**The Devices section (IMP-25, Beatlayer's own; WP-6.40)**

- **R24.** View ▸ Mixer Controls has a switch, Devices, straight after Performance Impact (M-VIEW-032f), unticked on a new install, that shows or hides the Devices section in the mixer of the view in front (`mixer_view.devices`), as the other sections' switches do (R12, R37): view state, not saved with the set, and no undo step. Its name and place are Beatlayer's own. (S14, S16)
- **R25.** The Devices section sits between the In/Out section and the Sends section, in every column, 112 points tall: room for eight rows of 14 points. Showing it takes its height from the clip slots in the Session View, and adds it to the Arrangement's mixer, as the other sections do (R1, R2). Its place and size are Beatlayer's own. (S14)
- **R26.** Each column's Devices section lists its track's top-level devices in chain order (MIDI effects, the instrument, audio effects), a rack as one row with its name: audio, MIDI and group tracks, returns and Main alike. A row holds the device's activator at its left, a small square lit while the device is on, and then the device's name as its title bar shows it (device-chains R22), cut with an ellipsis where it doesn't fit. A track with no devices shows an empty section. The rows follow the chain as it changes, on the next frame. (S14, S17)
- **R27.** A column with more devices than fit scrolls its rows with the wheel over its section, on its own, from the first row to the last; the scroll is view state. (S14)
- **R28.** Clicking a row's name selects its track and that device and shows it in the Device View, opening the detail area if it's hidden, as `device.select` does; a device that floats in its own window (IMP-14) brings that window to the front. Double-clicking a plug-in's name opens its editor window, or closes it, as its window button does (L-DEV-010). (S14, S17)
- **R29.** Clicking a row's activator runs `device.set_enabled` with the device's other state, one undo step named as a click on its title bar's activator is (device-chains R6), and changes only that device, whatever tracks are selected. (S14, S17)
- **R30.** Assistive technology reads each column's Devices section as a list named for its track's devices, each row as a switch named `Device Activator` with the device's name and state, and a button with the device's name. (S13)
- **R31.** `mixer_view.state` reports whether the Devices section shows and whether the return tracks scroll; `mixer_view.devices` and `mixer_view.return_tracks_scroll` are in the read class (R16). The devices a section lists, and whether each is on, are the query tool's `track` devices with their `enabled`. (S13)

**The extended meters, the mixer's height and column widths (WP-6.65)**

- **R32.** Dragging the mixer's top edge (I-DRAG-030), or the strip section's top border, in either view sets the height of that view's strip section (Volume); the sections above it move with it, and the clip slots or the lanes give up or take back the height. The section is 123 points tall in a new set (R1); it goes down to 64 points and up to the height that leaves the Session View its status row and one clip-slot row, or the Arrangement its rulers and one track row. Below 100 points the pan knob and its triangle hide and the activator, Solo and Arm move closer together. Each view's height is its own, view state: not saved with the set and no undo step. The heights are Beatlayer's own. (S18, S19)
- **R33.** While the strip section is at least 200 points tall, the extended meters show: each meter has tick marks across it every 6 dB from 0 to −60 dB; a volume field under the fader reads the track's volume as R6 does and takes a typed value as the Arrangement header's field does (R6), running `mixer.set_volume`; and a click on the peak level readout (R3) sets it back to the current peak. The 200-point threshold, the ticks' spacing and the field's place are Beatlayer's own. (S18)
- **R34.** Every meter marks its highest recent peak with a line that holds for 2 seconds and then falls to the current peak, at every height of the section. (S19)
- **R35.** While the extended meters show, a column at least 120 points wide labels each tick mark with its value in dB beside it; a narrower one shows only R3's labels. The width is Beatlayer's own. (S18)
- **R36.** Dragging the right edge of a track's title bar in the Session View (I-DRAG-004) sets that track's column width, from 40 to 400 points, 94 in a new set: in the grid, in both mixers and in the Arrangement's mixer, which share the Session View's columns (R2). With Option (Alt) held it sets every track's. Return columns take widths the same way, and so does the Main column, whose left edge widens it and shows the scene tempo and signature fields ([session-view.md](session-view.md) R26; I-DRAG-005). A column narrower than 94 points keeps its clip slots' launch buttons, the activator, Solo, Arm, the fader and the meter, and hides the pan knob, the peak level readout and the scale's labels. Widths are saved with the set; changing one isn't an undo step. The limits and the order things hide in are Beatlayer's own. (S18, S19)
- **R37.** Each view's mixer keeps its own sections. View ▸ Mixer Controls' section items, the Status Bar's menu beside the mixer toggle, and their keys change the mixer of the view in front, and each item is ticked as that mixer shows its section; a new set's Session and Arrangement mixers each show In/Out, Sends, Volume and Return Tracks. (S19)
- **R38.** `mixer_view.set_height` (a view, a height in points), `mixer_view.set_column_width` (a track or every track, a width in points) and `mixer_view.reset_peak` (a track or every track) are registered commands in the read class, and `mixer_view.state` reports each view's height and sections and each column's width. The section commands of R12 take an optional `view`, the view in front when it's left out. (S13)
- **R39.** The extended meters' volume field is a spin button for assistive technology named `Track Volume` with its value, and the peak level readout a button that resets it, named `Peak Level` with its value. At every interface zoom the heights and widths scale by the zoom (R19). (S13)
- **R40.** Each strip draws a gain-reduction bar (IMP-25) in both views' mixers: a thin 2-point bar beside the meter, from x 71.5 to 73.5 and y 11 to 119 of the strip section's corner, filled from the top on the meter's own 0-to-60 dB scale and drawn only while something reduces. It shows the most reduction any compressor or limiter on the track reported for the last block, in dB (zero for none), gates excluded: devices inside racks at any depth count, and a track with none reads 0. Each node reports its own reduction after every block (`Node::gain_reduction`, the device framework's `NativeDevice::gain_reduction`): the Compressor, the Glue Compressor, Multiband Dynamics and the Limiter report theirs, and a CLAP plug-in reports through CLAP's gain-adjustment metering extension where it implements it ([clap-hosting](clap-hosting.md) R24); the engine publishes the track's reading — the most reduction of any of them — with its meters, and the view redraws while it moves as the meters do (R10). The bar is a meter in the accessibility tree named `Gain Reduction` with its value in dB, and `mixer_view.state` reports each track's reading under `gain_reduction`. It needs no command: it follows what the devices report. (S14)
