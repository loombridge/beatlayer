# Mixer: volume, pan, the activator, sends, solo, and meters

- **Status:** implemented (every rule has a passing test)
- **Work packages:** WP-1.6; the crossfader (R23) since WP-3.17; Cue mode, the Cue Out, and the Cue Volume (R24 to R27) since WP-3.18; routing's part of solo (R10) and every track's inputs (R1) since WP-2.19 ([routing](routing.md))
- **Parity-matrix row:** Mixing and routing: Audio, MIDI, and Main tracks; Group tracks, nested; Return tracks and sends; Solo and cue modes
- **Checked against:** the Live 12 reference manual, read 10 October 2026, the UI parity reference's observations of Live 12.4.3 (10 October 2026), and the Constant Power crossfader law measured on Live 12.4.3's own meters on 15 October 2026.

## The behaviour

Every track ends in a mixer. Its signal comes in (a group's tracks, a return's sends, or, for Main, every top-level track and return), runs through its device chain, and then through its mixer: pan, volume, and the Track Activator, the switch that silences the track when it's off. Then it goes on to its group or to Main. A track's sends feed copies of its signal to the return tracks, each at its own level, taken after the mixer or, if the return's Pre/Post switch says Pre, before it. Each track shows its level on a peak meter and an RMS meter.

Solo lets you hear part of the song. While any track is soloed, only the soloed tracks, the groups that hold them, and the tracks inside soloed groups are heard. Solo in Place, an Options menu item, decides whether the return tracks stay audible, so a soloed track is heard with its reverb. Soloing a return plays everything sent to it and nothing else. A click on a Solo switch releases every other solo unless Cmd (Ctrl) is held or Exclusive Solo is off in Settings; Arm works the same way.

Beatlayer's engine applies every mixer change on the frame it's sent for and glides to it over 20 ms, so a fader move never clicks or steps. The document holds the settings; the engine works out who solo silences each time the solo switches change, without building a new program.

This spec covers the engine and the document. It leaves to other specs and packages: the Mixer view and the track headers' controls, with the Exclusive Solo and Exclusive Arm settings and the Cmd-click that chooses between `mixer.set_solo` and `mixer.solo_exclusive` (WP-1.9, WP-1.7); routing, track-to-track audio, unlimited returns, and the send knobs as controls ([routing](routing.md), WP-2.19); sends on return tracks (with the track context menu) and devices' sidechains ([routing](routing.md), WP-2.19b); Split Stereo Pan Mode (Phase 5); recording, which the Arm switch will start (WP-2.13); automation of these parameters (WP-2.12); and track delay (Phase 5).

## Rules

- **R1.** A track's signal is the sum of its inputs, then its device chain, then its mixer (pan, volume, and the Track Activator), and goes on to its group or Main, or where its routing sends it ([routing](routing.md)). A group's inputs are its tracks, a return's are the sends to it, Main's are the tracks and returns routed to it, and an audio track's its clips and, while it monitors, its input ([routing](routing.md) R10 to R12). (S1, S3, S4)
- **R2.** Volume is a linear gain on the track's signal: 1 is unity (0 dB) and 0 is silence (−∞ dB, rule G1). A fader at −6.02 dB halves the amplitude to within 0.1%, and every other level is the gain it names to the same accuracy. (S1, S2; [rule G1](../05-improvements.md); WP-1.6's acceptance)
- **R3.** Pan follows the constant-power law along sine curves: the left channel is scaled by √2·sin((1 − pan)·π/4) and the right by √2·sin((1 + pan)·π/4), for pan from −1 (left) to 1 (right). At the centre both are 0 dB; fully to one side that side is +3 dB and the other silent; the power, left squared plus right squared, never changes. As Live's Stereo Pan Mode does, pan scales each channel of a stereo signal by its own side's gain, so it balances a stereo signal and places a mono one. (S1, S6)
- **R4.** A track at unity volume and centre pan, with nothing soloed, passes its signal through to the bit, and summing tracks is plain addition, so a song with every mixer at rest renders as it did before the mixer existed. (S6)
- **R5.** A track whose activator is off has a silent output and silent post-fader sends; its pre-fader sends still carry its signal. (S1, S4)
- **R6.** A group sums its tracks' outputs (after their mixers), then runs the sum through its own chain and mixer. Groups nest. (S3)
- **R7.** Audio, MIDI, and group tracks have one send to each return track. A send's level runs from −∞ dB, where every send starts, to 0 dB; a send at −∞ dB adds nothing. Return tracks and Main have no sends in Beatlayer until the track context menu adds the returns' (which Live keeps off by default). (S4, S8)
- **R8.** Each return has a Pre/Post switch, Post in a new return. Post sends take each sending track's signal after its pan, volume, activator, and crossfade gain; Pre sends take it before them, straight after its device chain. (S4, S8)
- **R9.** Returns run after every track that sends to them, and feed Main. Main sums the top-level tracks and the returns, then runs its chain and its mixer (volume and pan). Main has no activator and no Solo. (S4, S8, S11)
- **R10.** In Solo mode (R24), while no track is soloed, solo silences nothing. While any track is soloed, the *solo path* is every soloed track, every group that holds a soloed track at any depth, and every track inside a soloed group. An audio, MIDI, or group track outside the path is silent: its output and its sends, pre-fader ones included. (S5, which says tracks that feed other tracks stay heard; since WP-2.19 "feed" covers routing into another track as well as a group's members: [routing](routing.md) R15)
- **R11.** While only audio, MIDI, or group tracks are soloed, the returns stay audible if Solo in Place is on and are silent if it's off. Either way they carry only the sends of tracks in the solo path. (S5)
- **R12.** Soloing a return silences the output of every track outside the solo path, other returns included, whether Solo in Place is on or off, while every send to the soloed return stays live, so it plays everything sent to it. (S5)
- **R13.** A soloed track keeps its own volume and pan. A soloed track whose activator is off stays silent, with its post-fader sends. (S5; the activator: Beatlayer's choice)
- **R14.** Solo never silences Main. (S5, S8)
- **R15.** `mixer.set_solo` solos or unsolos one track and leaves the others, as a Cmd-click (Ctrl-click) or a click with Exclusive Solo off does; `mixer.solo_exclusive` solos one track and unsolos every other in one undo step, as a click with Exclusive Solo on does. `mixer.set_arm` and `mixer.arm_exclusive` do the same for Arm. The UI picks between them by the setting and the modifier (WP-1.9). (S1, S9, S10)
- **R16.** Solo in Place is a song setting, saved with the set and changed as one undo step: on in a new set, and in a set saved before Beatlayer had it. A set saves it only when it's off, and saves a return's Pre/Post switch only when it's Pre and a send only when it's above −∞ dB, so a set without them saves as before. (S5, S10)
- **R17.** The engine's solo resolution says which groups hold a soloed track, for the half-lit Solo switch, and which tracks are heard directly. (S3)
- **R18.** Every change to a mixer gain (volume, pan, the activator, the crossfader's position, a send's level, and solo opening or closing an output or a send) takes effect on the frame it's sent for and glides there in a straight line over 20 ms, from wherever the gain is on that frame. A change that arrives during a glide starts a new one from there. So the gain never steps: no zipper noise and no clicks. (S7, which has Live working volume out for every sample; the glide's length is Beatlayer's choice)
- **R19.** Every track, returns and Main included, has a peak meter and an RMS meter per channel, measured after its mixer (pan, volume, the activator, and the crossfade gain) and before solo, and published after every block with the playhead. A peak shows at once and falls 20 dB in 1.7 seconds (11.8 dB a second, the fall-back of a broadcast peak programme meter); the RMS meter averages the squared signal with a 300 ms time constant, so a steady sine reads 3 dB below its peak. (S1, S2; the ballistics and where the meter taps are Beatlayer's choices)
- **R20.** The mixer's output depends only on the song and the frames changes are sent for, not on the block size: a render is the same to the bit at any block size. (Beatlayer's architecture, "Offline rendering")
- **R21.** Every mixer action is a registered command (D17) in the `edit` class, runnable by ID with JSON arguments, each one undo step: `mixer.set_volume`, `mixer.set_pan`, `mixer.set_mute` (the activator), `mixer.set_solo`, `mixer.solo_exclusive`, `mixer.set_arm`, `mixer.arm_exclusive`, `mixer.set_send`, `mixer.set_send_tap`, `mixer.set_solo_in_place`, the crossfader's `mixer.set_crossfader` and `mixer.set_crossfade_assign` (R23), and the cue's `mixer.set_cue_mode` (no undo step, R24) and `mixer.set_cue_volume` (R27). The default keys stay as [command-bindings.md](command-bindings.md) R5 gives them. (S10, S11)
- **R22.** Volume, pan, the activator, solo, Solo in Place, send levels, the crossfader's position, and a track's crossfade assignment reach the engine as parameter changes, with no new program and every node keeping its state. Only the Pre/Post switch, which moves where sends tap, and Cue mode (R26), build a new program, into which every mixer and send migrates. (Beatlayer's architecture, "Compiling the document into a program")
- **R23.** Every track but Main is on the crossfader's A side, its B side, or neither — one assignment at a time, set by its A and B assign buttons — and a track on neither passes through at every position. Main's crossfader mixes the sides by the default Constant Power curve: a track on A takes the curve's value at the position p (0 at the A end, 0.5 the centre, 1 at the B end; the fader runs straight between them), a track on B its mirror at 1 − p. The curve, fitted to the measured points: `cos(π/2·p)` up to p = 0.75, then a straight line in decibels through (0.78, −9.9 dB), (0.83, −14.9), (0.92, −37.0), and (0.95, −52.7), then a straight line in amplitude to silence at p = 0.96, where it stays; the near end is unity and the centre is −3 dB on each side. The gain sits in the track's mixer, after pan, volume, and the activator and before the meters, so the track's meter and its post-fader sends follow it. The position is a set setting, saved with it; like the other mixer gains it takes effect on the frame it's sent for and glides there in a straight line over 20 ms (R18), and the position and an assignment both reach the engine as parameter changes, with no new program (R22). `mixer.set_crossfader` sets the position (0 to 1, anything else refused) and `mixer.set_crossfade_assign` a track's assignment (Main refused); each is one undo step. (S12; Beatlayer ships Constant Power alone)
- **R24.** Cue mode is a set setting: the Main strip's Solo/Cue button, Options ▸ Solo Switches and Cue Switches, and `mixer.set_cue_mode` set the same one thing, off (Solo mode) in a new set and not written to a saved set while off. It is no undo step, and undoing or redoing another step leaves it as it is. It can be on only while Main's Cue Out and Main Out share no output channel ([routing](routing.md) R16): `mixer.set_cue_mode` refuses `enabled` true with an overlap, the button and both Options items are dimmed, and a routing change that makes an overlap later leaves the setting but ends Cue mode in effect, so the set plays in Solo mode (Solo Switches ticked) until the outputs part again. (S10, S13)
- **R25.** In Cue mode every track's Solo switch is its Cue switch, which `mixer.set_solo` sets as it sets Solo, and the Solo switch shows a headphones icon in place of "S". Cue switches silence nothing: the activators alone decide what Main plays, and R10 to R14 apply to Solo mode alone. Switching the mode keeps each track's switch, so a track soloed in Solo mode is cue-switched in Cue mode and the reverse. Every track but Main, group tracks and returns included, has one. (S5, S13; that the two modes share one switch per track isn't observed, to observe)
- **R26.** The Cue Out carries the sum of the cue-switched tracks, each taken after its device chain and before its volume, pan, crossfade gain, and activator, so its fader, pan and activator don't change it. The sum goes through the Cue Volume and plays on the device channels Main's Cue Out names, as a track on Ext. Out does ([routing](routing.md) R3), with the cue-switched tracks' delay compensation lined up, and takes no part in Main's meters. Cue mode on or off, or moving the Cue Out, builds a new program in which every node migrates; a Cue switch and the Cue Volume reach the engine as parameter changes and glide over 20 ms (R18), with no new program. The cue is the same to the bit at any block size (R20). (S13; the glide and the compensation are Beatlayer's choices)
- **R27.** The Cue Volume is Main's Preview/Cue Volume knob: a linear gain from 0 (−∞ dB) to 1.995 (+6 dB), 1 (0 dB) in a new set and where the knob resets to, a set setting saved only when it differs from 1. `mixer.set_cue_volume` sets it as one undo step named Change "Preview Volume", and a drag of the knob is one step. The Status Bar shows "Preview Volume: −6.0 dB" while it is dragged; Up and Down step it a dB as the volume fader's keys do (to observe). (S13)
