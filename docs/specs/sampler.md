## The behaviour

Simpler is Beatlayer's sampler: an instrument that plays one audio file, named in the set by its content hash and held by the media store, the way an audio clip names its file. A note plays a voice: a region of the sample, transposed from C3 by the note's own pitch, through an amplitude envelope, at a volume and pan a note's velocity scales.

The region is the part of the sample between two flags, Start and End, which every mode plays inside. Its Classic mode is polyphonic: S Start and S Length place the part a voice plays as shares of the region, the loop repeats that part's end with a crossfade from the loop's start, and the amplitude envelope is an ADSR whose stages shape the note. Its One-Shot mode is monophonic and plays the region straight through: a Trigger switch (on by default) plays it to the end whatever the note does, and with Trigger off a note ending fades it out; a Fade In and a Fade Out shape its ends. Its Slicing mode plays one slice per note: the Slice By chooser places the slices on the sample's transients, at beat divisions, at equal divisions, or leaves their making to the sample display (Manual places none automatically), and a note plays the slice its key names, C3 first, each slice running to the next one's start, and a Playback chooser lets slices sound one at a time (Mono), together (Poly), or on through the rest of the region (Thru). Both Classic and One-Shot share the pitch controls (Transpose, Detune, plus Glide and Portamento), the sample's Gain, the output's Volume and Pan, the Velocity > Volume amount, and the Voices and Retrig controls Classic uses.

A voice's filter runs between the sample's Gain and the output's Volume: the manual's low-pass, high-pass, band-pass and notch responses at 12 or 24 dB per octave, a Resonance, a Key amount that moves the cutoff with the played note, and a filter envelope with its own ADSR and an amount. The pitch envelope shapes each voice's pitch modulation through an ADSR envelope and an Amount control, moving pitch by up to 48 semitones each way. The LFO runs per voice: the manual's six waveforms, freely at 0.01 to 30 Hz or synced to a division of the set's tempo, rising over an Attack, restarted by a note at an offset or following the free-running phase, its rate moved by the played note, and four amounts — Volume, Pitch, Pan and Filter.

A Simpler preset ([device chains](device-chains.md) R19) holds parameter values only; the sample is the set's. A sample is decoded where a node is made, on the compiler's thread (an offline render does it on its render thread), and whole, into memory: the media store's cache shares it with every reader of the same file, and the sample's memory follows its length. A clip's first source is opened by the loader thread instead, so it can't hold up the compile queue ([audio-clips](audio-clips.md) R17); moving a device's sample there too is later work.

This spec covers the sampler's playback, envelopes, voices, slicing, filter, LFO, warp controls and pitch envelope. It leaves to [the device framework spec](device-framework.md) everything every native device has (parameters, smoothing, layout, automation, presets); the sample's display, with the region's flags, the loop brace and the slices, and its edits (Crop, Reverse, Normalize Volumes and IMP-28's) are [the multisampler spec's](multisampler.md) R54 to R63 (WP-5.15).

## Rules

- **R1.** Beatlayer ships Simpler, an instrument device type of its own layout, described like any native device: every parameter has a descriptor and a place in the layout. Its Mode chooses Classic (the default), One-Shot or Slice. (S1, S2)
- **R2.** A sampler plays the media its device names by content hash, decoded by the media crate at the engine's rate (S4); a device with no sample is silent. A note on starts a voice at the region's start, and the voice reads the sample at the pitch its key, the Transpose (whole semitones, −48 to 48, default 0) and the Detune (cents, −50 to 50, default 0) give, with C3 playing the sample at its own pitch. (S1, S4, S5)
- **R3.** The region is the part of the sample between its Start and End flags (R22), and every mode plays inside it. In Classic mode, S Start (0 to 100 %, default 0) and S Length (0 to 100 %, default 100), both shares of the region, place the part a voice plays: from S Start's share of the region for S Length's share of it, stopping at the region's end if that comes first (S Start 50 % and S Length 25 % play its third quarter), both read at the note that starts a voice. With the loop off, a voice plays that part once and ends; with the Loop switch on, it plays the part's end into its start, repeating the loop brace, which is the part's last S Loop Length share (0 to 100 %, default 100). One-Shot and Slice mode play the region from flag to flag (R10, R13), and S Start, S Length and S Loop Length change nothing there. The three are the parameters with the IDs Start, Length and Loop Length had. (S1, S14)
- **R4.** With the loop on, the Fade control's share of the loop's length (0 to 100 %, default 0) is a constant-power crossfade from the loop's end into its start: over that share the loop's end fades out as its start fades in, and the wrap resumes past the head the crossfade already played, so a looped sample doesn't click and keeps its level through the wrap. The crossfade takes at most half the loop, so a Fade past 50 % plays as 50 % (Beatlayer's own: the loop's end and start would overlap otherwise). (S1)
- **R5.** The amplitude envelope is an ADSR: Attack (0 to 20 s, default 0), Decay (0 to 20 s, default 0), Sustain (0 to 100 %, default 100) and Release (0 to 20 s, default 0.1 s). A note on starts it at Attack from its current level, a note off releases it from its current level over Release, and a voice ends when the envelope reaches zero. With no sample, no voice starts. (S1)
- **R6.** A note's velocity scales the voice's level by the Velocity > Volume amount (0 to 100 %, default 0): at 0 the velocity doesn't matter, at 100 % the level follows it in a straight line. Pan (−50 to 50, default 0) places the voice at equal level in both channels at 0. (S1)
- **R7.** Gain is the sample's gain stage (a gain, top +24 dB, default 0 dB, reaching −∞ dB: rule G1) and Volume the output level (a gain, the same range and default). Both are automatable like every parameter (rule G2). (S1, S3)
- **R8.** Voices (1 to 16, default 8) is how many voices Classic plays at once. A note that would make more fades the oldest sounding voice out over a short steal fade (5 ms, Beatlayer's own) instead of cutting it, so stealing doesn't click. With Retrig on, a note already sounding on the same key is faded out the same way. (S1)
- **R9.** With Glide on, a voice starts from the pitch of the last note played and slides in a straight line in semitones to its own pitch over Glide Time (0 to 1000 ms, default 0.1 s). Glide's mode is Glide (monophonic: the new note takes over the sounding voice, keeping its position and envelope) or Portamento (polyphonic: each note is a voice of its own). (S1)
- **R10.** In One-Shot mode the sampler is monophonic: a note fades any sounding voice out over the steal fade and starts its own, playing the region from its Start flag to its End flag (R3, R22). With Trigger on (the default) a note's end is ignored and the voice plays to the region's end; with Trigger off, a note's end fades the voice out over Fade Out. Fade In (0 to 2000 ms, default 0) reaches the voice's level from silence at the region's start, and Fade Out (the same range, default 0) fades the voice out ending at the region's end. (S1)
- **R11.** The sample comes into a sampler through `device.load_sample`: a file imported into the set's media store by content hash, kept by the device (as slot 0 of its `media` list, which the [eight-slot drum sampler](eight-slot-drum-sampler.md) fills to eight), saved with the set, and played back when the set is reopened. Dropping a sample on a sampler's Device View frame does the same. (S4, S6)
- **R12.** Every audio-thread operation allocates nothing, locks nothing and blocks nothing; the sample's decoded audio is shared with the set's other users of the same file and freed off the audio thread. (S2, S4)
- **R13.** Mode's Slice choice plays one note per slice, as many at once as Playback allows (R23) (S7). The slices come from Slice By (R14), inside the region between the flags (R3, R22): each has a start and an end, a slice runs from its start to the next slice's start, and the last to the region's End flag. A note plays the slice its key names — C3, Beatlayer's own base note (the manual says only that the slices play chromatically), is the first slice and each key above it the next — and keys outside the slices play nothing. One-Shot's rules carry over per slice: Trigger on (the default) plays the slice to its end (under Thru, to the region's end) whatever the note does, and with Trigger off a note's end fades it out over Fade Out; Fade In and Fade Out measure as R23 says. A slice plays at the sample's own rate with the instrument's Transpose and Detune, and Glide doesn't move it. There are up to 64 slices (the manual's maximum). (S7)
- **R14.** Slice By places the slices (S7). **Transient:** a peak-hold envelope with a short fall (20 ms, Beatlayer's own) and an onset wherever it rises past a threshold the Sensitivity sets (0 to 100 %, default 50 %): the threshold lies that share of 40 dB below the loudest the envelope gets, so a higher Sensitivity finds more slices. The device reads the sample for onsets a share of the region in each block, never all of it in one, so a long sample's slices are ready some blocks after it loads or Sensitivity changes, and the old slices play until then (Beatlayer's own). **Beat:** a slice every Division sixteenth notes (1 to 16, default 4) from the region's start; when warped, the sixteenth notes follow the sample's warp tempo, and when unwarped, the set's tempo. **Region:** the Regions parameter's number (1 to 64, default 16) of equally long slices. **Manual:** no automatic slices; slices are placed, moved and deleted by hand in the sample display. (S7, S10)
- **R15.** The filter's switch (off by default) sends a voice through it; off bypasses it entirely. It is [the instruments' filter](instrument-filter.md) (R1 to R13 there): its Type is low-pass, high-pass, band-pass, notch or Morph at 12 or 24 dB per octave; its Circuit is Clean (the default), OSR, MS2, SMP or PRD on the types each plays; Freq is logarithmic from 20 Hz to 20 kHz (default 1 kHz), Res runs from 0 to 100 % (default 0), Drive from 0 to 24 dB (default 0 dB) for the non-Clean low-pass, high-pass and band-pass, and Morph from 0 to 100 % (default 0 %) for the Morph type. It runs per voice, after the sample's Gain and before the output Volume (the manual: Gain before the filter, Volume after it). A set saved before WP-8.3 opens on Clean with Drive at 0 dB and renders as it did (instrument filter R15). (S1, S8)
- **R16.** The played note moves the cutoff through the Key amount (0 to 100 %, default 0): an octave per octave at 100 %, with C3 its centre. The filter envelope is an ADSR (0 to 20 s, 0 to 100 %, 0.1 s release; Beatlayer's defaults, the amplitude envelope's) whose Amount (−100 to 100 %, default 0) moves the cutoff up to four octaves each way at full. The manual's Velocity control for the cutoff is not built. (S1, S8)
- **R17.** The LFO's switch (off by default) runs one LFO per voice (S9). Its Wave is Sine, Square, Triangle, Saw Down, Saw Up or Random (a new value each of its cycles). Its rate is free, 0.01 to 30 Hz (default 1 Hz), or, with Sync on, a Division (1 to 16 sixteenth notes, default 4) of the set's tempo. It reaches full intensity over its Attack (0 to 2000 ms, default 0) from a note. With Retrigger on, a voice's LFO restarts at the Offset phase (0 to 100 %, default 0) on its note; with it off, a voice starts at the device's free-running phase and Offset does nothing (the manual). Key (0 to 100 %, default 0) speeds a voice's LFO up with its pitch — twice as fast an octave above C3 at 100 %. Its amounts (all −100 to 100 %, default 0) move the voice: Volume scales its level by one plus the amount times the LFO, never below silence; Pitch bends it up to twelve semitones each way; Pan sways it across the stereo field; Filter moves the filter's cutoff up to four octaves each way. (S1, S9)
- **R18.** Simpler provides Warp controls: a Warp toggle switch (off by default), the Warp Mode chooser (Beats, Tones, Texture, Re-Pitch, Complex, Complex Pro; default Beats), mode-specific settings (Beats preserve division and loop mode, Tones grain size, Texture grain size and flux, Complex Pro formants and envelope), the sample tempo in BPM (1 to 1000 BPM, default 120), and scale tempo (×2 and ÷2). When Warp is on, the sample plays at the set's tempo through its warp map — one marker at its start, on the sample's tempo the BPM control sets, until auto-warp's map ([warping](warping.md) R33 to R35) replaces it — following the tempo map from the note's start as a launched Session clip does, so a held note follows a tempo change. Every warp mode merged so far plays as Re-Pitch does, as they do for a launched Session clip ([warp modes](warp-modes.md)); when Warp is off, the sample plays conventionally at its own rate. Each warp setting is saved with the set and is one undo step. (S10)
- **R19.** The pitch envelope shapes each voice's pitch modulation through an ADSR envelope: Attack (0 to 20 s, default 0), Decay (0 to 20 s, default 0), Sustain (0 to 100 %, default 100) and Release (0 to 20 s, default 0.1 s). Its Amount (−48 to +48 semitones, default 0) scales how far the envelope moves the pitch from the note's base pitch: at +48 the peak pitch is shifted up by four octaves. Each parameter is automatable and modulatable (rule G2). (S1, S11)
- **R20.** Slices can be managed by hand in the sample display in Slice mode: double-clicking the waveform adds a slice at the clicked frame; double-clicking an existing slice removes it; pressing Delete or Backspace removes the selected slice; and dragging a slice moves it to a new frame. Each action is one undo step. Manual slices are drawn in `c.accent.0` and automatic slices in `c.text_dim.0`. In Slice mode, manual slices play chromatically from C3 in order, and with Slice By set to Transient, Beat or Region they split the automatic slices where they fall. (S7, S10)
- **R21.** On a track that plays the set's tuning ([tunings.md](tunings.md) R12, R14), a Classic or One-Shot voice reads the sample at the pitch R2 gives with its key's tuned pitch in place of the key, C3's twelve-step pitch still playing the sample at its own pitch; Transpose, Detune, Glide (R9), the LFO's Pitch (R17) and the pitch envelope (R19) add to it as they add to a key without a tuning. In Slice mode the key chooses the slice (R13) and the tuning changes nothing. The filter's Key (R16) and the LFO's Key (R17) follow the tuned pitch (Beatlayer's own). (S12, S13)
- **R22.** The region's Start and End flags are positions in the sample, counted in frames from its first frame as its slices are (R20), with Start before End; they bound what every mode plays (R3) and where slices fall (R13, R14). A sample loaded into the device (R11), or one that replaces it, gets a region of the whole sample. Dragging a flag in the sample display ([the multisampler spec](multisampler.md) R54) and `device.set_sample_region` (`edit`: the device, and `start` and `end` in frames, either left out staying where it is; a flag past the sample's end comes back to it, and a Start at or after End is refused) set them, one undo step per drag or command. They're saved with the set and read back as the `device` query's `region`: `start`, `end`, and the sample's length in frames. The flags are the sample's markers, as its slices and warp settings are, not automatable parameters (Beatlayer's reading of the manual). An edit that changes the sample's length moves them with the audio they mark, as it moves a multisampler layer's markers ([the multisampler spec](multisampler.md) R56). (S1, S14)
- **R23.** In Slice mode, Playback (a choice parameter: Mono, the default, Poly or Thru) chooses how slices sound together. **Mono**: one slice at a time, a note fading the sounding slice out over the steal fade (R8) and starting its own. **Poly**: slices sound together, as many as Voices allows, the oldest faded out past it, and with Retrig on, a slice already sounding is faded out when its key plays again, as in Classic (R8). **Thru**: one slice at a time, as Mono, but a slice plays on past its own end through the rest of the region to the End flag. Under Mono and Poly, Fade In and Fade Out measure from each slice's start to its end; under Thru, from the played slice's start to the region's end. Voices and Retrig act under Poly only. Changing Playback leaves voices already sounding as they are (Beatlayer's own). (S7, S14; Mono as the default is Beatlayer's own)
- **R24.** A set saved before the region existed, in an older schema version, opens and plays as it did (S15). A Simpler in Classic mode keeps its Start, Length and Loop Length values as S Start, S Length and S Loop Length, with its region the whole sample, so it plays the same part of the sample, and the automation, clip envelopes, macro mappings and modulation on those parameters act as before. A Simpler in One-Shot or Slice mode takes the part its Start and Length chose as its region, with S Start at 0 % and S Length at 100 %: the saved shares are kept until the sample's length is known and then become the frames playback rounded them down to, so the same frames play. Automation, clip envelopes, macro mappings or modulation on a One-Shot or Slice Simpler's Start or Length can't move its flags; the opened set names those devices in the Status Bar, once (Beatlayer's own). Saving writes the new form. (S14, S15)
- **R25.** Simpler's sample swaps for the next or previous similar sample from its display's Previous Similar and Next Similar buttons and with Cmd-Right and Cmd-Left, as [sound similarity](sound-similarity.md) R21 and R22 say: every parameter keeps its value, and the region (the Start and End flags, R22) resets to the new sample's whole length, with S Start, S Length and S Loop Length kept as shares of it; slices and warp markers are worked out again. (S16)
- **R26.** The display's menu holds Show Similar Files, Save as Similarity Reference and Return to Reference; the reference saves with the set and Simpler's presets and reads back in the query tool's `device`. (S16)
