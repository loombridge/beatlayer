# Tension: a string excited by a bow, hammer or plectrum, with its damper, termination, pickup and body, and the Filter/Global tab

- **Status:** draft
- **Work packages:** WP-8.22 (this spec, R1 to R21). The string is a waveguide of [the physical models](physical-models.md); voices, portamento, unison and the keyboard's tuning are [the instruments' voice section](instrument-voices.md); the MPE routings ride on [the instruments' modulation matrix](instrument-modulation.md); the tabs are [the device framework](device-framework.md)'s pages (R41 to R45)
- **Parity-matrix row:** Instruments: Collision, Tension, Electric (Tension)
- **Checked against:** the Live 12 reference manual (online edition), read 30 October 2026 (chapter 31, section 31.12: 31.12.1 to 31.12.3; 31.12.4 holds tips, not behaviour); the bowed-string papers in the sources table

## The behaviour

Tension models a string instrument. An exciter sets the string moving: a bow that keeps it sounding while the key is held, a hammer that strikes it once, a hammer that bounces against it, or a plectrum that plucks it. A damper can rest on the string and stop it; a finger and a fret terminate it; a pickup hears it at a position along it, and a body of a chosen type and size colours it. A vibrato moves its pitch. The second tab holds a multimode filter with its envelope and LFO, the MPE section, the keyboard's tuning and voices, portamento and unison.

The string is a waveguide; the bow, hammer, plectrum, damper and finger are each a model that meets it at a position, after the bowed and struck string models published in the acoustics literature. Every law here is Beatlayer's own reading of the manual's controls on those models.

## Rules

- **R1.** Beatlayer ships Tension (`beatlayer.tension`), an instrument listed under the browser's Instruments label, with factory presets of Beatlayer's own. Its body is in pages (device framework R41), tabs String and Filter/Global, with Volume (−∞ to 0 dB) on both. Every section but String and Keyboard has its switch; a section switched off does nothing and costs nothing. (S1, S6)
- **R2.** The exciter, with its switch (on in a new device), is Bow, Hammer, Hammer (bouncing) or Plectrum (Hammer by default). With it off, only the damper sets the string moving, when it lands or lifts. (S2)
- **R3.** Bow drives the string at its position by a friction curve against the bow's speed relative to the string there: the friction force is the bow's Force times μ(Δv) = μd + (μs − μd) × v₀ / (v₀ + |Δv|), Friction setting μs / μd from 1 to 4, solved each frame at the bow point, so the string sticks and slips and keeps sounding while the note is held; the bow's speed is Velocity times the note's velocity. The solution is clamped, so no setting locks it up or makes it grow without bound, and a combination that cannot make the string move is silent. (S2, S5; the curve's constants are Beatlayer's own)
- **R4.** Hammer strikes once: a mass (Mass) at Velocity meets the string at its position through a contact force k × c^2.5 for a compression c, k set by Stiffness, and leaves when the string pushes it back. Hammer (bouncing) keeps the hammer near the string after the strike, so it strikes again as the string swings back, until the bounces die away. Plectrum displaces the string at its position by Protrusion, stiffened by Stiffness, and lets go when the string's tension passes the plectrum's hold, once per note. Damping (not for Bow) adds a loss at the exciter's position while it touches. (S2, S5; the contact law and constants are Beatlayer's own)
- **R5.** The exciter's Position runs from the termination (0 %) to the string's middle (50 %); with Fix. Pos off, it is that share of the string at every key; with Fix. Pos on, it is that share of the string at C3 as a fixed distance, so on shorter strings (higher keys) it sits further towards the middle. Velocity, Position and the type's own controls have Vel and Key amounts (−100 % to 100 %). (S2; the reading of Fix. Pos is Beatlayer's own)
- **R6.** The damper, with its switch (on in a new device), is a mass on a spring meeting the string at its Position (with Fix. Pos): Mass, Stiffness and Damping set its press and its loss; it lifts at the note's start and lands at its end; with Gated on (the default) Velocity sets how fast it lands and lifts, and with Gated off it rests on the string only between notes. Mass, Stiffness, Velocity and Position have Key amounts. (S2; Beatlayer's own reading)
- **R7.** The string is the physical models' String waveguide (physical models R8, R9) tuned to the note: Decay (with Key) sets its decay while the note is held, Ratio (0 to 100 %) makes it decay that much faster after the note's end (0 % the same), Inharm stretches its partials and Damping (with Key) makes its high harmonics die faster. (S3, S6)
- **R8.** The string plays its note within 1 cent across the keyboard, measured with every section but the exciter and the string off; the body, the pickup and the filter change its spectrum and never its tuning. (S3, S6)
- **R9.** The vibrato, with its switch (off in a new device), moves the pitch by a sine at Rate (0.1 to 20 Hz) by up to a semitone at Amount 100 %, after Delay and fading in over Attack (0 ms to 10 s each); < Mod (0 to 100 %) adds the mod wheel's share to Amount; Error (0 to 100 %) varies each note's Rate, Amount, Delay and Attack by up to ±50 % from the device's seed. (S3; the ranges are Beatlayer's own)
- **R10.** The termination, with its switch (on in a new device), is a finger pressing the string against a fret at the string's end: Finger Mass (with Vel and Key) and Finger Stiff set how much of the wave the finger lets through and absorbs, Fret Stiff how sharply the fret reflects it; at its softest the end damps the high harmonics, at its stiffest it reflects them all. (S3; Beatlayer's own reading)
- **R11.** The pickup, with its switch (on in a new device), hears the string at Position (0 to 50 %, from the termination to the middle): a position near the end is brighter and thinner, near the middle fuller, as the string's modes have their nodes and antinodes there (physical models R7). With it off, the string is heard at its middle. (S3, S6)
- **R12.** The body, with its switch (off in a new device), filters the string through a body of Type (Piano, Guitar, Violin, Cello or Bass) and Size (XS, S, M, L or XL, M by default; each step larger lowering its resonances by a fourth): a modal bank of the type's 16 body resonances (physical models R1's modal resonators), its Decay (10 ms to 2 s), Str/Body (−100 % string only to 100 % body only) and Low Cut and High Cut. (S3; the types and their resonances are Beatlayer's own)
- **R13.** The filter, with its switch (off in a new device), is Analog's multimode filter (LP12 to Formant24, [Analog](analog.md) R7 and R8) with Freq and Res, each moved by the filter's LFO, envelope and the key by amounts from −100 % to 100 % while the envelope and LFO are switched on. The filter envelope has Attack (with Vel), Decay, Sustain (with Vel) and Release; the filter LFO plays Sine, Triangle, Rectangle, S&H (stepped) or Noise (gliding) at Rate in hertz or synced, after Delay and fading in over Attack. (S4, S6)
- **R14.** The MPE section: Pressure and Slide each have two destinations with amounts (chosen slots of the matrix), whose destinations are Off and each continuous control of the exciter, damper, string, vibrato, pickup, body and filter; activity lights show the newest note's values; Pitch Bend (0 to 24 semitones, 2 by default) and Note PB (0 to 48, 48 by default) are the bend ranges (instrument voices R14). (S4)
- **R15.** The keyboard: Octave (−3 to +3), Semi (−12 to +12), Detune (−50 to +50 cents in one-cent steps), Stretch and Error (instrument voices R17), Voices (1 to 32, 8 by default) and Priority (High, Low or Last, Last by default; instrument voices R3). (S4)
- **R16.** Portamento, with its switch (off in a new device), glides over Time (0 ms to 10 s) with Legato and Prop. (instrument voices R8, R9). (S4)
- **R17.** Unison, with its switch (off in a new device), stacks 2 or 4 copies at Detune (up to ±50 cents) and Delay (up to 20 ms) (instrument voices R11 to R13). (S4)
- **R18.** Every control is a parameter, automatable and modulatable (rule G2), and Tension's banks hold every one. (S6)
- **R19.** Sixteen voices with every section on render at 48 kHz inside the device budget. (S6)
- **R20.** The output is the same at any block size and run after run; nothing on the audio thread allocates, locks or blocks. (S6)
- **R21.** A bow held on a note sustains at a steady level, within 3 dB over ten seconds once settled; a hammer's and a plectrum's notes decay. (S2, S5)
