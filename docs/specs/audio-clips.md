- **Checked against:** the Live 12 reference manual (online edition), read 11 October 2026, and again for WP-1.4b on 11 October 2026 (chapters 2, 6, and 8); the UI parity reference's Settings rows (L-SET-018); and Live 12.4.6 on macOS, observed 11 October 2026 (S15): the Record, Warp & Launch page, and trimming and resizing a MIDI clip. ## The behaviour

An audio clip sits on an audio track's lane in the Arrangement, at a musical position and for a musical length. While the song plays, the clip plays its audio file from the clip's Start marker, so the moment it sounds follows the tempo map. With its Loop switch off, it plays until its End marker or until the clip ends in the Arrangement, whichever comes first. With the loop on, it still starts at the Start marker, plays into the loop brace, and repeats the brace for as long as the clip lasts. The clip's Gain scales its audio, and its fades bring it in from silence at its left edge and take it back to silence at its right edge, so a clip that starts or stops mid-waveform doesn't click. The Create Fades on Clip Edges setting gives new clips short fades of 4 ms.

A clip with Warp off plays unwarped, as Live plays it: at its file's own speed whatever the tempo, after the file is converted to the engine's sample rate. Its Start, End, and loop brace are positions in the file, in seconds, which is how Live shows them for an unwarped clip. A warped clip follows the set's tempo through its warp map instead, which [warping.md](warping.md) specifies (WP-4.2); only a warped clip can loop, and the rules below on looping apply to an unwarped clip only as a set saved before warping holds one.

The engine plays each audio track's clips with one clip player at the head of the track's device chain. The compiler hands the player the track's clips as one message (node data), so making, moving, or editing a clip never builds a new program.

Clips play their files from disk as they play, as Live's do (the disk streaming spec): a file of ten seconds or more streams, a shorter one is held in memory, so eight ten-minute stems cost the streams' buffers, a few megabytes, rather than the 1.8 GB the decoded files would take. Opening a clip's file reads the disk, so the engine does it on a loader thread of its own: a clip whose file isn't open yet stays silent for the few milliseconds that takes, and Play and Stop never wait behind a file being loaded. A clip keeps its stream through every edit that leaves it on its track.

Trimming a clip's left edge, or splitting it, keeps the audio where it plays by moving the clip's Start marker, as Live does: the part that starts later starts as much later in its file, and a looped clip's marker wraps inside its loop brace. The Create Fades on Clip Edges setting, On in a new install as Live's is, is on the Settings window's Record, Warp & Launch page.

This spec covers Arrangement playback of audio clips. It leaves to other specs and packages: warping, and with it looping as Live allows it, only on warped clips (WP-4.1 and WP-4.2, [warping](warping.md)); what happens when a clip is placed over another (WP-1.8b; see R8); the Session View's Clip Fade switch, whose fade is signal-dependent from 0 to 4 ms (S3, Phase 3); how streams buffer, preload, and count underruns, which is the disk streaming spec's; clip pitch, reverse, RAM mode, and high-quality interpolation (Phase 4); the disk overload indicator (the app shell's); Normalize; and MIDI clips, whose markers come with WP-2.1 (R16 keeps their offset until then). Fade curves, crossfades between adjacent clips, the fade handles and their limits, Create Fade and Delete Fades are R23 to R31 (WP-6.51).

## Rules

- **R1.** An audio clip plays only while the song plays, from the frame its start falls on through the tempo map (the nearest frame) to the frame its end falls on, which it doesn't play. Before its start and from its end on, it adds nothing. A deactivated clip adds nothing. (S5, S8)
- **R2.** A clip plays unwarped: its audio runs at the file's own speed, one frame of the file (at the engine's rate) to each frame of the song, whatever the tempo. A file at another sample rate is converted to the engine's rate first, so it keeps its pitch and speed. Across a tempo change, a clip's audio runs on unbroken, and only where its start and end fall depends on the tempo map. (S6, S7)
- **R3.** With its loop off, a clip plays its file from the Start marker, frame for frame, until the End marker or the clip's end, whichever comes first, and is silent from the End marker to the clip's end. The markers are positions in the file in seconds; a new clip's Start is the file's start and its End the file's end. Past the end of the file, a clip is silent. The file is aligned so its Start marker falls at the clip's start in time, and each song frame plays the file frame nearest that alignment: the clip's first frame plays the Start marker's frame, or a frame beside it when the clip starts between frames and the marker doesn't fall on one. (S2; the alignment is what lets the parts of a split clip play exactly what the whole did, R16)
- **R4.** With its loop on, a clip begins at its Start marker, wherever the loop brace is, and plays on into the brace; whenever it reaches the brace's end, it goes on from the brace's start on the very next frame, for as long as the clip lasts. The End marker doesn't apply while the loop is on. The brace is a start and an end in the file, in seconds; a new clip's brace is the whole file. The Start marker must be before the brace's end. Live doesn't allow looping an unwarped clip, so the Loop switch comes on only with Warp ([warping.md](warping.md) R14); a looped unwarped clip is a state an older set holds, which plays as this rule says. (S2)
- **R5.** A clip's gain scales its audio as a linear gain: 1 is 0 dB, the default, and 0 is −∞ dB. At 0 dB with no fades, a clip adds its file's samples unchanged, to the bit. The document accepts any finite gain of 0 or more. (S4, S10)
- **R6.** A clip's fade-in rises in a straight line, in amplitude, from silence at the clip's start to full level its length later; its fade-out falls in a straight line from full level to silence at the clip's end. A fade of length 0 is no fade. Fades are measured from the clip's edges in the Arrangement, whatever its markers and loop, and they multiply the clip's gain. A fade longer than the clip stops at the clip's far edge, and where a fade-in and a fade-out overlap, the lower of the two applies. (S1; the overlap is Beatlayer's choice, since Live's handles don't allow one, S1)
- **R7.** Create Fades on Clip Edges gives a new clip's edges fades of 4 ms, and is what a deleted fade goes back to while the setting is on; with the setting off, a new clip has no fades and deleting a fade removes it. `bl_model::EDGE_FADE_SECONDS` is the 4 ms, and `AudioData::with_edge_fades` gives a clip's data those fades. The setting is a preference, not part of the set (R22). The clips the browser adds to the Arrangement (WP-1.10) are the new clips built so far, and a recorded clip follows the setting too ([recording](recording.md) R4, WP-2.13); Delete Fades and the automatic 4 ms crossfades between adjacent clips are R26 and R29, and a split's parts and a duplicate keep the data they were made from. (S1, S3, S15)
- **R8.** Clips that overlap on one track each play, and their audio adds. Live never leaves clips overlapping (placing a clip over another trims the one beneath, which WP-1.8b's editing commands will do), so this is Beatlayer's rule for a state Live doesn't reach. (S9)
- **R9.** A mono file plays on both channels; a stereo file plays its left channel on the left and its right on the right; a file with more channels plays its first two. In a mono engine, a clip plays its file's first channel. (S7)
- **R10.** Wherever playback starts, jumps, or wraps at the Arrangement loop, a clip plays from the point in its file that the song position gives, so a start or a locate in the middle of a clip plays the middle of the clip, on the exact frame. (S8)
- **R11.** The clip player's output depends only on the song, its media, and the frames the transport acts on, not on the block size: a render is the same to the bit at any block size. (Beatlayer's architecture, "Offline rendering")
- **R12.** Making, deleting, moving, or resizing a clip, and changing its markers, loop, gain, fades, or activator, reaches the engine as the clip player's data, with no new program. The player and its data survive a recompile; a tempo change, which recompiles, sends the player its clips placed under the new tempo map. (Beatlayer's architecture, "Compiling the document into a program")
- **R13.** The audio thread never frees sample data or a stream: the player's old clip list, with the sources the new list didn't keep, goes to the drop thread when new data replaces it, and the player's sources go with the program that holds the player. (Beatlayer's architecture, "Real-time rules")
- **R14.** A clip whose media the engine can't find (missing, changed, or undecodable) plays silence, and the compiler reports its media, so a render says which files are missing. An engine on a device learns it when its loader tries the file (R18), and reports it then. (S7; [audio-import.md](audio-import.md), R9)
- **R15.** Every clip edit is a registered command (D17) in the `edit` class, one undo step, runnable by ID with JSON arguments: `clip.set_region` (the Start and End markers), `clip.set_loop` (the loop brace), `clip.set_loop_enabled` (the Loop switch, which refuses to come on for an unwarped clip), `clip.set_gain`, and `clip.set_fades`. Each refuses a MIDI clip and a value out of range, and leaves the document as it was. An audio clip's data is saved with the set; each new field is written only when it differs from a new clip's, so a set saved before WP-1.4 loads unchanged and plays its clips whole. `clip.set_region`, `clip.set_loop`, and `clip.set_loop_enabled` keep the markers in order (the End marker after the Start marker, and the Start marker before the brace's end while the loop is on), as setting them by hand in Live does; a trim or a split can leave them otherwise (R16). (D17; Beatlayer's architecture, "Project files and interop")
- **R16.** Trimming an audio clip's left edge, or splitting it, moves its Start marker, as Live moves a MIDI clip's (S15): a part that starts later than the clip did starts later in its file by the time between the two starts under the tempo map (seconds, since an unwarped clip's markers are times in its file), and a left edge dragged earlier moves the marker earlier, down to the file's start, where the edge stops. With the loop on, a marker moved to or past the loop brace's end wraps back inside the brace by whole lengths of it, as Live's does (S15). Nothing else in the clip's data changes, and an audio clip has no offset into its contents (`Clip::offset` stays 0, and a clip that has one is refused). Each part plays, at every song frame, the file frame the whole clip played there (R3's alignment), exactly, with two exceptions that follow from an unwarped clip's markers being seconds: a looped part's wrap in seconds and the player's wrap in whole frames differ when the brace's ends don't fall on frames at the engine's rate, by the brace's fraction of a frame for each time round (0.43 frames at 44.1 kHz for a brace of 0.3123 s), so such a part drifts from the whole by up to that much per wrap; and a brace that runs to the end of the file has a length the set doesn't record, so its marker can't wrap and goes on past the brace's end, where the part plays as far into the repetitions. Phase 4's warped clips, whose markers are beats, wrap exactly. With the loop off, a part that starts past the End marker plays silence, as the whole clip did there. A MIDI clip's Start marker moves too in Live (S15), but MIDI clips have no markers until WP-2.1, so a MIDI clip's offset changes instead (the arrangement editing spec, R16). A set saved in schema version 1, whose split and trimmed audio clips kept an offset in ticks, loads through a migration that moves each offset into the clip's Start marker the same way, wrapping it the same way, so it plays as it did. (S2, S11, S15)
- **R17.** Each clip reads its file through a source of its own (S13): a file of 10 seconds or more streams from disk, read a little ahead of where the clip plays, and a shorter one is held in memory, shared by every clip that plays it. Memory follows the streams' buffers, not the files' lengths: eight 10-minute stereo stems at 48 kHz, 1.8 GB decoded, play in about 5 MB. Audio a stream hasn't read in time plays as silence and is counted as an underrun, for the disk overload indicator (S13, R3). (S13, S14)
- **R18.** The compiler thread never opens or decodes a file. A running engine opens each clip's source on a loader thread: until the source is open, the clip isn't in its track's list and plays silence, and when it is, the compiler sends the track its clips again. Play, Stop, and every other message to the audio thread go through as soon as they would with nothing loading. An offline render (export, golden renders, `bl-cli render`) opens sources on its own thread and reads streams in blocking mode, so it plays every clip from its first frame and renders exactly what decoding the files whole gives. (S13, S14)
- **R19.** A clip keeps its source while it stays on its track and plays the same media: a new list for the track says which clips keep theirs, and the player moves those sources across from its old list at the block boundary, which allocates nothing. A clip moved to another track, or given other media, opens a new source there. Each streamed source has preloaded the points a jump lands on: the clip's start, its loop's start while the loop is on, and where the Arrangement loop starts if that's inside the clip; an edit that moves one asks for it again. (S13, S14)
- **R20.** While the song is stopped, every clip at the song position has its source ready to play from there, so Play, or a locate made while stopped, doesn't wait for the disk. (S13)
- **R21.** A clip's start, its loop wrapping, and a jump in the song (a locate, or the Arrangement loop wrapping) play its file from the new point on their exact frame, as R4 and R10 say: the clip player asks its source to cut, not to crossfade, and a jump to a point nothing preloaded plays silence until the disk has read it (S13, R5). (S13)
- **R22.** Create Fades on Clip Edges is an On/Off switch on the Settings window's Record, Warp & Launch page, labelled as Live labels it: the fourth row of the Warp/Fades section, below the Record section's seven rows and above the Launch and Tap Tempo sections (S12, S15, L-SET-018). The page draws every one of Live's rows in Live's order under Live's headings (Record, Warp/Fades, Launch, Tap Tempo); the ones no package has built are pending, drawn quiet, unusable, and announced to assistive technology with the package that brings them. The switch runs `settings.set_create_fades_on_clip_edges`, an `edit`-class command with an `on` argument, which agents run too, and is kept with the other preferences for the next start, never in the set or its undo history. It's On in a new install, as Live's was (S15), and preferences saved before the setting existed read as On. (S1, S12, S15)

**Fades and crossfades in the Arrangement (WP-6.51)**

- **R23.** Each fade of an audio clip has a curve, from −1 to 1, beside its length; 0, a new clip's, is R6's straight line. At a fraction *x* of the way through it, a fade-in's gain is *x*^*p* and a fade-out's (1 − *x*)^*p*, where *p* = 4^(−curve), so a positive curve bows the fade towards full level and a negative one towards silence. The law is Beatlayer's own. (S16)
- **R24.** Two audio clips on one track that meet, the earlier ending where the later starts, can share a crossfade: a span from a time before their boundary to a time after it, at least one of them past the boundary, over which the earlier clip's fade-out and the later clip's fade-in both run, with one curve. Across the span each clip plays what it would play there with its edge moved to the span's far end, as resizing it would (arrangement editing R7 and R16; R3 and R4 here): the earlier clip plays on past its end and the later one from before its start, each silent where it has no audio there. With the curve at 0 the two gains add to 1 at every frame, so the two parts of a split clip, crossfaded, play what the whole did. A span is in seconds about the boundary's place in the song, as fades are (R6). MIDI clips have no crossfades. Beatlayer's own: what each clip plays across the span. (S16, S17)
- **R25.** Every edit of a fade stops at its limits: a fade, a crossfade's part included, takes in no point where its clip goes round its loop brace (R4) and reads nothing before the start of its file; a clip's fade-in ends no later than its fade-out begins, so a crossfade reaches no further into either clip than that clip's other fade allows; and a crossfade lies between the two clips' other edges. (S16)
- **R26.** With Create Fades on Clip Edges on (R22), an edit that leaves two audio clips meeting on a track with no crossfade between them (placing, moving, resizing, splitting, pasting, recording, comping) gives them a crossfade of 4 ms, 2 ms each side of their boundary, with a curve of 0, in the same undo step, in place of their own fades at the boundary. A crossfade keeps its span and curve while its two clips go on meeting, wherever their boundary moves (both moved together, or one resized over the other); an edit that parts them ends it, and each edge it joined takes back a fade of 4 ms, or none with the setting off. With the setting off, clips that come to meet get no crossfade unless one is made (R27, R28). Changing the setting changes no clip. Beatlayer's own: the 2 ms each side, and what parting leaves. (S16, S17, S18)
- **R27.** While the pointer is over an audio clip on a lane at least 40 points tall, and over every clip while F is held (S-ARR-010; over an automation lane in Automation Mode, S-ENV-004 and I-HOLD-002), each of the clip's fades shows its handles (I-DRAG-017): one at its peak, where it reaches full level; one at its outer end, where it reaches silence, at the clip's edge or a crossfade's far end; and a curve handle between them. Dragging a peak handle along the clip sets the fade's length. Dragging the outer handle of a fade at an edge where another audio clip meets this one, out over that clip, makes or changes a crossfade over the span from the fade's peak to the handle, the peak staying where it is; at an edge no clip meets, the outer handle stays at the edge. Dragging a curve handle up or down sets the fade's curve, or the crossfade's. Handles snap to the grid while snapping is on, Cmd (Alt) doing the opposite. Each drag is one undo step, `Change Fade`, and Esc cancels it. Clicking a handle selects it, and a dotted line across the clip shows how far it can go (R25). Beatlayer's own: the lane height, the snapping, and that an outer handle moves only over a clip that meets its own. (S16, S19)
- **R28.** Create Fade (Cmd+Option+F, S-ARR-008; Create ▸ Create Fade, M-CREATE-010) acts on the time selection's audio tracks: a clip whose start the selection holds, and not its end, gets a fade-in from its start to the selection's end; a clip whose end it holds, and not its start, a fade-out from the selection's start to its end; and two audio clips meeting inside the selection get a crossfade over it. With clips selected instead, each boundary where two of them meet gets R26's 4 ms crossfade, whatever the setting. Each is kept within R25's limits; a clip the time selection holds whole, or holds neither edge of, is left as it is. The menu item reads Create Fade In/Out when the selection gives a fade, Create Crossfade when it gives a crossfade, and Create Fade, disabled, when it gives neither. One undo step, `Create Fade` or `Create Crossfade`. Beatlayer's own: a clip held whole. (S16, S17, S19)
- **R29.** Delete with a fade handle selected deletes that fade: with Create Fades on Clip Edges on it goes back to 4 ms, and a crossfade to R26's 4 ms crossfade; with it off the fade goes, and a crossfade with it, leaving its two clips meeting with no fade there. Delete Fades (Cmd+Option+Delete, S-ARR-009) does the same to every fade and crossfade of the selected clips. One undo step, `Delete Fade(s)`. (S1, S16, S19)
- **R30.** `clip.set_fades` sets each fade's length and curve. `arrangement.set_crossfade` (a track, the boundary where two of its audio clips meet, the span's lengths before and after it, and the curve) makes or changes a crossfade, refused where the clips don't meet or past R25's limits; `arrangement.delete_crossfade` deletes one; `arrangement.create_fade` does R28 on a time selection or clips, given or selected; `arrangement.delete_fades` does R29 on clips. Each is `edit`, one undo step, reading back through the query tool's `clip`, field `data`: each fade's length and curve, and a crossfade's span and the clip it meets. Fades and crossfades are saved with the set, written only where they differ from a new clip's, so a set saved before them opens with straight fades and no crossfades. From the keyboard, fades are made and deleted with Create Fade and Delete Fades, which the keymap binds; the handles themselves don't reach a screen reader, as in Live ([accessibility](accessibility.md)). (D17)
- **R31.** The engine plays fades and crossfades as the clip player's data (R12): each clip of a crossfade is given its span past its edge, read from its source, which preloads the earlier point the span starts at as it preloads a clip's start (R19). Each crossfade falls on its boundary's frame under any tempo map, tempo automation included; a render is the same at any block size (R11); and nothing on the audio thread allocates or frees (R13). (S14, S18)
