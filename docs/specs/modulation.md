## The behaviour

A parameter can change over time in two ways. Automation sets its value: an envelope says what the value is at each moment. Modulation moves the value from wherever automation, or the control, puts it, and never sets it: it acts on top, so the two work together, and when automation moves the value, the modulation's reach moves with it. Two kinds of thing modulate a parameter: a clip's modulation envelopes, which act while their clip plays, and modulator devices. On a volume or a send, a clip's modulation is a share of the level that can only bring it down, as far as silence; on pan it sweeps fully only from the centre, and its reach shrinks as the pan nears either side; on any other control it moves the value up or down from where the control is. A modulated knob shows its value as a segment of its ring in a colour of its own, and its pointer stays where the control or its automation puts it; a fader shows a mark under its thumb.

The modulators are devices that make a control signal and send it to parameters they're mapped to, on any track: Beatlayer's equivalents of Live's LFO and Shaper (cycles), Envelope Follower (the level of an audio signal), and Envelope MIDI, Shaper MIDI and Expression Control (driven by the notes and MIDI they receive). Each one's Map button maps it to a parameter, and its Multimap shows more slots, up to eight targets. In Modulation mode, the default, the target's own control still works and the modulator swings the value round it, or pushes it one way; in Remote Control mode the modulator alone sets the target, between a Min and a Max, as a macro does.

This spec covers how modulation and automation combine on a parameter, in the engine, on the control and in the envelope editors; the modulators' controls and mappings; and modulating plug-in parameters. It changes [clip-envelopes.md](clip-envelopes.md) R1 and R3, whose values and sum R4 and R5 replace, in the same package. It leaves to their own packages the devices' names (each modulator's package proposes its equivalent's name), their layouts, and the waveform and detector laws marked here as Beatlayer's own.

## Rules

**Modulation on a parameter (WP-5.9)**

- **R1.** Every parameter that can be automated can be modulated (rule G2): Volume, Pan and the Track Activator of a track's mixer, a send's level, and every parameter of every device, native or plug-in, a rack's macros included. The song's tempo isn't modulated (Beatlayer's own). (S1, S13)
- **R2.** A parameter's base, on each frame, is what sets its value: its envelope's value where one drives it ([automation.md](automation.md) R3); otherwise the value its macro gives where a macro maps it ([macros.md](macros.md) R7), or a modulator in Remote Control gives where one holds it (R16); otherwise its control's value. Modulation never changes the base, the control, or anything in the set; it moves the value the parameter plays away from the base, so automation that moves the base carries the modulation's reach with it. (S2, S4)
- **R3.** Each source modulating a parameter contributes a share of the parameter's travel (0 to 1, logarithmic where its taper is, [device-framework.md](device-framework.md) R1), by its own law (R4 for a clip, R14 for a modulator); the contributions add, their sum moves the base's position on the travel, the result is clamped to the travel's ends, and a stepped parameter (a switch, a choice, a whole number) plays the step nearest. The sum and clamp are Beatlayer's own, the plug-in kit's law. (S3, S14)
- **R4.** A clip's modulation envelope ([clip-envelopes.md](clip-envelopes.md) R1 to R3) holds values in modulation's own units and, while its clip plays, contributes: for Volume or a send's level, a share from 0 to 100 %, which brings the level's position down to that share of the base's, so it never goes above the base and reaches −∞ dB at 0 % (rule G1); for Pan, a share from −100 % to 100 % of the distance from the base to the nearer end, so a centred pan sweeps from hard left to hard right and a pan at either end doesn't move; for any other parameter, a share from −100 % to 100 % of the travel, added to the base's position. An envelope with no breakpoints, or whose clip doesn't play, contributes nothing, and the parameter plays its base. These laws are Beatlayer's own. (S2, S3)
- **R5.** A set saved before this rule loads each clip envelope with its breakpoints' values turned from the parameter's own units, which were added to the value, into the modulation (R4) that gives, at the parameter's value saved in that set, the value the envelope gave there, clamped to the modulation's range (Beatlayer's own). (S13)
- **R6.** Modulation is worked out on the audio thread frame by frame, with nothing allocated, locked or blocked and work per block bounded by its frames, and the value a parameter plays at a frame is the same at any block size. A clip envelope's modulation is held back by its node's delay, as automation is ([automation.md](automation.md) R4). A base glides as a control's change does ([device-framework.md](device-framework.md) R4) and the modulation acts on the gliding base without a glide of its own. When a source stops modulating (its clip stops, it's unmapped, or its device is switched off), the parameter plays its base again from the next frame. (S13)
- **R7.** On a modulated control, the pointer of a knob stays at its base (where an envelope drives it, at the envelope's value) and its ring shows, in the theme's modulation colour, a segment from the base to the value it plays; a fader shows a mark under its thumb at the level it plays. Both follow the playing value, which the engine publishes with the playhead after every block. Assistive technology reads a modulated control's value as its base, with the value it plays. (S2, S3)
- **R8.** In an automation lane's and a clip envelope editor's parameter chooser, a parameter that has an envelope carries an LED in the theme's automation colour, one that's modulated carries an LED in the theme's modulation colour, and one that's both carries both; the device chooser marks a device holding any such parameter the same way, and an overridden envelope's LED is grey. The two colours are Beatlayer's own and distinct. (S1, S2)
- **R9.** An Arrangement automation lane draws the parameter's automation envelope, which is its base, and not its modulation ([automation.md](automation.md) R13). The Clip View's envelope editor draws a clip's modulation envelope in the theme's modulation colour on its own scale (0 to 100 % for Volume and sends, −100 % to 100 % for the rest) and an automation envelope in the automation colour. An Arrangement clip's editor edits modulation only and has no Automation or Modulation switch; a Session clip's has both under its choosers, choosing which of the parameter's two envelopes it shows and edits. (S1, S2, S12)
- **R10.** A plug-in device's parameter is modulated as a native one is (R2 to R4, R14) and hears the value it plays on its frame: a CLAP plug-in's parameter marked modulatable takes the difference between that value and the base as the plug-in's own modulation events, so its value stays the base; any other CLAP parameter, and every VST3 and Audio Unit parameter, takes the value it plays as a parameter change on its frame. Modulation never writes into the set: the set saves the base, a reopened set gives the plug-in its base ([clap-hosting.md](clap-hosting.md) R11), and a value a plug-in reports back because modulation sent it is neither an undo step nor an edit. (S13, S15)
- **R11.** `modulation.state` (`read`) reports, for a track's mixer, its sends and its devices, each modulated or held parameter's base, the value it played in the last block, the sources modulating it (a clip's envelope, or a modulator and its slot, with each contribution) and what holds it (a macro, or a modulator in Remote Control). It's the readback of the commands that make modulation (R18, and the clip envelope commands of [clip-envelopes.md](clip-envelopes.md) R5). (S13)

**Modulators and their mappings (WP-5.10)**

- **R12.** Each modulator is a native device on the device framework ([device-framework.md](device-framework.md)): the equivalents of LFO, Shaper and Envelope Follower are audio effects, which pass their audio through untouched, and those of Envelope MIDI, Shaper MIDI and Expression Control are MIDI effects, which pass their notes and MIDI through untouched. A seventh, CV In ([cv-tools.md](cv-tools.md) R26, R27), is an audio effect with a CV input in place of an audio one, whose output is the voltage on it. All seven are listed under the browser's Modulators label (L-BRW-004), go anywhere their role goes in a chain or a rack, and save with the set, in presets, and in the device clipboard as any device does, their mappings aside (R19). (S5 to S10, S12)
- **R13.** A modulator's output is a signal from 0 to 1, and its Depth (Amount on the Envelope MIDI equivalent; Output Range Max on Expression Control's in Modulate mode) scales its effect from 0 % (none) to 100 % (full). Its Mod switch chooses Modulation, the default, or Remote Control for its mappings (R14, R16). (S5 to S10)
- **R14.** In Modulation mode, a mapping contributes to its target (R3) `depth · amount · u` with Polarity set to Unipolar, adding from the base upward (downward for a negative amount), and `depth · amount · (2u − 1)` with Bipolar, swinging round the base, where `u` is the modulator's output, `depth` its Depth and `amount` the mapping's Modulation Amount, from −100 % to 100 %. The target's control still works, and moves the base the modulation swings round. The law is Beatlayer's own, the plug-in kit's. (S5, S14)
- **R15.** The Map button arms its slot: lit, with every parameter in the set that can be a target (R17) tinted with the theme's mapping colour, and clicking any such parameter's control, on any track, maps it to the slot, one undo step (`Map Modulator`, Beatlayer's own name); the slot then shows the target's device and parameter names. Esc, or the Map button again, disarms it, changing nothing. Unmap, right of Map, removes the slot's mapping, one undo step. (S5, S11)
- **R16.** In Remote Control mode a mapping holds its target, as a macro holds a mapped parameter ([macros.md](macros.md) R11): the target plays the value at position `min + depth · u · (max − min)` of its travel, `min` and `max` the positions of the mapping's Min and Max (in the target's own units; Min above Max inverts), its control is drawn disabled and refuses hand moves and `device.set_param` (and the mixer's commands for a mixer control or a send), and its own envelope waits. Other modulation still acts on top. A parameter held by a macro, or by another modulator in Remote Control, can't be mapped in Remote Control: the mapping or the switch to Remote Control is refused, saying what holds it. (S5, S4)
- **R17.** A modulator maps any parameter R1 names, on any track, a rack's macros included, except its own parameters (Beatlayer's own). A modulator can map a parameter of another modulator, which takes the mapping's effect only if it comes later in the song's track and chain order, so the order of the work is the same at any block size (Beatlayer's own). Several modulators may modulate one parameter, and their contributions add (R3); one slot maps one parameter, and one modulator doesn't map a parameter twice (Beatlayer's own). The equivalents of LFO, Shaper, Envelope Follower, Envelope MIDI and Shaper MIDI, and CV In, each have eight slots, the first always shown and Show/Hide Multimap showing the other seven; Expression Control's has one slot on each of its five source tabs (Beatlayer's own). A modulator driven by audio, notes or MIDI (R24 to R27) maps no other modulator's parameters: it is worked out while its track runs, after every other modulator (Beatlayer's own). (S5 to S11)
- **R18.** `modulator.map` (device, slot, target) and `modulator.unmap` (device, slot) are `edit` commands with schemas and descriptions, run by ID through the agent API, and the query tool's `device` gives a modulator's mappings in its `mappings` field, their readback. Arming a Map button is view state (`modulator.arm_map`, `read`). The Mod switch, Polarity, and each slot's Modulation Amount, Min and Max are parameters of the modulator (`device.set_param`), so they can be automated and modulated (rule G2); the Mod switch and Polarity are the device's, and the amount and range each slot's own (Beatlayer's own). (S5, S13)
- **R19.** The set saves each modulator's mappings, a slot and the parameter it targets. A target that leaves the set (its device or track deleted) takes its mapping with it in the same undo step, and a target moved to another track keeps it. A modulator copied together with its targets (its track duplicated, or a rack copied with both inside) maps the copies of its targets; copied alone, or saved in a preset, it keeps its settings and no mappings. A set saved before modulators loads with none, and a mapping naming a parameter the set doesn't have doesn't load. (S13)
- **R20.** A modulator's output is worked out once every 32 frames, on a grid counted from the frame the transport last started (from the engine's first frame before it has ever started), and moves in a straight line from each value to the next, so a target never steps and the same song plays the same samples at any block size. The equivalents of LFO and Shaper, whose output depends only on time and their own parameters, reach a target on any track on the frames they're worked out for. Nothing a modulator does on the audio thread allocates, locks or blocks. The 32-frame grid is Beatlayer's own, the plug-in kit's control rate. A modulator works while the song is stopped too, from the engine's first frame, and starts again from the frame the transport starts on, so a free LFO moves its targets with the song stopped and a synced one, locked to the song position, holds still. A modulator driven by audio, notes or MIDI takes its level at the same grid points but runs in its track, and its output runs to the level taken at the start of each step rather than the next, so it follows its level one step behind; it works while the song is stopped as well (its audio and the notes it hears don't need the song), and when the transport starts it begins as a render that starts there does (Beatlayer's own). Switched on again while the song plays, it takes its level at once with nothing behind it, and its grid carries on where the song has got to, as if it had run through. Switched off when the transport starts, it begins when it is switched on. (S14, S13)
- **R21.** Every random part of a modulator (the Random waveform, Jitter, and Expression Control's Random and Random Amount; Jitter on the Shaper MIDI's equivalent) comes from a generator seeded per device and started again when the transport starts, so an export, a bounce or an agent's preview plays the same values each time, and as the song plays (Beatlayer's own). (S5, S10, S13)
- **R22.** The LFO's equivalent has a waveform chooser of the nine waveforms Sine, Up, Down, Triangle, Square, Random, Bin, Stray and Glider; Shape, which bends the waveform and is inactive for Random, Bin, Stray and Glider; Steps, which turns the waveform into up to 24 steps and is inactive for Random, Bin and Square; Jitter; Smooth; Rate, in Hz with a ×10 switch or in beat divisions of the song's tempo by its time-mode switch; Depth (R13); Offset, which moves the output's centre, 0 % centred, the output clamped to 0 to 1; Phase, from 0 % to 100 % of the cycle; Hold, which freezes the output and, released, carries on from the held value; and R, which restarts the cycle from its Phase, a switch parameter whose turning on restarts it, so automation can retrigger it (Beatlayer's own). In beat divisions the cycle is locked to the song position, Phase offsetting it; in Hz it runs freely, and starts again from its Phase when the transport starts (Beatlayer's own). Each waveform's shape is Beatlayer's own, keeps to 0 to 1, and repeats at the rate. (S5)
- **R23.** The Shaper's equivalent draws its output as a breakpoint envelope over one cycle in its display: a click adds a breakpoint, dragging one moves it (Beatlayer's own), Alt/Option-dragging a segment curves it by automation's law ([automation.md](automation.md) R12), and Shift-clicking a breakpoint deletes it; Clear removes every breakpoint, leaving the output at 0, and six preset buttons beside it each draw a shape of Beatlayer's own; Grid sets the display's divisions and Snap snaps breakpoints to them. Each edit is one undo step and the breakpoints save with the device. Its other controls: an output display; Jitter; Smooth; Phase; Offset; Depth; Rate in Hz or beat divisions; R, as the LFO's, inactive in beat divisions; and Trigger mode, Loop (the shape repeats at Rate), 1-Shot (it plays once each time its T switch turns on, a parameter that can be automated and mapped) or Manual (the Manual control's position, 0 to 100 %, picks the point of the shape it outputs). (S6)

*The LFO's waveforms.* With `p` the position in the cycle, from 0 to 1, bent by Shape (`p` raised to the power `4^(−s)`, `s` being Shape as a share from −1 to 1, so a positive Shape brings the change forward and a negative one holds it back; the random waves aren't bent): Sine is `0.5 − 0.5·cos(2πp)`, Up is `p`, Down is `1 − p`, Triangle is `1 − |2p − 1|`, and Square is 1 for the first half of the bent cycle and 0 for the rest. The four random waves draw from the generator R21 describes, once per cycle: Random holds a new value in 0 to 1 for the cycle, Bin holds 0 or 1, Stray holds a value that wanders from the last by up to a quarter either way, reflected at 0 and 1, and Glider runs in a straight line from the last cycle's value to this one's. Steps cuts a wave into that many evenly spaced levels (inactive for Random, Bin and Square). Jitter adds up to half its share of a draw either way, Smooth moves the output towards each new value by a share that falls from all of it at 0 % to 2 % at 100 %, and Offset then adds its share and clamps the output to 0 to 1. Hold keeps the output where it is and stops the cycle; released, a free cycle carries on from there and a synced one is shifted so the output carries on, then follows the song again.

*The Shaper's shape.* A list of up to 64 breakpoints, each a place `x` in the cycle (0 to 1), an output `y` (0 to 1) and the bend of the segment that starts there, by [automation.md](automation.md) R12's law; the output holds the first breakpoint's value before it and the last one's after it, and an empty shape outputs 0. A new Shaper plays the triangle (0, 0; ½, 1; 1, 0). The six preset buttons draw: 1 a rise, 2 a fall, 3 the triangle, 4 a pulse (high for the first half), 5 a staircase of four steps and 6 a curved decay. The shape belongs to the device: it is saved with the set and in its presets, copies with the device, and is edited by `modulator.set_shape` and the gestures R23 lists. Loop plays the shape once each cycle at the rate; 1-Shot rests at the shape's start before its first trigger, plays through in one cycle of the rate each time T turns on, and rests at the shape's end; Manual outputs the shape at the Manual control's share of the cycle.

*Mapping in the panel.* Slot 1's mapping cell is in the body, with the slot's Amount, Min and Max under it; Show Multimap is the device's expand toggle (L-DEV-004), which adds the other seven cells. Each cell shows the target by its device or track and its parameter, a Map button that lights while its slot is armed (`modulator.arm_map`), and Unmap. While one is lit, every control that can be a target is tinted with the theme's mapping colour (its own parameters aren't), and a click, or Enter on the control with the keyboard focus, maps it and puts the button out.

**Modulators driven by audio, notes and MIDI (WP-5.11)**

- **R24.** The Envelope Follower's equivalent outputs the level of the signal it hears: its Gain sets the input's gain, Rise smooths the output's rise and Fall its fall, and Delay delays the output by a time or a beat division (its Delay mode switch). It hears the track's signal at its place in the chain, or, with its sidechain switch on, a mix set by Sidechain Mix (0 % the track's own signal, 100 % the sidechain's) of that and a source chosen as a device's sidechain is: Audio From names the track and a second chooser its tap, Pre FX, Post FX or Post Mixer (the routing spec's track taps), with a rack's chains offered inside racks (WP-5.7). Direct and S.C. meters show the two levels. The detector's law and the Rise and Fall curves are Beatlayer's own. (S7)
- **R25.** The Envelope MIDI equivalent outputs an ADSR envelope: Attack, Decay, Sustain and Release, a slope for each of Attack, Decay and Release, and handles in its display that drag them. Each note it receives starts the envelope from the level it's at (Beatlayer's own), and the release starts when the last held note ends; with Sustain on it holds the Sustain level while a note is held, and off it releases after its decay; the Velocity switch scales the peak by the note's velocity. Loop Mode: Free (each note starts it), Sync (it starts again on every Sync Rate beat division while a note is held), Loop (it loops at Global Time's rate) or Echo (it repeats every Env Echo Time, each repeat Env Feedback's share of the last). Global Time scales every time, 1.00 leaving them as set. A trigger LED flashes on each start. (S8)
- **R26.** The Shaper MIDI equivalent outputs a breakpoint envelope, edited as the Shaper's (R23), that each note it receives starts: Cmd/Ctrl-click makes a breakpoint its sustain breakpoint (or, on the one that is, takes the mark away), one only and drawn larger, where the envelope holds while the note is held, going on to its end when the note ends; `modulator.set_shape` carries it as a `sustain` flag on the breakpoint. With Loop on it repeats at Rate while a note is held, past its sustain breakpoint. Rate is in Hz or beat divisions; Velocity at 0 % leaves velocity out, and higher makes soft notes give less and hard notes more; Jitter, Smooth, Offset and Depth are the LFO's; Time sets the time between echoes and Echo how much of each the next keeps. Its display shows the output, with an LED that flashes on each start. (S9, S11)
- **R27.** The Expression Control equivalent has five source tabs (laid side by side in Beatlayer's panel), each a Source chooser (Velocity, Modwheel, Pitchbend, Pressure, Keytrack, Expression, Random, Increment, Slide, Sustain), a curve, smoothing and its own slot (R17). Random gives a new value at each note, spread by Random Amount; Increment advances one of its Increment Steps (1 to 32) at each note and starts again from the first when the transport stops. The curve is Linear (two breakpoints, Curve A bending it) or S-shaped (three, Curve A bending the upper segment and Curve B the lower, Curve Link bending both in opposite directions, and X and Y placing the middle breakpoint), and its Min and Max bound its output; the curve can also be dragged in its display. Smoothing is Linear or Logarithmic, with Rise and Fall from 0 to 1,000 ms. Modulate mode takes Polarity and Output Range Max (R14); Remote Control takes Output Range Min and Max (R16). Per-note sources (Slide, and Pressure from an MPE controller) follow the most recent note held (Beatlayer's own). (S10)
- **R28.** A modulator driven by audio, notes or MIDI (R24 to R27) works its output out while its track runs, at its place in the chain, so the track holding a parameter it maps waits for its own in the schedule, as a track waits for a sidechain's source, and its effect on a later track lands on the frames it is worked out for (R20). These waits come after every route, in display order, and a mapping that would make two tracks wait on each other, because the target's track feeds the modulator's (through an output, an input, a send or a sidechain, or another such modulator's mapping), is refused, saying why; a routing made after the mapping that would close the loop is kept in the set and the mapping carries nothing. A target earlier in the modulator's own chain, which its input passes through (a send to the return track the modulator sits on counts as earlier), takes its effect one 32-frame step late: it reads what the modulator wrote 32 frames before, and a program with such a mapping runs in parts of at most 32 frames, so the lag is the same at any block size. A target in another chain of a rack that holds the modulator takes it too, since the rack's chains may run at once on different threads and the order they finish in must not show. A target later in its chain, or on its mixer, has no lag. These are Beatlayer's own. (S13)
- **R29.** Every modulator plays in an offline render (an export, a bounce, a freeze, an agent's preview) exactly as it plays in the song, sample for sample at any block size (R20, R21). (S13)

**The other four modulators (WP-5.11, Beatlayer's own)**

The **Envelope Follower** (`beatlayer.level_mod`, an audio effect with a sidechain section), **Envelope MIDI** (`beatlayer.gate_mod`), **Shaper MIDI** (`beatlayer.strike_mod`) and **Expression Control** (`beatlayer.play_mod`) take Live's names; the last three are MIDI effects. All four are listed under the Modulators label (R12), take Mod, Polarity and Depth and their slots' Amount, Min and Max as the first two do, and pass their audio and notes through untouched. *The Envelope Follower's detector.* The peak of what the detector hears (the track's signal, or the sidechain's signal when the sidechain section is on, which is the source through SC Gain mixed with the track's own by Mix, 0 % the track's, 100 % the sidechain's), across the channels, through Gain, then a one-pole smoother with the time constant Rise while the level climbs and Fall while it falls (0 ms follows at once). The output is that level, 1 at full scale, held at 1 above it, delayed by Delay. Direct and S.C. show the peak of the track's own signal and of what the detector hears, in dB from −60, and Level the output.

*The Envelope MIDI's envelope.* A note starts it from the level it is at: Attack runs to the peak (1, or the note's velocity with Velocity on), Decay to Sustain Level's share of the peak, and the last held key's end starts Release from the level it is at. Each segment bends by the automation law ([automation.md](automation.md) R12, as `curve_value`): a positive slope bows it below the straight line. Global Time scales every time. With Sustain off the envelope runs Attack, Decay and Release whatever the key does. Loop Mode: Free starts it on each note; Sync starts it again on every Sync Rate division while a key is held; Loop runs Attack, Decay and Release over and over while a key is held, with no Sustain, so a cycle is the three times together; Echo starts it again every Env Echo Time, each repeat's peak Env Feedback's share of the last, until it is under a 256th, whether or not the key is still held. Its display draws the envelope as its controls set it, with a held key's sustain as long as half the attack and decay together, and a reading of its level and the trigger LED.

*The Shaper MIDI's shape.* Up to 64 breakpoints as the Shaper's, one of them possibly the sustain breakpoint; a new one plays a rise to the top by a tenth of the cycle, which is its sustain breakpoint, and a fall to the end. A note starts the shape from its start and it runs through one cycle at Rate (a length in Hz, or in beat divisions of the song's tempo at the moment); with a sustain breakpoint it holds there while a key is held and goes on to its end when the last key comes up; with Loop it starts again at its end while a key is held, passing over the sustain breakpoint. The output rests at the shape's start before the first note and at its end after each. Velocity scales the output by `1 − v + v · velocity`, with `v` its share, so 0 % leaves velocity out and 100 % makes the output the velocity. Echo starts the shape again every Time, each repeat scaled by Echo's share of the last, until it is under a 256th. Jitter, Smooth and Offset are the LFO's, applied where the level is taken. The Shaper has no sustain breakpoint.

*The Expression Control's sources.* Each is a share from 0 to 1. Velocity and Keytrack (the key's number over 127) are those of the latest note; Modwheel, Expression and Sustain are controllers 1, 11 and 64; Pitchbend runs from 0 at the wheel's bottom to 1 at its top, ½ at the centre; Pressure is the channel's pressure or the latest held note's own; Slide is the latest held note's own; Random is a draw at each note, `½ + ½ · Random Amount · draw`, from the device's generator (a draw is made for every tab at every note, so the stream doesn't depend on the choosers); Increment is the step the notes have reached, from 0 at the first to 1 at the last of Increment Steps, going round, and it starts again at the first when the transport stops. Before anything is played every source reads 0, bar Pitchbend and Random at ½; when the transport starts what the last note said is forgotten unless a key is already down. The curve is Linear (`curve_value` from 0 to 1 bent by Curve A) or S (through the middle breakpoint (X, Y), the lower segment bent by Curve B, or by minus Curve A with Link, and the upper by Curve A), and the output is `Min + curve · (Max − Min)`. Smoothing follows the output at Rise while it climbs and Fall while it falls: Linear takes that long for the whole range, Logarithmic is a one-pole smoother with that time constant; 0 ms follows at once.
