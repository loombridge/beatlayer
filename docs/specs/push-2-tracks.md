## The behaviour

With Push 2 attached, five of its modes work on the set's tracks without the computer's screen. Browse Mode lists the browser's sounds and devices in columns across the display, and Load puts the chosen one on the selected track, replacing the selected device or adding to the chain. Device Mode shows the selected track's devices along the upper display buttons; the eight encoders play the selected device's parameters a bank at a time, racks unfold to show their chains' devices, and devices are deleted, switched off and moved from the hardware. Mix Mode sets one track's volume, pan, sends and routing (Track Mix) or one mixer control across eight tracks (Global Mix), unfolding group tracks and racks to mix what they hold. Clip Mode shows the selected clip and sets its loop, markers and, for audio, its warp mode, gain and transposition; in the step sequencing layouts, holding steps sets the held notes' nudge, length and velocity. Session Mode turns the 8 × 8 pads into an eight-track, eight-scene part of the Session View, launching clips and scenes, and Session Overview zooms the pads out so each one stands for eight tracks by eight scenes.

Every press and turn runs Beatlayer's registered commands, the same ones the window and agents run, so an undo, a readback or an agent sees exactly what Push 2 did. What Push 2 itself shows (its mode, the browser columns, the bank, the folds it has opened) is surface state: never saved with the set, never an undo step, and readable through `push.state`.

## Rules

### Throughout (every package here)

- **R1.** Everything a control does in these modes runs a registered command through the surface host, the same one the window's own control runs, with the same undo step and the same refusal ([Push 2](push-2.md) R34, [control surfaces](control-surfaces.md) R7). An encoder's gesture, from touch to release, is one undo step ([Push 2](push-2.md) R9); so is a gesture of encoders turned while a display button or a step is held, from the first change until the button or step is released (Beatlayer's own, as a drag in the window is one). (S9, S10)
- **R2.** What Push 2 shows and where it is (Browse Mode's columns and selection, Device Mode's bank and Edit Mode, the racks and tracks it has unfolded where they aren't the window's own state, the sends' shift, Session Overview) is surface state ([control surfaces](control-surfaces.md) R12): it isn't saved with the set, isn't an undo step, marks nothing changed, and `push.state` reads it ([Push 2](push-2.md) R34). (S9)
- **R3.** Push 2 follows the window: selecting a track, a device, a chain, a slot or a scene in the window shows it on Push 2 at once ([control surfaces](control-surfaces.md) R17), and what Push 2 selects is selected in the window; a change made in the window to anything Push 2 shows redraws it on the next display frame. (S5, S9)
- **R4.** Encoders move continuous parameters and step through lists (a browser column, a routing chooser, Warp Mode) by [Push 2](push-2.md) R9's steps, finer with Shift held, and draw what they control in their columns as [Push 2](push-2.md) R18 and R20 say; held buttons, taps and modifiers are [Push 2](push-2.md) R7 and R25's. (S6, S8, S9)

### Browsing and loading (WP-7.20)

- **R5.** Pressing Browse puts Push 2 in Browse Mode, which loads onto the selected track. What it lists and what Load does depend on the device last selected on that track: with an instrument, an instrument rack or a drum rack selected, it lists what replaces an instrument (Sounds, Drums, Instruments, and instrument plug-ins) and Load replaces it, as hot-swap does ([racks](racks.md) R45, `device.replace`); with an audio effect or audio effect rack selected, audio effects, replacing it; with a MIDI effect or MIDI effect rack selected, MIDI effects, replacing it; with nothing selected, on a MIDI track with no devices, everything a MIDI track can hold (sounds, drum kits, instruments, audio effects, MIDI effects and plug-ins), and Load adds it (`device.add`, `device.add_preset`); on an audio, group or return track with no devices, audio effects, added. Where nothing is selected on a track that has devices, Load adds as it does on an empty one (Beatlayer's own). Sounds lists the User Library's instrument presets, and Audio Effects and MIDI Effects their category's presets after the devices, in the folders the library keeps them in (Beatlayer's own); a plug-in, which `device.replace` doesn't take, is added next to the selected device as the browser adds it (`plugin.load`). (S1, S10)
- **R6.** Browse Mode's display is divided into columns. The leftmost lists the browser labels of what R5 offers, in the browser's order ([browser](browser.md) R2), with Collections after them; each column to its right lists what the item selected in the column before it holds (a label's or folder's folders and items, in [browser](browser.md) R6's order), and the column after an item that isn't a folder is empty. As the selection goes deeper the columns move left so the column in use and the one after it stay on the display; how many columns fit, and their widths, are Beatlayer's own arrangement. (S1, S10)
- **R7.** Each encoder scrolls the column over which it sits, moving that column's selection one entry per list step (R4), and choosing an entry fills the columns right of it again. The up and down arrows move the selection in the column in use one entry; the left and right arrows make the column to the left or right the one in use. (S1, S6)
- **R8.** The two rightmost upper display buttons move through the hierarchy: the seventh up, making the column to the left the one in use, and the eighth down, opening the selected folder into the column to its right (which of the two goes up is Beatlayer's own). (S1)
- **R9.** Beatlayer's native devices, and an empty rack of each kind, sit in the leftmost column's labels as the browser lists them ([racks](racks.md) R43), so Load on one adds the device as `device.add` does, with its default preset if one was saved ([device chains](device-chains.md) R13). (S1, S10)
- **R10.** While Preview is on, choosing an audio file or a preset in a column previews it as choosing it in the window's browser does ([browser](browser.md) R11, R27); Preview is the browser's own Preview switch (`browser.set_preview`), toggled by the Preview display button and lit while it's on, so Push 2 and the window switch the same thing. The preview is heard at the Preview/Cue Volume, which Shift with the master encoder sets in every mode ([Push 2](push-2.md) R10). Until the browser's preview takes the Cue Volume, the same turn also sets the browser's own preview volume (`browser.set_preview`), which `browser.state` reads (Beatlayer's own). (S1, S6, S9, S10)
- **R11.** Load loads the selected item as R5 says, in one undo step, and Push 2 stays in Browse Mode with the loaded device as the one Load replaces next, so a second Load swaps it again. Load does nothing while the selection is a folder or a label. Preview and Load sit on the lower display buttons, Load on the eighth and Preview on the seventh (Beatlayer's own arrangement). (S1)
- **R12.** Add Track puts Push 2 in Browse Mode with the track kinds in the leftmost column, MIDI, Audio and Return, and in the columns after each kind what R5 lists for an empty track of that kind. Load with a kind selected inserts an empty track of it after the selected track (`track.create`), inside the selected track's group when it's in one; with a device or preset selected it inserts the track and loads the device on it, in one undo step (Insert Device and Track). The new track becomes the selected one. (S6, S10)
- **R13.** Browse Mode reads the browser's index through the surface's read model and never waits for it: while the browser is still indexing, the columns show what's indexed so far and fill in as indexing goes on ([browser](browser.md) R10), and plug-ins come from the scanned list. (S9, S10)
- **R14.** `push.state` reports, in Browse Mode, what Load does (`add`, `replace` with the device, or `add_track`), the columns with their entries and selection, and the column in use; Preview reads back in `browser.state`. (S9)

### Devices and racks (WP-7.25)

- **R15.** Pressing Device puts Push 2 in Device Mode. The upper display buttons stand, left to right, for the selected track's devices in signal order, with an unfolded rack's devices after it (R22), eight at a time; the selected device's button and name are highlighted, and its parameters are on the eight encoders, the current bank of its banks of eight ([control surfaces](control-surfaces.md) R18 to R21), drawn as [Push 2](push-2.md) R20 draws a parameter. The device shown is the device in focus ([control surfaces](control-surfaces.md) R17). The lower display buttons stand for tracks at Device Mode's top level ([Push 2](push-2.md) R26). When the track has more than eight devices, the first and eighth upper buttons become arrows that move the eight shown by one (Beatlayer's own arrangement). (S2, S9)
- **R16.** Tapping a device's upper display button selects it (`device.select`), and the bank the surface last showed it is on the encoders ([control surfaces](control-surfaces.md) R18). Tapping the selected device's button again enters Edit Mode: the lower display buttons name the device's banks, the current one lit, and pressing one puts that bank on the encoders. In Edit Mode the leftmost upper display button returns to Device Mode's top level. (S2)
- **R17.** A device with more than eight banks shows an arrow on the rightmost lower display button in Edit Mode, standing for the banks after the seventh; pressing it shows the following banks with a back arrow on the leftmost lower button, which returns to the first ones. (S2)
- **R18.** Turning an encoder sets its parameter (`device.set_param`, a rack's macro by `device.set_param` on the macro), as R4 says, one undo step per gesture (R1); with automation on the parameter it overrides it as [Push 2](push-2.md) R9 says. A parameter a macro holds ([macros](macros.md) R11) doesn't move, and its value is drawn dimmed. A device that is switched off is drawn grey on the display, with all its parameters. (S2, S10)
- **R19.** Holding Delete and pressing a device's upper display button deletes the device (`device.remove`), one undo step, and the device after it in the chain becomes selected, or the one before it when it was the last (Beatlayer's own). (S2, S6)
- **R20.** Holding Mute and pressing a device's upper display button switches the device off, or on again when it's off (`device.set_enabled`). While Mute is locked ([Push 2](push-2.md) R24), each device's upper button switches that device until Mute is pressed again. (S2, S6)
- **R21.** Holding a MIDI effect's or an audio effect's upper display button ([Push 2](push-2.md) R7's hold) and turning any encoder moves the effect through its chain, one place for each list step (R4), only to places that keep the chain's order ([device chains](device-chains.md) R3), by `device.move`, as one undo step when the button is released. An instrument, and a device in a chain it can't leave, doesn't move. (S2, S10)
- **R22.** Racks are drawn with an icon for each kind, MIDI effect, instrument, audio effect and drum rack (Beatlayer's own icons). Tapping a selected rack's upper display button unfolds it, and tapping it again folds it: unfolded, the devices of its selected chain follow it on the upper buttons, with a line under the rack that runs to the last of them. The fold is the rack's devices view in the window (`rack.set_view` with the devices view, [racks](racks.md) R18, R19), so Push 2 and the Device View show the same; a drum rack doesn't fold or unfold from Push 2 and shows as the Device View has it. (S2, S10)
- **R23.** With a rack selected, the encoders play its first bank, macros 1 to 8 ([control surfaces](control-surfaces.md) R20); a rack's second tap unfolds it (R22) rather than entering Edit Mode; holding Shift and tapping it enters the rack's Edit Mode instead, its bank buttons offering Macros 1–8 and, while more than eight macros show, Macros 9–16 (Beatlayer's own). Pressing one of the unfolded devices' buttons selects that device, and the encoders play its parameters as R15 to R17 say. (S2, S10)
- **R24.** Holding a rack's upper display button shows its chains along the lower display buttons, eight at a time, the selected chain lit; pressing one selects that chain (`chain.select`), and the unfolded devices become that chain's. Releasing the rack's button gives the lower buttons back. (S2, S10)
- **R25.** Add Device puts Push 2 in Browse Mode listing every device that can go on the selected track, instruments included, and Load adds the chosen one after the device selected before Add Device was pressed (`device.add`, [device chains](device-chains.md) R12): a MIDI effect always before the instrument and an audio effect always after it. Loading an instrument on a track that has one replaces it (`device.replace`). (S2, S6, S10)
- **R26.** The selected device on Push 2 is the window's selected device (R3): selecting another device in the window, or adding, deleting or moving one there, redraws the upper buttons and the encoders' parameters. Device Mode on a track with no devices says so and offers Add Device (Beatlayer's own wording). (S9)
- **R27.** `push.state` reports, in Device Mode, the devices on the upper buttons with their depth and which is selected, whether Edit Mode is on, the bank and its page of banks, the eight parameters with their names and values, the chains shown while a rack's button is held, and whether Mute is locked. (S9)

### Mixing and track control (WP-7.26)

- **R28.** Tapping Mix puts Push 2 in Mix Mode, and tapping it again switches between Track Mix and Global Mix, Track Mix the first time ([Push 2](push-2.md) R22); Mix Mode entered from another mode opens in the view it was last in (Beatlayer's own). (S3, S6, S9)
- **R29.** In Track Mix the first upper display button chooses Mix and the second Input & Output, and the lower display buttons select tracks ([Push 2](push-2.md) R26). With Mix chosen, the encoders set the selected track's volume, pan and sends A to F, left to right (`mixer.set_volume`, `mixer.set_pan`, `mixer.set_send`); when the set has more than six return tracks, the two rightmost upper buttons become arrows that shift the sends shown by one. (S3)
- **R30.** In Track Mix, a track in Split Stereo Pan Mode ([mixer](mixer.md) R29) shows its two pan sliders in place of the pan control, on the second and third encoders (`mixer.set_split_pan_left`, `mixer.set_split_pan_right`), and its sends start on the fourth (where they sit is Beatlayer's own arrangement). (S3, S10)
- **R31.** With Input & Output chosen, the encoders set the selected track's routing and monitoring as its In/Out section does ([routing](routing.md)), left to right: Input Type, Input Channel, Monitor, Output Type and Output Channel, each a list stepped as R4 says (`routing.set_input`, `routing.set_monitor`, `routing.set_output`); a track without one of them leaves its encoder empty. The order is the In/Out section's, the places Beatlayer's own. (S3, S10)
- **R32.** In Global Mix the upper display buttons choose what the encoders set for the eight tracks shown: Volumes, Pans, then Send A to Send F; the encoders set it on each track (the same commands as R29). When the set has more than six return tracks the rightmost upper button becomes an arrow that shifts the sends' buttons by one, Volumes and Pans staying, round to Send A after the last (the wrap is Beatlayer's own). (S3)
- **R33.** In Global Mix, a track in Split Stereo Pan Mode shows its pan value under Pans but its encoder does nothing. (S3)
- **R34.** The eight tracks shown, in Global Mix and on the lower display buttons, are the session ring's ([control surfaces](control-surfaces.md) R28, R33): the Session View's columns in order, a folded group's tracks left out, then the return tracks, never Main. When there are more than eight, the left and right arrows in Global Mix move the ring one track, by `control_surface.move_ring` ([control surfaces](control-surfaces.md) R29), in place of selecting the track before or after ([notes on Push 2](push-2-notes.md) R45), and the Session View's ring follows. (S3, S9)
- **R35.** Holding Shift while turning an encoder sets the control finely ([Push 2](push-2.md) R9, R25). (S3, S6, S9)
- **R36.** Pressing Master selects the Main track, so Track Mix sets its volume and pan; pressing it again selects the track selected before. Master is lit while the Main track is selected. (S3, S6)
- **R37.** Group tracks, and tracks holding an instrument rack or a drum rack of more than one chain, are drawn with an icon for each (Beatlayer's own icons). Pressing a selected one's lower display button unfolds it, and pressing it again folds it: a group shows the tracks inside it after it, as its fold in the window does (`arrangement.fold_groups`), and a rack track its rack's chains, as the mixer's chain strips do (`mixer_view.fold_chains`, [racks](racks.md) R27); a line under it runs to the last of them, and the arrows reach those pushed off the display. Holding a lower display button arms its track instead ([notes on Push 2](push-2-notes.md) R4). (S3, S10)
- **R38.** An unfolded rack's chains are mixed like tracks: their volume and pan (`chain.set_gain`, `chain.set_pan`), and in a drum rack their sends to the rack's return chains (`chain.set_send`) in the send places; a chain has no sends to the set's return tracks, so those encoders are empty. With a drum rack unfolded, holding Select and pressing a pad selects that pad's chain and shifts the eight shown to it. (S3, S10)
- **R39.** Pressing Mute switches the selected track's Track Activator (`mixer.set_mute`), and Solo its Solo (`mixer.set_solo`); holding Mute or Solo and pressing a track's lower display button does it to that track. While either is locked ([Push 2](push-2.md) R24), each lower button switches its track. Mute and Solo are lit while the selected track is muted or soloed. A lower-button solo acts as a click on the track's Solo switch does, exclusive or not as the Exclusive Solo preference says ([mixer](mixer.md) R15). (S5, S6, S10)
- **R40.** Holding Delete and pressing a track's lower display button deletes the track (`track.delete`); holding Duplicate and pressing it duplicates the track (`track.duplicate`). Each is one undo step. (S6)
- **R41.** Mix Mode's display draws each of the eight places as [Push 2](push-2.md) R18 and R20 do: the track's or chain's name over its lower button in its colour, and over its encoder the control it sets, a volume as a vertical fader with its value, a pan and a send as an arc (the fader is Beatlayer's own look). (S3, S9)
- **R42.** `push.state` reports, in Mix Mode, Track Mix or Global Mix, Mix or Input & Output, the parameter Global Mix sets, the sends' shift, the eight tracks or chains shown with what each encoder sets and its value, what Push 2 has unfolded, and the Mute and Solo locks. (S9)

### Clip Mode (WP-7.27)

- **R43.** Pressing Clip puts Push 2 in Clip Mode, which shows the selected clip: the selected track's clip in the selected scene's slot, or, while the Arrangement is shown, the clip the Clip View shows. On an audio track whose slot is empty, the display offers to load a sample, and pressing its button opens Browse Mode listing Samples, where Load puts the file in that slot as a new clip (`session.drop_files`). On a MIDI track whose slot is empty, the display says the slot is empty (Beatlayer's own wording). (S4, S10)
- **R44.** Clip Mode's display is drawn in the clip's colour, with the clip's name highlighted at its top left. A MIDI clip shows its notes over time, each note's opacity its velocity; on a track holding a drum rack each note is drawn in its pad's colour. An audio clip shows its waveform. While the clip plays, the display follows its play position and scrolls with it. (S4)
- **R45.** For MIDI and audio clips, the second upper display button switches Loop (`clip.set_loop_enabled`). With Loop on, encoders set Loop Position and Loop Length (`clip.set_midi_loop`, `clip.set_loop`) and Start Offset, the Start marker inside the loop (`clip.set_midi_region`, `clip.set_region`); while Loop Position and Start Offset are at one place, moving Loop Position moves Start Offset with it. With Loop off, encoders set Start and End, the stretch that plays once. Each step is a beat, and a sixteenth with Shift held (the beat is Beatlayer's own). (S4, S10)
- **R46.** The first encoder zooms the display in and out around the marker of the position encoder last touched (Loop Position, Loop Length, Start Offset, Start or End), and changes only the display. (S4, S8)
- **R47.** An audio clip's Clip Mode also sets its Warp Mode (`clip.set_warp_mode`, a list as R4 says), its gain (`clip.set_gain`) and its Transpose in semitones (`clip.set_pitch`); Transpose with Shift held sets cents, the clip's Detune. (S4, S10)
- **R48.** Clip Mode's controls sit, left to right on the encoders: Zoom, then Loop Position or Start, Loop Length or End, Start Offset (with Loop on), then for an audio clip Warp Mode, Gain and Transpose (Beatlayer's own arrangement beyond the first encoder and the second upper button). (S4)
- **R49.** On a MIDI clip, in every pad layout, a Crop display button crops the clip to its loop (`clip.crop`), deleting the notes outside it, one undo step; Crop sits on the third upper display button (Beatlayer's own place). (S4, S10)
- **R50.** In the real-time playing layouts ([notes on Push 2](push-2-notes.md): 64 Notes, 64 Pads, 64 Slices), the display is folded: its pitch axis shows only the pitches that hold notes, every note of the clip fits on it, and notes recorded as they come in appear at once. Session Mode shows the same folded display (R71). (S4, S5)
- **R51.** In the step sequencing layouts, a translucent box marks the sequenceable area, the page of steps and range of pitches the pads edit, and a box at the display's left edge shows where that range sits among the pitches. Lines at the left edge mark the pitch ranges that hold notes, thicker where more notes are (Beatlayer's own look for both). (S4)
- **R52.** In the Melodic Sequencer the sequenceable area is one page of eight pitches; it moves along the clip with the loop length pads or Page Left and Right, and through the pitches with Octave Up and Down or the touch strip ([notes on Push 2](push-2-notes.md)), and the display follows it. (S4)
- **R53.** In the Melodic Sequencer + 32 Notes, the display fits every note of the clip, and selecting notes that are in the clip highlights their sequenceable area; the area moves along the clip with Page Left and Right or the loop length pads. (S4)
- **R54.** In a drum or Slicing layout's Loop Selector and 16 Velocities, the display shows only the selected pad's notes; the area moves along the clip with the loop length pads or Page Left and Right, and to another pitch by selecting another pad. (S4)
- **R55.** In any step sequencing layout, holding a step zooms the display to the page holding it, highlights the step's notes, and puts their note settings on the encoders: Nudge, the notes' offset from the step's grid line as a percentage of a step, negative before it (`note.move`); Length, coarse, in steps (`note.resize`); Fine, the length in hundredths of a step; and Velocity (`note.set_value`). Releasing the step gives the encoders back. A step pad pressed with no modifier toggles its note when it is let go within 300 ms ([Push 2](push-2.md) R7's tap), so holding it longer, or turning an encoder while it is held, leaves the note as it is (Beatlayer's own). (Each Length and Fine step's size is Beatlayer's own.) (S4, S10)
- **R56.** With several steps held, the encoders change every held step's notes by the same amount, each held within its range, and the display shows the lowest and highest value where they differ. (S4)
- **R57.** Holding an empty step and turning Nudge, Length, Fine or Velocity adds a note there (`note.add`) with the values set, at the pitch the step's row stands for, or the selected pad's note in a drum layout; the note and its changes are one undo step. Holding Select, pressing a drum pad and turning those encoders changes every note of that pad in the clip. (S4, S10)

### The Session View on Push 2 (WP-7.28)

- **R59.** Session Mode is [Push 2](push-2.md) R21's pad mode: tapping Session switches to it, and holding Session in Note Mode, or Note in Session Mode, switches while it's held. Session Mode changes what the pads and the Scene/Grid buttons do; the display stays in its display mode ([Push 2](push-2.md) R22). (S5, S6, S9)
- **R60.** In Session Mode the 8 × 8 pads are the session ring's slots ([control surfaces](control-surfaces.md) R28, R32): its eight tracks left to right in the columns and its eight scenes top to bottom in the rows, so the top-left pad (note 92) is the ring's first track in its first scene; a pad past the last track or scene is empty and dark. The Session View draws the ring's border round those slots ([Session View](session-view.md) R36). (S5, S8, S9)
- **R61.** Pressing a pad whose slot holds a clip launches it (`session.launch`), and releasing it is the release a Gate or Repeat clip needs (`session.release`, [launch quantisation](launch-quantisation.md) R17, R23). Pressing a pad whose empty slot is on an armed track records a new clip there (`session.record_slot`); on a track that isn't armed, it stops the track's clip as the slot's stop button does (`session.stop_track`), or does nothing when the slot has no stop button ([launch quantisation](launch-quantisation.md) R8). (S5, S10)
- **R62.** The eight Scene/Grid buttons launch the ring's eight scenes, top to bottom (`session.launch_scene`). Holding Select and pressing one selects that scene without launching it (`session.select`). (S5, S6)

- **R64.** Holding Shift and pressing a pad that holds a clip opens [Push 2](push-2.md) R27's colour picker for that clip, and tapping a ring pad gives the clip that colour (`clip.set_colour`, [themes](themes.md) R14), one undo step. (S5, S9, S10)
- **R65.** Holding Select and pressing a pad selects its slot without launching it (`session.select`), and the display shows the clip's name. (S5, S6)
- **R66.** Holding Duplicate, pressing a pad that holds a clip and then another pad copies the clip into that slot (`session.copy_clip_to_slot`), one undo step; further pads while Duplicate is held copy it again. Holding Delete and pressing a pad deletes its clip (`session.delete`). (S5, S6, S10)
- **R67.** The up and down arrows move the ring one scene, Octave Up and Down eight scenes, the left and right arrows one track, and Page Left and Right eight tracks, by `control_surface.move_ring` ([control surfaces](control-surfaces.md) R29, R31), as far as the set reaches; a button that can't move the ring is dark. - **R69.** Holding Layout in Session Mode ([Push 2](push-2.md) R7's hold) shows Session Overview while it's held; holding Shift and pressing Layout locks it on, and pressing Layout again unlocks it. (S5, S6)
- **R70.** In Session Overview each pad stands for a block of eight tracks by eight scenes, so the grid covers 64 tracks by 64 scenes: the pad in row r from the top and column c covers scenes 8(r − 1) + 1 to 8r and tracks 8(c − 1) + 1 to 8c of the 64-scene part the Scene/Grid buttons choose, the k-th button standing for scenes 64(k − 1) + 1 to 64k where the set has them.
