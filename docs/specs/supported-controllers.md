## The behaviour

Beatlayer drives seven controllers as native control surfaces: Novation's Launchpad X, Launchpad Mini MK3 and Launchpad Pro MK3, Akai Professional's APC mini mk2 and APC40 mkII, and Novation's Launchkey MK3 and Launchkey MK4. Plugged in, each is found by its ports and its identity reply and fills a free row of the Control Surfaces table, and from then on it plays the Session View, mixes the tracks in its session ring and turns the parameters of the device in focus, as each vendor's user guide describes for the controller with Live.

The grid controllers share one surface, the grid surface: a pad per slot of the ring, scene buttons beside it, arrows that move the ring, rows of buttons that stop, mute, solo, arm or select the ring's tracks, faders drawn on the pads, and a drum layout that follows the drum rack on the selected track. Each controller is a profile of it: its ports, its messages and its lights, from the vendor's published programmer's reference, and the controls it has. The keyboard controllers add pots, faders, transport buttons and, on the Launchkeys, a display.

Everything a controller does runs Beatlayer's registered commands; everything it shows comes from the set and the engine's state. Each controller has a simulated device for tests, so every rule here runs headless.

## Rules

### Every supported controller (WP-7.8, and each later package for its own controllers)

- **R1.** The Control Surface chooser lists the seven controllers by their product names: APC mini mk2, APC40 mkII, Launchkey MK3, Launchkey MK4, Launchpad Mini MK3, Launchpad Pro MK3, Launchpad X ([control surfaces](control-surfaces.md) R1). Each supports locking ([control surfaces](control-surfaces.md) R27). (S1)
- **R2.** When ports whose names a supported controller's surface port carries appear (R18, R25, R29, R35, R41, R51, R61, R72), no row names them, and a row is free, Beatlayer asks the input for its identity with the MIDI device inquiry (F0h 7Eh 7Fh 06h 01h F7h) and, when the reply names the controller's maker and, where the vendor's reference gives one, its model, fills the first free row with that surface and those ports. A row a person set to None isn't filled again for those ports while Beatlayer runs (Beatlayer's own). (S6, S12, S14, S16, S18)
- **R3.** A controller's surface port is the surface's alone ([control surfaces](control-surfaces.md) R3 and R4). Its other ports (a Launchpad's or Launchkey's MIDI port, the APC mini mk2's second port) stay ordinary MIDI ports: their notes reach tracks through their Track switch, and filling a row turns that switch on for an input seen for the first time, as every input starts ([MIDI I/O](midi-io.md) R3). (S5, S11, S15, S17)
- **R4.** Each controller reports its own mode by name through `control_surface.list` ([control surfaces](control-surfaces.md) R35): its layout or page (such as Session, Mixer: Volume, Drum, Note, Device Select), what its track buttons do (Clip Stop, Solo, Mute, Arm or Select) and what its faders and knobs reach. (S19)
- **R5.** Each controller has a simulated device for tests: virtual ports on the test MIDI system with the controller's port names, which answer the device inquiry as the controller does, apply every documented message to a model of its lights (each pad's and button's palette index or RGB colour and whether it's static, flashing or pulsing, each fader's and ring's position, the display's text), and send its pads', buttons', faders' and knobs' messages when a test presses or moves them. (S5, S7, S9, S11, S13, S15, S17)

### The grid surface (WP-7.8)

- **R6.** A grid controller's pads are its ring ([control surfaces](control-surfaces.md) R28): the pad in column c from the left and row r from the top is the ring's track c in scene r. (S4, S6, S12, S14)
- **R7.** Pressing a pad whose slot holds a clip launches it through `session.launch`, and releasing it ends a clip that plays while held, as a note mapped to the slot does by its launch mode ([remote mapping](remote-mapping.md)). Pressing a pad whose slot is empty records a new clip into it on an armed track (`session.record_slot`, [session recording](session-recording.md)) and otherwise stops its track's clip through `session.stop_track`, where the slot has a stop button; a pad past the last track or scene does nothing. (S6, S8, S19)
- **R8.** A scene button launches the ring's scene in its row through `session.launch_scene`; one past the last scene does nothing and is dark. (S6, S8, S12, S14)
- **R9.** A pad shows its slot's state, in the clip's colour unless the state gives another: an empty slot dark, an empty slot of an armed track dim red; a stopped clip in its colour; a clip queued to play flashing green; a playing clip pulsing green; a slot queued to record flashing red; a recording clip pulsing red; a pad past the last track or scene dark. A controller whose guide gives its own scheme follows it instead (R37). A scene button is lit dim white while its scene holds a clip, green while one of its clips plays, and dark otherwise (Beatlayer's own). (S4, S6, S8, S12, S19)
- **R10.** A clip's colour is shown as the entry of the controller's palette nearest to it by distance in RGB, the palette's RGB values being those the vendor publishes (Beatlayer's own way of choosing). (S5, S11, S13)
- **R11.** Flashing and pulsing are the controller's own, which keep time with the MIDI clock they receive. While a grid surface runs, it sends its output 24 clocks per quarter note at the set's tempo, each stamped with its moment ([control surfaces](control-surfaces.md) R8), stopping with the surface; the clock goes to the surface port only and starts no transport on it (Beatlayer's own). (S5, S9, S11, S13)
- **R12.** The arrows move the ring one track or one scene ([control surfaces](control-surfaces.md) R29), and each arrow is lit white while the ring can move its way and dark when it can't (Beatlayer's own step and lights where the guide gives none). (S6, S14, S16)
- **R13.** A row of track buttons (or pads) set to a track function acts on the ring's tracks, one per column, and shows each track's state, bright for the state named and dim otherwise: Stop runs `session.stop_track`, quantised as the launch quantisation says, red and bright while the track plays a clip; Solo runs `mixer.set_solo`, blue and bright while soloed; Mute switches the track activator with `mixer.set_mute`, yellow and bright while the track is heard (not muted); Arm runs `mixer.set_arm`, red and bright while armed; Select selects the track as a click on its title bar does (`session.select`, and `arrangement.select_tracks` so the devices and the drum layout follow it), in the track's colour, bright for the selected track. A button past the last track, or one whose track has no such control (a return track's arm or stop), is dark. Where a controller's buttons have one colour, bright is on and dim is off. (S8, S10, S12, S14, S16)
- **R14.** Faders drawn on the grid (on the Launchpad X and Pro MK3) set the ring's tracks' volume, pan or a send, or the device in focus's bank ([control surfaces](control-surfaces.md) R17 to R21), as an absolute control reaching each parameter from its bottom to its top, with the controller's own fader positions sent back whenever the parameter changes, whatever changed it. A volume fader is green, Send A purple, Send B blue, a pan fader the track's colour; a fader past the last track is hidden (S6; the device faders' colour, the bank's index in the palette's rainbow, is Beatlayer's own). (S5, S6, S9, S10)
- **R15.** A grid controller follows the selected track's instrument: on a track whose first instrument at the top level is a drum rack its note layout is the drum layout (R16), and on any other track the controller's own note layout, which plays tracks through its MIDI port (R3). Notes from the controller are heard on the tracks that monitor them, an armed track with monitoring on Auto among them ([MIDI I/O](midi-io.md) R10). (S2, S6, S10)
- **R16.** The drum layout is four 4 × 4 areas: the lower left area plays the sixteen pads the drum rack's pad view shows, the area above it the next sixteen, and the right-hand areas the thirty-two after those, each area bottom row first, left to right. Up and Down move the shown pads by sixteen and Left and Right by four, scrolling the drum rack's pad view with them, and scrolling the pad view in the window moves the layout too. A pad whose drum rack pad holds a chain is lit yellow, the selected drum rack pad blue, an empty one dark; pressing a pad plays it into the track ([control surfaces](control-surfaces.md) R5) and selects that drum rack pad. (S2, S6, S10)
- **R17.** Leaving: a grid surface's stop ([control surfaces](control-surfaces.md) R9) puts out every light it set and sends the message that gives the controller back its standalone state (R18, R25, R29, R35, R41, R51, R61). (S5, S7, S9, S11, S13)

### Launchpad X (WP-7.8)

- **R18.** The Launchpad X surface uses the input and output whose names contain "LPX DAW"; "LPX MIDI" stays a MIDI port (R3). It starts by sending F0h 00h 20h 29h 02h 0Ch 10h 01h F7h (DAW mode) and then F0h 00h 20h 29h 02h 0Ch 00h 00h F7h (the Session layout), and stops by sending F0h 00h 20h 29h 02h 0Ch 10h 00h F7h (Standalone). (S5)
- **R19.** In the Session layout the grid's pads are notes 11 to 88 on channel 1 of the DAW port, the note being ten times the row counted from the bottom plus the column, so the top row (81 to 88) is the ring's first scene. The scene buttons down the right are controllers 89, 79, 69, 59, 49, 39, 29 and 19, top to bottom; the top row's are 91 Up, 92 Down, 93 Left, 94 Right, 95 Session, 96 Note, 97 Custom and 98 Capture MIDI, and the logo is 99. A pad or button is lit by sending its note or controller with the palette index as its value, on channel 1 to stay, 2 to flash and 3 to pulse. (S5, S6)
- **R20.** Pressing Session while the Session layout shows switches between the clips and Mixer, which opens on Volume (Beatlayer's own). In Mixer, the scene buttons choose, top to bottom: Volume, Pan, Send A, Send B, Stop Clip, Mute, Solo and Record Arm. The chosen one is bright and the others dim white; pressing the chosen one again goes back to the scene buttons. Volume, Pan, Send A and Send B show eight grid faders over the ring's tracks (R14): vertical for Volume and the sends, horizontal for Pan with the top row the ring's first track; Stop Clip, Mute, Solo and Record Arm turn the grid's bottom row into that track function (R13), the other rows keeping their clips. Holding a Mixer button and releasing it after pressing other controls goes back to the view before it. (S6)
- **R21.** The grid faders are set up with F0h 00h 20h 29h 02h 0Ch 01h 00h <orientation> then four bytes per fader (its index, unipolar 0 or bipolar 1, its controller number, its colour) and F7h, and shown with the DAW Faders layout (00h 0Dh); their positions go to and come from the DAW port's channel 5 on the faders' controller numbers, which are Beatlayer's own: 21 to 28. Pan faders are bipolar, the others unipolar. (S5)
- **R22.** Capture MIDI (98) starts and stops Session Record when pressed and released within 500 ms (`session.record`), and runs Capture MIDI (`midi.capture`) when held longer (Beatlayer's own split between the guide's two uses of the button). It is lit red while Session Record is on and dim white otherwise. (S6, S19)
- **R23.** On a track whose instrument is a drum rack (R15), the surface sends F0h 00h 20h 29h 02h 0Ch 0Fh 01h F7h (the simple drum rack, which Beatlayer drives), lights its pads on the DAW port's channels 9, 10 and 11 as R19 does on 1 to 3, takes its pads' notes from channel 9 and plays the drum layout (R16), the arrows lit when they can move; on another track it sends 0Fh 00h, leaving Note mode to the controller. Note shows the layout and Custom the controller's own modes, which Beatlayer leaves alone. (S5, S6)
- **R24.** The mode buttons the controller doesn't let the DAW light (Session, Note, Custom) are left to it; Session's own two colours are left at the controller's defaults. (S5)

### Launchpad Mini MK3 (WP-7.8)

- **R25.** The Launchpad Mini MK3 surface uses the ports whose names contain "LPMiniMK3 DAW"; "LPMiniMK3 MIDI" stays a MIDI port. It starts by sending F0h 00h 20h 29h 02h 0Dh 10h 01h F7h and then 00h 00h (Session) in the same header, and stops with 10h 00h. (S7)
- **R26.** Its grid and lights are the Launchpad X's (R19): pads 11 to 88, the top row's 91 Up, 92 Down, 93 Left and 94 Right, 95 Session and 96 to 98 the controller's own Drums, Keys and User. The right-hand column's 89 to 29 are scene buttons for the ring's first seven scenes, and 19 is Stop/Solo/Mute. (S7, S8)
- **R27.** Stop/Solo/Mute steps the grid's bottom row through clips, Stop, Solo and Mute and back to clips, each a track function (R13); the button is lit white for clips, red for Stop, blue for Solo and yellow for Mute. (S8)
- **R28.** Drums, Keys and User are the controller's own layouts on its MIDI port; while one shows, the ring stays where it was and its pads are the controller's. The Mini has no faders and no Mixer. (S7, S8)

### Launchpad Pro MK3 (WP-7.8)

- **R29.** The Launchpad Pro MK3 surface uses the ports whose names contain "LPProMK3 DAW"; "LPProMK3 MIDI" stays a MIDI port and "LPProMK3 DIN" is left alone. It starts by sending F0h 00h 20h 29h 02h 0Eh 10h 01h F7h and then 00h 00h 00h 00h (the Session layout, page 0), and stops with 10h 00h. The controller reports each layout it shows with the same layout message, and the surface follows it. (S9)
- **R30.** Its grid is notes 11 to 88 lit as R19 says. Its buttons are controllers: Shift 90; along the top 91 Left, 92 Right, 93 Session, 94 Note, 95 Chord, 96 Custom, 97 Sequencer and 98 Projects; down the left 80 Up, 70 Down, 60 Clear, 50 Duplicate, 40 Quantise, 30 Fixed Length, 20 Play and 10 Record; down the right 89 to 19, the scene buttons; the Track Select row 101 to 108; and the track controls along the bottom, 1 Record Arm, 2 Mute, 3 Solo, 4 Volume, 5 Pan, 6 Sends, 7 Device and 8 Stop Clip. Chord, Custom, Sequencer and Projects are the controller's own. (S9, S10)
- **R31.** Record Arm, Mute, Solo and Stop Clip make the Track Select row that track function (R13), and pressing the lit one again gives the row back to selecting tracks: with no track control chosen, a Track Select button selects its ring track (Beatlayer's own, as the guide doesn't say). Volume, Pan and Sends show grid faders (R14) in the Fader layout's pages 0, 1 and 2, set up with fader banks 0, 1 and 2; pressing Sends again steps to the next send, and pressing Volume, Pan or Device again gives the Session layout back. The four banks' faders take controller numbers 21 to 28, 29 to 36, 37 to 44 and 45 to 52 (Beatlayer's own, as the reference leaves them to the host). Device shows the Fader layout's page 3 with eight faders over the device in focus's bank, the Track Select row choosing among the selected track's first eight devices (`device.select`), the chosen one bright. (S9, S10)
- **R32.** Shift with a pad selects its slot (`session.select`) without launching; Clear with a pad deletes its clip (`session.delete`); Duplicate with a pad copies the clip to the slot below, replacing what's there (`session.copy_clip_to_slot`); Shift with Duplicate and a pad doubles the clip's loop (`clip.duplicate_loop`); Quantise with a pad quantises the clip's notes to sixteenths (`note.quantize`). Play starts the transport when stopped and stops it when running (`transport.play_stop`); Record starts and stops Session Record, and Shift with Record runs Capture MIDI. Shift with the track controls runs Undo (Record Arm), Redo (Mute), the metronome (Solo) and Tap Tempo (Sends). While Shift is held, the buttons it changes light gold. (S10, S19)
- **R33.** Holding Session shows Session Overview: each pad stands for an eight-by-eight block of the Session View, the ring's block light brown, blocks holding a playing clip pulsing green and the others blue, a block past the set dark; pressing a pad moves the ring to its block ([control surfaces](control-surfaces.md) R29), and releasing Session goes back. (S10)
- **R34.** On a track whose instrument is a drum rack, Note shows Beatlayer's drum layout (F0h 00h 20h 29h 02h 0Eh 0Fh 01h F7h, lit and read as R23 says); on any other track the controller's own Note mode (0Fh 00h). Fixed Length and Record Quantise are left to the controller's own Sequencer and to Beatlayer's settings respectively: the surface doesn't change them (Beatlayer's own, as their effect in the Session isn't given). (S9, S10)

### APC mini mk2 (WP-7.9)

- **R35.** The APC mini mk2 surface uses the input and output named for its first port (the first of its two ports in the list, whatever the platform calls them; the second is never offered to a row); its second port carries Note mode to tracks (R3). It starts by sending F0h 47h 7Fh 4Fh 60h 00h 04h 00h then Beatlayer's version (major, minor, fix) and F7h, and takes the reply's nine values as the faders' positions, for Takeover Mode: the surface tells the shell where each fader rests whenever the parameter the fader reaches changes, so a fader that crosses its parameter on its first message takes it under Pick Up (Beatlayer's own). It stops by putting out every light. (S11, S12)
- **R36.** The grid's pads are notes 00h to 3Fh on channel 1, 00h at the bottom left, rising left to right and then up a row, so the top row (38h to 3Fh) is the ring's first scene. The track buttons are notes 64h to 6Bh, the scene buttons 70h to 77h top to bottom, Shift 7Ah; faders 1 to 8 are controllers 30h to 37h and the ninth 38h, on channel 1. (S11)
- **R37.** A pad is lit by a note-on of its note with the palette index as velocity, the channel giving its brightness or motion (channel 1 at 10 % to channel 7 at 100 %, channels 8 to 11 pulsing and 12 to 16 blinking at the vendor's rates). A stopped clip shows its colour at 50 % brightness and a playing clip at 100 %; a queued clip blinks its colour at an eighth; a recording clip pulses red at a quarter; a launch waiting to record into an empty slot blinks red at an eighth; an empty slot of an armed track is red at 25 % (the brightness levels are Beatlayer's own within the guide's dim and bright). The track and scene buttons' single lights take 0 for off, 1 for on and 2 for blinking. (S11, S12)
- **R38.** Without Shift, a scene button launches its scene (R8) and a track button runs its track function (R13), Clip Stop at first.
