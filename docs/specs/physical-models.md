## The behaviour

Collision, Tension, Electric and Corpus make their sound by simulating an object that rings when something strikes, plucks, bows or blows it: a bar, a marimba's tuned bar, a stretched membrane, a plate, a string, a pipe or a tube. Beatlayer simulates them in two ways, both in `bl-dsp`, so an instrument and an effect share them without depending on each other. A modal resonator is a bank of decaying sine partials, each a two-pole resonator tuned to one of the object's natural frequencies, whose ratios come from the physics of its shape. A waveguide is a delay line that a wave travels round, losing a little at each pass, tuned to its note by a fractional delay and made inharmonic by an all-pass filter in its loop; its ends can reflect as a closed or an open end.

Both kinds take an excitation signal, one per voice, and both read the controls the instruments' manuals describe: the decay, a material that makes high partials die faster, a radius or size, an inharmonicity that stretches or squeezes the partials, the position where the object is struck and the positions where it is heard. They stay stable at every setting and sample rate, run every voice in the plug-in kit's lanes and allocate nothing on the audio thread.

Every partial ratio, decay law and position law here comes from acoustics references and published synthesis papers, never from Live's sound.

## Rules

**Modal resonators**

- **R1.** `bl_dsp::physical` has a modal resonator for six shapes, each a table of partial ratios to the fundamental: Beam, a free bar (1, 2.756, 5.404, 8.933, 13.34, 18.64, then from the second partial n at (2n + 1)² / 3.0112²); Marimba, a bar tuned so its second partial is two octaves up and its third about 10 times the fundamental (1, 4, 10, then each of the free bar's ratios from its fourth, multiplied by 10 over the free bar's third); Cantilever, a bar clamped at one end, for a tine (1, 6.267, 17.55, 34.39, 56.84, the higher ones at (2n − 1)² / 1.194²); Membrane, a rectangular membrane whose mode (m, n) sits at √((m / a)² + (n / b)²) over the (1, 1) mode's, a and b its sides; Circular Membrane, at the zeros of Bessel functions over the first (1, 1.594, 2.136, 2.296, 2.653, 2.918, 3.156, 3.501, 3.600, 3.648, and on); and Plate, a simply supported rectangular plate whose mode (m, n) sits at ((m / a)² + (n / b)²) over the (1, 1) mode's. Each partial sounds within 1 cent of its table ratio times the fundamental, from 40 Hz to 4 kHz at 44.1 to 192 kHz. (S1, S4, S6)
- **R2.** A Membrane's and a Plate's Ratio sets a / b, from 0.25 to 4 (1 by default, Beatlayer's own range), and their modes are taken in order of frequency. (S1, S6)
- **R3.** A modal resonator has a number of partials set by its quality: 8, 16, 32 or 64 (Collision's Eco, Low, Medium and High), 32 for Meld's resonators; a partial whose frequency reaches 0.45 of the sample rate is left out, so a high note plays fewer partials and none aliases. (S1, S4; the counts are Beatlayer's own)
- **R4.** Inharmonicity I, from −1 to 1 (0 by default), moves partial n's ratio rₙ to rₙ^(1 + I / 2): positive values push the upper partials up, negative values bring them down, and the fundamental stays. (S1; Beatlayer's own law)
- **R5.** Decay sets the fundamental's time to fall 60 dB, from 1 ms to 60 s; Material m, from 0 to 1, sets how the other partials' times follow it: partial n falls 60 dB in Decay × rₙ^(−2(1 − m)), so at m = 1 (glass, metal) every partial lasts as long as the fundamental and at m = 0 (wood, rubber) a partial twice as high lasts a quarter as long. Measured on a struck resonator, each partial's time is within 5 % of this. (S1, S7; Beatlayer's own law)
- **R6.** Brightness b, from −1 to 1 (0 by default), weights partial n's gain by rₙ^(b / 2), so positive values lift the upper partials and negative values cut them, without changing their decays. (S1; Beatlayer's own law)
- **R7.** Partial n's gain is the product of the shape's mode shape at the excitation position and at the listening position, each from 0 (the centre) to 1 (the edge), as the references give the shapes: a bar's free modes, a membrane's and a plate's products of sines across each axis (a circular membrane's Bessel functions along a radius), and a string's sin(nπp). A partial whose mode has a node at the excitation position doesn't sound: struck at the centre of a string (p = 0.5), its even harmonics stay silent below −60 dB. A stereo resonator has two listening positions, one per channel. A position runs along a bar's length from its middle to an end, along a membrane's or a plate's diagonal from the centre to a corner, and along a circular membrane's radius. Each partial's shape peaks at 1, and the gains of a channel are scaled to sum to at most 1, so no position or brightness makes the output louder than the partials together. (S1, S7)

**Waveguides**

- **R8.** A waveguide has a delay loop whose length gives the note: an integer delay, a fractional delay by a Thiran all-pass, and the group delays of its loop filter and dispersion filter subtracted, so its fundamental is within 1 cent of the note for every key whose fundamental lies below a quarter of the sample rate, at 44.1 to 192 kHz, and is harmonic within half a cent at Inharm 0. A loop filter (a one-pole low-pass in the loop and a gain) sets its decay, from Decay, and its damping of high harmonics, from Material or Damping; a dispersion all-pass in the loop stretches its partials, from Inharm, as a stiff string's fₙ = n f₁ √(1 + B n²) with B from 0 to 0.001 at Inharm 0 to 1, two all-passes whose coefficient puts the sixth harmonic (the highest below one radian per sample) where the law does, which holds the others within 3 cents up to the sixth. (S2, S8, S9)
- **R9.** A String is a waveguide reflecting with inverted sign at both ends, so it plays every harmonic. A Tube, closed at both ends, plays every harmonic too, reflecting without inversion at both. A Pipe has a closed end and an end whose Opening, from 0 % to 100 %, moves its reflection from closed to open: at 0 % it plays the odd harmonics only, an octave below a Tube of its length, and at 100 % every harmonic, like an open pipe; between, the even harmonics rise as the opening grows: the far end's reflection runs from closed (+1) to open (−1), so the loop's gain is scaled by |2 × Opening − 1|, and at 50 % the loop has no gain left and the excitation passes through with no ring. (S1, S6, S8)
- **R10.** A Pipe's or Tube's Radius, from 0 to 1, lengthens its decay and keeps more of its high harmonics as it grows, and adds an end correction of 0.6 times the bore's radius to each open end's length, so a very large radius lowers the note: the loop's length is worked out with the correction, and at the largest radius on an open Pipe the fundamental falls by up to a semitone. The bore's radius is 0.0495 × Radius × the pipe's length, a closed end takes no correction, a Pipe has one open end and its far end counts by its opening, and Radius multiplies the decay by 1 + Radius and moves Material towards 1 by its fraction. (S1, S6; Beatlayer's own scale)
- **R11.** A waveguide takes its excitation at a position along it, from 0 (an end) to 0.5 (the middle), and is heard at a pickup position, so its harmonics are shaped as a string struck and heard there. A waveguide can also be driven by a nonlinear interaction at its excitation position, a model that reads the guide's velocity there and returns a force (a bow's friction, a hammer's or a plectrum's contact), and can have a damper and a terminating finger or fret, each a loss and a partial reflection at its position; the laws of each are in the instrument's spec. The excitation and the listening positions are combs read between samples by four-point Lagrange interpolation, which keeps the low harmonics' nulls below −60 dB and lets a position move every frame without a click. (S2, S8)

**Throughout**

- **R12.** Both kinds take one excitation input per voice and run their voices in the plug-in kit's lanes; a resonator's state is made with it, its partial tables are worked out off the audio thread when the shape, quality, ratio or inharmonicity changes and handed over without freeing on the audio thread, or made over in place, without allocating, by `ModalTable::rebuild` for every shape but the circular membrane, whose Bessel zeros are searched for. A bank's voices are laid out as the kit's filter bank's are: its stride is the voices rounded up to a multiple of 16, the excitation is one value per voice per frame, and the output holds the left listening position's voices then the right's for each frame. (S10)
- **R13.** No setting grows without bound: every modal partial's pole lies inside the unit circle and every waveguide's loop gain is below 1 at every frequency, at 44.1 to 192 kHz; two resonators in series, the first's output exciting the second, stay bounded too. A property test drives random settings and excitations for ten seconds of audio and finds the output's peak below +24 dBFS and falling after the excitation stops. The excitation enters each partial and each waveguide scaled by 4 times its pole's, or its loop's, distance from the unit circle (at most 1), so a sine at a partial's or a harmonic's frequency comes out at most 12 dB louder however long it rings, and one resonator's output can drive another's. (S7, S8)
- **R14.** Denormals are flushed in every resonator's state, so a decay of 60 s that has fallen silent costs no more than silence: the states are flushed on the control grid, and a waveguide's loop signal is faded to exact zero below 10⁻¹² (240 dB below full scale). (S10)
- **R15.** Sixteen resonators of 32 partials, or sixteen waveguides, render at 48 kHz inside the architecture's device budget, and nothing on the audio thread allocates, locks or blocks. (S10)
- **R16.** A resonator's output for a frame depends only on its excitation, its settings and its seed (for a random hit position, device framework R29), never on how blocks fall: the same at any block size, run after run. (S10)
- **R17.** A change of Decay, Material, Brightness, a position, Radius or Opening moves the running resonator smoothly, without a click, on a grid of 32 frames counted from the start of the bank's life, so the render is the same at every block size; a change of tuning moves a modal resonator's partials at once and a waveguide's delay through its fractional part without a click, for a move of up to a sample or two of delay; a larger one steps the delay line's read point once per sample of it. (S10; Beatlayer's own)
- **R18.** `bl-dsp` depends on no engine, model, device or UI crate; the models use the plug-in kit's SIMD lanes and nothing else of the kit. (S10)
