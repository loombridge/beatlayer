# Electric: an electric piano of a hammer, a fork, a damper and a pickup

- **Status:** implemented (every rule below has a passing test)
- **Work packages:** WP-8.23 (this spec, R1 to R14). The tine and tone bar are modal resonators of [the physical models](physical-models.md); voices and per-note bends are [the instruments' voice section](instrument-voices.md); the oversampler is [the device framework](device-framework.md) R8
- **Parity-matrix row:** Instruments: Collision, Tension, Electric (Electric)
- **Checked against:** the Live 12 reference manual (online edition), read 30 October 2026 (chapter 31, section 31.5: 31.5.1 to 31.5.5); the Live 12 release notes, 12.0 (every instrument plays MPE)

## The behaviour

Electric models an electric piano. A key throws a hammer at a fork: a thin tine that the hammer strikes and a heavier tone bar coupled to it, which rings on. A pickup beside the tine turns its motion into the sound, adding the harmonics of its own nonlinearity as the tine swings closer and further; a damper stops the fork when the key is released and adds its own small noises. The global section holds the volume, the voices, the tuning and the bend ranges.

The device's sections, Hammer, Fork, Damper/Pickup and Global, sit across the top of the panel, each a heading over its main knobs, and the table of further controls under them is that of the section clicked last (layout L-DEV-063). Every law here is Beatlayer's own reading of the manual's controls on a modal model of the fork.

## Rules

- **R1.** Beatlayer ships Electric (`beatlayer.electric`), an instrument listed under the browser's Instruments label, with factory presets of Beatlayer's own. Its body is Live's single page, 345 points wide (layout L-DEV-063): the four sections Hammer, Fork, Damper/Pickup and Global across the top, each a heading over its knobs, and under them a black display with the mechanism drawn at its left and, at its right, the table of the section clicked last: a click on a section's heading or the space round its knobs shows its page (a pages node in the regions style, device framework R41; `device.set_page` names it). Hammer is the page a new device shows. (S1, S6)
- **R2.** The hammer strikes the tine once per note with a force Force's Amount (0 to 100 %) sets, moved by velocity and key (Vel and Key amounts, −100 % to 100 %): a raised-cosine pulse whose width Stiffness sets (soft, 5 ms; hard, 0.2 ms), moved by velocity and key, so a harder hammer is shorter and brighter. Force's Amount, Stiffness, Noise and Pitch open at 50 %; every Vel and Key reads 0 to 100 with no unit, 50 where it does nothing, 100 the old +100 % and 0 the old −100 %. Noise (0 to 100 %, 50 % in a new device) adds a burst of band-pass noise from the device's seed, one stream per note, at Pitch (100 Hz to 10 kHz, read in per cent, 50 % being 1 kHz) decaying over Decay (1 ms to 1 s, read in per cent, 50 % being about 32 ms), its level following the key by Key (0 to 100, 50 doing nothing, up to 12 dB over four octaves from C3 at 100); the burst enters the pickup with the tine's motion. (S2; the pulse and ranges are Beatlayer's own)
- **R3.** The tine is the physical models' Cantilever resonator at the note's pitch (physical models R1); Color (0 to 100 %, 50 % neutral, below it darker and above it brighter) tilts its partials' gains from the fundamental to the upper partials (physical models R6's Brightness); Decay (read in per cent, from 100 ms at 0 to 60 s at 100 %, evenly in octaves, 50 % being 2.4 s) is its decay while the key is held; Key (0 to 100, 50 doing nothing) moves its level with the key by up to 12 dB over four octaves from C3. (S3, S6)
- **R4.** The tone bar is a Beam resonator (physical models R1) tuned to the note's fundamental and driven by the tine, so the fundamental rings on after the tine's high partials die; its Decay (in per cent, on the tine's scale) is its own. (S3, S6; the coupling is Beatlayer's own)
- **R5.** Release (read in per cent, from 10 ms at 0 to 10 s at 100 %, 50 % being 316 ms) is the fork's decay, tine and tone bar together, after the key is let go: from the note's end both fall 60 dB within Release, and a tine or tone bar that already decays faster keeps its own decay, since the damper only shortens one. (S3)
- **R6.** The tine and tone bar ring at their pitch across the keyboard within 1 cent, and their decay follows Decay and Release within 5 %. (S3, S6)
- **R7.** The pickup turns the tine's displacement x into a signal through a nonlinearity set by its geometry: Type R (electrodynamic) responds to the change of a magnetic field, as 1 / (d − x)² differentiated; Type W (electrostatic) to capacitance, as 1 / (d − x); each is a balanced pair, the same law mirrored on the other side of the tine, so that straight in front the response is odd and only odd harmonics come out, and R's differentiator passes the note's own pitch at unity down to 100 Hz (a lower note falls with its pitch); d is Distance (closer, more distortion), from one to four times the tine's full swing, and the tine's position is bent smoothly short of 0.92 d on either side; Symmetry (0 to 100 %, 50 % in front) offsets the tine's rest position against the pickup's centre, so away from 50 % even harmonics rise. Input drives the fork's signal into it and Output sets its level, each read in per cent (0 silent, 42 % unity and 100 % +12 dB, along a power law; both open at 42 %), Output moved by Out Key (0 to 100, 50 doing nothing, up to 12 dB over four octaves from C3). (S4; the laws are Beatlayer's own readings of the two pickup types)
- **R8.** The pickup's nonlinearity runs at four times the sample rate through the device framework's oversampler (device framework R8), and the device reports that latency. (S6)
- **R9.** The damper lands when a note ends, stopping the fork as R5 says, and adds a short noise: Tone (dark to bright) its colour, Level (0 to 100 %, a square law, 50 % a quarter of the full level) its level, and Att/Rel (−100 % to 100 %, 0 in a new device) where it sounds, at the note's start (the damper lifting), its end (landing) or both; it is a burst of about a tenth of a second added after the pickup. (S4)
- **R10.** The global section: Volume (−∞ to 0 dB), Voices (1 to 32, 32 by default; a chooser of whole numbers; instrument voices R3), Semi (−48 to +48), Detune (−50 to +50 cents, read as a plain number, 0.00), Stretch (instrument voices R17), PB Range (0 to 24 semitones, 12.00 by default, read as a plain number) and Note PB (0 to 48 semitones, 0 by default: Live opens Electric with no per-note bend; instrument voices R14). (S5)
- **R11.** Every control is a parameter, automatable and modulatable (rule G2), and Electric's banks hold every one. (S6)
- **R12.** Sixteen voices render at 48 kHz inside the device budget. (S6)
- **R13.** The output is the same at any block size and run after run; nothing on the audio thread allocates, locks or blocks. (S6)
- **R14.** A note's sound depends on its velocity through the hammer's force and stiffness alone, as their Vel amounts give, so at every amount 0 two velocities sound alike but for level: the velocity scales the note's level after the pickup, so it never changes how hard the pickup is driven. (S2)
- **R15.** What Live reads in per cent only is read so here, on laws that are Beatlayer's own (every range and default as Live reads it, observed in Live 12.4.16 on 2 November 2026): the noise's Pitch spans 100 Hz to 10 kHz and Decay 1 ms to 1 s, the tine's and the tone bar's Decay 100 ms to 60 s and Release 10 ms to 10 s, each evenly in octaves; Input and Output are the power law of R7, the damper's Level R9's square law, and every Vel and Key (0 to 100, 50 doing nothing) is the old amount −100 % to 100 % halved and shifted. Tine and Tone (0 to 100 %, 50 % each in a new device) are the levels of the tine and of the tone bar in the sound: 50 % leaves each as the fork rings it, 0 silences it and 100 % doubles it, the bar being driven by the tine whatever the tine's level. (S3; the laws are Beatlayer's own; Live's are not measured)
- **R16.** The panel holds Live's controls in Live's order and kinds and none else (layout L-DEV-063). Across the top: Stiffness and Noise (small knobs), Tine and Tone, Symmetry and the Type stack (R over W), Volume. The Hammer table: Stiffness (Vel, Key), Force (Amount, Vel, Key), Noise (Pitch, Decay, Key). The Fork table: Tine (Color, Decay, Key), Tone (Release, Decay). The Damper/Pickup table: Damper (Tone, Att/Rel, Level), Pickup (Input, Output, Key), Distance. The Global table: Keyboard (Voices, Semi, Detune, Stretch, PB Range, Note PB). The pickup opens at R, the first of its two types. A new device opens at 50 % on every knob and amount but Input and Output (42 %), Att/Rel (0 %) and Volume (0 dB). (S1; Live's panel observed in Live 12.4.16, 2 November 2026)
