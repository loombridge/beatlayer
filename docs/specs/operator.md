## The behaviour

Operator is an FM synthesiser with four oscillators, A to D, each a sine or another waveform with its own envelope and level. An oscillator either sounds at the output, a carrier, or modulates another oscillator's phase, a modulator; eleven algorithms set which oscillators modulate which. An oscillator that nothing modulates can modulate itself through its Feedback. Each oscillator's frequency is a ratio of the note's, coarse and fine, or a fixed frequency that ignores the key. Its waveform is one of a list of built-in waves or a user wave drawn from the levels of its first 16, 32 or 64 partials.

Seven envelopes shape the sound: one per oscillator, and the filter's, the pitch envelope and the LFO's. Each can loop while a note is held, repeat on the beat, repeat on the song's sixteenths, or ignore the note's end. Time scales every envelope. An LFO, at sub-audio or audio rates, moves the oscillators and the filter. The filter is the instruments' shared filter, followed by a waveshaper. Velocity, the key, aftertouch, the pitch wheel and the mod wheel each reach two destinations from a list. Glide slides each new note from the last; Spread doubles each note into two detuned copies, left and right.

The device is drawn as Live draws it: a shell of sections across the body, global, the four oscillators, the LFO, the filter and the pitch envelope, and a display whose contents follow the section selected.

## Rules

**The FM core (WP-8.6)**

- **R1.** Each voice has four operators, A to D, in the plug-in kit's lanes. An operator's output is its level times its envelope times its waveform read at its phase plus the sum of its modulators' outputs scaled to radians: a modulator whose output reaches amplitude a moves its carrier's phase by up to 4a radians (index β = 4a), times FM Drive where a route moves it (Beatlayer's own scale). Its phase advances by its frequency each frame. With one modulator at ratio 1 into one carrier, the carrier's sidebands sit at the Bessel amplitudes Jₙ(β) of their index within 0.5 dB, for β from 0.5 to 8. (S1, S9)
- **R2.** Algorithm chooses one of eleven graphs, in the manual's order, each read top to bottom, a modulator's output feeding the phase of the operators under it and the carriers summed to the output: (1) D → C → B → A; (2) C and D → B → A; (3) C → B, and B and D → A; (4) D → B and C, and B and C → A; (5) D → C, and C → A and B, with A and B the carriers; (6) D → C → B, with A a second carrier; (7) B, C and D → A; (8) D → C and B → A, carriers C and A; (9) D → A, B and C, three carriers; (10) D → C, with A and B further carriers; (11) A, B, C and D, all carriers. Every modulator is a later letter than the operator it modulates. Switching one operator on at a time, with the others off, reaches the output only where its graph makes it a carrier. (S1; the graphs are Beatlayer's reading of the manual's algorithm figure, checked against the figure on 1 November 2026, which corrected the draft's graphs for 3 to 10)
- **R3.** An operator that no other operator modulates in the chosen algorithm has Feedback, from 0 to 100 % (0 % by default), modulating its own phase by its output averaged over its last two frames, at up to 1.5 radians at 100 %; it does nothing on a modulated operator, which shows it greyed. At 100 % Feedback and full level the output stays bounded at 44.1 to 192 kHz. (S1, S2, S7; the scale and the averaging are Beatlayer's own)
- **R4.** An operator's frequency is the note's times its ratio: Coarse (0.5, then 1 to 48 in whole numbers, 1 by default) plus Fine (0 to 1,000, read as thousandths, 0 by default). With Fixed on (off by default), it is Freq (1 to 1,000 Hz) times Multi (0.1, 1, 10, 100, 1,000 or 10,000), whatever the key, down to 0.1 Hz; a fixed frequency at or above 0.45 of the sample rate is silent. Osc < Vel, from −100 % to 100 % (0 % by default), moves the ratio by up to an octave at velocity 127 (down for negative amounts), and its Q switch rounds the velocity-shifted Coarse to the nearest Coarse step (Fine is added after). (S2, S7; the ranges and the velocity law are Beatlayer's own)
- **R5.** An operator's Level runs from −∞ to 0 dB (0 dB for A and −∞ dB for B, C and D in a new device, Beatlayer's own defaults) and its switch, on by default, removes it from the graph when off, as if its level were −∞ dB. (S2, S7)
- **R6.** Wave offers the built-in waveforms Sine, Sine 4 Bit, Sine 8 Bit, Saw 3, Saw 4, Saw 6, Saw 8, Saw 16, Saw 32, Saw 64, Saw D, Square 3, Square 4, Square 6, Square 8, Square 16, Square 32, Square 64, Square D, Triangle, Noise Looped and Noise White, and User. Each saw and square is resynthesised from the number of harmonics in its name; Sine 4 Bit and Sine 8 Bit are sines in 16 and 256 levels, Saw D and Square D their naive digital forms; Noise Looped is a fixed 1-second loop of noise from Beatlayer's own generator, read once a second whatever the key, Noise White fresh noise from the device's seeded generator. Every wave but the two noises and the D forms is played from the plug-in kit's band-limited wavetables, so at the top key nothing folds back above −80 dB. (S2; the list is Beatlayer's reading of the manual's naming, and the bit depths are its own)
- **R7.** A user wave is the levels of its first 16, 32 or 64 partials (0 to 1 each, the first at 1 and the rest at 0 in a new user wave), with Repeat, Off or from 0 to 100 %: off, partials above the count are silent; on, the drawn pattern repeats above it, copy k (from 1) scaled by (1 − Repeat)^k, until the partials pass half the sample rate at the lowest key. Its table is built off the audio thread and swapped in through the kit's cache, never freed on the audio thread; each partial plays within 0.1 dB of its level. (S2, S7; Beatlayer's own Repeat law)
- **R8.** Phase, 0 to 360° (0° by default), is where an operator starts; with Retrigger on (on by default) each note on a free voice starts it there, and off it runs freely from where it was; a stolen, retriggered or legato voice keeps its phase, so taking it never clicks. (S2, S7)
- **R9.** Antialias, on in a new device, runs the operators at twice the sample rate through the device framework's oversampler (device framework R8), and the device reports that latency; off, they run at the sample rate. Interpolation, on in a new device, reads the operators' and the LFO's tables with interpolation; off, without. Tone, 0 to 100 % (70 % by default), limits the partials every operator's table plays: at 100 % up to half the sample rate, and each 25 % below that halves the limit, so lower Tone is darker and folds back less. (S2, S6; the laws are Beatlayer's own)
- **R10.** The carriers' outputs are summed and divided by the number of carriers in the algorithm, so changing algorithms never makes the output louder than a single carrier at the same level (Beatlayer's own normalisation). Nothing the core does on the audio thread allocates, locks or blocks, and its output is the same at any block size. (S1, S10)

**Operator, part 1 (WP-8.12)**

- **R11.** Beatlayer ships Operator (`beatlayer.operator`), an instrument listed under the browser's Instruments label, with four oscillators on the core (R1 to R10), each with its switch, Coarse, Fine, Fixed, Freq, Multi, Level, Wave, Feedback, Phase, Retrigger, Repeat, Osc < Vel and Q, Level < Vel (0 to 100 %, the voice section's velocity law, [polysynth](polysynth.md) R15's) and Level < Key (−100 % to 100 %, up to ±6 dB per octave from C3), all parameters. (S7; the ranges are Beatlayer's own)
- **R12.** Each oscillator's envelope has Initial, Peak and Sustain levels (−∞ to 0 dB) and Attack (straight in amplitude), Decay and Release (exponential, Release to silence) times from 0 ms to 20 s; a voice ends when every carrier's envelope has reached silence after its release. (S4; the time range is Beatlayer's own)
- **R13.** The filter's, the pitch's and the LFO's envelopes have Initial, Peak, Sustain and End levels (−100 % to 100 %) and Attack, Decay and Release slopes from −1 to 1 (0 straight): a segment's shape is x^(2^(−2s)) of its share of the way for slope s, so positive slopes start fast and end slow and negative ones start slow. (S4; the curve is Beatlayer's own)
- **R14.** Each envelope's Loop mode is None (the default), Loop, Beat, Sync or Trigger. Loop: once a held note's envelope reaches Sustain, it returns from its current level to Initial over Loop Time (0 ms to 20 s) and starts its attack again, until the note ends. Beat: the envelope starts again from its current level every Repeat (1/16, 1/8, 1/4, 1/2, 1, 2, 4 or 8 bars or beats, Beatlayer's own list) after the note's start, measured in song beats at the tempo, unquantised. Sync: as Beat, its first restart on the next sixteenth of the song's grid after the note's start and each later one a Repeat after that, while the song plays; with the transport stopped, as Beat. Trigger: the envelope runs Attack and Decay and goes straight into Release whatever the note's length, so the note's end changes nothing. (S4)
- **R15.** Time, from −100 % to 100 % (0 % by default), multiplies every envelope time by 2^(4 × Time) — 16 times longer at 100 %, 16 times shorter at −100 % — without changing any level, and leaves Beat's and Sync's Repeat and the matrix's Time alone; Time < Key (−100 % to 100 %) multiplies them by 2^(−k × Time < Key) for a note k octaves above C3; each envelope's Time < Vel (−100 % to 100 %) by 2^(−2 × Time < Vel × (v − 0.5)) at velocity v from 0 to 1. (S4, S6; the laws are Beatlayer's own)
- **R16.** The pitch envelope, with its switch (off in a new device), moves the oscillators whose Destination A switch is on (A to D, and the LFO's rate for its LFO switch) by its value times Pitch Env (−100 % to 100 %), an envelope level of 100 % at Pitch Env 100 % being 48 semitones; Destination B sends it to one more target with Amount B. (S6; the 48-semitone scale is Beatlayer's own)
- **R17.** Volume (−∞ to 0 dB), Pan, Pan < Key (low notes left, high notes right, at 100 % hard left and right four octaves either side of C3) and Pan < Random (each note panned by a seeded random share up to that amount) are parameters; Transpose (−48 to +48 semitones) moves every note, notes already sounding included; Voices (1 to 32, 6 by default) and Retrigger are the voice section's (instrument voices R3, R5, R6), Voices at 1 playing Mono with Legato on. (S6, S8)
- **R18.** Operator's body is a shell and display (device framework R41): a strip of sections across the body — the global section with its display, then Oscillators A, B, C and D, the LFO, the filter and the pitch section — each section showing its main controls, and the display showing the selected section's further controls (its page). Selecting a section is view state; a new device shows Oscillator A's page. (S6, S7; the order follows the manual's 31.9.10 list)
- **R19.** Every control besides the menu's options is a parameter, automatable and modulatable (rule G2), and Operator's banks for control surfaces hold every one of them in named banks of eight. (S10)
- **R20.** Per-note pitch bend follows the voice section (instrument voices R14), on by default: Enable Per-Note Pitch Bend is a menu option of the title bar's menu, with 48 semitones for a per-note bend of ±1. (S7, S8)
- **R21.** Sixteen voices with four operators each render at 48 kHz inside the device budget, and the device's output is the same at any block size, Beat and Sync envelopes included through the engine's transport. (S10)
- **R22.** Operator ships with factory presets of Beatlayer's own. (S10)

**Operator, part 2 (WP-8.13)**

- **R23.** The LFO, with its switch (on in a new device), plays Sine, Square, Triangle, Saw Up, Saw Down, Sample & Hold and Noise (band-pass filtered noise), each band-limited, at Rate (0 to 100) in Range Low (50 s a cycle to 30 Hz, logarithmically), Hi (8 Hz to 12 kHz) or Sync (a division from 1/64 to 8 bars of the song's tempo), Rate < Key (0 to 100 %, doubling per octave at 100 %), Amount (0 to 100 %) and Amt < Vel, and its own envelope (R13) scaling it. Retrigger restarts it per note; off, one free-running LFO is read by every voice. Its Destination A switches send it to Oscillators A to D (their levels, as tremolo, at up to their whole level) and to the filter's cutoff (up to four octaves) with Amount A; Destination B sends it to one target from R25's list with Amount B. (S3; the depths are Beatlayer's own)
- **R24.** The filter is the instruments' filter (instrument filter R1 to R13), off in a new device, with Freq < Vel (−100 % to 100 %), Freq < Key (0 to 100 %, 100 % doubling the cutoff per octave from C3), Freq < Envelope (−100 % to 100 %, 100 % reaching nine octaves at an envelope level of 100 %) and its envelope (R13). After it, a waveshaper with Off, Soft, Hard, Sine and 4 Bit curves (the multisampler's, R39 there), Shaper Drive (0 to 24 dB) and Dry/Wet (0 to 100 %; at 0 % bypassed). (S5)
- **R25.** Velocity, Key, Aftertouch, Pitch Bend and Mod Wheel each have Destination A and Destination B choosers with an amount (−100 % to 100 %), as chosen slots of the matrix (instrument modulation R2): the destinations are Off, Osc Volume A, B, C and D, Osc Crossfade A/C and B/D (moving level from the first to the second), Osc Feedback, Osc Fixed Frequency, FM Drive, Filter Frequency, Filter Res, Filter Morph (on the Morph type), Filter Drive (not on Morph), Filter Envelope Amount, Shaper Drive, LFO Rate, LFO Amount, Pitch Envelope Amount, Volume, Panorama, Tone and Time. The manual's Filter Q (Legacy) isn't offered. PB Range (0 to 48 semitones, 5 by default) is the wheel's range. (S6; the default is Beatlayer's own)
- **R26.** Glide (off in a new device) is the voice section's polyphonic Portamento over Glide Time (0 ms to 10 s), envelopes not restarting on a legato note (instrument voices R7, R8). Spread, 0 to 100 % (0 % by default), plays each note that starts while it is above 0 as two copies, detuned by ±Spread × 0.25 semitones and panned hard left and right, each 3 dB down, starting at the same phase, so their mono sum begins in phase and loses nothing but the beating of the detune; a note keeps the Spread it started with. (S6; the detune scale is Beatlayer's own)
- **R27.** In the oscillator display a person draws a user wave's partials: dragging across the bars sets each partial the pointer crosses to its height, as one undo step per gesture, named Change Partials; the menu's Even Only and Odd Only restrict a drag to those partials, and Normalize (on by default) scales the levels so the loudest is 1. `device.set_partials` (`edit`) sets an oscillator's partial levels, count and Repeat as one undo step, and the query tool's `device` result reports each oscillator's partials under `partials`. (S2, S7; the gesture and the command are Beatlayer's own)
- **R28.** The title bar's menu (C-DEVICE-015) holds the manual's options after Save as Default Preset, each a registered command: Copy Oscillator settings from one oscillator to another (`operator.copy_oscillator`, `edit`, one undo step), Set All Envelope Levels to maximum, minimum or middle for the envelope shown (`operator.set_envelope_levels`, `edit`), Even Only, Odd Only and Normalize for the harmonics editor (settings of the view, read class), Play By Key in the filter frequency's menu (setting Freq to 466 Hz and Freq < Key to 100 %, one undo step), and Enable Per-Note Pitch Bend (R20). (S5, S7; the command names are Beatlayer's own)
- **R29.** The displays draw from the device's display data (device framework R35 to R37): an oscillator's envelope or its partials (switched by its Envelope and Oscillator buttons), the filter's envelope or its response, the LFO's envelope, the pitch envelope, and the global display's algorithm graph; `device.display` reports the same, headless. (S6, S7)
- **R30.** Every new parameter of part 2 automates, saves and reads back like any parameter; nothing on the audio thread allocates, locks or blocks, and a partial edit while notes play swaps the table without freeing on the audio thread. (S10)
