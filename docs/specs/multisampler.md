- **Checked against:** the Live 12 reference manual (online edition), read 17 October 2026: chapter 31, sections 31.10 "Sampler" (31.10.1 to 31.10.11) and 31.11 "Simpler" (its sample display and 31.11.7 "Context Menu Options for Simpler"); chapter 23, sections 23.2 and 23.2.4.1; and the SFZ format reference at sfzformat.com, read 17 October 2026

## The behaviour

Beatlayer's equivalent of Live's Sampler (this spec calls it the multisampler; its name in the app is Sampler) is an instrument that plays a multisample: a list of sample layers, each a sample file with its own key zone, velocity zone and sample select zone, and its own root key, tuning, level, start and end points and loops. A note plays every layer whose three zones take it, each layer through the zones' fades, so neighbouring samples crossfade across the keyboard and across velocities. Round Robin turns layers that share a key zone into a rotation, one per note. The device's title bar carries six tabs, as Live's does: Zone opens the zone editor in a view of its own above the Device View, and the other five show their controls in the device: Sample (the selected layer's waveform and playback), Pitch/Osc, Filter/Global, Modulation and MIDI. Everything the tabs hold is the device's own; the per-voice modulation of the Modulation and MIDI tabs runs inside the device, on top of the device-wide modulation of WP-5.9.

An SFZ file, the open text format for multisample instruments, imports as a multisampler holding its regions as layers. Two improvements build on the sampler devices. IMP-28 adds non-destructive edits to the sample displays of the multisampler and of Simpler, Beatlayer's equivalent of Simpler ([the sampler spec](sampler.md)): trim, cut, reverse, fade and normalise, each one undo step kept with the set, the source file untouched; Live's own Crop and Reverse stay in the context menu. IMP-27, the autosampler, renders an instrument across a range of notes and velocities, offline for software and in real time through the audio interface for hardware, into a new multisampler.

This spec covers the multisampler, the IMP-28 edits in both sampler devices' displays, and the autosampler. It leaves to [the device framework spec](device-framework.md) what every native device has (descriptors, smoothing, the generic controls, presets); to [the sampler spec](sampler.md) Simpler's own playback; to WP-5.9 and its spec modulation from outside the device; to WP-5.6 the Drum Rack's pads; to [the external devices spec](external-devices.md) the external instrument the autosampler plays hardware through; and to Phase 7 Lock to Control Surface.

## Rules

**The device and its layers (WP-5.13)**

- **R1.** Beatlayer ships an instrument device type of its own layout, described like any native device. Its title bar carries six tabs after the name, Zone, Sample, Pitch/Osc, Filter/Global, Modulation and MIDI; one of Sample to MIDI is shown in the body at a time (Sample in a new device), and Zone shows or hides the zone editor, a view of its own above the Device View (L-DEV-007). Which tab shows and whether the zone editor is open are view state, saved with the set and no undo step. Each tab carries an indicator, lit while a modulation routing of R40 to R46 targets one of its controls. (S3, S4, S18, S22)
- **R2.** The device holds an ordered list of layers. Each layer has its sample, named by content hash and held by the media store as a clip's audio is; its name (the file's name until renamed); a key zone (lowest and highest key, 0 to 127), a velocity zone (1 to 127) and a sample select zone (0 to 127), each with a fade at each end; and its own settings (R16 to R20). The list has no fixed length: 512 layers play, save and reopen. A layer whose media is missing plays silence and is reported, as a clip's is. The layers save with the set and a set reopens with them. (S1, S4, S7, S19, S21)
- **R3.** `device.add_layers` adds one layer per file, after the selected layers or at the end, each with every zone covering its whole range and no fades (S7), its root key from the file's sampler metadata where the file has it and C3 otherwise (Beatlayer's own), Sample Start and Sample End at the file's ends, and its loop points from the file's first loop where it has one, with Sustain Mode left off (Beatlayer's own). Dropping samples on the zone editor's layer list runs it, adding them at the drop point. Dropping one sample on a MIDI track's empty Device View still makes Simpler (I-DROP-008). (S7, S16, S22)
- **R4.** A note on plays every layer whose key zone holds its key (moved by Key Zone Shift, R33), whose velocity zone holds its velocity, and whose sample select zone holds the Sample Selector's value at that moment; each such layer sounds with the note, and a note that matches none plays nothing. With Round Robin on, R8 then chooses among them. (S4, S7)
- **R5.** A zone's fade at its low end runs from the zone's lowest value, where the layer is silent, to the fade's inner edge, where it reaches full level; the high end's fade mirrors it. Within a fade, the share x of the way from the silent edge to the full one gives the layer's gain: x with Fade Mode Lin, sin(x·π/2) with Pow, the device's one Fade Mode applying to every fade of every zone (Pow in a new device, Beatlayer's own). A layer's gain is the product of its three zones' gains, taken at the note's start. Dragging a zone's top corner sets its fade (R10). A rack chain's key, velocity and chain select zones follow the same law with Lin ([racks](racks.md) R32): at every value a rack's fade and a multisampler's give the same share. (S4, S7)
- **R6.** The Sample Selector is a parameter from 0 to 127 (default 0), automatable and modulatable, and the indicator above the select editor drags it. Moving it changes which layers a new note plays, never a layer already sounding. (S7, S18)
- **R7.** With Auto Select on, every note on selects the layers it plays, in the layer list, the zone editor and the Sample tab, until the note ends; the selection is view state and no undo step. Auto Select is off in a new device (Beatlayer's own). (S4)
- **R8.** With RR on (off in a new device, Beatlayer's own), the layers a note matches are grouped by identical key zones, and the note plays one layer of each group; Round Robin Mode chooses it: Forward the next below the group's last played in list order, from the top after the bottom; Backward the next above, from the bottom after the top; Other one chosen at random but never the one played last, where the group has more than one; Random one chosen at random. Forward is the mode in a new device (Beatlayer's own). Each group's place is the device's, kept across notes and reset when the transport starts; the random choices come from a generator seeded from the device, so an offline render plays the same layers every time. (S5)
- **R9.** Reset Interval (None, the default, or 1/16, 1/8, 1/4, 1/2, 1 Bar, 2 Bars, 4 Bars or 8 Bars, Beatlayer's own list) restarts every group on each multiple of the interval in song time from 1.1.1 while the transport plays: after it Forward's next note plays the group's top layer, Backward's its bottom layer, and Other's and Random's the layer the group played first since the transport started. (S5)
- **R10.** The zone editor shows the layer list at the left, with Auto, Lin/Pow, RR and the Key/Vel/Sel chooser above it, and beside it the chosen editor: one bar per layer on a scale from C-2 to G8 for keys, 1 to 127 for velocity, or 0 to 127 for sample select with the selector's indicator above. Dragging a zone's left or right edge resizes it, dragging its body moves it, and dragging a top corner sets that end's fade, each drag one undo step and each acting on every selected layer alike; a zone keeps at least one value, and its fades stay inside it. A right-click in an editor offers Small, Medium and Large, which change only the view. Hovering a layer or a zone shows its name, sample and ranges in the Status Bar's message area (L-STAT-002). The Hot-Swap Sample button at the zone editor's top left, always shown, opens the selected layer's folder in the browser, and the sample chosen there replaces the layer's sample as one undo step. (S4, S6, S7, S16, S22)
- **R11.** A click selects a layer, Shift-click extends the selection and Cmd-click (Ctrl-click) adds or removes one, and selecting a layer shows it in the Sample tab. The layer list's context menu, in the order of S6, runs Delete, Duplicate, Rename, Distribute Ranges Equally and Around Root Key (R12), Small, Medium and Large, Show in Browser, Manage Sample, Normalize Volume and Normalize Pan (R13), Select All With Same Range (the layers whose range in the editor in view equals the selected layer's), and the four sorts, each ascending or descending; every item that changes the layers is one undo step. (S6, S22)
- **R12.** Distribute Ranges Equally gives the selected layers (all of them when one or none is selected), in list order, equal shares of the range of the editor in view, without fades: over a range of m values starting at v (128 keys from 0, 127 velocities from 1, or 128 select values from 0), layer i of n (counting from 0) takes the values from v + ⌊m·i/n⌋ to v + ⌊m·(i+1)/n⌋ − 1 (Beatlayer's rounding). Distribute Ranges Around Root Key works on key zones: the distinct root keys, in order, split the keyboard where two neighbours meet, ⌊(a+b)/2⌋ the last key of the lower root a and the next key the first of the higher root b, the lowest root's zone starting at 0 and the highest's ending at 127; layers that share a root key divide that root's zone evenly among themselves in list order. (S6)
- **R13.** Normalize Volume sets each selected layer's Volume so its sample's largest sample magnitude between Sample Start and Sample End reaches 0 dBFS; Normalize Pan sets each selected layer's Pan so its left and right channels play at equal RMS level after the pan; a silent layer, or a mono one for Normalize Pan, keeps its value. Both are one undo step. (S6, S9)

**The Sample tab (WP-5.13)**

- **R14.** The Sample tab shows the selected layer: its waveform, drawn from its peaks, with markers for Sample Start, Sample End, Loop Start and Loop End, and the Release Loop, each dragged as one undo step; the Sample chooser, which selects another layer; Cmd or Ctrl with the wheel zooming the waveform's time; a vertical zoom slider that scales only the drawing; and B, M, L and R, which draw both channels, their sum, or one channel. Its context menu holds Show in Browser, Manage Sample, Normalize Volume and Normalize Pan (R13). Hovering the waveform shows the sample's name, length, rate and channels in the Status Bar's message area. (S8, S9, S22)
- **R15.** A layer's voice reads its sample at the pitch its key, its Root Key (0 to 127, the key that plays the sample at its own pitch) and its Detune (−50 to 50 cents, default 0) give, a semitone a key, with the device's Transpose, Detune and pitch modulation added (R32 to R36). Its Volume (a gain, −∞ to +24 dB, default 0 dB; rule G1) and its Pan (−50 to 50, default 0, with Simpler's pan law) set its level and place before the device's own. (S8, S18, S19)
- **R16.** A voice plays its layer from Sample Start to Sample End, frames in the sample (default the whole sample), unless a loop holds it (R17, R18); Sample Start is before Sample End. With Link on (off in a new layer), the voice starts at Loop Start instead, and the Sample Start the layer keeps comes back when Link goes off. (S9)
- **R17.** Sustain Mode chooses the sustain loop: No Sustain Loop (the default) plays straight through to Sample End, or until the volume envelope's release ends; Sustain Loop plays to Loop End, goes on from Loop Start and repeats; Back-and-Forth plays to Loop End, then backwards to Loop Start, then forwards, and repeats. Sample Start ≤ Loop Start < Loop End ≤ Sample End. With a sustain loop on and Release Mode Off, the loop goes on until the release ends. (S9)
- **R18.** Release Mode, which applies only while a sustain loop is on, decides what the note's end does: Off (the default) keeps the voice in the sustain loop, never past Loop End; Release plays on from where the voice is through Loop End to Sample End; Release Loop does so and then loops from the Release Loop point to Sample End until the release ends; Back-and-Forth Release Loop does the same back and forth. The Release Loop point lies between Loop End and Sample End and is Loop End in a new layer (Beatlayer's own). (S9)
- **R19.** A loop's Crossfade (0 ms, the default, up to half the loop's length) fades the loop's end out into its start as the wrap comes, constant-power while the title bar's Use Constant Power Fade for Loops is on (the default) and linear while it's off, so a looped layer doesn't click at the wrap. The loop's Detune (−50 to 50 cents, default 0) retunes the voice while it plays inside a loop. (S2, S9)
- **R20.** While Snap is on (off in a new device), every Sample Start, Sample End, Loop Start, Loop End and Release Loop a person or a command sets lands on the nearest zero crossing of the layer's left channel: a frame where it is zero or changes sign. A loop's marker menu offers Snap Markers, which snaps that loop's two markers once, as one undo step, whatever Snap says. (S8)
- **R21.** Reverse (a switch, off by default, automatable and modulatable) plays every layer backwards, from Sample End back through the sustain loop while one is on, to Sample Start, without making a file; a voice takes it at its start. (S8)
- **R22.** Interpolation (Normal, the default, Good or Best, Beatlayer's own default) chooses how a voice reads between the sample's frames: Normal in a straight line between neighbours, as Simpler does, Good with a four-point cubic, Best with a windowed-sinc kernel of Beatlayer's own that keeps a sine transposed up to an octave above its root free of images and aliases to −80 dB. At its root key and no detune, a voice plays the sample's frames exactly in every mode. (S9)
- **R23.** RAM Mode (off in a new device, Beatlayer's own default) decides how the layers are held: on, every layer's sample is decoded whole into memory, shared with the set's other readers; off, a layer shorter than 10 seconds is held whole and a longer one streams from disk as a long clip's file does, with the start of its playback held in memory so a note never waits for the disk. (S9, S21)

**The volume envelope and voices (WP-5.13)**

- **R24.** Every envelope of the device has Initial, Peak and Sustain levels and, except the volume envelope, an End level (0 to 100 %); Attack, Decay and Release times (0 to 20 s); and Attack, Decay and Release slopes (−100 to 100 %, default 0). A note on takes the envelope from Initial to Peak over Attack, from Peak to Sustain over Decay, holds Sustain, and at the note's end goes to End (silence for the volume envelope) over Release from its level then. Within a segment the share x of its time gives the share x^e of its way, where e = 4^(−s) for slope s (s from −1 to 1): a positive slope moves fast at first, a negative one slowly, zero in a straight line (Beatlayer's own curve). The volume envelope's defaults are Initial 0 %, Peak 100 %, Sustain 100 %, Attack 0, Decay 0 and Release 50 ms (Beatlayer's own); a voice ends when its volume envelope has released. (S9, S11)
- **R25.** An envelope's Loop is None (the default), Loop, Beat, Sync or Trigger. Loop: a note still held when the decay ends goes back from Sustain to Initial over Time (0 to 20 s, default 0) and starts again. Trigger loops as Loop does, and a note's end doesn't cut short the attack and decay already under way: the release starts when the decay ends (Beatlayer's reading of the manual's Trigger). Beat: a note still held after Repeat (a note value from 1/64 to 8 Bars, default 1/4) from its start goes again from its level at the attack's rate to Peak, and so on each Repeat; Sync does the same on the multiples of Repeat in song time from 1.1.1. (S9, S11)
- **R26.** Time (−100 to 100 %, default 0) multiplies every envelope's times by 10^(Time/100); Time < Key (−100 to 100 %, default 0) multiplies them by 2^(−TimeKey·(key − 60)/12), so at 100 % an octave above C3 halves them; each envelope's Time < Vel (−100 to 100 %, default 0) multiplies its segment times, not Repeat, by 1 − TimeVel·velocity, velocity from 0 to 1 (Beatlayer's own laws; the manual names what each scales). (S9, S11)
- **R27.** Voices (1 to 32, default 32, Beatlayer's own default) is how many notes sound at once, a note's layers together counting as one. A note that would make more fades the oldest sounding note's layers out over Simpler's 5 ms steal fade. With Retrigger on (off by default), a note on a key already sounding fades that key's layers out the same way and starts again, rather than taking another voice. (S11, S19)
- **R28.** The device's Volume (a gain, −∞ to +24 dB, default 0 dB) and Vel > Vol (0 to 100 %, default 0, scaling the level by velocity as Simpler's R6 does) are Beatlayer's own global controls, there so the device's output reaches −∞ dB (rule G1) and follows velocity; Pan (−50 to 50, default 0) places every layer; Pan < Rnd (0 to 100 %, default 0) moves each note's pan by a random share up to its amount of the full width, from the device's seeded generator. (S11, S18)
- **R29.** The title bar's context menu (L-DEV-009) holds the device commands and Group, Fold, Show Preset Name, Save as Default Preset (C-DEVICE-007 to C-DEVICE-012), Use Constant Power Fade for Loops (on in a new device; one undo step) and the conversion to Simpler (R62), in S2's order; Lock to Control Surface is Phase 7's (C-DEVICE-014). While Show Preset Name is on and neither a preset nor Rename has named the device, the title shows the first layer's name, and the device's own name with no layers. (S2, S22)
- **R30.** Every action is a registered command with a schema, a description and a permission class, run by ID, each change one undo step saved with the set: `device.add_layers` (from files, `external`, as `device.load_sample` is), and `device.delete_layers`, `device.duplicate_layers`, `device.rename_layer`, `device.move_layers`, `device.sort_layers`, `device.set_layer_zone`, `device.set_layer`, `device.set_layer_sample`, `device.distribute_layers` and `device.normalize_layers` (`edit`). Each reads back in the query tool's `device` result under `layers`; the parameters read back through `device.parameters`; Auto Select's selection and the open tab read back in `device_view.state`. The layers, the open tab and RAM Mode are the device's settings, not automation targets; every parameter is automatable and modulatable (rule G2). (S18; D17)
- **R31.** Nothing on the audio thread allocates, locks or blocks: the layers reach the node as node data, so a zone or layer edit sends data and no new program, the decoded samples arrive off the audio thread and are freed on the drop thread, and every voice and buffer is made with the node. (S19)

**The Pitch/Osc tab (WP-5.14)**

- **R32.** Transpose (−48 to 48 semitones, default 0) and Detune (−50 to 50 cents, default 0) move every voice's pitch. Spread (0 to 100 %, default 0) plays each layer of a note as two voices at half the level each, detuned by ± Spread × 25 cents (Beatlayer's own depth); both count as the note's one voice in R27. (S10)
- **R33.** Key Zone Shift (−48 to 48 semitones, default 0) moves the key R4 uses to choose layers, and never the pitch they play at: with a shift of +12, a key plays the layers mapped an octave up, at its own key's pitch relative to each layer's root. (S10)
- **R34.** Glide (Off, the default, Glide or Portamento) and its Time (0 to 1000 ms, default 100 ms) work as Simpler's (the sampler spec, R9): Glide is monophonic, the new note taking over the sounding note, which goes on with its layers, position and envelopes and slides in pitch, a straight line in semitones, from where it is to the new key, Portamento polyphonic, each new note, with the layers its key chooses, sliding from the last note's key. A note taken over answers to its new key's note off. (S10, S19)
- **R35.** The pitch envelope (R24's envelope, its levels −100 to 100 %) moves every voice's pitch by its value times Amount (−48 to 48 semitones, default 0), and the modulation oscillator's with it. (S10)
- **R36.** The modulation oscillator (off by default), one per voice, is never heard itself. Its Type is one of Beatlayer's own waveforms (Sine, Triangle, Saw, Square and Noise, and Saw and Square limited to 4, 8, 16 and 32 harmonics); without Fixed it runs at the note's frequency times Coarse (0.125 to 48, default 1) plus Fine/1000 (Fine 0 to 1000, default 0), and with Fixed at Freq (1 Hz to 10 kHz, default 100 Hz) times Multi (0.01, 0.1, 1 or 10, default 1). Its depth is Volume (0 to 100 %, default 0) shaped by its own envelope (R24, R25), with Vol < Vel (−100 to 100 %, default 0) scaling it by velocity. A switch chooses FM, the default, or AM (Beatlayer's own). FM multiplies the voice's read rate by 2^(2·depth·osc), so full depth swings two octaves each way; AM multiplies the voice's level by 1 − depth·(1 − osc)/2 (Beatlayer's own laws). The oscillator runs once for a note, whatever the number of layers it plays. (S10)

**The Filter/Global tab (WP-5.14)**

- **R37.** The filter (off by default) runs per voice after the shaper or before it (R39). Its Type is low-pass, high-pass, band-pass, notch or Morph, each at 12 or 24 dB per octave; Freq is logarithmic from 20 Hz to 20 kHz (default 1 kHz) and Res 0 to 100 % (default 0); Morph (0 to 100 %, default 0) sweeps the Morph type from low-pass at 0 through band-pass at 25 %, high-pass at 50 % and notch at 75 % back to low-pass at 100 %, each stop the pure type and the places between a crossfade of the two neighbours, its context menu snapping it to any of the four. The circuit is Clean, the kit's state-variable filter, as Simpler's is (the sampler spec, R15); Drive (0 to 24 dB, default 0) drives the signal into it for low-pass, high-pass and band-pass. (S11, S19)
- **R38.** The filter envelope (F. Env, off by default) is R24's envelope with its levels from −100 to 100 %, moving the cutoff by its value times Amount (0 to 72 semitones, default 0), so it does nothing at 0. (S11)
- **R39.** The shaper (Shaper, off by default) shapes each voice with Soft (tanh), Hard (a clip at full scale), Sine (a sine fold) or 4bit (sixteen levels), driven by Amount (0 to 100 %, default 0, a drive of up to 24 dB; Beatlayer's own curves and drive); its direction switch puts it before the filter (the default) or after it. (S11)

**The Modulation and MIDI tabs (WP-5.14)**

- **R40.** The auxiliary envelope (off by default) is R24's envelope with its levels from −100 to 100 %, sent to Destination A and Destination B (R46) with Amount A and Amount B (−100 to 100 %, default 0). (S12)
- **R41.** LFOs 1, 2 and 3, each off until its switch is on, run per voice with the waveforms Sine, Square, Triangle, Saw Down, Saw Up and S&H (a new random value each cycle); a free rate from 0.01 to 30 Hz (default 1 Hz) or, synced, a note value from 1/64 to 8 Bars (default 1/4) of the set's tempo; Attack (0 to 10 s, default 0) to full intensity; Retrig (off by default), which restarts its phase at Offset (0 to 360°, default 0) on each note, Offset doing nothing while Retrig is off, as Simpler's LFO does; and Key (0 to 100 %, default 0), speeding it up with pitch, twice as fast an octave above C3 at 100 %. (S12, S19)
- **R42.** LFO 1 moves the voice by its Vol (0 to 100 %: at 100 % the level swings between full and silence), Pan (0 to 100 %: at 100 % from hard left to hard right), Filter (0 to 24 semitones each way, on the cutoff) and Pitch (0 to 100 %: at 100 % an octave each way) (S12's scales; Beatlayer's own depths). LFOs 2 and 3 send to Destination A and B with their amounts (R46), and each has a stereo mode: Phase (0 to 360°, default 0) runs the right channel's LFO that far ahead of the left's, and Spin (0 to 50 %, default 0) runs the right channel's that much faster. Sent to Volume, each channel follows its own LFO; to every other destination, the mean of the two. (S12)
- **R43.** The MIDI tab gives Key, Velocity, Release Velocity, Aftertouch, Modulation Wheel (CC 1), Foot Controller (CC 4) and Pitch Bend each a Destination A and B (R46) with Amount A and B (−100 to 100 %, default 0). As sources, Key is (key − 60)/64, Velocity and Release Velocity are 0 to 1 (Release Velocity 0 until the note's end), Aftertouch is the latest channel or key pressure for the note from 0 to 1, the two controllers 0 to 1, and Pitch Bend −1 to 1. (S13)
- **R44.** Pitch Bend Range (0 to 24 semitones, default 2, Beatlayer's own default) is how far a full pitch bend moves every voice, from the 14-bit wheel. (S13)
- **R45.** A routing's amount scales its source across the destination's span: Volume multiplies the level by 1 + amount × source, never below silence; Pan moves across the whole width; Pitch by up to 24 semitones; Sample Start and Loop Length by up to the whole of the layer's playback span; Sample Selector by up to 127; Filter Freq by up to 8 octaves; Osc Pitch by up to 24 semitones; Pitch Env Amount by up to 96 semitones, Filter Env Amount by up to 72 and an LFO's Rate by up to 29.99 Hz, their parameters' whole ranges; Time by up to its whole range, a factor of 100 on the envelopes' times; LFO 1 Amount scales the four amounts of LFO 1 by 1 plus the sum; every other destination by up to its parameter's whole range. Routings to one destination add, and the result is clamped to the parameter's range. Sample Start, Loop Length and the Sample Selector act as a note starts, on the values its MIDI sources and the auxiliary envelope's Initial level give then; the rest follow their sources while the note sounds. (S12, S13)
- **R46.** The destinations, for the auxiliary envelope, LFOs 2 and 3, and the MIDI tab, are Beatlayer's own list of 21: None (the default), Volume, Pan, Pitch, Sample Start, Loop Length, Sample Selector, Filter Freq, Filter Res, Filter Morph, Filter Drive, Shaper Amount, Osc Volume, Osc Pitch, Pitch Env Amount, Filter Env Amount, LFO 1 Rate, LFO 2 Rate, LFO 3 Rate, LFO 1 Amount and Time. Every destination and amount is a parameter, automatable and modulatable (rule G2). (S12, S13, S18)

**SFZ import (WP-5.14)**

- **R47.** An SFZ file (`.sfz`) imports as a multisampler: dropped from the browser or the desktop on a MIDI track's Device View or the drop area, it makes the device on that track or on a new MIDI track; dropped on a multisampler's zone editor, it adds its regions as layers there. `device.import_sfz` (`external`, since it reads files) does the same by path, with a `device` to add layers to, or a MIDI `track` to put the new multisampler on, or neither for a new MIDI track named for the file. The browser lists `.sfz` files with the samples, and Load Selected loads one onto the selected MIDI track or a new one. Adding to an existing multisampler brings its layers and no device settings. The import is one undo step, and its samples join the set's media as imported audio does, by content hash, where they lie. (S14, S17, S21; the SFZ import is Beatlayer's own)
- **R48.** The file is read as UTF-8 text (Latin-1 where it isn't valid UTF-8). `//` comments run to the line's end and `/* … */` comments between their marks; `#define $NAME value` replaces `$NAME` in what follows, and `#include "file"` reads a file relative to the including file's folder. Opcodes are `name=value`; a `sample` value runs to the line's end or to the next `name=`, and both slashes separate folders. A note value is a number or a name such as `c#4` or `eb3`, with `c4` MIDI note 60 (which Beatlayer and Live call C3). `<control>`'s `default_path` goes before every sample path, which is relative to the `.sfz` file's folder; its `note_offset` and `octave_offset` move every key zone and root key down by the offset, so the instrument plays as the format says. Every `<region>` is one layer, taking the opcodes of the `<global>`, `<master>` and `<group>` above it, each lasting until a header of its own kind or a higher one, its own opcodes winning. (S17)
- **R49.** A region's opcodes set its layer: `sample` its sample; `lokey`, `hikey` and `key` (which also sets the root key) its key zone; `lovel` and `hivel` its velocity zone; `pitch_keycenter` its Root Key (`sample` reading it from the file, note 0 where the file has none); `xfin_lokey` and `xfin_hikey` the key zone's low fade, silent at the first and full at the second, and `xfout_lokey` and `xfout_hikey` its high fade, the zone widened to hold both; the `…vel` crossfades the velocity zone's fades in the same way; `tune` its Detune, a tune past ±50 cents moving the Root Key by a semitone and keeping the rest; `transpose` its Root Key, moved the other way; `volume` its Volume in dB; `pan` its Pan, scaled from ±100 to ±50; `offset` and `end` its Sample Start and Sample End. (S17)
- **R50.** `loop_mode` sets the layer's loop: `no_loop` no sustain loop; `loop_continuous` Sustain Loop with Release Mode Off; `loop_sustain` Sustain Loop with Release Mode Release; and `one_shot` no loop, the layer's voices playing on to Sample End whatever the note does (a layer setting, `one_shot`, that no control shows and `device.update_layers` sets). `loop_start` and `loop_end` (or `loopstart`, `loopend`) set Loop Start and Loop End, the file's first loop standing in where they're missing, and with no `loop_mode`, a sample with a loop of its own loops continuously and one without doesn't. `loop_crossfade` sets the loop's Crossfade in seconds. (S17)
- **R51.** Opcodes that set one of the device's own controls (`amp_veltrack` as Vel > Vol; `ampeg_attack`, `ampeg_decay`, `ampeg_sustain` and `ampeg_release` as the volume envelope; `fil_type`, `cutoff` and `resonance` as the filter, `lpf_1p` and `lpf_2p` as low-pass 12 dB, `lpf_4p` as 24 dB, `hpf_…` the same for high-pass, `bpf_2p` as band-pass and `brf_2p` as notch, a `cutoff` switching the filter on and `resonance` in dB from 0 to 40 read as 0 to 100 %; the `fileg_…` and `pitcheg_…` envelopes, their `depth` in cents read in semitones (a negative filter envelope depth turns its levels upside down, Peak and Sustain below 0, with Amount its size); `bend_up` and `bend_down` as Pitch Bend Range; `direction=reverse` as Reverse; `xf_keycurve` and `xf_velcurve` as Fade Mode, `gain` as Lin and `power` as Pow) take the value most regions have, the first region's on a tie. (S17)
- **R52.** Regions with `seq_length` and `seq_position` turn Round Robin on in Forward mode, their layers ordered by `seq_position` within each key zone; regions with `lorand` and `hirand` windows and no sequence turn it on in Random mode. (S17)
- **R53.** Every other opcode and header (among them `trigger` other than `attack`, `group` and `off_by`, `locc` and `hicc`, the `sw_` keyswitches, `lochan` and `hichan`, `ampeg_delay` and `ampeg_hold`, `<curve>`, `<effect>`, `<midi>` and `<sample>`) is left unread; a region whose `trigger` is `release`, `release_key`, `first` or `legato` is left out. The import's result lists each opcode left unread or settled by R51's majority, with how many regions use it, and each region left out and why; a region whose sample is missing or isn't audio is left out and named. A file with no region left imports nothing and changes nothing. A damaged or foreign file never crashes Beatlayer: it imports what it can read, or nothing. (S17; Beatlayer's own)

**Sample editing in the sampler devices' displays (WP-5.15, IMP-28)**

- **R54.** Simpler's sample display shows its sample's waveform with the region's flags, the loop brace in Classic mode and the slices in Slice mode (automatic slices drawn in one colour and manual ones in another), and both its display and the multisampler's Sample tab zoom with Cmd or Ctrl and the wheel or a vertical drag (dragging down zooms in about where the drag began and up zooms out, as Live's rulers do) and pan with a horizontal drag, as Simpler's display does; dragging a flag or marker sets it as one undo step. Shift-drag on the waveform selects a span of the sample (a plain drag zooming and panning as in Live), Esc clears it, and a click with no drag plays the sample from the clicked point through the device, at its root pitch (C3 for Simpler, the layer's root key for the multisampler) and the device's settings, until the button is released, changing nothing in the set; in Slice mode it plays the region from the point, not a slice. (S15, S20; the selection and audition gestures are Beatlayer's own)
- **R55.** A sample's edits are kept with the sample in the device (with each layer, in the multisampler): an ordered list, saved with the set, of Trim (keep the selection), Cut (remove the selection, closing the gap), Reverse (the selection, or the whole sample with none), Fade In and Fade Out (over the selection, a straight line in gain from silence to full or full to silence) and Normalize (the selection, or the whole, scaled so its largest sample magnitude reaches 0 dBFS). Each comes from the display's context menu, after Live's own items, and from `device.edit_sample`, and each is one undo step named for it. The source file is never written. (S20)
- **R56.** An edit that changes the sample's length keeps the markers on the audio they marked: a marker after a cut moves earlier by the cut's length, a marker inside it moves to the cut's start, and a marker outside a trim's span moves to the span's nearer end; Simpler's Start, Length and Loop are shares of the sample and stay as they were. (S20; Beatlayer's own)
- **R57.** The engine plays the edited sample: the edit list is applied to the decoded sample off the audio thread, kept by the source's content hash and the list, and reaches the node as its sample does (R31), while the display draws the edited sample's peaks. The same source with the same edits is applied once and shared. (S19, S20)
- **R58.** Revert Edits, in the display's context menu and as `device.edit_sample`'s `revert`, clears a sample's edit list as one undo step. (S20; Beatlayer's own)
- **R59.** The context menu's Crop (removing the sample outside Simpler's region, the sampler spec's R3, or outside a multisampler layer's Sample Start and Sample End) and Reverse (the whole sample) stay as Live has them: each writes a processed copy, with the sample's edits applied, through the shared writer of [the audio clip editing spec](audio-clip-editing.md) (R1 to R3): a WAV of 32-bit floating-point samples, which holds the source's bit depth exactly, at its rate and with its channels (rule G3), in the project's `Samples/Processed/Crop/` or `Samples/Processed/Reverse/` folder, and the device or layer then plays the copy, with its edit list empty, as one undo step; the original file stays as it was. Crop moves a layer's markers as a Trim does and makes Simpler's region the whole of the copy; Reverse leaves the markers where they are. A set never saved has no project folder, and both are refused there with a message in the Status Bar, as recording refuses (recording R3). (S15, S20, S21)
- **R60.** Simpler's context menu's Normalize Volumes sets its Gain so the region's largest sample magnitude reaches 0 dBFS, as one undo step (`device.normalize_layers` with `volume`, which acts on Simpler as well as the multisampler); the multisampler's Normalize Volumes and Normalize Pan are R13's. Show in Browser and Manage Sample show the sample in the browser and the File Manager. (S9, S15)
- **R61.** Dropping a sample on the multisampler's Sample tab display replaces the selected layer's sample, keeping its zones and settings; dropping it there with Option (Alt) held adds it as a new layer after the selected one, with the selected layer's three zones, so the two sound together (`device.add_layers` with `after` and `like`). Each is one undo step; a replaced sample's edits go with it. (S20; the gestures are Beatlayer's own)
- **R62.** Simpler's title bar menu converts it to a multisampler and the multisampler's converts it to Simpler, each as one undo step that keeps the device's place, name and sample: Simpler becomes one layer with its whole zones, its region as Sample Start and Sample End, its loop as a sustain loop, its envelope, volume, pan, velocity and voices as the multisampler's (its filter, LFO and Glide carry once the multisampler has controls of its own for them, WP-5.14; until then the result names them as not carried); a multisampler becomes Simpler in Classic mode playing its first layer. Slicing and One-Shot don't carry over to the multisampler, and layers past the first don't carry over to Simpler; the conversion's result names what didn't. (S2, S15)
- **R63.** Every edit and command here is registered with a schema, a description and a permission class: `device.edit_sample` (`edit`; it reads back in the query tool's `device` result under `saved.sample_edits`, or in the layer's `edits`), `device.crop_sample` and `device.reverse_sample` (`edit`; they read back as the device's or layer's media), `device.convert` (`edit`; it reads back as the device's kind, and its result names what didn't carry over), and `device.audition_sample` (`read`, changing nothing). The document's own forms are `device.add_sample_edit`, `device.clear_sample_edits` and `device.set_processed_layer_sample`, which `device.edit_sample`, `device.crop_sample` and `device.reverse_sample` run once they have checked the selection and written the copy. (D17)

**The autosampler (WP-5.16, IMP-27)**

- **R64.** Autosample… sits in an instrument's title bar context menu among its device-specific options (C-DEVICE-015), for any instrument device: a native instrument, a plug-in instrument, or the external instrument for hardware. It opens the autosampler's dialog for that device, and `device.autosample` runs it with the same settings, as one command in the `external` class, because it writes files and may play hardware. (S20, S22; the placement is Beatlayer's own)
- **R65.** The dialog's settings, with Beatlayer's own ranges and defaults: Lowest Note and Highest Note (C-2 to G8, defaults C1 and C6), Step (1 to 12 semitones, default 3), Velocity Layers (1 to 16, default 1), Note Length (0.1 to 30 s, default 2 s), Release Tail (0 to 30 s, default 1 s), Round Robins (1 to 8, default 1), Detect Loops (off by default), and Name (the instrument's name by default). It shows how many samples the settings make and how long the render will take. (S20)
- **R66.** The notes sampled are the Lowest Note and every Step above it up to the Highest Note, which is always sampled. Velocity layer k of n plays at velocity round(127·k/n) and takes the velocity zone from round(127·(k − 1)/n) + 1 to round(127·k/n). Each sampled note's key zone runs halfway to its neighbours, the key ⌊(a + b)/2⌋ between sampled notes a and b going to a, the lowest zone reaching down to 0 and the highest up to 127, so every key plays; each sample's Root Key is its note. With Round Robins r above 1, each note and velocity is sampled r times, the r layers sharing their zones. (S5, S7, S20)
- **R67.** For a native or plug-in instrument, the autosampler renders offline, as export does, through the instrument alone with its current settings, without the devices before or after it on its track, borrowing a plug-in as an export does ([offline renders with plug-ins](offline-plugin-renders.md) R1 to R4). The notes play one after another, each held for Note Length, released, and followed by its Release Tail before the next starts; the render is the same every time, at any block size. (S20, S21)
- **R68.** For the external instrument, the autosampler plays in real time with the audio engine running and the transport stopped (refused otherwise): each note goes out on the device's MIDI To port and channel and its sound is recorded from the device's Audio From channels, with the device's reported latency (its round trip and Hardware Latency) taken off, so each sample starts on its note's start. A dropout during a note records that note again. (S20; [the external devices spec](external-devices.md))
- **R69.** Each sample is a 32-bit float WAV at the engine's rate, holding Note Length and Release Tail, with the instrument's output channels (rule G3: a mono Audio From gives mono files), written to `Samples/Processed/Autosample/<Name>/` in the project folder (Beatlayer's own folder) as `<Name> <note> v<velocity>.wav`, with ` rr<n>` before the extension for a round robin, and joined to the set's media. A set never saved is refused, as R59. (S20, S21)
- **R70.** The result is a new MIDI track straight after the source's track, named Name, holding a multisampler with one layer per sample in note, velocity and round-robin order, the zones of R66, Round Robin on in Forward mode where Round Robins is above 1, Vel > Vol 0 %, the volume envelope at Attack 0, Sustain 100 % and Release equal to Release Tail, and Voices 32; the track and its device are one undo step, and undoing it leaves the files on disk, as recorded files stay. (S20; Beatlayer's own)
- **R71.** With Detect Loops on, each sample gets a sustain loop inside its held part, between a quarter of Note Length and nine tenths of it: both ends on rising zero crossings of the left channel, chosen so the 10 ms before Loop End best match the 10 ms before Loop Start (the smallest difference in Beatlayer's own measure), with Sustain Mode Sustain Loop, Release Mode Release and a 10 ms Crossfade; a sustained sound then plays on past Note Length while the key is held, and a sample where no loop matches within −40 dB is left without one and named in the result. (S9, S20)
- **R72.** The autosampled instrument matches its source: played at a sampled note and velocity for Note Length, the difference between its output and the source's render of the same note stays 60 dB below the source's level; a key between two sampled notes sounds within 1 cent of its equal-tempered pitch, as the source plays it. (S20)
- **R73.** While it runs, the dialog shows its progress and the note it's on; Cancel stops it, deletes the files it wrote and leaves the set as it was. Its result, and `device.autosample`'s, lists the files, notes, layers and loops made, the samples left without a loop, and the time it took; the new track reads back in the query tool's `track` result and its layers under `device`'s `layers`. (S20; D17)

**Tuning systems (WP-6.9)**

- **R74.** On a track that plays the set's tuning ([tunings.md](tunings.md) R12, R14), a layer's voice reads its sample at the pitch R15 gives with its key's tuned pitch in place of the key, its Root Key still naming the twelve-step pitch at which the sample plays as recorded; Transpose, Detune, Spread, Glide, the pitch envelope, the routings to Pitch and the pitch wheel add to it as they add to a key without a tuning (R32 to R35, R44, R45). The zones choose layers by key number (R4, R33), and the Key modulation source and Time < Key (R26) follow the key number too (Beatlayer's own). (S23, S24)

## Not built yet

- Auto Select (R7) follows the notes the shell sends, those of the computer keyboard; notes from hardware MIDI ports or clips go to the engine directly, so they select nothing yet (`shortcut:` in the shell, upgrade with the engine publishing the layers each node plays).
- A layer over ten seconds is read in the background behind its held start, and its tail stays in memory once read (R23's streams hold only a window); upgrade to evicting what no voice is near. A file whose rate isn't the engine's, and a render that must repeat, decode whole.
- The zone editor overlays the foot of the main view rather than making the detail area taller; the layer list's menu items, zone drags and marker drags have no keyboard form (the accessibility spec lists them); Manage Sample waits on the File Manager (WP-2.25), and Convert to Simpler on WP-5.15.
- The Best interpolation does not band-limit content above half the output rate when a layer is transposed up, so a sample with energy near the top of the spectrum can alias an octave up.

- The filter's circuit models other than Clean (OSR, MS2, SMP and PRD) are not built, as in Simpler; the multisampler's filter is the kit's state-variable filter with Drive in front.
- The formats the manual's import names (REX files, ACID Loops and Soundtrack Loops) are not read; SFZ is Beatlayer's multisample import.
- The tabs' controls are the generic panel's (the Pitch/Osc, Filter/Global, Modulation and MIDI tabs draw every parameter as a knob, switch, chooser or number field in the order R32 to R46 list them); the envelopes have no graph to drag, and the device is as wide as its widest tab.
- The filter's Res, Morph and Drive (and the Filter Res, Filter Morph and Filter Drive destinations) and the shaper's Amount reach the kit's filter once a block, from each note's last frame (`shortcut:` in the device, upgrade with per-frame values in the kit's filter bank); the filter runs over a note's layers together, after their volume envelopes and pans, so the shaper after the filter sees the released envelope.
- Sample Start, Loop Length and the Sample Selector as destinations (R45) are read as a note starts, from its MIDI sources and the auxiliary envelope's Initial level; a source that moves later (a controller, an LFO) doesn't move them.
- A multisampler has 96 notes at a time (32 sounding, the rest fading out); one past that is dropped.
- SFZ: the opcodes beyond R48 to R53's list are left unread and reported, among them `trigger` other than `attack`, key switches, `group` and `off_by`, controller ranges, effects, curves and the format's v2 opcodes; the SFZ drop on the Session view's slots and drop area isn't covered; a crossfade needs both its ends; `lorand` and `hirand` only switch Random mode on, their windows aren't read.
- A preset of the multisampler holds its parameter values only (device chains R19), as Simpler's does: its layers are the set's.
- The multisampler's MPE response, Lock to Control Surface, and dropping several samples on an empty MIDI track at once are not covered here.
