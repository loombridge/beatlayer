The manual gives no range, default, unit or buffer length for any of Looper's controls, and says nothing about how a device asks Live's transport for a change: those are Beatlayer's own, and every place this spec settles one it says so.

## The behaviour

Looper records what it is fed and loops it, so a musician can build a loop by layering: Record takes the first pass, Overdub adds further layers of the loop's length, Play sounds the buffer without recording, and Stop silences it. A large Multi-Purpose Transport Button walks between those states with one press, two quick presses, or a two-second hold. Clear empties the buffer, Undo takes back the last overdub pass and then becomes Redo, and ×2 and ÷2 change the buffer's length. Speed and the octave arrows change how fast (and so how high) the buffer plays, Reverse plays it backwards, and Feedback decides how much of the old layers survives each pass. Recorded material can be dragged out as a clip or replaced by a dropped file, and another track can feed Looper's output back through its own devices while Looper overdubs.

The framework behind the device (parameters, descriptors, smoothing, layout, automation, display data) is the [device framework spec](device-framework.md). What its buttons do to the song's transport and tempo is its own; the transport itself is [transport](transport.md)'s and the launch grid it follows is [launch quantisation](launch-quantisation.md)'s.

## Rules

- **R1.** Beatlayer ships Looper, an audio-effect device type of its own layout: every parameter has a descriptor and a place in the layout, and it reports no latency. (S1, S4, S8)
- **R2.** Looper holds one loop buffer of its own, made with its node off the audio thread and never resized: two channels of 30 seconds at the engine's rate. Nothing in Looper's audio-thread path allocates, locks or blocks. The 30 seconds are Beatlayer's own, a whole number a test can check. (S4, S8)
- **R3.** Looper's parameter values are saved with the set like any native device's; its buffer is not, so a set reopens with an empty buffer, and a Looper saved in Record or Overdub reopens stopped. That the manual doesn't say is the reason this is Beatlayer's own rule: it is the safer one, since the audio would otherwise have to be written into the set. (S1, S8)
- **R4.** Transport is a choice parameter of Record, Overdub, Play and Stop, automatable like every other (G2) and stopping at Stop by default. A change to it takes effect as R5 to R8 say. (S1, S3, S4, S8)
- **R5.** While the song plays, a change to Transport takes effect on the next line of the launch quantisation grid: the global grid, or the step Looper's Quantization chooser names when it names one (R17). While the song is stopped, the change takes effect at once, whatever the chooser says. Like a Session clip's launch, the frame is worked out once, when the change arrives, and a queued change keeps it; a change back to the state in effect before its frame takes the queued one back. (S1, S5)
- **R6.** Record writes the input over the buffer from its first frame and sets the buffer's length when it ends, so it overwrites whatever was there. It ends when Transport changes (R5), or, with Record Length at a fixed number of bars (R18), when that many bars have been recorded, and then Transport becomes Play or Overdub as R19 says. A recording also ends when the buffer is full, 30 seconds after it began, whatever its length: a fixed length that would run past that is cut there. The recorded frame count is what the tempo rule (R22) works from with the song stopped. (S1)
- **R7.** Overdub plays the buffer and adds the input to it, frame by frame, from the buffer's start, and loops for as long as Transport stays Overdub. It never changes the buffer's length: the layers take the length of the recording that made it. (S1)
- **R8.** Play sounds the buffer, again and again, without recording; Stop silences the buffer and leaves it as it is. Both take their effect as R5 says. A queued change acts on its frame, wherever the loop's position is then. (S1)
- **R9.** Clear (`device.looper.clear`) empties the buffer: pressed while Transport is Overdub and the song plays, it keeps the buffer's length and Looper's tempo and clears only the samples; in any other state it resets the length and Looper's tempo as well, so Looper is as it was before anything was recorded. It empties only the frames the buffer holds, not the whole 30 seconds. It changes nothing in the set, so it is not an undo step. (S1, S8)
- **R10.** Undo (`device.looper.undo`) puts the buffer back to what it was when Overdub was last entered, so everything the current overdub pass has added is gone and the earlier layers are exactly as they were; pressed again with nothing since, it does nothing. Redo (`device.looper.redo`) puts what the last Undo removed back, bit for bit, and does nothing when there is nothing to redo. Both are exactly the samples, not a copy of the shape: a buffer undone and redone is the same frames. (S1)
- **R11.** The Multi-Purpose Transport Button's gestures are commands that set Transport or run R9 to R10: `device.looper.press` is one press — from an empty buffer it sets Record, from Record, Overdub or Stop it sets Play, and from Play it sets Overdub, so single presses walk between Play and Overdub; `device.looper.press_twice` is a quick double press, which sets Stop from Play or Overdub; `device.looper.hold` is a two-second hold, which runs Redo when a Redo is waiting, Undo when Transport is Play and an Undo is, and Clear otherwise. Each runs through R5, so a press while the song plays lands on the grid. (S1, S8)
- **R12.** ×2 (`device.looper.double`) doubles the buffer's length and repeats what it holds across the new half, so both halves sound the same and play one after the other. ÷2 (`device.looper.halve`) halves the buffer's length and keeps the half that plays from the playhead, so the loop continues from the same material; the other half is dropped. (S1)
- **R13.** Speed is the buffer's playback rate, from 1/8 to 8 times, logarithmic, 1 by default; a frame of output advances the loop's position by the rate in effect, so doubling it doubles the pitch and halves the loop's duration. The two octave arrows (`device.looper.octave_up` and `device.looper.octave_down`) multiply or divide Speed by two, kept inside its range. Both are subject to R5. (S1)
- **R14.** Reverse (a switch, off by default) plays the buffer backwards from the position it is at. Material added while Reverse is on is added forwards: when Reverse goes off, the original material plays forwards again and what was recorded under Reverse plays backwards. Turning Reverse over is subject to R5. (S1)
- **R15.** Feedback is the share of the buffer that survives a repetition while Transport is Overdub, from 0 to 100 %, 100 % by default. At 100 % the layers never fall; at 50 % each repetition halves what was there before it, so a layer recorded then is 1/2 after one pass, 1/4 after two, and so on. A change to the setting takes effect at the next repetition, and Feedback does nothing in Play, where every repetition sounds the same. (S1, S8)
- **R16.** Input → Output is Always, Never, Rec/OVR or Rec/OVR/Stop, Always by default, and decides when the input is heard: Always hears it in every state; Never never; Rec/OVR only while Transport is Record or Overdub; Rec/OVR/Stop in every state but Play. The input is heard at unity, added to whatever the buffer plays, so what the buffer plays is never taken away by it. (S1)
- **R17.** Quantization names a step of the launch quantisation menu, 1 Bar by default, and is what R5's grid line is counted on. The manual names the chooser but not its entries or its default: the list and the default are Beatlayer's own. **Not built yet:** the set's global launch quantisation cannot be named yet, so the chooser lists the menu's steps only. (S1, S5, S8)
- **R18.** Record Length is "x bars" by default, the open-ended length, or a fixed 1, 2, 4, 8, 16 or 32 bars. The list is Beatlayer's own: the manual names the open-ended default and fixed number of bars, not which ones. (S1, S8)
- **R19.** The control beside Record Length is a choice of Play and Overdub, Play by default, and names what Transport becomes when a fixed-length recording ends: with Play the buffer plays back, with Overdub the new layers go on. The node leaves Record on its own, so it asks for Transport to be written back (R24); until the shell serves that request the chooser still shows Record. The manual calls it "the button next to this chooser"; its name and default are Beatlayer's own. (S1, S8)
- **R20.** Tempo Control is None, Follow song tempo (the default) or Set & Follow song tempo. It decides what Looper's recorded tempo means. Looper keeps one: the tempo the buffer was recorded at, which is the song's tempo at the playhead when Record began, however long the recording is and however it ends, unless the song was stopped and R22 worked another out from the frames. (S1, S8)
- **R21.** With None, the buffer plays at the rate Speed gives and the song's tempo doesn't touch it, so a loop recorded at 120 BPM still plays as it was recorded when the song plays at 140. With Follow, the rate in effect is `song tempo ÷ recorded tempo`, times Speed, read at each stretch: a loop recorded at 120 BPM with Speed at 1 plays at 140/120 of its recorded speed while the song plays at 140 BPM, and follows every later change. With Set & Follow the rate is Follow's, and the song's tempo is set from the recording as R22 says. (S1, S8)
- **R22.** With a fixed Record Length and the song stopped, the frames recorded are taken as exactly that many bars, and that is Looper's recorded tempo: `bars × the bar's ticks ÷ the frames recorded`, through the tempo map (R7). With the open-ended length and the song stopped, the guess takes the recorded frames as the whole number of bars nearest to what Looper's tempo was when recording began, and that many bars over those frames is the recorded tempo, so a guess can come out twice or half what the person played. With Set & Follow, the song's tempo is set to the recorded tempo once it is known, as one undo step of its own, named `Change "Song Tempo"`; the manual doesn't say whether Live's tempo change is undoable, and this is Beatlayer's own answer. (S1, S6, S7, S8)
- **R23.** Song Control is None (the default), Start, or Start & Stop. With Start or Start & Stop, Looper entering Play or Overdub asks the song to start if it is stopped; with Start & Stop, entering Stop asks it to stop, on the frame the transport change acts on (R5). Looper never writes the song's tempo or transport itself: it asks, and the reply is the main thread's. The manual doesn't say how a device asks Live; Beatlayer's device publishes its request with its display data (R24/R26), and the shell runs it. Start while the song already plays does nothing, since there is nothing to start. (S1, S6, S8)
- **R24.** Looper publishes, with its display data, the state its display shows and one unserved request at a time: nothing, a start of the song, a stop of the song at a musical position, a tempo to set, or a Transport value to write back (after a fixed-length or full-buffer recording ends, after Clear resets Looper, and for a Looper reopened from a set saved in Record or Overdub, which starts stopped), each with a serial number that rises with every request. The shell reads the newest data of every Looper in the set as it reads any display, and serves each serial it hasn't served: a start runs `transport.play`, a stop schedules a `transport.stop` on its position, a tempo runs `song.set_tempo` as R22's undo step, and a Transport value is written to the device's Transport parameter outside the undo history. The node keeps matching the document's value, so the write changes nothing the node acts on. A request is served once, so a slow reader can't serve one twice, and an engine that stops leaves none behind. **Not built yet:** the shell's serving of a published request; the device's publishing of it, with its serial, is in and tested. (S4, S6, S8)
- **R25.** The Multi-Purpose Transport Button and Looper's other controls are registered commands, the ones R9 to R14 and R11 name, each with a schema, a `read` permission and a description, runnable by ID with `device` naming the Looper (the selected device if left out); they change no document value except R22's tempo, which its own rule makes an undo step, and each reads back through R26's readings and `device.parameters`. **Not built yet:** the commands themselves and their readback; the device side, the actions R9 to R14 name, is in and tested. (S4, S6, S8)
- **R26.** Looper's display cell is its own middle band, black like any cell: its data publishes the state, the position in the loop and the buffer's length in frames, and the recorded tempo. `device.display` reads them as readings: `State` (the transport state, or `Empty`), `Position` and `Length` in bars, beats and sixteenths through the tempo map (R7), and `Tempo` in BPM — so a person sees the position and length the manual describes while it records and plays, and an agent reads the same headlessly. (S1, S4)
- **R27.** A render is bit-identical at any block size, and repeats run after run; nothing in Looper's path depends on how blocks fall, and its buffer starts empty. (S4)
- **R28.** Looper is an Insert source and target for another track's Audio From and Audio To, as 29.25.1 describes, and Beatlayer adds the routing rules that fit it to [routing](routing.md). **Not built yet:** it waits on a later package, since this one runs past a large task's size; until then the routing spec lists it as a device entry that has to arrive with it. (S1, S2)

## Details

- The device type's ID is `beatlayer.looper`; its name is Looper, as Live's.
- Looper's parameters are Transport (Record, Overdub, Play, Stop), Quantization, Record Length, the Play/Overdub control beside it, Speed, Reverse, Feedback, Input → Output, Tempo Control and Song Control, in that order; R4 to R22 give each one's range, default and meaning.
- The buffer is two channels of 30 seconds at the node's rate, held planar as other device buffers are; nothing in it is touched between uses except by R6 to R12 and R17.
- The recorded tempo is a Bpm: 120 before anything is recorded, and the tempo a recording was made at afterwards.
- Looper's display data is a fixed run of values: the transport state, whether the buffer is empty, the position and length in frames, the recorded tempo in BPM, and the request (its kind, its serial, its musical position in ticks as two values, and its tempo). `bl_devices::display` turns the first of those into R26's readings and leaves the request to the shell.

## Not built yet

- **Looper's commands and the shell's serving of its requests (R11, R13, R24, R25).** The device side of Clear, Undo, Redo, ×2, ÷2 and the Multi-Purpose Transport Button's gestures is in and tested; the commands that reach it — with their schemas and readback — the two octave arrows (which set Speed) and the shell running a published start, quantised stop, tempo or Transport write-back wait for a following package.
- **The drag-out clip (29.25's Drag me! area).** Dragging the buffer out makes an audio clip from it, written off the audio thread and warped in Re-Pitch, so it plays as it was heard. This needs the audio thread to hand its buffer back to the main thread, which no engine path does yet: the display handoff is per-block and far too small. It waits for a following package.
- **The dropped file.** Audio dropped on Looper's display area replaces the buffer, decoded off the audio thread. It waits with the drag-out clip, for the same handover in reverse.
- **The feedback routing of 29.25.1 (R28).** The routing rules that let another track take Looper as an Insert source and feed its output back into Looper wait for a following package, as the task allows for a package that runs past its size.
- **Following the set's global launch quantisation (R17).** The model's launch quantisation has no Global choice to name, so the chooser lists the menu's steps only.
- **A save-and-reopen test, and a case in `rtsan/tests/devices.rs`.** The device's audio-thread path is covered by the allocation trap in every test of `crates/devices/tests/looper.rs`, and a set reopens with an empty buffer (R3, tested by building the node from a saved Record), but there is no case in `rtsan` and no test through a saved and reopened document yet.
