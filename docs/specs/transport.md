# Transport: play, stop, continue, the insert marker, and the Arrangement loop

- **Status:** implemented (R1 to R30, R35 and R36 each have a passing test)
- **Work packages:** WP-1.5 (R1 to R20); WP-6.24 (the song start time and the quantised locate that locators and the scrub area use, R21 and R22); WP-6.59 (the scrub area's held loop, R23); WP-7.30 (following an outside tempo, starts a follower decides, and Phase Nudge, R24 to R29); WP-7.12 and WP-7.14 hook an incoming clock and a Link session to R24 and R25; WP-6.74 (playing a time selection, R35 and R36)
- **Parity-matrix row:** Views and workflow: Arrangement View; Control Bar
- **Checked against:** the Live 12 reference manual, read 10 October 2026, and for R21 to R23 the online edition, read 20 October 2026; and the UI parity reference's observations of Live 12.4.3 (10 October 2026)

## The behaviour

The transport plays the Arrangement. Play starts playback from the insert marker, the flashing line a click in the Arrangement leaves; Stop stops it, and a second Stop returns to the start of the arrangement, 1.1.1; Continue Playback resumes where playback stopped rather than at the insert marker. The Arrangement Position fields in the Control Bar, the Home key, and Return Play Position to 1.1.1 move the insert marker. With the Arrangement Loop switch on, playback that reaches the end of the loop brace jumps back to its start, again and again, until the switch goes off or playback stops. The brace's start and length are musical positions (the Control Bar's Loop Start and Loop Length fields), so they follow the tempo map.

Beatlayer keeps three things in the engine: whether the song plays, the song position (the playhead: the next frame to play, which stays where playback stopped so Continue can resume there), and the insert marker. The loop brace and its switch are part of the set. The engine applies each transport action on an exact frame, splits its audio blocks where the transport starts, stops, jumps, or loops, and publishes the playhead for the UI after every block.

This spec covers the Arrangement's transport. It leaves to other specs and packages: time selections and Loop Selection (S-ARR-011: WP-1.8b), dragging and nudging the brace (I-DRAG-019: WP-1.8a; S-LOOP-007 to S-LOOP-010 and S-ARR-012: WP-6.73), the scrub area, whose jumps are quantised by the global launch quantisation (WP-1.8a), Follow (WP-1.8a), locators and Set Song Start Time Here (Phase 6), punch in and out and the metronome (Phase 2), and the Session View's launch quantisation, which decides when clips and scenes start (Phase 3). The transport here starts, stops, and jumps on the frame it's asked for, unquantised, as the Arrangement's controls do.

## Rules

- **R1.** Play starts playback from the insert marker. A new set's insert marker is at 1.1.1. (S1, S6)
- **R2.** A start is sample-exact: the first frame played is the frame the insert marker falls on through the tempo map (the nearest frame), and a start asked for on a frame of the engine clock happens on that frame, whatever the audio block size. (S1; WP-1.5's acceptance)
- **R3.** Stop, while playing, stops playback. The insert marker doesn't move, and the play position stays where playback stopped. (S1)
- **R4.** Stop, while stopped, returns the insert marker and the play position to 1.1.1. Live's double-click on Stop is a Stop that stops, then a Stop while stopped. (S1, S2)
- **R5.** Play/Stop (the space bar, and the Playback menu's Play item, which reads Stop while playing) stops while playing, as R3, and plays from the insert marker while stopped, as R1. It never returns to 1.1.1. (S1, S5, S6)
- **R6.** Continue Playback (Shift-Space) plays on from where playback last stopped, not from the insert marker. While playing, it does nothing. (S1, S5, S6)
- **R7.** Setting the play position (the Arrangement Position fields) moves the insert marker. While playing, playback jumps there at once. While stopped, nothing plays, and Continue still resumes where playback stopped. A position, a locate's, a scrub's or the insert marker's, is held between the song's start and a million quarter notes, so an absurd value from an agent can't overflow the song's frame arithmetic (Beatlayer's own). (S1, S7)
- **R8.** Return Play Position to 1.1.1 and the Home key (Fn-Left on a Mac) set the play position to 1.1.1, as R7 does. The menu item runs `transport.return_to_start` and Home runs `transport.locate` at 1.1.1 (added in WP-1.18). (S1, S2, S5, S6)
- **R9.** Move Insert Marker To Playhead (Cmd-Shift-Space, Ctrl-Shift-Space) puts the insert marker at the play position: where the song is playing, or where it stopped. (S5, S6)
- **R10.** Play From Insert Marker (Option-Space, Ctrl-Space, in the Arrangement) starts playback from the insert marker, as Play does while no song start time is set; with one set, it still starts from the insert marker (R21). While the Arrangement has a time selection, it plays the selection instead (R35). (S4, S5, S6)
- **R11.** Play, while playing, starts again from the insert marker.
- **R12.** The insert marker and the play position never go before 1.1.1: a position before it is taken as 1.1.1. (S1, S8)
- **R13.** With the loop on, playback that reaches the loop brace's end goes on from its start: the frame after the last one before the end is the start's frame, sample-exact at any block size. It loops until the loop is switched off or playback stops. (S3)
- **R14.** Playback that starts before the brace plays into it and then loops. Playback at or after the brace's end plays on and doesn't loop, including when the brace moves behind a playing playhead. (S3)
- **R15.** The brace's start and end are musical positions: they fall on frames through the tempo map, so playback wraps on the frame the map gives the end, even inside a tempo ramp, and a new tempo map moves the loop's frames with its bars while the playhead keeps its beat. A launched scene's tempo ([session-view.md](session-view.md) R27) moves them the same way, with the scheduled commands and the Session View's frames. (S3, S8)
- **R16.** The brace (its start and length) and the Loop switch belong to the set: they're saved with it, a set saved before Beatlayer had them loads with the default, and each change is one undo step. (S3, S7)
- **R17.** The brace starts at 1.1.1 or later and is longer than zero. A change that breaks either is refused and changes nothing. (S3, S8)
- **R18.** A new set's brace starts at 1.1.1 and is four 4/4 bars (16 quarter notes) long, with the loop off.
- **R19.** Every transport action is a registered command, runnable by ID with JSON arguments (D17). Playback commands are in the `read` class, because they change nothing in the project or on disk; the loop commands are `edit`, because undo reverses them. Their default keys: Space runs Play/Stop, Shift-Space Continue Playback, Home sets the play position to 1.1.1, Option-Space (Ctrl-Space) Play From Insert Marker in the Arrangement, and Cmd-Shift-Space (Ctrl-Shift-Space) Move Insert Marker To Playhead there. Play, Stop, and the loop commands have no key of their own in Live's shortcut list. (S5, S6)
- **R20.** After every audio block, the engine publishes for the UI, through a triple buffer and without a lock, whether it's playing, the playhead in frames and ticks, the insert marker, and the loop it plays, all from the same block, so the UI's playhead shows where the engine is. (WP-1.5's acceptance; the architecture's "Real-time rules")
- **R21.** While the set has a song start time ([time-editing.md](time-editing.md) R10), Play, and Play/Stop while stopped, start playback from it instead of from the insert marker or the time selection, and leave the insert marker where it is (Beatlayer's own). Play From Insert Marker (R10), Continue Playback (R6), a locate (R7) and a second Stop (R4) are as they were. (S9, S10)
- **R22.** A quantised locate (`transport.locate` with `quantized` set) moves the playback position as R7 does, except that while the song plays the jump waits for the next line of the global launch quantisation after the frame on which the engine receives it ([launch-quantisation.md](launch-quantisation.md) R13), with quantisation off or None at once, and lands on that line's exact frame at any block size. While stopped it locates at once. A second quantised locate before the line replaces the first (Beatlayer's own). A line at or past the Arrangement loop's end lands as the playhead wraps, and a tempo change while the jump waits keeps the line and the target on their beats. The engine works out the line from its own playhead, as it does for a clip's launch, with nothing allocated or locked. Locator jumps and clicks in the scrub area use it ([time-editing.md](time-editing.md) R5, R6, R11). (S11, S12)
- **R23.** A held scrub loop (`transport.scrub` with `held`, [time-editing.md](time-editing.md) R25) is a loop of its own beside the Arrangement loop: from the moment it's asked for, playback jumps, as R22's quantised locate does, to the span's start, and plays the span again and again, wrapping on its exact frames as R13 does, until it's released; then playback carries on from where it is, and the Arrangement loop applies again. While stopped, it starts playback at the span's start. The Arrangement loop's brace and switch don't change, and nothing is an undo step. (S13, S12)
- **R24.** While the song follows an outside tempo (a follower: an incoming MIDI clock with EXT on, [MIDI clock](midi-clock.md) R17; a Link session, [Link](link.md) R3 and R6), the engine plays each block at the tempo the follower gives for it instead of the tempo map's. Each block carries a song speed, how fast the song moves against the map, and every part of the engine that places things in song time honours it, so the song's ticks advance at the followed tempo across the block and everything placed at a musical position (clips' notes and warped audio, the metronome's clicks, the automation of every other parameter, the loop's end, the launch quantisation's lines, scheduled commands, clock out) falls on the frame the followed tempo gives it, at any block size, whatever tempo changes the map holds there. Unwarped audio plays at its own speed, as it does at any tempo. The tempo map's own tempo, its automation included, isn't played; positions, bars and the playhead's ticks are the song's as before, and only the frames between them change. The speed reaches the audio thread as a number per block, never as a new tempo map or program, so nothing is allocated or compiled for it, and any followed tempo from 20 to 999 BPM over any map works. A jump the follower asks for ([MIDI clock](midi-clock.md) R19, [Link](link.md) R6) is a locate on its frame, as R7's while playing. An offline render has no follower. Each event sits on a whole frame of the tempo map, so where the map is much faster than the followed tempo, one of its frames spans several played frames and an event can land half that many from where the followed tempo puts its beat (about 25 frames at 20 BPM over a 999 BPM map); a clip that plays at its own speed starts on the frame its start is nearest and lasts as many frames as it is long. (S14; Beatlayer's design)
- **R25.** A follower may decide when a start happens. Then a start (R1, R6, R10, R21, or a Session launch while stopped) waits for the frame the follower gives and plays from the start position on that frame; Stop while it waits cancels it, and a second start keeps the wait; Stop, or Play/Stop, while the song plays and a restart waits stops the song and cancels the restart. A follower's own start or stop acts as Play, Continue Playback or Stop does, on the frame it gives. The engine publishes a start that waits, and the frame it waits for, with the playhead (R20). An incoming clock's Start, Continue and Stop ([MIDI clock](midi-clock.md) R22) and a Link session with peers ([Link](link.md) R7, R8) decide starts this way; with no follower, starts happen as R1 to R6 and R21 say. (S14; Beatlayer's design)
- **R26.** When following ends (EXT off, the last sync input off, Link off, the follower gone), the next block plays at the tempo map's tempo at the play position, with a speed of one: the song keeps its beat, and the frames after it move with the map, as they do when a new tempo map arrives. Nothing in the set changes. (S14; Beatlayer's design)
- **R27.** While Phase Nudge Up or Phase Nudge Down is held and the song plays, the song plays 4 % faster or slower than the tempo it otherwise plays at (the tempo map's, with no follower), through the same song speed as R24: the Tempo field, the tempo map, the set and its automation don't change, and on release the next block plays at that tempo again, the song's beat staying where the nudge moved it. With both held, the one pressed last nudges. While stopped, a nudge does nothing. (S15; the 4 % and both held are Beatlayer's own)
- **R28.** The Control Bar's Phase Nudge Down and Up buttons (L-CTRL-006) nudge while the mouse holds them, and show pressed while they do. Each press and release runs `transport.phase_nudge` with a `direction` (`down` or `up`) and `held` (true on the press, false on the release), a `read`-class command as R19's are, since it changes nothing in the set or on disk; `transport.state` reports `phase_nudge` (none, down or up). A nudge still held when the window loses the mouse, or when the agent that started it disconnects, ends; an agent can't schedule a held press, which has no session to end with (releasing one can be scheduled). The buttons are key- and MIDI-mappable as momentary controls ([map modes](map-modes.md)); Live binds no key to them. In the accessibility tree they are buttons named Phase Nudge Down and Phase Nudge Up, their pressed state reported. (S15, S16; ending a held nudge and the names are Beatlayer's own)
- **R29.** While an incoming clock or a Link session with at least one other peer leads (R24), the Phase Nudge buttons are disabled and `transport.phase_nudge` is refused, because the follower keeps the phase ([MIDI clock](midi-clock.md) R19, [Link](link.md) R6). (S14, S15; Beatlayer's own)
- **R30.** F9 (S-TRANS-006; Playback ▸ Record to Arrangement, M-PLAY-007) switches the transport's recording to the other state: `transport.set_recording` with `recording` left out, as a person's key gives it, switches it, and an agent still names the state. Shift+F9 (S-TRANS-007; M-PLAY-008) arms the selected tracks: `mixer.set_arm` with `arm` left out toggles the first selected track's switch and sets the others to match, as C does (S-TRACK-013). That Arm Recording to Arrangement means arming the selected tracks is the maintainers' reading of the shortcut, not an observation of Live's. (S17)
- **R35.** Option-Space (Ctrl-Space on Windows and Linux; S-TRANS-003) with a time selection plays from the selection's start and stops on the selection's end, on that exact frame at any block size, with the play position resting there. The insert marker doesn't move. The Arrangement loop doesn't apply while it plays: the Loop switch reads off (`transport.state` has `loop_on` false) and playback goes straight through the loop's end, whether the selection lies before, inside or after the loop. When the song stops, at the end or by Stop, Play/Stop, Play or a new start, the switch reads on again if the set's loop is on; the set's loop itself never changes, and nothing is an undo step. With no time selection, Option-Space plays from the insert marker and doesn't stop (R10). The engine runs it as `transport.play_selection` with a `start` and an `end` in ticks, which an agent runs by ID; the app turns the key into that command from the Arrangement's time selection, as Cmd-L's Loop Selection reads it. A selection with no length plays as no selection does. (S18; the engine command, and a jump past the end that stops playback, are Beatlayer's own)
- **R36.** Space (S-TRANS-004, the same key as Play/Stop, S-TRANS-001) and the Control Bar's Play, with a time selection, play from the selection's start and go on past its end, with the Arrangement loop applying as it always does. Stopping and pressing Space again plays from the selection's start again, as the insert marker doesn't move. While stopped, Play/Stop does this; while playing, it stops (R5). `transport.play_selection` with no `end` does the same, and with a song start time set (R21) it starts from that instead, as Play does. (S18; the song start time winning over the selection follows R21, which names it, and is Beatlayer's own)
