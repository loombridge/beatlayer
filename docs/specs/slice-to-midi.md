# Slice to New MIDI Track

- **Status:** partial (R1 to R14 implemented, R7 reworded for WP-7.33; R15 to R17 drafts for WP-7.33b)
- **Work packages:** WP-4.16 (R1 to R14); WP-7.33 (R7's samplers on frame-exact regions); WP-7.33b (Simpler's own Slice to Drum Rack and Slice to New MIDI Track, R15 to R17)
- **Parity-matrix row:** Audio clips and warping: Slice to New MIDI Track (transients, warp markers, beat divisions, slicing presets)
- **Checked against:** the Live 12 reference manual (online edition), read 17 October 2026, and the UI parity reference's observations of Live 12.4.3 on macOS (10 October 2026); for R15 to R17, the online edition read 23 October 2026 (sections 13.1, 31.11.1.3 and 31.11.7)

## The behaviour

Slice to New MIDI Track cuts an audio clip into slices by time, ignoring what the audio holds musically, and gives each slice a note of its own. With an audio clip selected, the command (in the Create menu and the clip's menu) opens a dialog: slice at a beat division, at the clip's transients, or at its warp markers; choose a slicing preset; and choose whether the slices keep the clip's warped timing. Beatlayer then makes a new MIDI track holding a Drum Rack with one chain per slice, each chain a sampler playing its slice, and a MIDI clip with one note per slice, rising chromatically, so the clip plays the slices back in their order and timing. Reordering the clip's notes, or swapping pads, resequences the slices. Beatlayer also offers a preset of its own that slices into one sampler in its Slice mode instead of a Drum Rack.

A sampler in Slice mode offers the same in its own menu, from the slices it already has: Slice to Drum Rack replaces the sampler with a Drum Rack of its slices, so the track's clips play the same slices from pads, and Slice to New MIDI Track leaves it and makes a new track with such a Drum Rack and a clip that plays the slices in order.

This spec covers the command, the dialog, where slices fall, the track, devices, and clip it makes, and slicing presets. It leaves to other specs: what a Drum Rack is and does ([drum racks](drum-racks.md)), what Beatlayer's sampler and its Slice mode play ([sampler](sampler.md)), where a file's transients and warp markers come from ([warping](warping.md)), and the Convert to New MIDI Track commands beside it in the menus (Phase 9). The sampler's own commands (R15 to R17) take their slices from the sampler.

## Rules

- **R1.** Slice to New MIDI Track (Create ▸ Slice to New MIDI Track, M-CREATE-011; an audio clip's menus, C-CLIP-SES-019 and C-CLIP-ARR-027) is enabled with one audio clip selected, in either view, and disabled otherwise. It opens the slicing dialog, a modal window. (S1, S5)
- **R2.** The dialog holds, in this order (Beatlayer's own), the Slice By chooser, the Slicing Preset chooser, the Preserve warped timing switch, and Cancel and OK. Slice By offers the beat divisions 8 Bars, 4 Bars, 2 Bars, 1 Bar, 1/2, 1/4, 1/8, 1/16, and 1/32 (the Arrangement's Fixed Grid choices, Beatlayer's own list), then Transients, then Warp Markers; it opens on 1/16 and Preserve warped timing on (Beatlayer's own defaults). Warp Markers isn't offered for an unwarped clip. (S1, S5)
- **R3.** The clip is sliced over its region, the part of its file it plays: from its Start marker to its End marker, or with the loop on, from the earlier of the Start marker and the loop's start to the loop's end, as Crop takes it (audio clip editing R14). Beatlayer's own: the manual doesn't say which part is sliced. (S6)
- **R4.** The first slice starts at the region's start. With Transients, another starts at each of the file's transients inside the region; with Warp Markers, at each of the clip's warp markers inside it; with a beat division, at every division from the region's start, counted in the clip's beats, or for an unwarped clip in the set's tempo from the clip's start (Beatlayer's own). Each slice runs to the next one's start, and the last to the region's end. (S1, S6)
- **R5.** Slicing into more than 128 slices is refused, and the dialog says to choose a coarser division or slice a shorter clip (S1). The sampler-in-Slice-mode preset (R10) takes at most 64 slices, the sampler's limit, and is refused past it. (S1, S3, S6)
- **R6.** With Preserve warped timing on, the slices are cut from the clip's warped audio: a file rendered from the clip as Consolidate renders it (audio clip editing R20, R1 to R6), at unity gain and without its fades, so each slice sounds as it did in the clip, and its note falls where the slice played. With it off, they are cut from the clip's own file, unwarped, and each note falls where its slice starts in the file, counted from the clip's start in the set's tempo. Beatlayer's own: how the warped slices are made. (S1)
- **R7.** The new MIDI track goes directly after the clip's track, takes the clip's name, and is selected (Beatlayer's own). With a Drum Rack preset (R9), it holds a Drum Rack ([drum racks](drum-racks.md)) of one chain per slice, in order; each chain holds Beatlayer's sampler ([sampler](sampler.md)) in One-Shot mode, with Trigger on, playing exactly its slice, its region landing on the slice's first and last frames; each chain receives its slice's note and plays C3, so the slice sounds at its own pitch (drum racks R25). The rack's macros are mapped as the preset says, each moving that parameter on every chain's sampler. Each sampler's region flags ([sampler](sampler.md) R22) sit on its slice's first frame and on the frame its slice ends. (S1, S4, S6)
- **R8.** The slices take notes rising chromatically from C1 (note 36), the first pad a new Drum Rack shows (drum racks R11; Beatlayer's own, the manual saying only that the order is chromatic). A slicing of more than 92 slices would pass G8, the highest MIDI note, so the staircase wraps to the lowest note and carries on (Beatlayer's own). (S1, S6)
- **R9.** The MIDI clip goes where the audio clip is: in the Arrangement at its start and with its length, or in the Session View in the new track's slot of the same scene. It holds one note per slice, at the time its slice plays (R6), lasting until the next slice's note, the last until the region's end, at velocity 127 (Beatlayer's own), so the notes rise as a staircase and playing the clip plays the slices in order, each within 1 ms of where the audio clip played it. The clip loops when the audio clip does, its loop brace over the whole clip. (S1, S2)
- **R10.** Beatlayer's own, beside Live's (rule 1 of the improvements): a factory preset that slices into one sampler in Slice mode instead of a Drum Rack. The track then holds one sampler whose slices are the slice points (sampler R13), the first slice on C3, and the clip's notes rise from C3. (S3, S6, S7)
- **R11.** The Slicing Preset chooser lists Beatlayer's factory presets, its own content, first (the Drum Rack preset, the default, and R10's), then the user's presets in the User Library's `Defaults/Slicing/` folder. A user preset is a Drum Rack preset whose first chain is the pattern: each slice's chain copies that chain's devices, with the first sampler in it loaded with the slice, and the rack keeps the preset's macros and their mappings. A preset whose first chain holds no sampler is refused. Beatlayer's own: the folder's name and the pattern chain. (S1, S6)
- **R12.** Slicing leaves the audio clip and its track as they were (Beatlayer's own). (S1)
- **R13.** `clip.slice_to_midi_track` takes the clip, `by` (a division, `transients`, or `warp_markers`), `preset`, and `preserve_warped_timing`, and returns the new track, its clip, and how many slices it made; the dialog's OK runs it. It's a registered command in the `edit` class, one undo step, reading back through the query tool's `song`, field `tracks`. (S7)
- **R14.** Slicing runs off the audio thread, with its progress in the Status Bar; the new track's samplers play under the real-time rules; a set with a sliced track saves and reopens with the same track, devices, slices, and clip, and plays the same. (S7)
- **R15.** Slice to Drum Rack, in a sampler's title bar menu and its sample display's menu among its own options (C-DEVICE-015), is enabled while the sampler is in Slice mode with at least one slice. It replaces the sampler, in its place in its chain and with its name, with a Drum Rack of one chain per slice, in order. Each chain receives the note its slice played in the sampler ([sampler](sampler.md) R13), plays C3, and holds a copy of the sampler (its sample with its edits, its warp settings and every parameter) in One-Shot mode, its Trigger switch and fades as the sampler's, with its region flags on its slice's first frame and on the frame its slice ends, or, under Thru, on the region's End flag. Under Mono and Thru every chain is in one choke group ([drum racks](drum-racks.md) R4), so one slice sounds at a time as before; under Poly, none is (Beatlayer's own). The track's clips then play the same slices. The sampler's own automation and mappings go with it, as deleting it would take them (Beatlayer's own). It is one undo step. (S3, S8)
- **R16.** Slice to New MIDI Track, in the same menus and enabled the same way, leaves the sampler as it is and makes a new MIDI track directly after the sampler's track, named after the sampler and selected, holding a Drum Rack made as R15 makes one, and a MIDI clip that plays the slices in order: one note per slice on its chain's Receive note, at the time its slice starts in the region at the sample's tempo (its warp tempo while Warp is on, [sampler](sampler.md) R18, or the set's tempo while it's off, as Slice By's Beat counts, [sampler](sampler.md) R14), lasting until the next slice's note, the last until the region's end, at velocity 127 as R9's notes, the clip as long as the region and looping over all of it. It goes in the new track's slot of the selected scene while the Session View shows, or at the insert marker in the Arrangement (Beatlayer's own). It is one undo step. (S3, S8)
- **R17.** `device.slice_to_drum_rack` and `device.slice_to_midi_track` are registered commands in the `edit` class, taking the sampler: the first returns the new Drum Rack and its chains and reads back through the query tool's `track`, field `devices`; the second returns the new track, its clip and the number of slices, reading back through `song`, field `tracks`. Each is refused, with the reason, unless the device is a sampler in Slice mode with a slice. Both run off the audio thread, their samplers play under the real-time rules, and a set holding what they made saves, reopens and plays the same, as R14 says. (S7)
